Elision modes are style properties; group elisions belong to the frame#

Status: accepted · Date: 2026-11-23

Context#

An elision leaves a stretch of one timeline out of a drawing: every row crossing the stretch stops at its start and resumes at its end, with a narrow gap between holding one ellipsis (TimeToShare’s drawing specification, sections 2 and 8.9). A manifest stores its elisions in its graphical object, each on one timeline. A root is drawn whole in up to several cards: its own card when it is in no group, and an ancestor card behind the frame of every group one of its descendants is a member of (section 8.8). For those cards the editor lets the user choose what is elided: nothing, the stored elisions, or gaps an ancestor card derives by itself around its member, at a proportion and on rows the user may set.

Two questions follow. Where does that choice live? And where is an elision drawn whose timeline lies in a group, that is a member or a member’s descendant, which its member card draws but which the frame’s other member cards share an axis with?

The requirements:

  1. The manifest draws what the editor shows: an export, and any other implementation of the drawing specification, draws the elisions the user chose. A manifest holds musical time and its graphics; settings of the workspace and of the viewer change nothing drawn.

  2. One choice per root: every card of a root shares one address ({id: R, part: ["card"]}), so a statement about the root’s cards reaches all of them; a stored elision of the root must not be drawn on one of its cards and ignored on another for a reason the user cannot see.

  3. Switching loses nothing: the stored elisions stay while another choice is drawn, and return with it.

  4. One command per switch, undone in one step, through the four commands only.

  5. A frame keeps one axis: the member cards of a frame convert linearly between their shared start and end (TimeToShare’s README, “Groups and the graphical object”); an elision drawn in one member card alone would break that.

Considered options#

  • A workspace setting per block or per root: the choice would stay in the workspace file, so an exported manifest would draw differently from the canvas and another program could not reproduce it (1).

  • A member of the manifest beside the elisions (a mode on a timeline, or a flag on each elision): a new schema key for what the drawing does, read by no rule of the cascade; a choice of “the gaps the card derives” would have no stored object to hang on, and switching would rewrite or delete stored elisions (3).

  • Properties of the style cascade on the cards: four properties, elision-mode (auto, full, proportional, custom), elision-proportion, elision-depths and elision-display, never inherited, set by one rule on the root’s card address, the rule that already carries the display hiding its ancestor cards. The cascade already gives layers (the specification’s defaults, the workspace’s rules, the manifest’s own), rule ranks and named styles; the manifest carries the rule, so an export draws it (1); the address reaches every card of the root (2); the stored elisions are untouched by a switch (3); a switch is one style (4).

  • The same properties chosen per card: the cards of a root have no address of their own by design, and one card drawing a root’s stored elisions while another derives its own would contradict (2).

For an elision whose timeline lies in a group:

  • Drawn in the member card alone: breaks the frame’s shared axis (5).

  • Marked as a group’s elision in the manifest: a stored fact that the timeline already states; a timeline lies in a group exactly when a groupstamp on it would cross the frame, so the marker could only disagree with it.

  • Held by the frame: read from the timeline, never stored, an elision whose timeline lies in a group is that group’s. It is drawn across the frame, at one place in every member card, with one width for all of them, the frame drawn shorter by its gaps, and carried into the ancestor cards behind the frame over their member’s span. The frame reads elision-mode as an ungrouped root’s card does, from a rule on its own address {id: G}.

Decision#

Elision modes are style properties, and group elisions belong to the frame:

  • The drawing specification defines the four properties, their initial values (auto for the first three, ellipsis name for elision-display) and what each container reads (section 2.4): elision-mode by a card, an ancestor card and a frame, the other three by a card and an ancestor card. auto draws the stored card elisions where the root’s tree holds any and the derived gaps otherwise, so a manifest storing elisions draws them without a rule.

  • A group elision is never counted for auto and never switched by a card’s mode: an ancestor card carries the frame’s gaps in every mode. A root that is itself a member of a group has only group elisions, so the editor offers Custom on none of its cards and says why.

  • Hidden is the rule’s display: none, apart from the mode, so hiding the ancestor cards keeps what they draw when they return.

  • The editor writes every switch as one style of the root’s rule or the frame’s, and the switch from the derived gaps to Custom as one add of those gaps exactly as drawn; it reads what a mode resolves to, the proportion and every gap from layout’s record of each container, Drawing.elisions, never from a drawing tried with a command applied.

  • Core reads no cascade: it lists the stored elisions (elisionsOn, elisionsUnder, groupElisions) and every timeline a card may derive gaps on (defaultElisionTimelines), where the addresses of derived gaps resolve.

Consequences#

  • A derived gap’s proportion may be auto, the largest at which the gaps fit beside the member’s line and the card fits into the view centred on the frame: the one value of a drawing that reads the placement’s view. Each canvas therefore draws at its own pane, settled after its size stops changing; a stated proportion makes the drawing independent of the view.

  • A rule written by hand, or by the workspace’s defaults, sets the modes of many roots at once, as any style rule does.

  • No new command and no new schema key beyond the four properties; no dependency.

Review this decision if an application needs different elisions on two cards of one root, by 2027-05-23.

Primary sources: TimeToAlign/TimeToShare (sections 2, 2.4, 8.8 and 8.9).
