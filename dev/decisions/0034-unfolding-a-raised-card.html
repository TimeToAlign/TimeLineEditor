Unfold a raised ancestor card from its member card by one tweened progress#

Status: accepted · Date: 2026-11-22

Context#

A group member that is a child timeline is drawn in its member card with the faded lines of its ancestors, and its root is drawn whole in an ancestor card behind the group’s frame (TimeToShare’s drawing specification, section 8.8). When the root’s timeline editor opens, the canvas raises that ancestor card over the frame; when it closes, the card falls back behind the frame. Raising changes only the order the block shows its drawing in (stackingOf, raisedLayers); the drawing itself is the same. The member card and the raised card draw the same timelines at different ordinates: the raised card’s root line lies on the member card’s ancestor line of it, so the member’s own row and every timeline between lie some units off their rows in the member card, more with conversion maps or bands between. Rows of the raised card that the member card does not draw (siblings, the root’s bands beyond the member) appear with it, and what the member card alone draws goes.

The switch is abrupt: the user does not see that the raised card is the member card’s own timelines, laid out at the root’s proportions. The raise should read as one card unfolding into the other, and the close as the same motion backwards.

The requirements:

  1. Rows travel: each row of the raised card starts where the member card draws the same timeline and moves to its own place; a row the member card does not draw unfolds out of the gap between its neighbours and blends in; what the member card alone draws blends out. The card opens left and right from the member card’s rectangle to its own.

  2. The layout’s geometry: the motion is read from what layout records (each drawn line’s card and ordinate, each box’s rectangle and range), never from offsets written in the canvas, so it follows any layout.

  3. Interruption: closing during the opening reverses from where the motion stands, and the reverse takes the time the distance left needs.

  4. Reduced motion: under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce the card switches at once.

  5. Input: the canvas never waits for a motion; what the pointer reads is the drawing at rest.

  6. Reuse: one small module, its pure part testable without a DOM.

Considered options#

  • FLIP over the drawn elements (Svelte’s animate: and the arranger’s own slide, measuring each element’s screen rectangle before and after an update): FLIP moves one element from where it was to where it is. The raise moves no element: the raised card’s elements never were in the member card, whose own elements stay where they are. The pairing is by timeline, between two different sets of elements, so there is nothing for FLIP to measure.

  • Svelte transitions (in:, out:, transition:): they run when an element enters or leaves the DOM. The raised card’s elements are drawn before, during and after the raise; only their layer changes.

  • Web Animations on each element (as the arranger’s settle moves a dropped card’s copy): each element would need its own keyframes, and a reversal would read every animation’s current time. Animating a clip rectangle’s x, y, width and height needs them as CSS properties of an SVG rect, which the engines do not support alike.

  • Interpolating between two drawings by one progress value: a pure function pairs the member card’s rows with the raised card’s by timeline ID and gives, for a progress t from 0 (the member card’s places) to 1 (the drawing at rest), each element’s vertical shift and opacity and the clip rectangle. One reactive number drives t; the block node writes the results as SVG attributes (transform, opacity, a clipPath), which every engine draws alike. Reversal is that number set back to 0: it starts from its current value.

  • Svelte’s Tween or Spring (svelte/motion) to drive t: a Tween does what is needed, but loading svelte/motion makes its prefersReducedMotion media query at once, so every module loading it needs a matchMedia before it is imported, which the canvas’s component tests give the page only when they mount it; the canvas already reads reduced motion from a MediaQuery it makes when it mounts. A Spring would also overshoot the rows past their places and has no fixed duration. The number is driven by requestAnimationFrame in a few lines instead, eased by svelte/easing’s cubicInOut.

Decision#

Interpolate between the two drawings by one tweened progress, with no new dependency:

  • app/packages/arranger/src/animation/unfold.ts, pure and independent of TiLiE’s drawing: pairRows(from, to) pairs two lists of rows by key and gives each row of to its shift from − to (a row from lacks takes the shift interpolated between its paired neighbours by its ordinate, the nearest one’s beyond them, and enters); frameAt(motion, t) (shift × (1 − t), opacity 1 for a paired row and t for an entering one or for an element of no row); rectAt(from, to, t); durationOf(from, to, reduced) (UNFOLD_DURATION 400 ms for the whole way, in proportion for a part of it, 0 under reduced motion); easing cubicInOut (UNFOLD_EASING).

  • app/packages/arranger/src/animation/raise.ts, the canvas’s reading: raiseUnfold(manifest, drawing, card, measure) takes the rows of the member card the ancestor card is aligned to (DrawnBox.ancestor) and of the ancestor card from layout’s lineAxes, the clip’s start from the member card’s rectangle and its end from the bounds of everything the ancestor card draws, and gives each element of the card’s range the motion of the timeline it belongs to (core’s indexOf(…).owners); the card’s rectangle and shadow belong to no row and blend in, so the member card beneath blends out.

  • app/packages/arranger/src/animation/unfolding.svelte.ts, the binding: class Unfolding(raised, reduced) follows the raised root, holds the root folding back until its progress reaches 0, unfolds a newly raised root from 0 and reverses from the current value; its progress is a Progress (progress.svelte.ts, the one progress of every motion between two drawings), a $state number moved over animation frames (requestAnimationFrame, performance.now()), stopped when the block node is destroyed.

  • BlockNode.svelte draws each raised card in its own layer (g.tilie-raised), clipped and shifted while it moves, letting the pointer through while it moves. The canvas’s hit tests, marks and commands read the stacking of the open editor at once (raisedRoot), never the motion.

A switch from one raised root to another unfolds the new one from 0 and drops the old one at once.

Elisions moving sideways#

Elisions change a card’s horizontal axis, never its rows: a mode switched on a root or a frame, a gap added, removed, moved or widened, or another proportion of an ancestor card’s derived gaps moves what the card draws left or right, and an ancestor card hidden or shown takes the whole card away or brings it. The same switch would hide what moved, so the same requirements hold, read sideways: each element starts where the drawing before drew its positions and moves to where the drawing after draws them; an ellipsis or a label inside a gap that stays travels with the gap, one inside a new gap enters in place; rows move only where elision labels shown or hidden change them; the canvas never waits; reduced motion switches at once.

The same progress and the same pure module serve it, extended by what a sideways motion needs:

  • unfold.ts adds the horizontal reading: Knot (an abscissa and the position of the card’s root drawn there), positionAt and abscissaAt over a card’s knots, startOf (where the drawing before draws what an extent of the drawing after draws), spanAt, and Placed, an element scaled and shifted along x, shifted along y and faded, with placedAt, framePlaced and composed, which combines a raise’s frame with a sideways motion.

  • animation/elide.ts reads layout’s records into those knots: both ends of every line layout records in a card (one per run of an elided line) and both edges of its own gaps, each at its position on the card’s root; a frame’s knots from the runs of its exposure. blockMotion(before, after, measure) gives each card and frame whose elisions changed its motion (sidewaysMotion, frameMotion), an ancestor card newly shown the raise’s unfolding with what lies outside its member’s span opening out of the member card’s ends (unfoldingMotion), and a card no longer shown its drawing before, folding back (leaving).

  • animation/cardMotions.svelte.ts, the binding: class CardMotions follows a block’s drawing with an $effect.pre and, where the drawing changes, sets the motion from the drawing before (the one other place a state is written in an effect, as Unfolding sets its progress), then runs a Progress from 0 to 1. Nothing moves for a block’s first drawing, an unchanged one, under reduced motion, or while the drawing is a gesture’s or a control’s preview, which must lie where the pointer puts it; a change during a motion starts from the drawing it replaces. BlockNode.svelte places every element it holds by placedOf, letting the pointer through while it moves, composes it with the raise on a raised card, and draws the leaving cards after the block’s rectangle.

Maintenance and dependencies#

No dependency: cubicInOut is Svelte’s own (svelte/easing, 5.57.1, the version the lockfile holds). The pure modules are tested with exact values (tests/unfold.test.ts), the binding under Vitest’s fake timers, which fake requestAnimationFrame and performance.now (tests/unfolding.svelte.test.ts); the end-to-end spec ancestor-cards.spec.ts asserts the states at rest before and after the editor is open, the first state of a raise and that it moved, and the plain switch under reduced motion. Review the choice of the progress if svelte/motion stops making its media query at load, by 2027-05-22. Another motion of the canvas between two drawings reuses unfold.ts with its own reading of the records.

Primary sources: https://svelte.dev/docs/svelte/svelte-motion, https://svelte.dev/docs/svelte/svelte-animate, https://www.w3.org/TR/SVG2/masking.html#ClipPathElement, https://www.w3.org/TR/css-masking-1/#the-clip-path.
