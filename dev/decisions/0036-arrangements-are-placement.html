Arrangements are placement; a root drawn once along the row#

Status: accepted · Date: 2026-11-23

Context#

The arranger places blocks freely on the canvas, and the bundle editor places a block’s items, its groups’ frames and its ungrouped roots’ cards, freely in the block. Two needs go beyond free places: blocks lined up without placing each by hand, and a bundle’s groups read along a timeline they share. The second is the common case of a bundle: the movements of a score, or the takes of a session, are children of one root, each a member of its own group beside the timelines aligned to it. Each such root is drawn whole as an ancestor card behind each group’s frame (TimeToShare’s drawing specification, section 8.8), so groups one under another draw the same root once per group, each copy at the scale of its own frame.

Two questions follow. Where does an arrangement live? And when groups stand side by side, how is a root several of them hang on drawn?

The requirements:

  1. A manifest knows nothing about where it is shown (the three layers of AGENTS.md): it holds musical time and its graphics, never a canvas’s layout.

  2. The canvas draws what an export draws: the drawing specification defines every input of a drawing, so another program given the same inputs draws the same.

  3. One command per change, undone in one step, through the four commands only.

  4. Proportions are kept: under a group’s frame a root is drawn at that frame’s scale, and wherever the scale changes the drawing shows it.

  5. Predictable: what is drawn follows from the rules and from orders the user chose, never from an order the user cannot see; where the rules cannot hold, the user is told why.

  6. Switching loses nothing: an item’s stated place survives an arrangement and returns when the block is free again.

Considered options#

Where an arrangement lives:

  • A key of the manifest, in its graphical object: a manifest would carry where its items stand on one canvas (1), and two blocks of one manifest could not be arranged apart.

  • A workspace setting, outside the journal: an arrangement changes what is drawn, so it would escape undo (3), and a script drawing the block from its manifest and placement would not reproduce it (2).

  • A member of the placement, the drawing’s input that already holds the stated places (places): arrangement beside it, stored with the block and set by a journalled set; the blocks’ own arrangement a member of the workspace, set the same way.

How a shared root is drawn side by side:

  • Each group keeps its own ancestor card, as one under another: the root drawn once per group, the copies side by side at different scales, so no line reads the shared timeline across the groups.

  • Partial: groups whose order along a root conflicts keep their own copy, the others share one. Which group keeps a copy depends on an order of the groups the user never chose (5).

  • Free places, the root stretched between them: the stretches of the root outside the frames would be drawn at scales the frames do not show, and moving a group would stretch the root (4).

  • One row, the root drawn once: the groups in one row, every root they hang on drawn once behind it, the root at each group’s frame’s scale over that group and changing scale only between two groups, in a gap that leaves out what the room cannot show, or at a seam where nothing needs leaving out; where no row satisfies the rules, horizontal is refused with the reason.

Decision#

Arrangements are placement, and a root that groups hang on is drawn once along the row:

  • A block’s arrangement is a member of its placement (the drawing specification’s section 1): null, or {direction, order}, vertical or horizontal, order the IDs of its groups and ungrouped roots. The workspace’s arrangement of its blocks is null or {direction, columns?}, vertical, horizontal or matrix, packed in the blocks’ order. Each is one set; an arranged content keeps its stated place and does not use it.

  • Side by side, a group hangs on a root when one of its members lies inside it; its first such member anchors it there, over that member’s stretch on the root. Along every root the stretches follow the row without overlapping, and in every group the anchors lie in the order of their roots. Core decides the row and the roots’ order from these rules (rowOf), keeping the order given wherever they leave it free, and stores a horizontal arrangement as its row; one the rules refuse is refused (arrangement_conflict) with the conflict’s reason. A stored horizontal arrangement that a later edit breaks is drawn one item under another, the drawing stating the conflict, and returns when the conflict is gone.

  • Each root is drawn once, by its ancestor card behind the first group of the row hanging on it, the once card; its line is drawn in runs, one per anchor at that anchor’s frame’s scale, and between two anchors in a gap of whole units or, where none fits, at a seam, a jump of the axis that leaves out nothing and is no element of the drawing.

  • Matrix arranges blocks only: in a block, ancestor cards reach past their frames and would cross their neighbours in a grid.

  • In a group’s editor, while its block stands side by side, the cards anchoring the group are pinned and a drop that would change an anchor is refused, so the roots’ order stays what the groups make it.

Consequences#

  • The workspace format kept its version: an absent arrangement is none, a file stating null is read as none and none is written, so every file written before keeps its bytes.

  • A once card has one exposure: the editor lists the gaps the row cuts in it read-only, its stored card elisions between its first and last anchor are kept and not drawn, and its derived elisions take the least proportion of its groups.

  • The specification’s fixtures pin the drawing: the arranged bundle drawn horizontal is derived by hand, and a bundle whose groups overlap on their root, asked horizontal, draws as it does without an arrangement.

  • No new command, no schema key and no dependency.

Review this decision if a bundle needs groups side by side along roots in an order the rules refuse, by 2027-05-23.
