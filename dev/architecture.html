Architecture#

Overview#

TimeLineEditor (TiLiE) is a browser editor and visualization tool for musical timelines represented by timetoalign. It works with three layers, each named by one word only:

  • a manifest is one TimeToShare manifest as the TimeToShare schema (version 0.2.0) describes it, of the one shape every manifest has, {timetoshare, timelines, "@context"?, id?, name?, meta?, groups?, measure_maps?, resources?, flow_maps?, virtual_timelines?, statements?, provenance?, graphical?}, whatever it holds: no timeline, one root timeline, several, a group, or several groups beside ungrouped timelines. Every manifest is an alignment bundle. Its statements say what the manifest itself, every timeline and every region stand for: statements, property–value pairs about that thing (a work, a recording, an edition, a performer), and provenance, the annotations of those pairs (who stated one, when, how, on what basis, and who believes it, true, unlikely or false), under an inline JSON-LD @context, as TimeToShare’s description specification defines them; the drawing does not read them. A timeline has exact coordinates, children at exact offsets, conversion maps, regions and flow control, and a length that may be null, not known; its flow control is a list of elements in five roles, each at a coordinate: a jump, whose destinations (targets) each go to a jump target, a region, a child or the timeline itself, or by a distance, a jump target, a break, a section break and a barline; an element some actor believes false in its provenance is rejected, and no flow reads it; the manifest’s flow_maps and virtual_timelines hold the orders in which a root’s stretches are played and the timelines they make; the measure maps its conversion maps read belong to the manifest, not to a timeline; graphical holds the style rules of the manifest’s own drawing, its groups’ display orders, its timelines’ band orders, its named timestamps (a named position of a timeline, drawn as its display lists: a wedge, stacked labels of its name, ID and coordinate, and a cross-section line, which a groupstamp’s runs across every member card of its group’s frame) and its named MatchStamps (alignment anchors, each joining two match timestamps on two timelines, with the IDs of the MatchClaims behind them) and its elisions (each a stretch of one timeline left out of the drawing, drawn as a narrow gap holding one ellipsis, with a name, a width and a display; an elision on a timeline that lies in a group is that group’s and elides its frame, every other one elides the cards that draw its timeline’s root whole; Elisions); resources lists the files that come with the manifest, such as its MatchClaim table, and the timelines each concerns. Every timeline, group, measure map, conversion map, region and flow-control element has an id, unique across the manifest, and so may the manifest; a name is what is displayed. A manifest with an id or a name is a named bundle, one with neither an anonymous bundle. A manifest knows nothing about where, or how often, it is shown;

  • a block carries one manifest, entirely its own, together with its placement, the other inputs of its drawing: position, scale, graphical length, spacing (the gap between the items of its drawing), margin (the empty space above its first item and below its last), requested size, the stated places of its drawing’s containers and, where it has one, its arrangement, which places its items in an order instead of at their places (Arrangements). A block’s styling is its manifest’s graphical; the block has no style layer of its own;

  • a workspace is the canvas: its name, its blocks, their order and, where it has one, their arrangement, its default style rules, the workspace settings, and the command journal that produced them, with the sessions of its editors. Block ids are the workspace’s namespace, and an address names anything in it.

TiLiE is local-first: the browser communicates with a local Python server, which is meant to own loading through timetoalign, heavy computation and exports, while the browser keeps a small editing core. This keeps the library’s capabilities close to the operations that need them and interaction responsive without a request per keystroke.

What exists today and what is planned#

tilie-server starts a FastAPI server, on loopback for its user (tilie) or publicly for the hosts it is named for (tilie --public-host), serves the built editor, and answers, authenticated by the run’s token in a local run, GET /api/health, POST /api/measure-maps, which expands a manifest’s measure maps through timetoalign, POST /api/match-claims, which checks a package’s MatchClaim table against its manifest and answers its columns, POST /api/match-claims/provenance and POST /api/match-claims/belief, which read the provenance of given MatchClaims and record a belief in a new table, POST /api/match-claims/events, which answers the events on both sides of given MatchClaims, POST /api/flows, which plays a timeline’s flow control through timetoalign, POST /api/flows/flow-maps, which answers the manifest with that timeline’s flows written, /api/reconcile/musicbrainz, MusicBrainz as a Reconciliation Service API endpoint, and, in a local run only, GET /api/folders, GET /api/files and PUT /api/files, which list and read the workspace, manifest and table files below the server’s root folder and write workspace files there, never a path through a hidden folder or to a hidden file (The server). The editor is one component that the standalone page mounts and that any other page can mount through a framework-free entry (Embedding). It is a header and the canvas, with an inspector drawer on the right. It opens, saves, imports and exports files, through the server’s file routes in its own file dialog where a server offers them and through the browser’s pickers otherwise, autosaves in the browser, inspects and edits the selected block’s manifest and the statements of its bundle, timelines and regions, looking their values up in public services, and reads the MatchClaim table a block’s manifest names through the server to show its potential MatchStamps on the canvas and name, update and remove MatchStamps from them (The editor); a block, a group or a timeline opens in an editor of its own on the canvas (Editors); everything it changes goes through the commands of core.

Loading sources through the server, raster exports, a generated API client, collaboration over a network and automation endpoints are not implemented. Nor is editing flow control in the editor: it shows a selected flow-control element read-only and records the user’s belief in it, and its name is set as any element’s; an element is added, moved, retargeted or relabelled only through core’s commands and the Manifest tab. Nor is a display of flows: the client’s flows and withFlowMaps have no caller in the editor, which neither shows a timeline’s computed flows nor writes them into a manifest. Sections and sentences that say “planned” describe the intended architecture, so that additions have explicit boundaries; everything else describes behaviour that exists.

The container hierarchy#

A manifest nests its content in four containers, and its drawing draws each of them as a container of its own (TimeToShare’s README, “Containers”): semantically, the bundle (the manifest) holds groups and timelines, a group has timelines as its members, and every timeline, root or child, has five facets, markers, conversion_maps, children, regions and flow_control; graphically, the block draws the bundle, a frame a group, a card a timeline drawn as a root, and a band a facet, at its timeline’s line. All four graphical containers are boxes, drawn as rectangles with rounded corners; “box” is the generic word and names none of them. A child timeline is content of its parent’s children facet, drawn as a line in its parent’s children band, never in a card of its own unless a group has it as a member. A band draws nothing; it is the unit that holds and orders what its timeline has of its facet. Manifest content and addresses take the semantic words, the drawing’s containers the graphical ones, and every package reads the hierarchy through one model:

A root that contains a member, a child timeline some group names, is drawn as that member’s ancestor lines and may also be drawn whole as an ancestor card: a card held by the block behind the frame of the member’s group, at the proportions of the root’s own axis, its line continuing the member’s ancestor line of the root (the drawing specification’s element type ancestor_card, section 8.8). The defaults show it; a rule setting its display to none hides it. An ancestor card has no place (its key {item: G, member: R} names none, Holding "ancestor"), no bands of its own and no entry in places; only a displayed one is recorded, as a DrawnBox carrying ancestor {group, member, groups} (groups every group whose frame it lies behind: [group], or, for a root drawn once behind a row of groups side by side, every group hanging on it in the row’s order, group the first; Arrangements), its lines as DrawnAxis records carrying the same ancestor, after every item’s lines, and the block grows to hold it.

core holds the registry (CONTAINERS in manifest/containers.ts): for each kind its level, its element type and the place members it may state by how it is held. containersOf(manifest) lists every container of a manifest with its holder, memoized per manifest, and placeMembersOf answers what a container may state. A block’s places maps the ID of a group or of an ungrouped root (a root that no group names and that holds no member) to its stated place, a group’s entry holding in its cards, under a member’s ID, the stated width, height, spacing and margin of the member’s card in that group’s frame; it is the drawing’s places (TimeToShare’s drawing specification, sections 1 and 8.6). After every command each touched block fits its places to the drawing, so a command never fails for a stale entry: an entry that placed a timeline’s card before the command and places nothing after it moves to that timeline’s one card (a root that became a member of G, from places[T] to places[G].cards[T]; a member that left its group, back) unless that card’s entry is stated already, and a timeline drawn in several cards after the command gets no moved entry; every other entry that places no container goes; each entry is then cut to what the registry lets its container state as it is held, so a root that becomes a member keeps only its card’s size, spacing and margin.

A timeline’s band order lists line, its own line, and its five facets, each standing for the band drawing it, top to bottom (section 6.4). Its own band order is its entry of the manifest’s graphical.order, which lists line and any of the facets; its inherited one is its parent’s effective order, else the default (children and conversion maps above the line, markers, regions and flow control below); its effective one, the one it is drawn with, is its own completed against the inherited one, else the inherited one: the stated entries keep their order, and every facet the entry leaves out keeps the side of line it has in the inherited order and lies beyond the stated entries on that side, in the inherited sequence (core’s bandOrderOf and inheritedBandOrder). A group’s display order is its entry of graphical.order, else its members (displayOrder).

Layout records every container of a drawing as one DrawnBox in Drawing.boxes (Layout and writers); the arranger and the editor read that list and derive nothing from elements or descriptor order. The arranger’s level table says what each level of the canvas may do with each kind (Levels and permissions).

IDs and addresses#

Every element of a manifest, timeline (root or descendant), group, measure map, conversion map at any depth, region and flow-control element, has an ID, and so may the manifest itself; so does every named timestamp, named MatchStamp, alignment anchor and resource; every ID is unique across the manifest (TimeToShare’s README, “Identity”). A statement has neither an ID nor an address: statements and provenance are members of the element holding them, changed through that element (Commands); a statement’s value names an element of the manifest by an IRI reference to its ID (#cpt_1). An ID is any non-empty string without control characters, /, spaces, colons and URIs included, and no word is reserved; TiLiE keeps it verbatim and never parses it, so a timeline, group or manifest without a name is named by its whole ID. Where core creates an element itself, the segment that covers an empty segment line (Invariants), it mints {prefix}_{N} (clt_1) with the smallest N whose ID the manifest does not hold, following timetoalign’s scheme, as it mints the IDs of the default timestamps and of every named timestamp, elision and named MatchStamp a command adds (Commands); a command that adds any other element carries its ID. IDs repeat freely across blocks, each manifest being its own. A block ID is ten characters from 0-9a-z, drawn from the platform’s cryptographic random source (mintBlockId).

An address names anything a command can target. It is an object, never a string composed from IDs: {} the workspace (WORKSPACE), {block} a block, which draws its bundle, and {block, id?, facet?, part?} an element of the block’s manifest, whose members are those of the manifest’s own address, the drawing specification’s (section 2.2):

The manifest’s title is {part: ["name"]} whether or not the manifest has an ID: the manifest’s own ID addresses nothing, so giving or changing it changes no address, and a command aimed at {block, id} with the manifest’s ID is refused with unknown_target; a title exists only for a named manifest, so on an unnamed one {part: ["name"]} names nothing (unknown_target). An element drawn more than once (a member in several frames with everything it draws, an ancestor line above several members) keeps one address, which every drawing of it carries; so the cards of one timeline share theirs, a style rule selecting an element styles every drawing of it, and selecting it marks every drawing of it.

resolveAddress(view, address) in core is the one place that decides what an address names. It returns a record of one kind: workspace, block, title, frame, timeline, card, band, entry (with its kind and index), measure_map, timestamp, elision (a stored one, with its timeline), matchstamp, anchor (with its MatchStamp) or part (with its owner and word), carrying the block, the located timeline and the containers where there are any (a group’s frame, every card of a timeline, every drawing of a band), or a CommandError: invalid_address for a malformed address, unknown_target for one that names nothing, each at [<address key>]. A part resolves only where it is drawn: an address of a part the element’s drawing does not draw names nothing (unknown_target); the address of a card’s derived gaps, which depend on the view and the cascade that core does not read, resolves, as a part of its timeline, wherever an ancestor card may derive one on that timeline (defaultElisionTimelines), drawn or not. resolveElement(manifest, element) does the same within one manifest. Addresses are compared as whole objects; addressKey(address) is an address’s one string form, the only key a map or a Svelte keyed block takes for it, and no package writes an address as any other string or reads anything out of its key. indexOf(manifest) is what every reader of a manifest needs once, memoized per manifest: its containers, the ancestry of its timelines, the timeline that owns every timeline and entry ID, and the elements of its drawing.

The core package#

@timetoalign/tilie-core is framework-free: no DOM, no Svelte, no network. Its main entry holds what a host and the packages above core use: manifest and workspace I/O, the commands and preview, the store, the resolver, the registry, the band and order helpers, the error types and the domain types. @timetoalign/tilie-core/drawing holds the drawing’s primitives for layout and the writers (the elements a manifest draws, among them each timeline’s named timestamps, namedTimestamps(manifest, timeline) in canonical order, memoised per manifest, the selector test, the member drawings); @timetoalign/tilie-core/numbers the exact arithmetic. The package needs Node 22.18 or later, as @timetoalign/timetoshare does.

Numbers#

Rational is an exact fraction over BigInt, always normalized, built from a [numerator, denominator] pair, a bigint, a safe integer or a decimal integer string, never from a JavaScript float. A Real is a value as Python’s numeric tower holds it, an exact Rational or a float, combined and compared as the tower does (a sum involving a float is a float, a comparison is exact). fromFloat returns the exact binary value of a float and serves arithmetic that must be exact on a float’s value, such as the writers’ page transform; it never re-expresses a coordinate. A Coordinate is {unit, numberType, value} whose value is what the axis’s number type declares: a bigint for int, a number for float, a Rational for fraction. A value on its timeline’s own axis is bare, read and written as timetoalign’s number_from_json and number_to_json do: an int axis reads a JSON integer, or an integral JSON number as that integer; a float axis reads a JSON number, or an integer as the float it equals exactly; a fraction axis reads only a pair; every integer is int64-bounded. Arithmetic and comparison work within one axis only and throw AxisMismatchError across units or number types.

Values on timelines#

A conversion map is evaluated as timetoalign evaluates it. compileMap(map, context) (maps/compile.ts) reads a map’s parameters once per map object and returns an evaluator, a closure over those parameters; every evaluation computes its result anew on Python’s numeric tower, exact where the position and the parameters are exact, a float as soon as a float takes part, and where timetoalign decides on doubles (a TableMap off its exact path, a PiecewiseMap’s interval, a measure view’s measure) so does the evaluator, by bisection over a Float64Array of the map’s own parameters. No value is cached. A measure view reads the expanded rows of its MeasureMap from context, as the server’s measure-maps route answers them, and without them gives nothing and says so. express (maps/express.ts) writes a result on the map’s target unit in that unit’s number type, as timetoalign’s express_as does, an int rounded half to even. A timestamp (maps/timestamp.ts) is the cross-section of a timeline, its anchor, at a position exact on the anchor’s own axis: everything a cross-section line drawn there crosses. A CrossSectionSpec names the anchor and its members, the timelines its timestamp holds, each a TimelineAxis (its unit, number type, length and drawn maps, in drawing order; timelineAxisOf; and, where a reader of the canvas gives them, its ordinate, the ordinate of the drawn line at which the reading crosses it, and its mapOrdinates, by map ID the ordinate of each of its maps’ drawings in that card) at its exact offset from the anchor’s zero: by the canvas’s convention first the anchor’s ancestors, parent first (ancestorMembers: an ancestor’s coordinate is its descendant’s plus the offsets up to it, and it carries no map; an ancestor’s span holds its descendant’s, so it is crossed wherever the anchor is), then the timelines its line may cross (the anchor’s segments and its displayed descendants drawn with a line of their own, with the segments of each, in traversal order); nothing marks a member as a segment, a segment is a member like any other. A member read by a group (the other members of a groupstamp’s group, Show coordinates) also has a scale, its drawn end over that of the member holding the anchor, since the members of a group start and end together: its coordinate at an anchor position p is (p − offset) × scale, and its span ends at offset + length / scale. timestampAt(spec, position, knots) gives the anchor’s part, then the part of each member whose closed span [offset, offset + length] holds the position (a boundary two contiguous members share is crossed by both), each with its coordinate in its number type and each drawn map’s value, which every reader shows without computing it again. A row (Row {timeline, map}) is one of those values: a member’s own coordinate (map null) or one of its maps’ values. rowAxis(spec, row, knots) reads a row both ways, forward from an anchor position to the row’s value and back from a value to the anchor position where the row takes it; it is the one path by which anything evaluates or inverts a map, so the values shown, the position a pointer picks on a row (snapOnRow) and the position an edited value gives (positionFor) never disagree. A pin (Pin) is a timestamp held for editing: its spec, the expanded MeasureMap rows it is read with (or why they are not at hand), the timestamp, and named, the named timestamp it shows, or null; pinAt(pin, position) reads it anew at another position. positionFor(pin, row, value) gives the anchor position at which a row takes a typed value: a coordinate’s at its member’s offset plus the value, a map’s value inverted over its member’s span and settled on the anchor’s axis (a float on a fraction axis to the nearest multiple of 1/denominator, any value on an int axis to the nearest integer); a row the timestamp does not show, a value no position gives, a position outside the timeline’s [0, length] and one outside the anchor’s (an ancestor’s value beyond the anchor’s span) are refused with invalid_value. timestampRows(pin) lists the rows as every reader shows them (the parts top to bottom by their timelines’ ordinate, a part without one last, the timestamp’s own parts keeping the anchor first; per part its rows top to bottom as drawn, its coordinate at its ordinate and each map at its entry of mapOrdinates, a row without one after those with one, so a part without ordinates lists its coordinate, then its maps; each row with its label, its value or the text shown instead, and whether an edit moves the timestamp: a coordinate always, a map while it is invertible over its timeline’s span), and timestampJson(pin) writes the timestamp as one JSON value: the named timestamp’s members as the manifest holds them, or {timeline, coordinate} for an unnamed one (the anchor’s coordinate as a bare value), followed by mapping, keyed by ID in part order, each crossed timeline but the anchor its coordinate dict and each map its value (null where it gives none). The inverse is maps/invert.ts’s: invertible(map, context) says whether a map is strictly monotone, decided exactly on its own parameters, over the span the context names (a LinearMap whose scalar is not 0, a linear TableMap and a PiecewiseMap rising or falling strictly over what the span reaches, a ChainMap whose every link is), and invert(map, value, context) gives the position, exact on exact operands and a float as soon as a float takes part, or null; a previous TableMap, a ConstantMap, a CombinationMap and the four measure views are never invertible.

A position is picked on the row the pointer hovers, by snapOnRow(spec, row, p, h, knots): p is the pointer’s distance in units from the anchor’s zero and h half a device pixel in units. On every row of a member of an int axis (samples, ticks, pixels), its coordinate’s and its maps’ alike, the picked value is the integer nearest to p minus its offset, a tie going up, so such a timeline steps by 1 whatever the granularity and no map’s value axis decides its step. On a coordinate row of any other member the picked value is the simplest of the member’s number type within h of p minus its offset; on a map’s row of any other member it is the simplest of the map’s target number type (the value’s own kind where the map names no target unit) within hv of the map’s value at p, hv being half the spread of its values at p − h and p + h, and that value is brought back to the anchor’s axis exactly through the map’s inverse; a map without an inverse there, or giving no number, falls back to its member’s own coordinate. The result is held to the member’s span within the anchor’s [0, length]. The simplest value is snapValue(value, reach, numberType, granularity) (number/grid.ts), never finer than the workspace’s granularity, which is a cap: on a fraction axis the nearest k/d for each divisor d of denominator, ascending; on a float axis the value rounded to 0, 1, … decimals places; on an int axis the nearest multiple of each power of ten from the largest not above 2·reach down to 1. The first within reach wins; where none is, the finest is taken (the nearest multiple of 1/denominator, decimals places, the nearest integer), a tie going up each time; clampValue holds a value to a span, below only where the length is not known. Every comparison is exact on the doubles’ exact values (fromFloat), so the position is exact and no float constructs it; zooming in reaches finer values by itself, since a device pixel then covers fewer units. number/display.ts gives a coordinate’s two texts and its coordinate dict. The raw text, rawText(value, numberType, unit), is the value as Copy writes it (copyValue: a fraction n/d, or n when the denominator is 1, a float its shortest round-trip repr, an int its plain digits), a space and the unit’s schema name (219/16 quarters, 0.1 seconds, 514041 samples). The pretty text, prettyText(value, numberType, unit, decimals?), is the number, a space and its unit’s pretty unit, prettyUnit(unit), a symbol, an abbreviation, else the unit’s name: ♩ for quarters, 𝅝 (U+1D15D) for whole notes, ms, min, smp, ticks, px, pt, m, cm, mm and in, any other unit its name; a fraction is mixed and its digits never grouped (13 11/16 ♩), an int is grouped by three with single spaces (514 041 smp), so only on an int axis, a float is its repr, and a value without a unit is its number alone. Seconds are a clock with no pretty unit after it (prettyUnit gives s, shown beside a field): hours h only when not 0, minutes ′ when not 0 or after hours, seconds ″ always, a part after a shown one padded to two digits, and decimals only where the seconds are not whole (42.5″, 1′15.5″, 1h02′05″, 0″, the fraction 121/2 as 1′00 1/2″, the float 1e-05 as 0.00001″); the split is exact, on the rational or on the decimal digits of the float’s repr, never in float arithmetic. Without decimals the pretty text is exact. With decimals it is rounded: a float to decimals places as Python’s round rounds it, then spelled; a fraction mixed while its denominator is at most 2520, else its exact decimal where that ends, else ≈, before the sign, and 12 decimal places; an int as it is; seconds split after rounding. The coordinate flag rounds with the granularity’s decimals, and a drawing’s coordinate label is the pretty text rounded so with decimals 3 (layout’s coordinateText, the drawing specification’s section 5.2). coordinateDict gives the coordinate dict {unit, number_type, value}. parseAxisValue(text, numberType) (number/parse.ts) reads typed text as the exact value it spells, never through a JavaScript float: on a fraction axis an integer, n/d, a mixed fraction a b/c or a decimal, on a float axis a decimal (0.1, .5, 1e-07), on an int axis digits, plain or grouped by three with single spaces, each with an optional sign; other text, a zero denominator and an exponent beyond ±1000 are refused with invalid_value, and whether a negative value is allowed is the caller’s to say.

The manifest value, load and save#

A manifest held by core is always in the complete form, every key with a default present. Its types are read-only views of the schema package’s generated complete forms: Manifest of the package’s CompleteManifest, holding root timelines (TimelineManifest, whose length is a value or null and whose children carry their offset), groups, measure maps and its own statements and provenance, its meta a JSON object; a timeline and a region hold their own statements and provenance likewise, kept as given (manifest/statements.ts). statementHolders(manifest) walks the elements that may hold statements once, in subject order, the manifest first, then root by root the timeline, its regions in their order and its children depth first, each with its path, and statementHolder(manifest, id) finds one; skeletonOf(manifest) is the manifest’s statements alone at the paths they have in it, with the full context, memoised per manifest: the schema package’s statement functions (withPair, withCandidate, withBelief, withoutPair, …) run on that skeleton, never on a held manifest, whose lossless numbers a structural copy would lose. The @context. A held manifest keeps of its @context only the prefixes it declares itself, those that are no format term and no prefix of TimeToShare’s catalogue with the same namespace, and only while its statements and provenance use them; with none left it has no @context (withStatementContext), so a workspace file does not repeat the format’s terms in every manifest. The full context, the format’s terms and every prefix the statements use, each namespace from the manifest’s own declaration else the catalogue’s (withFullContext), is added where a manifest leaves core: manifestFileValue(value) is that one boundary, the value with every ID written #id and the full context, which saveManifest writes, the schema check, the warnings and the unknown keys read, and the Manifest tab shows. A value on a timeline’s own axis (length, offset, region start and end, a flow-control element’s coordinate) is bare (AxisValue). Every number is a JsonNumber: a plain number, a bigint beyond 2^53, or a JsonFloat for an integral float, so that 600.0 is written back as 600.0. The graphical object is the one exception: its declarations hold canvas numbers, so it is read plain at the schema boundary, a declaration’s 2.0 stored as the double 2 and an integer beyond 2^53 refused with invalid_value unless a double holds it exactly; a style command’s set and the workspace’s graphical are read the same way. A key the schema does not declare is kept where it was found: values are spread, never rebuilt from known fields, so unknown keys survive every command and are written back. core reads, orders and writes no JSON of its own: the reader, the canonical order and the standard writer are the schema package’s.

normalizeManifest(value) is the one path from a value with bare IDs (a workspace’s manifest, a command’s change) to a manifest: validate it through schemaChecked (below), complete, put in canonical order, apply the segment locks, check the invariants root by root, complete the styles. It returns the manifest or a LoadError, and holds no type assertion: the validator’s assertion types the value. A command that has already checked a fragment continues from completion, so a fragment is validated once; a value canonical order cannot order is refused with the schema’s first problem. loadManifest(text) is the path from a manifest file: it reads the text in any of TimeToShare’s serializations with the schema package’s loads, validates the value as the file writes it, makes its IDs bare and normalizes it as above; its problems are invalid_json or invalid_yaml for a text that does not parse as the serialization it is read as (the reader’s message naming the place), the first schema_violation with the path the validator reports, or every invariant violation of a schema-valid manifest; unknown keys are never problems. A value declaring another version than 0.2.0 is refused at ["timetoshare"]; there is no migration. saveManifest(manifest, file) checks the manifest the same way, throwing InvalidManifestError with every problem, and writes its file value (manifestFileValue: IDs as #id, the full @context) with the schema package’s dumpsFile as the manifest file {minimal, styling, serialization} says: in the minimal or the complete form, with or without its graphical object, in canonical YAML (yaml), pretty JSON (json) or RFC 8785 JSON (jcs), each member not given as for a new bundle (minimal, with styling, YAML), byte-identical to what the Python writer produces. Import reads a file with readManifestFile, which is loadManifest and also tells how its text writes the manifest, so that it can be written back as it came: minimal when the value equals its own minimal form, with styling when it holds a graphical object, and in the serialization the text is in (jcs when it is byte for byte the RFC 8785 JSON of its value, json for any other JSON, yaml otherwise). An entry a command adds or replaces (a conversion map, region or flow-control element) is checked by the same schema where it will lie before it is stored: the manifest with the value in place is completed, ordered and checked whole, as a settled manifest is, so what the value names, a measure view’s measure map, an element a statement’s value names, is found wherever it lies in the manifest. A timeline it adds is checked first alone, as the one root of a manifest of its own beside the manifest’s measure maps, what its elements state set aside, and then with their statements and provenance in place in the whole manifest, so a value may name any element of it.

IDs in a file. A manifest file writes every ID and every reference to an ID as # followed by the ID (TimeToShare’s README, “Written in a file”); a manifest held by core, and everything built on it, holds them bare. loadManifest reads a file’s #id into the bare ID with the schema package’s withBareIds, and saveManifest writes the file form with its withFileIds. The schema states the file form, so every schema check of a manifest core holds goes through one function, schemaChecked, which validates its file value (manifestFileValue), returns the value as core holds it (bare IDs, the held @context) and maps the path of a problem back to the bare manifest (idFormPath: a key of graphical.order loses its #); a file is validated as written, before its IDs are made bare; the invariants and their messages work on bare IDs and quote them so. The workspace file holds IDs bare throughout, its manifests included, and so do the data-id attributes of a drawing’s SVG.

Commands#

Every edit is a command, a plain JSON object {type, ...} carrying its author and time once journalled, and there are four: add, set, remove and style. Each is aimed at addresses, and the kind of its target, resolved once, decides what it does. apply(view, command) is a pure reducer: it never modifies its input and returns either the new view or a CommandError {code, message, path}. It parses the command, dispatches it by type and target kind, applies it atomically (one refused change refuses it whole), and settles every block the command touched: in each touched manifest every groupstamp whose timeline is no group’s member any more (a group removed, a member gone from its group) is made an ordinary timestamp (withGroupstampsOnMembers), so a command ending a membership is never refused for the groupstamps it leaves; then the manifest is checked by the schema and the invariants once, and its block’s places fitted to its containers.

  • add {into, <kind>: value} adds a block into workspace; a root timeline (completed, the locks of its place applied, its ID not yet held in the manifest; a root may carry an offset) a group ({id, name?, meta?, members}, its members existing timelines, each joining it as set groups joins a group) or a measure_map ({id, name?, meta?, rows}) into a block; a child, region, flow_control element or map into a timeline. The value carries its id, except a named timestamp, an elision and a named MatchStamp, which core gives the smallest free ID of their kind (below). A named timestamp is added into a timeline (any timeline, a segment included): {name, coordinate, display?, groupstamp?}, the name and the coordinate required (invalid_value) and nothing else (unknown_property), the name non-empty, the coordinate a bare value on the timeline’s own axis within [0, length] (invalid_value, or invalid_coordinate for a value of another number type), and display a list of display words from wedge, name, id, coordinate and cross_section, each at most once (invalid_value: “‘w’ is no display word”, “‘w’ is given twice”), stored in that canonical order, the schema’s default ["wedge", "name", "cross_section"] where it is left out, and groupstamp a boolean (readGroupstamp), true only on a group member, a timeline a group lists or a descendant of one (core’s groupsHolding, manifest/elements.ts), else refused with invalid_value, and false where it is left out (the editor gives groupstampByDefault: on a group member, unless the innermost open editor is a timeline editor); each refusal lies at [addressKey(into), "timestamp", <member>]; it is given the smallest timestamp_N the manifest does not hold, which depends on the manifest alone, so a replay mints it again, and written into the manifest’s graphical.timestamps in canonical order (by timeline, coordinate, id). Only the coordinate on its own timeline is stored, never a map’s value. An elision added into a timeline (at any depth, a segment included) is {start, end, name?, width?, display?}, the start and the end required (invalid_value) and nothing else (unknown_property): bare values on the timeline’s own axis, the start at or after 0 and the end at or before a known length (elision_stretch, worded as TimeToShare’s validators word it), the end after the start (elision_stretch at end); the name non-empty; width exactly one of {ratio} (above 0, at most 1), {span} (a bare value on the timeline’s axis above 0, non_positive_number otherwise) and {canvas} (a finite number above 0), and the standard gap where it is left out; display words from ellipsis, name, id, coordinate and continuation, each at most once, stored in that order, the schema’s default ["ellipsis", "name"] where it is left out. It is given the smallest elision_N the manifest does not hold and written into graphical.elisions. Added into a block, elision is a non-empty list of such values, each naming its timeline (elision_timeline for an ID naming no timeline), given their IDs in list order: one command storing several elisions at once, as the editor stores the gaps a card derived (The inspector). Every problem of an elision lies at [addressKey(into), "elision", …] (a list’s i-th at […, "elision", i, …]), so no elision a command makes reaches the schema check. A root timeline added into a block is given, within the same command, two default timestamps (withDefaultTimestamps): origin at the zero of its number type and, where its length is known, length at its length, each with the display ["wedge", "coordinate"], no groupstamp and the next free timestamp_N; a child is given none when it is added. A child that joins a group (set groups, or an add of a group listing it) is given the same two as groupstamps, their display ["wedge", "coordinate", "cross_section"], so that their lines cross the group’s frame and the ancestor lines in its card; a timestamp of it already named origin or length at that value is made a groupstamp instead, cross_section added to its display. Only joining gives them: one deleted later stays deleted. Once made they are named timestamps like any other. The editor gives every root of a manifest it imports the same pair when the manifest has no graphical object (importedManifest), which would state its own timestamps. A named MatchStamp, matchstamp, is added into a block: {name, anchors, match_claims}, each required (invalid_value) and nothing else (unknown_property), the name non-empty, anchors a non-empty list of alignment anchors in screen order (top to bottom, then left to right), each a pair of coordinates {timeline, coordinate} naming a timeline of the manifest and a bare value on its own axis within it, no anchor given twice, and match_claims the IDs of the MatchClaims behind it, kept in the order given. It is given the smallest matchstamp_N, each anchor the next anchor_N and the display ["line"], the middle anchor in screen order (the upper of two middles) ["line", "name"]; every coordinate becomes the match timestamp lying there, a named timestamp with matchstamp: true: the one the manifest holds at that exact value of that timeline, else a new one with the next timestamp_N, no name, the display ["cross_section"] and groupstamp: true where its timeline is a group member, else false (a match timestamp taken over keeps its own), so at most one lies on a coordinate and an ordinary named timestamp is never taken for one. The rules of a MatchStamp (an anchor joining two timelines, its anchors one connected set, a claim ID listed once) are the schema’s, checked on the settled manifest (schema_violation).

  • set {changes: [{target, values: {...properties}}]} sets properties of blocks and elements; the properties are nested under values, so none collides with target. One gesture is one command, so moving five blocks is one set with five changes.

  • remove {targets} removes blocks and elements, mixed, every target resolved before anything is removed; a block goes with every block whose parent chain reaches it. A removed timeline takes along its subtree; the named timestamps and the elisions lying on it or on its subtree; its id from every group’s members and every display order; its own and its descendants’ band orders; every entry of a style rule’s select.addresses addressing a removed element, a rule left with none going too, and every rule whose select.within names it. A group left without members stays. A removed timeline or region, with every timeline and region removed along with it, is taken out of what every element of the manifest states (withoutReferencesTo): a value that is a node naming it ({id: "#X"}) goes, as does a provenance entry whose value names it, #X leaves every basis and stated_by, a service #X goes, and a list, node, container or provenance left empty loses its key; a reference into another manifest (other.yaml#X) stays. A removed named timestamp takes along every select.addresses entry naming it or one of its parts, its labels and its cross-section line, a rule left with none going too. A named MatchStamp goes with its anchors and every match timestamp no other MatchStamp names; a removed timeline or match timestamp takes along every MatchStamp an anchor of which names it, and leaves the timelines of every resource; an alignment anchor alone is refused with not_removable. A removed group goes alone: its members stay, roots ungrouped and children in their parents. A removed measure map goes alone too. Removing the last root leaves the block empty. A segment, a child of a segment line, whose removal would leave a gap in its line is refused with segment_not_removable (“Removing segment ‘S’ of line ‘L’ is not supported yet”); one that goes with its line, an ancestor or its block is removed with it.

  • style {select, set, name?, blocks?} puts a style rule into the manifest of each listed block, or of every block with an element the selector matches; its addresses name bare IDs, as every command does, and a file writes them #-first. With name, the declaration becomes a named style in that manifest’s styles. A rule whose selector equals an existing one is merged into it; a property set to null is removed, and a rule left empty is dropped.

A name set on a block is its manifest’s, the title; on a timeline, group, measure map, conversion map, region or flow-control element the element’s own; null states none. A named timestamp and a named MatchStamp keep a name, so null is refused for them (invalid_value), except on a match timestamp, whose name is optional: null deletes it. The workspace’s name is a non-empty string (invalid_value otherwise). A timeline’s is_segment_line makes its children its segments, or plain children again; it and its subtree then take the locks their places decide (Invariants), and a line turned off loses the lock its place gave it for being one: a child or a group member is unlocked, a root that is no member keeps its own locked. A group’s order is its display order, every member once, or null to delete the entry (invalid_order otherwise). A timeline’s order is its band order, or null so that it inherits again; any other value is refused with invalid_order and the first problem in the terms of TimeToShare’s validators, such as 'regions' is missing from the band order of 'score'. place merges into the block’s places entry of an item or of a timeline’s one card: a member given a value is stated, one given null is no longer stated, and a place left empty is deleted. A member’s card is set through its group: a group’s cards, {[T]: {width?, height?, spacing?, margin?}}, merges each member’s value into places[G].cards[T] by the same rule, a card place left empty and then a group entry left with nothing being deleted; cards: {} and a member value of null are refused with invalid_value, and a timeline that is no member of the group with unknown_place ('T' is no member of group 'G'). A timeline’s place sets its card only where it has one card: a timeline drawn in no card of its own or in the frames of several groups is refused with not_an_item ('T' is drawn in the frames of several groups), and a member’s card given x, y, length or scale, through either target, with not_an_item too ('T' is a member: its card takes only width, height, spacing and margin). The card address {block, id: T, part: ["card"]} takes no set (unknown_target). A MatchStamp’s anchors rewrite its anchors, as an Update of it to the current potential MatchStamp gives them: an anchor joining the same two match timestamps as one it has keeps that anchor’s ID and display, any other is new; where the anchor showing the name is gone and no kept one shows it, the middle one does; the match timestamps the new anchors need are made, those no MatchStamp names any more go, and the same anchors in another order change nothing.

Arrangements. An arrangement is placement, never part of the manifest (Arrangements). A block’s arrangement is null, deleting the member, or {direction, order}: direction vertical or horizontal, order a list of IDs of its groups and ungrouped roots, kept as given, an ID naming no item or named again being valid (readItemArrangement). A horizontal one is stored as the row the rules give from the order given (core’s rowOf), so a later reading of the block finds the row it is drawn in; one the rules refuse is refused with arrangement_conflict, its message the rules’ reason ("Theme A" and "Theme B" overlap on "Score" (8–16 ♩)). An edit that later breaks the rules, an offset moved, keeps the stored arrangement, which is then drawn one item under another until the conflict is gone. The workspace’s arrangement, of its blocks, is null or {direction, columns?}: direction vertical, horizontal or matrix, columns a positive integer that only a matrix states (invalid_value: “only a matrix has columns”; readCanvasArrangement), a matrix without it taking the smallest number of columns whose square holds the blocks (columnsOf). Each is one set, one journal entry.

Statements. What an element states is changed only whole: one set of statements (an object of property–value pairs) or provenance (a list of annotations), or both in one change, on {block} (the manifest’s own, which, like name, are properties of the block’s manifest and so no member of an add of a block, refused there with unknown_property), on a timeline at any depth, a segment included, or on a region replaces that member; null removes it. The Metadata tab builds every change with the schema package’s statement functions and sends it as one such set, so undo takes it back in one step. Core reads the shape alone (invalid_value, “statements is an object of property–value pairs, or null”, “provenance is a list of annotations, or null”) and leaves every other rule to the schema check of the settled manifest, a refusal being schema_violation with the schema package’s message at the offending value’s path in the manifest: “’#nope’ names no element of this manifest” at [<block key>, "manifest", "timelines", 1, "children", 0, "statements", "dcterms:source", 0, "id"], “‘foaf:maker’: the prefix ‘foaf’ is not declared in @context”. A prefix a statement uses is taken from the manifest’s own @context, else from TimeToShare’s catalogue, and the held @context follows the statements (The manifest value, load and save). A group takes neither member (wrong_target_kind). The manifest’s own statements lie outside every element of the manifest, so a group or timeline editor refuses to change them (No side effects).

Where a timeline lies. Two properties change a manifest’s structure; the Properties tab sends groups (The inspector), and no gesture sends parent yet. parent is null, making the timeline a root, or {timeline, offset}, making it a child of that timeline at that bare offset, its subtree with it, by the rule that places every child (it may grow an unlocked parent, as below). Refused: unit_mismatch for a parent of another unit; segment_structure when the moved timeline is a segment or a segment line, or the new parent a segment line; cycle for the timeline itself or one of its descendants; a locked parent that would have to grow, as for any child. groups is the list of the groups the timeline is a member of, distinct group IDs in any order: the timeline joins each listed group it was no member of, as its last member, and leaves each it was a member of that the list leaves out, so a timeline may be a member of several groups and is drawn in one card in each of their frames. Refused: invalid_value for a value that is no list of group IDs or that lists one twice, unknown_target for an ID that names no group, nested_member when the timeline would hold or lie inside another member of a group it joins. Ids never change; a moved timeline keeps its band order and its card’s stated size, and settling reshapes its places entry for its new holder.

A command is atomic. Ids are never settable, nor a timeline’s unit or number_type, nor a root’s offset. A flow-control element is never changed in place: its role, its coordinate and what decides the flow stay as they were added, and it is removed only with its timeline (not_removable). A set gives it a name and a label, and changes its provenance only by a belief: the actor’s earlier belief, compared as the IRI it stands for under the manifest’s @context, is replaced in place, else the belief joins the first entry, else the element gains one entry holding it alone; every entry’s paradata and every other actor’s belief are kept, so no command erases provenance, and an element is believed again only by the actor who rejected it. provenance itself is no property a set gives (unknown_property). A set that changes nothing, a value written equal to the stored one or values that undo each other, is refused with invalid_value (“the command changes nothing”); the values written are compared path by path, and a block where a change reached past its path (a whole manifest, a move, an offset, a group) is compared whole. A position on an element’s own axis is a bare value, normalized as its axis’s number type selects: an integer on a float axis is stored as that float, an integer on a fraction axis or a coordinate dict is refused with invalid_coordinate, and a pair is reduced to lowest terms. The caller mints the id of what it adds, so that replaying a journal is deterministic: a block id with mintBlockId, an element id unique in its manifest (manifestIds lists the IDs a manifest holds, an elision’s among them, and nextId(prefix, ids) gives the smallest {prefix}_{N} they do not hold); an ID the manifest already holds is refused with duplicate_id, the value’s own ID at [addressKey(into), <kind>, "id"], and a given ID nested inside the value (such as a child’s own child) at its location in the manifest the command would leave ([<block key>, "manifest", "timelines", 0, "children", 0, "children", 0, "id"]).

preview(view, command) is the reducer without the schema check, the invariants and the no-op refusal: what a gesture shows before its release, so a preview can never differ from what the release stores (The gesture engine).

A refusal’s path follows one rule: it starts with the key (addressKey) of the address of the target at fault, followed by the property or kind at fault ([<address key>, "length"]), or by "manifest" and a location in the manifest the command would leave. The problems of an add’s value lie under the address it is added into, at [addressKey(into), <kind>, …]: a block added to the workspace at [addressKey({}), "block", …], its duplicate ID at [addressKey({}), "block", "id"]. No path starts with a bare block id; only a malformed command names the malformed member of the command itself. A message quotes an ID as 'x' and names an address by its words, addressWords(view, address): the workspace, a bundle by its manifest’s name or id (“bundle ‘Mixed’”, “the anonymous bundle”), an element by its kind and its name, else its ID, followed by its bundle unless the view holds that block alone (“timeline ‘Score’ in bundle ‘Mixed’”), a band, a card or a part by its owner (“the regions band of timeline ‘Score’”, “the line of timestamp ‘Downbeat’”), and an address naming nothing by its members (“‘gone’ in bundle ‘Mixed’”, “block ‘0000000009’”). The path keeps the key; the words are for the reader only. The codes are stable strings, exported as ErrorCode:

  • reading: invalid_json, invalid_yaml, invalid_command, invalid_coordinate, invalid_value, schema_violation;

  • the workspace file: unsupported_format, unsupported_version, unknown_member, missing_member, unknown_session, unreferenced_session, session_referenced_twice, journal_mismatch;

  • the workspace: invalid_block_id, key_mismatch, unknown_parent, parent_cycle, invalid_index, duplicate_index, non_finite_number, non_positive_number, invalid_order, unknown_place;

  • addressing: invalid_address, unknown_target, unknown_property, wrong_target_kind, id_not_settable, not_removable (the workspace and an alignment anchor), duplicate_id, not_an_item;

  • structure: unit_mismatch, segment_structure, cycle, nested_member;

  • editors: no_editor, not_inside, side_effect;

  • arrangements: arrangement_conflict;

  • elisions: elision_timeline, elision_stretch;

  • lengths and Delete: child_exceeds_parent, locked, length_below_content, segment_end_fixed, timestamp_exceeds_length, elision_exceeds_length, segment_not_removable;

  • locks: segment_never_locked, unlocking_not_supported;

  • manifest invariants: region_end_before_start, negative_child_offset.

Invariants#

The manifest invariants are timetoalign’s, checked on every load and over the manifest every command would leave: every value on a timeline’s own axis reads as that axis’s number type; a region does not end before it starts. That a unit admits a timeline’s number type (TimeToShare’s one table of units, UNITS, refused as schema_violation at the timeline’s number_type), that IDs are unique across the manifest, that a jump destination’s to and a break’s ends.after name an element that is there and not rejected, that a measure view’s measure_map names one of the manifest’s measure maps, that a conversion map converts from its timeline’s unit and a map another map holds from its holder’s source unit, that a child has its parent’s unit and lies within its parent’s known length, and that a segment line’s segments are contiguous, are TimeToShare’s rules, checked by its validator; so a removal that would leave a destination’s to or a measure view’s measure_map naming nothing is refused. A negative child offset is valid in the schema but not supported yet: core refuses it with negative_child_offset at the child’s offset and the message A negative child offset is not supported yet: child '<id>' has offset <offset>.; a root’s offset may be negative. The catalogue in app/packages/core/tests/README.md lists every row.

Locking. locked states whether a timeline’s length is fixed; it defaults to false. A segment is never locked: its segment line’s lock governs it, so reading a manifest unlocks every segment, and a segment is written with false. A segment line that is a child, a segment or a group member is locked, and is written with an explicit true, in the minimal form too. A locked that contradicts its place is refused: true on a plain segment with segment_never_locked (“A segment is never locked: lock its segment line ‘’ instead.”), false on such a segment line with unlocking_not_supported. A locked timeline’s length cannot be set (locked). An unlocked timeline’s length may be set, never below the farthest point a child reaches (length_below_content) and never past its parent’s known length (child_exceeds_parent); a segment line fixes its segments’ ends, and a length that would move one is refused with segment_end_fixed; a named timestamp lying exactly at the old end follows the end to the new length (replaceTimeline), so length stays at the end, while any other named timestamp keeps its place, and a length below one is refused with timestamp_exceeds_length, naming the first in canonical order; an elision ending at the old end follows the end while the new length lies after its start, and is refused with elision_exceeds_length otherwise; any other elision keeps its place, and a length that would leave one ending beyond it is refused with elision_exceeds_length; a length of null is always taken. A child added into, or moved within, an unlocked timeline past that timeline’s known end grows that timeline, and only that timeline, to the child’s end, provided the grown length passes the same checks; a locked timeline refuses the child with child_exceeds_parent. A parent never grows because a child grew, as in timetoalign.

Segment lines. A segment line of known length that an edit leaves without segments is given one: a segment from 0 covering the line’s whole length, of its unit and number type, unlocked as every segment is, its id the smallest {prefix}_{N} of its unit’s timeline class that no id of the manifest holds (core’s withSegmentLinesCovered). This happens before the schema checks every edited value: a manifest set whole (the Manifest tab’s Apply), a block or timeline added, and every command’s settling. In a manifest set whole, the tree of every root whose lines were covered then takes the locks its places decide, as is_segment_line gives them, so a member segment line that Apply gave its first segment is locked; the tree of a root the cover left alone is taken as the text states it. Reading a manifest never applies it, so a file holding such a line is refused as the schema’s validator refuses it. A segment line of unknown length is left as it is, since nothing fixes where its segment would end.

Unknown length. A length of null means that the length is not known, on any timeline. It is a fact the manifest states, never inferred and never a violation; every check that needs an end runs where the end is known and is skipped where it is not. A child of unknown length reaches its offset, so adding it past the end of an unlocked timeline of known length grows that timeline to the offset.

Two rules go beyond what the library enforces, because the editor addresses and stores values directly: a value on a timeline’s own axis must already carry the axis’s number type, and a parent grows only to a value its own number type holds exactly. A child shares its parent’s unit but may have another number type; its end, offset plus length, follows Python’s numeric tower.

MatchClaims and potential MatchStamps#

A package’s MatchClaim table lies beside its manifest, never in it, and core never holds it in a workspace; what it reads of one is the columns the server answers (The server), in core’s matches/ (decisions/0029): the table’s typed columns of exact values and their check (columns.ts), which builds, once per columns, the map from claim ID to row (claimRowOf) that the comparison and the editor read; the belief filter (beliefs.ts): its four states, true, undecided, unlikely and false (BELIEF_STATES, in the order the editor offers them), its default, every state but false (DEFAULT_BELIEFS), and its check (checkBeliefFilter), all TimeToShare’s and re-exported, as timetoalign reads the same ones; believedBy, the believer’s state for every claim, its belief, the most critical of several (false, unlikely, undecided, true: ANCHOR_BELIEFS), or undecided where it holds none; the row mask beliefMask keeps every row whose state is one of the kept states (null where all four are kept); and anchorBelief, the most critical state among the claims stating one anchor, by which a shown anchor is coloured; the believer and every actor compare as the IRIs they stand for under the manifest’s @context (actorIri: a compact IRI expanded by its prefix, TimeToShare’s expandIri, else as written), as timetoalign compares them, so a compact and an absolute IRI of one person are one actor wherever the editor compares actors; the anchor index built from the columns under that mask (anchorIndexOf, anchors.ts, sort.ts), with the unique coordinates, the AlignmentAnchors (two coordinates on two timelines that a claim states) and the claims stating each, the potential MatchStamps (each connected set of anchors, numbered by its first coordinate), the matched spans and the segments between two timelines (both not yet wired), and the hit tests (coordinateNear, coordinatesWithin); and the comparison of a named MatchStamp with the index (checkMatchStamp, compare.ts): which of its anchors the index still states, in the order of its alignment_anchors, with the rows of the claims stating each (statedBy), which of its MatchClaims are missing from the table, left out by the belief filter (claimsFilteredOut), stating other anchors or new, whether it is out of date (an anchor no longer stated, any such MatchClaim, or an anchor it lacks of the potential MatchStamps its coordinates now fall into), and the potential MatchStamp an Update rewrites it from: the one holding its stated anchor that shows the name, else the largest of those its coordinates fall into, none when no anchor of it is stated. The commands that name, update and remove a MatchStamp are core’s ordinary ones (Commands).

The workspace view#

A workspace is held as a View, an immutable plain value: meta (format: "tilie-workspace", version: 7), name, settings, graphical, arrangement where the blocks are arranged (Arrangements on the canvas), and blocks, a map from block id to block. The name is never empty, untitled (UNTITLED_WORKSPACE) for a new workspace, and gives its file’s default name. graphical holds the workspace’s default style rules, the cascade’s layer between the built-in defaults and each manifest’s own; like the name, it is changed by commands. The settings say how the editor is used on the workspace, and no command changes them (The store and its journal): grid {size, snap} (8 units, snapping), blockDefaults (scale 1, length 400, spacing 8, margin 0, no requested size) and granularity {denominator, decimals} (DEFAULT_GRANULARITY, 2520 = lcm(1, …, 10), so every tuplet up to tens, and 3), the caps on a position picked on a timeline: denominator, a positive safe integer, for fraction axes, and decimals, an integer from 0 to 15, for float axes, which also sets the places a float is shown with; an int axis has no setting (Values on timelines); manifests, how each block’s manifest is written to a file (below); and matchClaimFilters, by block id, which MatchClaims of the block’s MatchClaim table are shown: {timelines, beliefs}, the claims between two of the listed timelines (null for every timeline, a list in code-point order) whose state for the user is one of beliefs, a list of belief states, each once, held in the order of BELIEF_STATES; {timelines: null, beliefs: ["true", "undecided", "unlikely"]} for a block without an entry (matchClaimFilterOf); the table itself is never held. A workspace file whose settings.granularity does not read as one is read with DEFAULT_GRANULARITY, and a settings.manifests or settings.matchClaimFilters that is missing or no object as none, each of its entries that does not read being left out (a filter written in an earlier shape gives its block the default), since settings lie outside the journal and nothing replayable is lost; every other member is read strictly. A block has an id; a parent, the canvas group holding it, null in this version; an index, its order key among the blocks of the same parent; and the inputs of its drawing besides the manifest’s own: x and y (the canvas position of its drawing’s place origin), scale, length, spacing, margin, width and height (each null for the content’s own size), places, arrangement where its items are arranged (Arrangements), and manifest. An arrangement that is not there is none: no view holds an arrangement of null, a set of null deletes the member and a file stating null is read without it (Commands). emptyWorkspace() is the empty one. A view’s problems, by block, are a block stored under another key, a malformed id, a missing parent or a cycle of parents, a malformed or shared order key, a canvas number that is not finite, a scale, length or size that is not positive or a spacing or margin that is negative, a malformed place or one that names no container (unknown_place), and every schema or invariant problem of a block’s manifest.

Manifest files. settings.manifests says how each block’s manifest is written to a file, by block id: {minimal, styling, serialization, location?} (ManifestFile), the minimal or the complete form, with or without the manifest’s graphical object, and yaml (canonical YAML), json (pretty JSON) or jcs (RFC 8785 JSON); and, where the editor knows it, location, where the manifest’s file lies: a non-empty POSIX path relative to the workspace file’s folder (a workspace without a file counts the server’s root as its folder), as a manifest’s resources locate its files relative to the manifest, refused with invalid_value (“expected a relative POSIX path”) when it starts with /, holds a backslash or names a scheme or a drive. Import stores the entry its file implies (The header and the File menu), and Include styling and Minimal, in the Manifest tab and in the Bundle tab, change it (The inspector), each through configure({manifests}), outside the journal; a manifest imported or located through the server’s files adds its location, and a Save into another folder rewrites every location relative to it (The header and the File menu). A block without an entry, one whose manifest came from no file or one of a workspace file that states none, is written minimal, with its styling, in canonical YAML. An entry outlives its block, which undo may bring back.

Sibling order is a fractional index, a string key that compares with ordinary string comparison, so that a block moves between two others by taking a key between theirs (indexBetween, indicesBetween, isIndex), compatible with Rocicorp’s fractional-indexing 4.0.0 and written in core (decisions/0016).

The store and its journal#

createStore(workspace, {author?, clock?}) holds a workspace, {view, journal, sessions}, and returns view, journal, sessions, editing, entries, listed, changes, openings (the count of every open), canUndo, canRedo, dispatch(command), configure(change), undo(), redo(), open(target), done(), cancel(), snapshot(), setAuthor(name) and subscribe(listener). dispatch stamps the command with its author (by default Gerhardt; the editor gives it the user’s name, The user) and its time (ISO 8601, UTC, from the injected clock), applies it, runs the side-effect guard while an editor is open, and appends {command, author, time}; it returns null, or the CommandError of a refused command, which leaves the store unchanged.

The journal is the authority. Replaying the journal from the empty workspace, named untitled, reproduces the workspace. createStore verifies this before it returns: it replays the journal to its end and compares the result’s name, graphical, arrangement and blocks with the workspace it was given (the settings are no command’s and are never compared), and throws JournalMismatchError {entry, path} naming the first differing path when they differ, an empty journal beside a non-empty view included, and a file stating a name that no command of its journal gave (["name"]). The editor shows that error as it shows a refused file. A journalled add of a block carries every member of its placement, the blockDefaults it was added with filled in, so replaying never reads the settings. readWorkspace does not replay; reading and verifying are two costs (Performance). The decision record is decisions/0019.

Settings outside the journal. configure(change) changes any members of the settings, each given in part, at once: it journals nothing, is never undone or redone, and is refused with the first problem of the settings it would leave, at ["settings", …]. It gives a new view, so the workspace is marked unsaved and autosaved, and every view the store shows afterwards, at any step of undo, carries the settings last configured.

Undo by steps. A change is one command at any depth, owned by the innermost session whose own entries hold it, the session of the editor it was made in, or by none for a command of the main journal. A change that the level in view owns itself is a step of its own; a closed session’s changes go by runs, a step being a maximal run of consecutive changes owned by one closed session. undo reverts the latest applied step, redo re-applies the earliest undone one, so redo mirrors undo step for step, and a new command discards the undone changes. So a closed session holding a1, then a closed session of b1 and b2, then a2 is undone in three steps, a2, then b1 and b2 together, then a1. Views are kept per change with structural sharing; an undo past the views the store holds applies the changes from the latest view it holds, or from the empty workspace at the start of a loaded journal.

Editor sessions. Every editor is a session: the commands made in it between its opening and Done or Cancel. The store keeps one level per open editor above the main journal; the open editors are editing, outermost first, each {session, target, kind, earlier}: its session id (ten characters from 0-9a-z), its target ({block}, or {block, id} of a group or a timeline) and its kind (bundle, group or timeline; any other address has no_editor). entries, listed, dispatch, undo and redo act on the innermost level: undo inside a session stops at its start.

  • open(target) opens an editor; with one open, the target must lie inside the innermost target and not be it (not_inside). When the last entry holding an applied change on that level is the session of the same target, that session is unfolded: taken off the level, keeping its id, its changes undoable and redoable again. Otherwise a fresh session opens, and earlier names the last session of the same target, listed read-only in the Journal tab.

  • done() closes the innermost editor; a session holding entries is stored under its id in sessions, and {session, author, time}, stamped at Done, is appended to the level below. A session left without entries records nothing.

  • cancel() closes the innermost editor and restores exactly the state at its opening.

  • changes counts what Cancel would discard: with o the changes applied at opening, flattened at every depth, c those now and p the length of their common prefix, (o − p) + (c − p).

  • snapshot() returns the workspace as Save writes it, every open editor folded innermost first as Done would fold it, writing only applied changes.

No side effects#

An editor changes only what it edits. While an editor is open, dispatch compares the view a command would leave with the view before it and refuses the command with side_effect (”<what> lies outside <target>: an editor changes only what it edits”, “lie” where <what> names several things) at the path of the first difference outside the innermost editor’s target, in this order: the workspace’s name, its graphical or its arrangement of the blocks; another block; for a group or timeline editor, its block’s scale, length, spacing, margin, index, parent or arrangement; a key of the block’s places that the target does not contain, except that a member’s editor may change its group’s length, scale, width and height and the width of every sibling’s card (they are shared, or kept in proportion by a stretch); and the manifest outside the target, compared with the target reduced to its id and with only the style rules that match an element outside it: first each element, then what each timeline and region states apart from it (“the statements of timeline ‘clt_1’ lie outside timeline ‘clt_3’”, at [<key of its address>, "statements"]), then the manifest’s own members, its statements and provenance among them (“the manifest’s statements in bundle ‘Mixed’ lie outside timeline ‘Score’”, at [<block key>, "manifest", "statements"]), then the members of its graphical, its named MatchStamps compared whole, so a group or timeline editor refuses to name, update or remove one; the @context is left out, since it follows from the statements compared. So a group or timeline editor changes nothing stated outside its target: it refuses a set of its block’s own statements, and a removal inside the target that would prune a reference to a removed element from what an element outside it states. The command’s change set, the blocks and settings it touched and the paths it wrote, narrows the comparison to them, with the same result as comparing everything: where every path lies inside the target and no change reached past its path, or the manifest is unchanged, the manifest is not compared at all. An entry of graphical.order lies inside when the target contains its group or timeline, and a style rule selecting bands lies inside when every band it matches belongs to a contained timeline. A block’s x, y, width and height are the container adapting, never a side effect; removing or renaming the edited element is refused at the target. The bundle editor contains its whole block.

What a target contains is contains(manifest, container, element): a timeline contains itself, everything that belongs to it or to a descendant, its bands among them, and their parts, but no ancestor line; a group contains itself, its title and whatever its members contain.

The workspace file#

A workspace is saved as one pretty JSON file, whatever file form its manifests are exported in, by default <name>.tle.json after the workspace’s name: {meta, name, settings, graphical, blocks, journal, sessions}, every manifest embedded whole with bare IDs, never the canvas engine’s state, so a text diff of two files shows only real changes. writeWorkspace(workspace) writes {view, journal, sessions} with TimeToShare’s dumps in its json serialization; Save and the autosave write writeWorkspace(store.snapshot()). A journal entry is exactly {command, author, time} or {session, author, time}, and a session exactly {target, entries}, so sessions nest; every session is reached exactly once from the journal (unknown_session, unreferenced_session, session_referenced_twice), and one without entries is refused with invalid_value. readWorkspace(text) reads the file with TimeToShare’s lossless parseJson and returns the Workspace or a LoadError listing every problem at its path in the file; a file of another format or version (such as “workspace version 6 is not supported; this editor reads version 7 only”) is refused for that alone. name and arrangement are the members a file may leave out: a file without a name holds a workspace named untitled, and an empty or non-string name is refused with invalid_value; the workspace’s arrangement and every block’s, written after its places, are written only where there is one, so every file written before arrangements existed keeps its bytes, and a stated null is read as none. There is no migration. writeWorkspace(readWorkspace(text)) returns the text it read, byte for byte, for a file that states its name. A block’s manifest is a TimeToShare manifest that timetoalign reads with AlignmentBundle.from_dict, or with Timeline.from_dict or TimelineGroup.from_dict when it holds one root or one group.

Autosave#

The editor keeps the last text of a workspace in the browser’s IndexedDB, in the slot its storage option names (decisions/0018). Every change is written one second after the last, and a pending write is made at once when the page is hidden or the editor is destroyed; the text is writeWorkspace’s, deferred to the write so the command path never serializes. Beside the text the slot holds the name of the workspace’s file and its path on the server or, where the browser can store it, the file’s handle, so a restored workspace is saved back into its file. One editor at a time writes a slot, holding the Web Lock tilie-autosave/<slot>; a second one shows “Autosave of this workspace is active elsewhere, so changes here are not autosaved.” When the editor opens and the slot holds anything but an empty new workspace, it asks “Restore the unsaved workspace from …?”, with the time of the workspace’s last journal entry; Restore opens it under its file’s name, with the file’s path or handle where one was kept, marked unsaved, and Discard empties the slot. A saved text whose journal is refused shows “The autosaved workspace cannot be restored.” with its problem. A failing autosave is reported once, in a message, as an AutosaveError whose code is indexeddb_unavailable or indexeddb_failed, and editing goes on.

Layout and writers#

The drawing of a manifest is specified in the schema repository, not here: TimeToShare’s drawing specification defines the graphical object, arithmetic, default styles, font, layout, element descriptors, page, and SVG and TikZ profiles completely enough that another implementation writes the same text byte for byte. The specification, together with the SVG and TikZ fixtures of a schema version, pins the drawing; there is no drawing schema, and a drawing’s JSON form is internal.

The drawing#

@timetoalign/tilie-layout exports draw(manifest, placement, layers, metrics), a pure deterministic function implementing sections 1 to 8 of the specification for every manifest. The placement supplies the graphical length of every item without one of its own, the block’s spacing (the gap between its items at their default y) and margin (the empty space above its first item and below its last), the requested block size and the stated places; the layers are the ordered Graphical objects of the cascade, from lowest to highest precedence: the specification’s defaults, the workspace’s graphical, and the manifest’s own; the metrics are the one advance-width table of the two bundled drawing fonts, DejaVu Sans and Noto Music. The returned Drawing holds:

  • width, height and origin, the block-local position of the place origin (Ox, Oy);

  • elements, every drawn element in descriptor order;

An item carries its place with the defaults applied and as stated; a card its drawn size before the scale, its spacing (the gap between its rows and between its bands: a member’s card’s own, a block-held card’s its item’s, else 4), its margin (likewise, else 0), its contentCount, how many contents it holds (its root’s row, its root’s present bands and its ancestor bands, one on each side holding an ancestor line) and its stated width, height, spacing and margin; a band its timeline, facet, signed level (1, 2, … above its timeline’s line, −1, −2, … below), the number of displayed elements it holds directly, and line, the ordinate of its timeline’s line, which tells apart the records of a timeline drawn twice. Only a band that draws an element is recorded, on a timeline whose line is displayed;

  • axes, one DrawnAxis per line drawn, or, on a card whose axis has gaps, one per run of the line between them (the stretches drawn at full scale), run (the run’s number from 0) set from the second run on, the runs of a line sharing its address, and lineAxes keying the later ones with # and the run’s number: the timeline’s address, the place key of the card whose member drawing draws it, its line, the abscissas xStart and xEnd of its semantic start and end (not of a circle, chevron or end mark; an ancestor line clipped to its member; the reach’s where the end is not known), its unit and numberType, the exact own-axis values start and end there (end null where the length is not known), its segments when it is a segment line ({id, offset, length} on the segment line’s own axis, in canonical order, a length the end section 3.4 gives where the segment’s own is not known), its maps, the maps drawn at its line, its own and its segments’, in the order they are drawn ({id, owner, xs, xe, yc}: the map’s ID, the ID of the timeline it spans, the abscissas of its ends and the ordinate of its centre; none for an ancestor line), its mark, on an ancestor line the wedge an editor shows at a position on it, at the abscissa 0 (AxisWedge: its tip ordinate, the corners of its base and its style), drawn as a named timestamp’s wedge on that line is, on the side of the line away from the member, in the style of a wedge held by the ancestor line that no named timestamp holds, null on every other line, ancestor {group, member} on a line of an ancestor card, as its DrawnBox carries it (the ancestor cards’ lines come after every item’s, so a timeline drawn in a card of an item has its first line there), and its marker (null for an ancestor line, which draws none): where a coordinate marker of the line lies at any abscissa: the wedge’s tip ordinate and the two corners of its base (WedgeCorner, defined in rotation.ts: an offset across from the tip and an ordinate) from where its markers band starts (or would start), the cross-section line’s extent and style (crossSection: span, the top and bottom ordinates of the card drawing the line, which every cross-section line of the line crosses, gap, the stretch of it that a line leaves where the wedge at the abscissa 0 and its label stack stand, null where they leave none, and the style of one that no named timestamp holds), and descendants, the IDs of the displayed descendants that line crosses; and the named timestamps the line holds (AxisTimestamp: its ID, the abscissa of its cross-section line, that line’s style, null where its display does not list it, and segment, the card’s top and bottom ordinates, the span its cross-section segment covers, gap included).   • arrangement, only for an arranged block (DrawnArrangement, Arrangements): its direction, its row, the items in the order they are drawn in (for horizontal the row), its roots, the roots drawn once from the top (none for vertical), and refused, where horizontal is refused, the conflict (ArrangementConflict {groups, roots, reason}), the items then drawn one under another;

  • unmapped, the code points neither bundled font maps, measured with the table’s fallback advance.

Every manifest is drawn the same way: a grey block that opens with the manifest’s title (its name, else its whole id; an anonymous bundle has none); each group a frame, a rounded rectangle holding the group’s title and its members’ cards one under the other in display order; each timeline drawn as a root in a card, an ungrouped root in one of its own and a member in one in the frame of each group that has it, a raised rounded rectangle with a shadow offset to the right and down. The block has one horizontal layout: every item starts its lines after one name column and ends them with one right margin, and the members of a group share their frame’s graphical length and scale. An item’s scale scales everything in it about its top left corner. Every container’s spacing is the gap between its own contents, finite and at least 0, and multiplies no other: the block’s between its items at their default y (8 by default), a frame’s between its cards (8), a card’s between its rows and between its bands (4). Beside it every container has a margin (0 by default, finite and at least 0, likewise its own): empty space inside its padding above its first content and below its last, the same above and below, which makes the container twice the margin higher and moves its contents down by the margin. The title lies at the block’s top left corner, above every item; an item placed higher grows the block upward. Within its card a timeline is drawn with its line and its five bands, stacked in its band order; each child is drawn whole in a row of its parent’s children band, and the children bands of the lines of one row are laid out together; the defaults hide the regions and flow control bands of every timeline in a card but the card’s own. In the flow control band (layout/flow.ts, sections 6.4 and 6.6) a break, a section break and a barline are each a vertical line with its own label, a jump target draws nothing, and a jump draws one arc to each destination whose distance is not 0, labelled with that destination’s label, else its manifestation; the jump’s own label and name label no arc and are not drawn. A to naming a rejected jump target is no place, so no arc goes there. A rejected element is drawn faded, its label with it, and keeps its place in the rows. No root draws a mark of its start or end. A named timestamp is drawn by its timeline, at any depth, as its display lists: a wedge in that timeline’s markers band; up to three labels stacked outward beside the wedge, its name, its ID and its coordinate with its unit, in that order (core’s TIMESTAMP_LABELS), each a label element; and a dashed light-blue cross-section line held by its timeline’s line across the whole card drawing it, from the card’s top border to its bottom border, cut only where its own wedge and label stack stand, so hiding the markers band keeps the line; a timeline drawn in several cards draws its timestamps in each. A timestamp named on a child or on an ancestor is drawn the same way: its timeline decides only where its primary coordinate’s wedge and labels stand. A secondary coordinate of a named timestamp (a coordinate on another timeline of the card it crosses: the timeline’s own, its ancestors’ and its descendants’ among them) is drawn as the primary one is, by the wedge and labels its display lists, in that timeline’s markers band or beside its ancestor line, its coordinate label written in that timeline’s number type; the default rules hide every element of one and turn its wedge −45, and a rule selecting coordinates: secondary shows them (core’s cardStamps lists them per card and timeline, CardStamps.secondaries; layout’s markers.ts and ancestors.ts read that one list, so a hidden one takes no room). A wedge turns about its tip by its style’s rotation (Style.rotation, section 6.6), a whole number of degrees from −180 to 180 that rules set and that is never inherited, mirrored with the side of the line its band lies on; where no rule sets it, every wedge turns 0, a groupstamp’s included; a turned wedge carries its stack of labels along its own axis, and the stack’s place and the gap it leaves in the cross-section line follow from the rotation (stacks.ts). MarkerElement carries the tip and the two corners of the base (corners), which rotation.ts computes from the sine and cosine of the rotation, each rounded to four decimal places from one table of the sines of 0 to 90 degrees (sineCosine, wedgeCorners), so every unturned wedge keeps its exact numbers; a turned wedge widens its timeline’s extent to its farthest corner (wedgeSpan). A groupstamp, a named timestamp with groupstamp: true on a group’s member, draws after every item, before the alignment anchors, one dashed line per frame holding a card that draws its timestamp, at its abscissa, from the upper border of the frame’s topmost member card to the lower border of its bottommost, less the card its own cross-section line crosses (groupstamps.ts: frameMemberLines, the one statement of a frame’s member lines; groupSpans; groupstampElements); each piece is a cross_section at the timestamp’s line address {id, part: ["line"]} in that line’s style, and takes no room. The other members’ coordinates derive from the group and are never stored; on each member card the line crosses they are the timestamp’s secondary coordinates, which a rule showing coordinates: secondary draws with their wedges and labels. A group reads a position across its members by the proportion of their drawn lengths, and only where both lengths are known: over the card of a member of unknown length the group gives no coordinate, so that piece of the line is drawn faded (groupLinePieces, the one rule of the frame’s line: the pieces of the frame spans cut at the borders of such cards, each in the line’s style or, over such a card, with the opacity 0.35 in place of the one cascaded; whole and faded where the timestamp’s own member is of unknown length), the secondary wedge there is faded and has no coordinate label, and a coordinate listed for it has no value. A match timestamp draws, by default, its cross-section line alone, and no name label without a name. Each alignment anchor of a named MatchStamp is drawn after every item and takes no room (anchors.ts, section 8.7): where its display lists line, the shortest line between the cross-section lines of its two match timestamps, never one drawn in an ancestor card (section 8.8; timestampSegments) (horizontal at the middle of their vertical overlap where they overlap, else between their nearer ends), the element alignment_anchor {id: A} held by the block; where it lists name, the MatchStamp’s name centred on that line’s midpoint, a label {id: A, part: ["name"]} held by the line. Core’s manifestElements lists them after every item, after one cross_section per groupstamp and frame holding it (held by the frame), so a style rule addresses each as any other element. A segment line draws its segments on its own line, separated by boundaries. A top-level timeline ends with an arrowhead (open for a segment line) or a filled circle when locked; a child ends with an open diamond. A root of unknown length is scaled to its reach, the farthest coordinate its content reaches, and a line whose end is open runs on into three chevrons. A member that is a child also draws its ancestors beside its line as ancestor lines, each on the side opposite to where the ancestor’s own band order puts its children band (below the member by default), in the outermost band of that side, its ancestor band, clipped to the member, running on into a double chevron where the ancestor reaches beyond it; a named timestamp of an ancestor whose position lies within the member’s span, its ends included, and a secondary coordinate on that ancestor’s line are drawn beside the ancestor line, the timestamp at full opacity and a secondary coordinate faded as the line is; an ancestor line draws no mark of the member’s start or end, which the member’s origin and length groupstamps show instead (Commands); unless a rule hides it, its root is drawn whole as an ancestor card behind the group’s frame, after the title and before the first frame (block/ancestors.ts, section 8.8; The container hierarchy). display: none removes an element from the drawing and its layout; section 2.4 states every way a hidden element hides others. A manifest without timelines or groups, such as one holding only the statements about a work, is imported like any other and draws its block with its title alone; links are never drawn.

Elisions#

Elisions are drawn as the specification’s sections 3.2, 6.6 and 8.9 state, by elisions.ts with block/ancestors.ts and block/places.ts:

  • Card and group elisions. Whether an elision is a group elision is read from its timeline and never stored. One whose timeline lies in a group (the timeline or one of its ancestors is a member, core’s groupsHolding, as for a groupstamp) belongs to that group’s frame, to each frame where the timeline lies in several groups; every other one is a card elision of its timeline’s root, drawn in the cards that draw that root whole, the root’s own card where it is ungrouped and its ancestor cards, never in a member’s card. Core lists them without drawing anything: elisionsOn(manifest, T) the stored elisions on a timeline, elisionsUnder(manifest, R) the card elisions of a root’s tree, groupElisions(manifest, G) a group’s.

  • Modes are style properties (decisions/0035). Four properties of the cascade, never inherited, decide what a container draws: elision-mode (auto, full, proportional or custom), read by a card, an ancestor card and a frame, and, read by a card and an ancestor card, elision-proportion (auto, or a number above 0 and at most 1), elision-depths (auto, or the depths chosen) and elision-display (what every derived gap shows, initially ellipsis name). Every card of a root R carries the address {id: R, part: ["card"]}, so one rule there sets the mode of all of them, beside the display that hides its ancestor cards; a frame’s rule is addressed {id: G}. full draws no card elision, custom the stored ones, proportional the gaps an ancestor card derives, and auto the stored ones where the root’s tree holds any and the derived ones otherwise. A group elision never counts and is never switched by a card’s mode; a frame draws its group elisions under auto and custom, none under full and proportional.

  • Derived gaps. An ancestor card drawing proportional derives gaps where it lies outside its member’s span, its protected span: on the root and on each ancestor of the member down to its parent with its siblings, one depth each, on the depths chosen, deepest first, each stretch one piece whose middle is left out, its elided coordinates rounded outwards to whole units and its two ends kept at full scale. The pieces are drawn at the proportion r of their natural widths: the stated one, or, for auto, the largest at which they fit beside the member’s drawn line and the card fits in the view centred on the frame (ancestorProportion), the one value of a drawing that reads the placement’s view. A derived gap has no ID; its address is {id: T, part: ["elision"]} on its timeline T.

  • The elided axis. A container that draws gaps has an axis linear run by run (CardAxis, one builder for cards and frames): each piece of a stored elision is one gap, of its natural width or of the width its width gives (widthAs writes a drawn width in each stored form exactly), a piece clipped where an earlier elision or the protected span took the stretch, and a stored elision not drawn takes nothing. A group elision’s stretch lies over proportions of its member’s length, so its gap lies at one x in every member card, and the frame is drawn shorter by the sum of its gaps’ shrinks; each ancestor card behind the frame carries those gaps over its protected span with the frame’s widths (carriedGaps), exact on an exact axis of the root. An ancestor card’s axis also has two seams, where its member’s span meets the frame’s borders (frameRoom): jumps that leave nothing out and draw nothing, a row crossing one drawn whole, so that what lies outside the member’s span is drawn outside the frame; a root drawn once behind a row of groups side by side has more, between its groups (Arrangements). A member card’s ancestor rows lie at the heights of the same rows in its ancestor card (alignedRows).

  • What a gap draws. Every row crossing a gap stops at its edge and resumes after it (cuts on lines, maps and region braces). The gap itself is an elision element, an invisible extent from the card’s top border to its bottom border, or across the frame’s member cards, which a rule may fill; it holds exactly one ellipsis, centred in the gap and on the card or the member cards together, its continuations where its display lists continuation, and its labels, name, ID and elided coordinates, centred below the ellipsis. The labels’ room is a first-fit stack in the markers band of the elision’s timeline, after the named timestamps’ stacks, so labels shown may move rows; where the labels of neighbouring gaps would touch or overlap, a stack takes the next tier of the band, as a named timestamp’s labels do, and a gap never widens and no proportion changes for a label. A carried gap draws its ellipsis and no label.

Arrangements#

An arrangement places a block’s items, its groups’ frames and its ungrouped roots’ cards, in an order instead of at their places, as the specification’s sections 1, 8.5, 8.6 and 8.8 (Drawn once) state. It is a member of the placement, Placement.arrangement, beside places: it says where the drawing puts things and is never part of the manifest, so the block stores it (Block.arrangement) and any program passing the same placement draws the same (decisions/0036). An arranged item takes no x and no y from places, which keeps them for when the block is free again; every other member of its place applies.

  • The item order is core’s itemOrder(manifest, order): the items the stored order names, each at its first mention, an ID naming no item passed over, then every item it leaves out, in drawing order. vertical lays the items out one under another in that order, each at its default x and y (placeItems given the order), their ancestor cards behind them as in a free block, recorded and written in the same order.

  • Side by side, core decides what stands where (manifest/arrangement.ts, exported from /drawing). A group hangs on a root when one of its members lies inside that root, a child or deeper; its first such member in its display order is its anchor there, the member its ancestor card is aligned to, and the anchor’s stretch runs on the root’s axis from its start, the offsets down to it summed, to its end as drawn (section 3.4), exact (anchorsOf). Along every root the stretches of the groups hanging on it follow the row from left to right without overlapping, touching allowed, and in every group the anchors lie in the top-to-bottom order of their roots. rowOf(manifest, order) gives the Row {row, roots, anchors}, the row keeping the given order wherever the stretches leave it free and the roots following by ID where no group orders them, or the ArrangementConflict {groups, roots, reason}: two stretches overlapping on a root, the row in a cycle, or the roots in a cycle, worded as the specification words them ("Theme A" and "Theme B" overlap on "Score" (8–16 ♩), "G" holds "R" above "S", "H" holds "S" above "R"). An ungrouped root, or a group no member of which lies inside a root, hangs on nothing and keeps its place in the order. anchorChange(manifest, group, order) says why a display order would give a group another anchor (Arrangements on the canvas).

  • The row (block/arrangement.ts, rowLayout): the items packed from the left in the row, the row gap max(S, 48) apart (S the block’s spacing; the standard gap and 8 on either side of it), each as wide as it is drawn. Every root a group hangs on is drawn once, by its ancestor card behind the first group of the row hanging on it, the once card, whose DrawnBox.ancestor.groups lists every group hanging on it; that root’s ancestor cards behind the other groups draw nothing and are not recorded. The roots lie one under another, their cards never overlapping, each line low enough that every group can lie on all of its roots: in a group anchoring on several roots, each later anchor’s card is moved down, the gap above it grown, until it lies on its root’s line, no card changing its size. The highest frame or once card lies where the first item would; an item hanging on nothing stands at the row’s top. A once card that a rule hides draws nothing and keeps its place, so hiding it moves nothing.

  • Runs, gaps and seams. A once card’s line is drawn in runs, over each anchor’s stretch at the scale of that anchor’s frame, the run continuing to either side of the stretch. Between two anchors next to each other along the root, the scale changes in the room right of the left anchor’s frame: in a gap of origin arrangement where whole units of the root’s axis fit there, at least the standard 32 wide, its elided coordinates rounded outwards to whole units, drawn as every gap is with the card’s elision-display and addressed as a derived gap {id: R, part: ["elision"]}; else at a seam (PlacedSeam), a jump of the axis that leaves out nothing, has no address, draws nothing and is listed in no exposure, across which a row crossing it is drawn whole. A timeline starting at a seam starts on the run right of it, one ending there ends on the run left of it. Left of the first stretch and right of the last, the card draws the elisions its mode gives, as the ancestor cards behind the first and the last group would, at the least proportion of the root’s ancestor cards behind every group hanging on it; between the first stretch and the last only the arrangement’s gaps are drawn, and stored card elisions lying there are kept and not drawn. On an exact axis of the root every position is exact.

  • Refusal. Where rowOf refuses the row, the block is drawn vertical in the item order, and the drawing states the conflict (Drawing.arrangement.refused); nothing else refuses an arrangement.

Packing is one function, layout’s packed(sizes, {direction, gap, start, columns?}) (arrangement.ts): vertical left-aligned, each content gap below the one before; horizontal top-aligned, each gap right of the one before; matrix row by row in columns columns, each column as wide as its widest content and each row as high as its highest, each content at its cell’s top left. The row of a block is packed with it, and so are the blocks of an arranged canvas (Arrangements on the canvas).

The pipeline#

draw (block.ts) orchestrates four stages over one item model, in which a frame with its cards and a block-held card (a frame-less item with one card) take one code path: the places of every item, stated or default, and the block’s place origin (block/places.ts); the block’s one horizontal layout over all its items (block/horizontal.ts); each item laid out in its own coordinates (block/items.ts); and the shift into the block with the Drawing’s records (block/emit.ts, with the axes from block/axes.ts). A member drawing (member.ts) takes one timeline as the root: traversal.ts walks its timelines once into an index of children by parent and sources by line, fixes the axis (the length, or the reach, memoized per drawing) and every timeline’s end; timeline.ts lays out every line with its bands, one module per kind of band filling them (markers.ts, maps.ts, children.ts, regions.ts, flow.ts); rotation.ts turns a wedge’s corners; stacks.ts places the label stacks carried by turned wedges and the gaps they leave in cross-section lines (markers band and ancestor lines share it); groupstamps.ts draws the groupstamps’ lines across their frames from the drawn lines and boxes, after every item, its groupLinePieces the one rule of a frame line’s pieces (shared with the hovered and pinned marks, groupCrossSections); slots.ts (rowSlots) gives, after the first fits and from the rows alone, the conversion maps bands of every row that are laid out together their shared slots, which mapSlots (maps.ts) finds by key and first fit; rows.ts lays out the rows of children bands at every depth, ancestors.ts the ancestor lines, and block/ancestors.ts the displayed ancestor cards behind their frames; elisions.ts decides the elisions of every card and frame and draws their gaps; block/arrangement.ts lays out a block arranged side by side, its once cards included, over layout’s one packing function (arrangement.ts). Line ends, chevrons, the brace and every element’s bounds are geometry.ts’s, which the writers share. The style cascade (Cascade, INITIAL_STYLE, DEFAULT_LAYER) decides each property per element by the specification’s layer order and rule rank, the rules bucketed by the selector members that discriminate them; text widths come from the advances of the fonts’ one metrics table, measured once per distinct text. Layout knows nothing about a page.

The writers#

@timetoalign/tilie-writers exports toDescriptors(drawing), writeSvg(placed) and writeTikz(placed, options). toDescriptors returns the ordered SVG-shaped descriptors that the canvas and both writers share; every descriptor carries its source: the index of the drawn element it renders, that element’s type and address, and its role (primary, or shadow for a card’s shadow). placed is a list of {drawing, x, y, scale}; the writers find the bounding box of all placed drawings, add a margin of 16 on every side, translate each drawing to the page and apply its scale. writeSvg returns one SVG page; writeTikz a complete LuaLaTeX document, or with standalone: false the tikzpicture alone, fontDirectory naming the directory of DejaVuSans.ttf and NotoMusic-Regular.ttf, the second a luaotfload fallback of the sans font for every code point DejaVu Sans does not map and Noto Music does; the SVG’s font-family is DejaVu Sans, Noto Music. Both round the exact value of every double to four decimal places, ties to even.

pnpm -C app/packages/writers review, from the repository root, draws every manifest fixture of the installed schema version (not the minimal twins nor the corpus ones), the resize and place cases and the manifests whose application layer shows a band the defaults hide, which are TimeToShare’s drawing fixtures, and writes SVG and PNG files plus index.html under app/packages/writers/review/, rasterized with rsvg-convert --zoom 2 under a fontconfig that exposes only the two bundled fonts (decisions/0017). emit-drawings writes those fixtures into a TimeToShare checkout and never rewrites the hand-derived figures. draw draws any manifest file to SVG and TikZ the same way (AGENTS.md). The application exposes no PNG, JPG, PDF or .drawio export.

The canvas#

@timetoalign/tilie-arranger builds the canvas on Svelte Flow (@xyflow/svelte), which supplies pan and zoom, node dragging, the selection rectangle, keyboard focus and culling (decisions/0020). It has two levels: at the workspace level, the arranger, blocks are placed freely; inside a block, its groups and timelines are arranged in editors, modes of the same canvas. The drawing comes from the manifest, never from an editable drawing surface: a person edits the manifest through commands, never by dragging an arrow endpoint, so a musical relation keeps its meaning whatever the display. Every rule is plain TypeScript tested without a browser; the Svelte components connect those rules to Svelte Flow.

One node per block#

project returns one Svelte Flow node per block, in index order. A node’s id is the block id; its position is the block’s x and y, the canvas position of the drawing’s place origin, less the drawing’s origin times the scale (nodePosition), so a drawing that grows left or up moves nothing else; while the workspace arranges its blocks, the place their packing gives it instead (blockPlaces, Arrangements on the canvas). Its width and height are the drawing’s width and height times the scale, given again as measured with handles: [], so Svelte Flow never measures a node; one it had to measure would be mounted even outside the view. selected follows the selection, zIndex is the block’s rank by index, and data carries the block, its drawing and descriptors, its selected elements, the elements a Delete would take with them, the open editor and the addresses inside the edited element. With an editor open, every other node is neither selectable nor draggable and carries tilie-outside. A node whose inputs are unchanged is returned as the same object, so a change to one block re-renders one node.

drawingOf(block, graphical, metrics, overrides?, pane?) draws a block, its arrangement included, with the layers DEFAULT_LAYER, the workspace’s graphical and the manifest’s own, for the pane of the canvas showing it, the placement’s view, which only the automatic proportion of an ancestor card’s elisions reads, so the pane is read only for a manifest that draws an ancestor card (Elisions). Each canvas has its own pane (Pane, pane.ts, the Canvas’s pane prop): its size left of the inspector drawer, the first size measured taken at once and every later one PANE_SETTLE (150 ms) after the last, so dragging the window or the drawer redraws once, when it stops; until the canvas is measured a drawing takes layout’s default view. Every drawing a canvas shows, and every drawing its gestures and keys read, is drawn at its pane. Drawings are memoized per block object and recomputed only when the workspace’s graphical, the metrics or that pane change identity, descriptors per drawing. A drawing is also kept by its manifest and the values of its placement, the 32 placements of a manifest drawn last, so a resize, which measures the block at many placements on every pointer move, and the preview’s block, which draws one of them again, draw each placement once. Neither is stored.

BlockNode renders the descriptors as Svelte elements inside one <svg> per node, whose viewBox is the drawing’s block; every attribute is the text the SVG writer writes, so the node’s element tree equals the exported SVG’s. Text is rendered as text, never parsed as markup. Every drawn element carries its address as the SVG writer writes it, data-id, data-facet and data-part (the part words joined by one space), each only where the address has that member; the writers’ ADDRESS_ATTRIBUTES is the one list of these attributes. The drawings use the DejaVu Sans and Noto Music files bundled by TimeToShare as two faces, “TiLiE DejaVu Sans” and “TiLiE Noto Music”, named in that order in the drawings’ font family, with no unicode-range, so that a code point DejaVu Sans does not map falls back to Noto Music; the canvas is mounted once both faces have loaded.

Marks. The node draws marks after the drawing, never part of it, its descriptors or an export, and never stored. Every mark is one overlay element in one vocabulary: .tilie-mark, its kind in data-mark (selection, hover or holding), the element address it marks, without its block, in data-marks as its addressKey ({} for the block), and the kind of box in data-box (block, frame, card or band; none for a boundary’s line). A box’s mark copies the outline of its own rectangle (a card’s, not its shadow’s). The last drawn card or frame under the pointer inside the edited element is hovered, the block itself never, and a segment boundary under the pointer is hovered in the accent colour; a selected box shows its selection mark and no hover mark. A hover change adds or removes one mark and touches no drawn element. Every other selected element carries data-selected. Every box shows a holding mark whenever the selection holds an element drawn as part of it other than itself, read from the boxes’ ranges, never from where a box lies: 3 px dashed on a card, 2 px dashed on a frame, 1 px dotted on the block, whose node also carries data-contains-selection, measured on screen. In a timeline editor the hovered and the selected bands of the edited timeline are marked the same way (data-box="band"). Stroke widths are divided by the zoom.

The box reader. boxes.ts is the one reader of Drawing.boxes and of the descriptors’ sources. A press, a hover and Enter are classified from the drawing: a press reads the hit element’s source, a CanvasSource (a descriptor’s source, or an AffordanceSource {kind: "affordance", address} of a named timestamp the drawing draws nothing of, told apart by isAffordance), never the DOM target’s form, and Enter takes its target from the drawing by address. It decides what a press hits (a box, a band or another drawn element), what it selects (pressSelection), what is hover-marked, and which boxes hold part of the selection; while an editor is open, only what lies inside the edited element. Which editor a double-click opens is decided from the same hit.

Levels and permissions#

One table in code, LEVELS, holds every rule of each level: move, nudge (the arrow keys, on the arranger only), reorder, bands, marquee, itemsFree, frameWidthPinned, remove, order, open, lock and resize. rulesOf(editing, {locked, readonly, arranged}) is the one answer to whether something may run: a locked canvas turns off move, nudge, bands and resize and keeps the rest; a read-only canvas turns off everything that writes, opening an editor included, and still selects, pans and zooms. Where the level’s contents are arranged (arrangedOf(view, editing): on the arranger the workspace’s arrangement, a matrix with its columns; in a bundle editor the edited block’s, a horizontal one the rules refuse read as vertical, as it is drawn; none in a group or timeline editor), the rules carry it as arranged and turn move and nudge off, the arranged drag and keys taking their place; a locked or read-only canvas drops it, so the lock turns the arranged drag off as it turns moving off. From the two tables, fieldsOf(box, level) lists the place members a Properties field shows for a container at a level, in core’s place order, each with whose place it writes and whether the level edits it.

The gesture engine#

Every drag inside a drawing (moving an item, reordering a frame’s cards, moving a band) and every resize handle is one engine, pointerGesture(strategy, options), an attachment driven by a strategy: item, arranged, held, band, resize or gap (gestures/). The strategy says whether it takes a press, what value the pointer stands for, how that value is previewed and which command stores it; the engine owns everything else once. A press the strategy takes marks its element nodrag nopan before Svelte Flow reads it, so neither a node drag nor a pan starts, on a mouse and a touch screen alike, and captures the pointer; a press it refuses is the canvas’s (a click, a pan, a marquee). A press released within 4 px (CLICK_DISTANCE, Svelte Flow’s paneClickDistance) is a click and selects; past it the gesture starts, selecting the dragged box unless it is an item in the selection in the bundle editor, and every move whose value differs is previewed; the release dispatches one command and withdraws the preview; the click a browser fires after a drag is swallowed. Escape and a cancelled pointer end a started gesture without a command. Every gesture’s preview is the command applied, the block drawn as drawingOf(preview(view, command)), and only that block’s node is replaced; the projection never runs again for a preview, and a gesture can never show what the release would not store. Blocks are dragged by Svelte Flow itself, snapped by the canvas (Numbers on the canvas).

The canvas reads the store through liveStore(store), the one reactive adapter, which the editor makes once per store and passes to the canvas, the inspector and its editing bar: each getter (view, editing, entries, listed, journal, sessions, changes, openings) reads the store again after every change it reports, so no component keeps a copy. Undo and redo are exact, because the canvas shows nothing between two commands that the next command does not store or discard. Each gesture is one command:

  • a drag of n blocks is one set with n changes {x, y}; a keyboard move (an arrow key one grid step, Shift and an arrow key eight) is one set;

  • a resize of a block, a frame or a card is one set;

  • a change of block order is one set of index; Delete is one remove;

  • in the bundle editor, a move of one item, or of every item in the selection by the same travel, is one set;

  • in the group editor, a drop of a member card is one set of the group’s order, or nothing when the order is unchanged;

  • in the timeline editor, a move of a band, by drag or by key, is one set of the timeline’s order;

  • a drag of a gap’s edge, or a step of it by key, is one set of the elision (Elisions on the canvas);

  • a choice of the Arrange menu is one set of the workspace’s or the edited block’s arrangement; on an arranged canvas a drop or arrow key of a block is one set of its index, and in the bundle editor of an arranged block a drop or arrow key of an item one set of the block’s arrangement (Arrangements on the canvas).

Opening an editor, Done and Cancel are store operations, as undo and redo are, never commands. While a block, or a frame or card in it, is dragged or resized, the canvas reports the placement it previews (onlive), which the inspector’s fields follow.

Numbers on the canvas#

Canvas numbers are doubles, and the positions Svelte Flow reports carry binary noise. Before a number is stored it is quantized: a position to the grid when the workspace snaps to it, every other number (and every position without snapping) to 1/2048 unit, the units per em of the bundled fonts’ advance table, so that at an integral font size and scale every text-derived edge and plot origin is stored exactly. While blocks are dragged, Svelte Flow snaps them to the grid and the canvas aligns the dragged block horizontally: its left edge or plot origin moves onto the nearest left edge or plot origin of another block within 6 screen pixels, and a vertical guide is drawn there while the alignment holds; the other dragged blocks move by the same offset.

Resizing#

Every block, frame and card is resized the same way: a block, a frame, an ungrouped root’s card and a member’s card each offer three modes wherever their level resizes them.

  • Scale sets scale: everything grows or shrinks with its proportions, text included. Only the four corner handles are offered; the new scale is the larger of the two axis ratios, never below 1/8.

  • Box sets width and height: the container changes and the content keeps its place at the top left. A request at or below the content’s own extent stores null (a place’s member unstated), so the container follows the content when it grows.

  • Stretch sets length from the left and right handles, spacing from the top and bottom ones and both from a corner: along time lengths and distances stretch, vertically the gaps between the container’s contents grow or shrink, while markers, line widths and text keep their size. A container’s contents are a block’s items, a frame’s cards, and a card’s root row, its root’s present bands and its ancestor bands, one on each side holding an ancestor line. A container with exactly one content (a block of one item, a frame of one card, a card of its root’s row alone, without a present band or an ancestor band) has no gap, so its vertical Stretch sets its margin instead, the equal empty space above and below its contents, and leaves its spacing as it is; with two or more contents, or none, it sets its spacing. The arranger counts a container’s contents from the drawing’s records (verticalStretchOf), by one rule for the block and for every frame and card. The content grows in piecewise-linear steps, as the drawing specification derives them, so the values that put the dragged edge under the pointer are solved for numerically, each candidate value drawn once; the floors are 8 for length and 0 for spacing and margin, and a value the search leaves where it was drawn is not stated. A container enlarged beyond its content keeps its proportion to the content while it stretches, and a request no larger than the content becomes null.

In every mode the dragged edge ends under the pointer and the opposite edge stays put; a left or top handle also stores the new x or y. A block’s horizontal stretch scales every x, length and width its places state by the ratio of the lengths (blockStretchPlaces); its vertical stretch changes its spacing alone, or its margin alone when it holds one item, moving the items at their default y and keeping every stated y and height. A frame or card resizes by changing the block’s places (section 8.6). A frame or a block-held card: Scale sets its scale from a corner; Box its width and height from any of eight handles; Stretch its length and spacing (a frame’s the gap between its cards, a card’s between its rows and between its bands), or margin for a frame of one card or a card whose only content is its root’s row, every stated width or height of it and of the cards it holds following its content in proportion. A member’s card: Scale sets its group’s scale; a horizontal Stretch its group’s length, the stated widths of the frame and of every member’s card following; a vertical Stretch the card’s own spacing, or its own margin when it holds its root’s row alone, the stated heights of the card and its frame following; Box the card’s own width and height; as its frame decides where it lies, it scales from its bottom right corner and sizes from its right and bottom edges. In the group editor the frame keeps its width while its members fit in it: a horizontal Stretch of the members runs from the right only and states the frame’s width at its drawn width while the cards fit; past it the frame’s width is unstated and the frame grows with the cards. Sizes are in the item’s own coordinates, before its scale, quantized to 1/2048 unit.

When exactly one box that the level resizes is selected, a toolbar centred 10 px above it offers Scale, Box and Stretch, also the keys 1, 2 and 3; it is placed by the rule of Svelte Flow’s NodeToolbar (getNodeToolbarTransform) applied to the box, so it follows the box through every resize, pan and zoom. While boxes are moved, a block dragged by Svelte Flow or an item, a member card or a band dragged inside a drawing, the toolbar and its handles are not shown at all, and they come back at the bounds the move leaves, also when it stores nothing: the gesture strategies that move boxes say so (moves), and the canvas’s gesture slot reports it while such a gesture is under way. A resize through a handle and a move by keys hide nothing. The pressed button shows its mode’s handles. A card’s handles act on the card in the frame being worked in, the edited group’s or the frame of the card dragged; a selected card address that covers several cards (a member of several groups) with no such frame known offers none. The handles are the arranger’s own, since a box’s size during a resize is whatever its drawing measures at the previewed placement. The mode is the viewer’s own and stays when the selection changes.

Selection, marquee and deletion#

The selection is a set of addresses, {block} and {block, id?, facet?, part?}, compared as whole objects. It alone decides what the canvas shows as selected; Svelte Flow’s own flags are reset to it. A selected address is marked wherever it is drawn, so a timeline drawn both as an ancestor line and as a member is marked on both lines.

  • A click on a drawn element selects that element only. A press on a box selects that box: a card (or its shadow), a member’s or an ungrouped root’s, selects the timeline’s cards {block, id: T, part: ["card"]}, marked in every frame that draws one (a root’s ancestor card selects the root’s cards the same way, isAncestorCard), a frame outside its cards the group, the block’s own area outside its frames and cards the block.

  • In a timeline editor, a click on an offered band selects it, {block, id: T, facet: F}; a selected band counts as its timeline’s line for the contains-selection marks, and a selection rectangle never selects a band.

  • Shift and a click add the address to the selection, or remove it. A click on the empty canvas, and in an editor a click anywhere outside the edited element (on its group, on anything else faded, on another block), clears the selection, in the marquee mode too; Shift and a click there leave it.

  • Escape cancels a gesture in progress; with none it clears the selection, and with nothing selected it closes the innermost editor.

  • A focused block is selected with Enter or Space. A drag on a block, on one of its elements included, moves the block, first selecting it alone when neither it nor an element of it is selected.

A plain drag on the empty canvas pans; Shift and a drag draw a selection rectangle, which replaces the selection with the blocks wholly inside it (in an editor, the elements). The Marquee button (“Add to selection (marquee)”) switches to an additive mode, which is also how a touch screen selects several things: a drag on the empty canvas adds what lies wholly inside, and every click, Enter or Space toggles its address.

Picking. While a panel awaits an element of the manifest (the Metadata tab’s composer, “Pick on the canvas”), the canvas is given picking, a callback, and picks instead of selecting: a primary press on a block is taken before anything inside it sees it, and a press on a timeline’s line, card, band or a part of it, or on a region or its label, answers the callback with {block, id} of that timeline or region (picking.ts, pickable, reading the press as the inspector reads a selected address); any other press, on the block’s own area, a frame, a title, a named timestamp, a map or a flow-control element, picks nothing and is refused with “Pick a timeline or a region; Escape cancels.”; a press taken so starts no drag or pan, and its release and click reach nothing more. Escape, wherever the focus is, cancels the pick (the editor’s Picking, pick.svelte.ts, which holds one pick at a time).

Delete or Backspace removes exactly the selected addresses as one remove, blocks and elements mixed; a selected part stands for its element. While elements are selected, what a Delete would take with them is marked with data-affected. A band is never deleted: its address is left out, and a selection of bands alone is refused with “A band is not deleted; select its elements to delete them.” The card address of a root drawn only in ancestor cards is left out too, since it stands for no element of its own. A selection holding a manifest’s title is refused whole, since a title goes only with its block. Inside an editor the edited element, or a part standing for it, is never deleted: it is left out, the rest of the selection is removed, and a selection naming nothing else is refused with “Close its editor to delete the edited element.”; selecting it marks nothing data-affected. A segment that would leave a gap, or a part of one, is dispatched like any target and refused whole by core (“Removing segment ‘S’ of line ‘L’ is not supported yet”). The canvas draws no text about a refusal: it reports every refusal, of a command, a key, a drop or a pick, through its onrefuse prop, and the editor posts it to the message bar as information (The component and the page).

Editors#

An editor is a mode of the canvas on one element, never a pane or a second canvas. There are three kinds, chosen by the box that is hit, never by what it holds: the block opens the bundle editor, a group’s frame the group editor, a timeline’s card or line, a root’s or a child’s, the timeline editor; an ancestor card opens its root’s timeline editor. While a timeline editor is open on a root, or on a descendant of it that no member card below the root draws (no timeline from the root’s child down to the edited one is a group member), the root’s displayed ancestor cards are raised over every frame where the root is in no group, or is itself a group member that draws an ancestor card (another member descends from it): such a root stands in its own group’s frame while its ancestor card lies covered behind the frame holding its descendant, so a double-click on that card opens the root’s timeline editor and raises it (raisedRoot, the one decision of the raise). The raise changes only the order the block node shows the drawing in, never the drawing: one stacking, the ancestor cards not raised in record order, then every other frame and card, then the raised ancestor cards (stackingOf; the block node draws its layers by it, raisedLayers). The raised cards unfold out of the member cards they are aligned to, each row of a card moving from its place in the member card to its place in the raised card while the card’s rectangle and the rows the member card does not draw fade in, and fold back into them when the editor closes, in 400 ms (Unfolding and animation/, decisions/0034), at once under reduced motion; a moving card takes no pointer, and the canvas reads the stacking at rest from the start. What the canvas reads follows the raised cards: the pointer reads their lines everywhere and no other line inside them, coordinates are placed on them (Show coordinates), and the marquee and the editor’s focus count what they draw, which they leave out while the cards lie behind the frames. A double-click, a double tap (two taps within 300 ms and 10 px) or Enter on one selected box or timeline opens its editor; a double-click on another element inside a card or frame opens the innermost card or frame under the pointer, a child’s line the child’s editor, and a band opens nothing. Inside an editor, every box or timeline inside the edited element opens its own editor on top; a double-click on the edited element, or on anything inside it that opens no editor, centres the view on the edited element again as opening does, and one outside it closes the innermost editor, as Done does. The open editors, outermost first, form the editing path, the store’s editing, one source for the canvas, the inspector and the test hook.

Opening an editor animates the view in about one second (FOCUS_DURATION; at once under reduced motion) so that the edited element is centred left of the inspector drawer with a 10 % margin, the zoom between 0.05 and 4; closing animates back to where that level was entered from; a change of the canvas’s size with an editor open centres the element again. The view follows the editing path through the store’s subscription (editorFocus.svelte.ts). Everything outside the edited element is faded to 0.3 and inert: nothing there is hovered, selected or moved, a click there clears the selection, and a drag there pans. Opening an editor selects its edited element (editedSelection): the block in the bundle editor, the group in the group editor, and in the timeline editor the timeline’s cards where it is drawn in one, else the timeline itself (a child, which has no card). After every other change of the store, an editor closing, an undo and a redo included, the editor’s session prunes the selection by one rule (existing): it keeps an address that still resolves and, in an editor, lies inside the edited element (the block itself in the bundle editor), the edited element and its card included, so a change made to the selected edited element keeps it selected; a selected band is kept while its timeline’s editor is the innermost open editor of its block. Inside, elements are selected by click and by marquee (elementsInRect); what an ancestor card draws, an affordance on its lines included, counts only while that card is raised, so an element drawn there and outside it is taken by its drawings outside, and one drawn only there is not taken (the stacking’s behind, which the focus reads too).

In the bundle editor an item is in the selection when the selection holds its frame or card, the cards of one of its group’s members, or another element its group or root contains by address; a drag on such an item moves every item in the selection by the same travel, the pressed item’s place quantized, and keeps the selection; a drag on another item selects and moves it alone. In the group editor a dragged member card (one that anchors its group on a root drawn once excepted, Arrangements on the canvas) previews the order a drop would give, before the first card whose middle lies below the pointer (memberOrderAt), the other cards sliding apart (slide) only while a member drag previews, and at once under reduced motion. The dragged card itself never slides: it lies hidden at the slot it would drop into while a copy of it follows the pointer. However the drag ends, stored, cancelled or released in place, the copy then moves from where it was dropped to where its card is drawn (releaseMotion, settle: √d × 120 ms for a screen distance d, easing out), then goes and the card shows; under reduced motion the copy goes at once.

An editor has no side effects (No side effects); a refused command leaves everything as it was, and its refusal (onrefuse) names what lay outside. Done keeps the editor’s changes as one entry of the level below; Cancel restores everything as it was when the editor opened. Both are the inspector’s.

Bands#

In the timeline editor the bands of the edited timeline, a root’s or a child’s, are arranged (bands.ts). A band is offered only in the timeline editor of its own timeline while that editor is the innermost open one, and only while it is recorded; the arranger, the bundle editor and the group editor offer none. The visible entries are the timeline’s recorded bands and line, top to bottom, a band’s position being the middle of its rectangle and line’s the timeline’s line. A band dropped at ordinate y moves, in the effective band order, to just after the last other visible entry above y, or before the first when none is (bandDrop), read against the drawing at the press; the line is an entry like a band, so a band dropped across it changes sides, and a release in place writes nothing. Either move is one set of the timeline’s order, its value the new band order, or null when that equals the inherited order; moving a timeline’s bands also moves those of every descendant without an entry of its own. A press on an offered band is hit where it lies in the band’s rectangle, edges included, after every drawn element.

Overlays, culling and per-viewer state#

The resize handles and the alignment guides lie in one overlay layer in front of the nodes and keep their screen size at every zoom. The canvas controls (zoom in, zoom out, fit, Lock, Arrange on the arranger and in a bundle editor, Marquee, Show coordinates, Show matches) are a vertical toolbar of icon buttons at the bottom left, each with an accessible name and a tooltip naming its key. Above 500 blocks only the blocks in view are mounted (Svelte Flow’s onlyRenderVisibleElements); below that count culling costs more than it saves (Performance).

The selection, the viewport, the lock, the resize mode, Show coordinates and Show matches belong to the viewer: the editor holds them, and they are never stored in the workspace or its file. An arrangement is not the viewer’s: it is placement, stored in the workspace, journalled and undone like any change. The open editors are the store’s: a file saved with an editor open holds its session folded, and a reopened file opens no editor. A newly opened workspace is fitted into view. Svelte Flow’s attribution link is hidden (proOptions.hideAttribution, which its MIT licence permits); the About dialog credits it.

Elisions on the canvas#

A gap, its ellipsis and its labels are one thing to the pointer (gaps.ts, over layout’s Drawing.elisions and the gaps’ elements): a press on any of them selects the elision, a stored one as {block, id: E}, a derived one as {block, id: T, part: ["elision"]}, and a gap carried into an ancestor card selects its group elision. Only a stored elision’s gaps take handles, a card elision’s in its card and a group elision’s in its frame. A derived one is selected and never edited on its own: Delete on it is refused (“Automatic elisions are not deleted one by one: choose Custom to edit them.”), and choosing Custom for its root stores the card’s derived gaps as stored elisions (The inspector).

A selected stored elision shows a handle on each edge its gaps reach, at the ordinate of its timeline’s line (GapHandles.svelte), and above them the gap mode menu (ModeMenu.svelte, the resize toolbar’s own menu) with two toggles, Coordinates and Width, one or both pressed and never neither, held per canvas, Coordinates alone at first. A drag of an edge by Δx (the gap strategy of the gesture engine, gapDrag): with Coordinates alone, the elided coordinate at that edge moves by Δx over the canvas units per unit of its timeline on the run beside that edge (edgeScale), snapped to the timeline’s start and known end, its children’s, and the ends of what the gap may not cross (an ancestor card’s protected span, a frame’s other gaps) where one lies within the snapping distance, else to the simplest value within the pointer’s reach, and the gap keeps its drawn width, so the timeline slides under the handle; with Width alone the drawn width changes by Δx and the coordinates stay; with both the drawn width follows the moved edge, so the gap swallows the line and what lies beyond the edge stays put. A width is written in the elision’s own form (widthAs; a standard gap as {canvas}), never wider than its natural width. A flag at the dragged edge shows its value, and the release is one set of the elision (gapCommand). [ and ] (by the character typed) choose the edge the arrow keys move, ← and → move it one grid step under the pressed modes (gapStep), and 1 and 2 toggle the modes; the lock hides the handles and disables the menu. With Show coordinates on, nothing is read inside a gap, and the gap under the pointer shows a flag saying what it leaves out (“Score, 0–16 ♩, elided”, gapHoverAt).

cardAxis.ts is the one map between abscissas and the coordinates of the timelines a card or a frame draws (cardAxisOf, cardAxisAt, exposureAt): over the exposure’s runs, each position the proportion of the axis it lies on, the card’s root or, across a frame, the member holding its timeline, each run with its own scale, so a handle reads the run beside its edge.

The arranger and the editor split the gap commands. The arranger builds every command that reads the drawing’s gaps, for the canvas and the inspector alike: a handle’s or a key’s set (gapCommand), the one add storing the gaps a card derived as it draws them (defaultsStored: in drawing order, the elided coordinates exactly, {canvas: g} unless the gap is the standard one, the card’s elision-display, no name), and the one remove of a root’s card elisions (defaultsRemoval). It exports them with the readers the inspector needs, exposureAt, cardAxisAt, piecesOf and stretchText, the gap labels’ spelling of a stretch. The editor builds the rest from those readings (inspector/elisions.ts): the switches of a mode as style commands, the proportion, depths and display settings, Add elision, a group’s Remove all, and the set of each field of a selected elision.

Motion. When a block’s drawing changes its gaps (a mode switched, a gap added, removed or changed, a proportion moved), every card and frame concerned moves its elements sideways from where the drawing before drew their positions to where the drawing after draws them, rows moving only where labels are shown or hidden, and an ancestor card shown or hidden unfolds out of its member card or folds back into it (animation/elide.ts, cardMotions.svelte.ts; decisions/0034). Nothing moves during a gesture’s or a control’s preview, under reduced motion or for a block’s first drawing, and the pointer always reads the drawing at rest.

Arrangements on the canvas#

Two levels of the canvas arrange their contents (Arrangements), through the Arrange menu (ArrangeMenu.svelte, on the toolbar of the gap modes’ ModeMenu, ModeButtons.svelte, in a Svelte Flow panel beside the controls, opened by the toolbar’s Arrange toggle; it reads nothing of the canvas, and arrangeMenuOf in arrangeMenu.ts, its one model, says what is stated and what a choice stores, so the inspector’s level tab draws the same menu): on the arranger the blocks, Free, Vertical, Horizontal or Matrix with its Columns (− and +), the workspace’s arrangement; in a bundle editor the edited block’s items, Free, Vertical or Horizontal, the block’s arrangement, Horizontal disabled where rowOf refuses the items’ order, its reason below the choices and in its title; none in a group or timeline editor. A choice is one set (canvasArrangementCommand, itemArrangementCommand): Free states null; a block’s Vertical or Horizontal the items in the order they are shown arranged (shownItemOrder, the drawing order where nothing is stored), core storing Horizontal as its row; Matrix its columns, the smallest number whose square holds the blocks when first chosen (columnsOf).

  • Blocks. While the workspace arranges its blocks, every node lies where packed puts it in the blocks’ order (index), 32 canvas units apart (ARRANGEMENT_GAP), from the top-left corner of all the blocks’ stated places, the least x and the least y among them, so that reordering never moves the start (blockPlaces, arrangedPlaces); each block’s stated x and y are kept and not used, and no block’s drawing changes. Svelte Flow drags a node as ever, without snapping or guides; the others are shown at the places its drop would give them (arrangedDrop), and the release stores the moved block’s index alone, between its new neighbours’ (blockIndexCommand, indexBetween), nothing where its place is unchanged. The nodes not dragged go to their new places by a CSS transition of 200 ms (data-arranged on the canvas), at once under reduced motion.

  • Items. In the bundle editor of an arranged block a press on an item, a frame or an ungrouped root’s card, reorders instead of moving (gestures/item.ts hands it to the arranged strategy, gestures/arranged.ts): a copy of the item follows the pointer, the others slide to the places the drop gives them (the preview being the command applied), and the release stores one set of the block’s arrangement with the new order, after which the copy settles where its item is drawn, as a member card’s does (endReorder). Side by side, a drop is refused where the moved group would leave its place among the stretches of a root it hangs on (rowRefusal, a clause per root: "G" stays right of "A", "B" and left of "C" on "R"): the stored order stays previewed, nothing is stored and the reason is reported through onrefuse; an item hanging on nothing goes anywhere.

  • Where a content drops is one rule, orderAt(entries, dragged, point, direction) (reorder.ts), read against the places before the drop: before the first other content that does not precede the point, a content preceding it when its middle lies at or above it (vertical), at or left of it (horizontal), or, in a matrix read row by row, when its row lies above the point’s or it lies in the point’s row with its middle at or left of the point; the group editor’s memberOrderAt is its vertical call. An arrow key moves the one selected block or item arrangedStep places along the arrangement (steppedOrder; nothing beyond either end), refused side by side as a drop is (Order, keys and the lock).

  • Cards in a group’s editor. A frame’s cards keep their display order and are reordered as ever (Editors). While the block draws its items side by side, a card whose member anchors the group on a root is pinned (isPinned): a press on it is not taken, so the roots’ order stays the groups’; a drop of another card that would give the group another anchor is refused (heldRefusal, core’s anchorChange: would anchor "coda" on "Score" instead of "Recapitulation").

  • A root drawn once. The once card is an ancestor card like any other: never dragged, opening its root’s timeline editor and raised in it. It counts for every group of its ancestor.groups: a group’s editor zooms out to the once card behind it whichever group it is keyed by (elidedReach), and the stretch a handle of its gaps may not cross spans every group’s anchor. A gap an arrangement cuts is a derived gap to the canvas: selected, never edited (subjectOf gives none) and never deleted, Custom storing only the gaps a card derives by itself; cardAxis.ts gives each edge of it the scale of the run beside that edge.

  • Motion. When an item’s place changes (a choice, a drop, a step), its elements move from its old place to its new one (itemMotions, first in blockMotion, by the same progress as the elisions’ motion), a member card of a moving frame with its frame; on a switch to or from Horizontal the once card moves sideways into its runs while the root’s other ancestor cards fade out or in, none unfolding.

Show coordinates#

The Show coordinates button (“Show coordinates (C)”), under the Marquee button, switches between reading positions and editing named timestamps. While it is on, a line is hovered anywhere from the line out to the outermost map drawn at it, within 8 device pixels (HIT_TOLERANCE) of the screen the window is on, the canvas following the device pixel ratio when the window moves to another (deviceRatio, a watched resolution query), and the flag shows the timestamp of the position there (Values on timelines): its cross-section, read from a spec the arranger builds from what a cross-section line in the hovered line’s card crosses (crossSectionSpec: the hovered line’s timeline as the anchor, then every other timeline the card draws, as a line, an ancestor line or a segment of a line, cardTimelines, each at its offset from the anchor’s zero with the maps drawn for it in the card, then the anchor’s ancestors the card does not draw, without maps), part by part, top to bottom where the reading crosses each timeline (each member’s ordinate: its own line, its ancestor line in the hovered line’s card, or the segment line holding it), each part headed by its timeline’s name, with that timeline’s coordinate and one row per map drawn for it, top to bottom as drawn (a map drawn above the line before the coordinate), the hovered row highlighted, each value its pretty text rounded with the granularity’s decimals (flagParts, rowValueText). What the pointer hovers, the line or one map, is the row the position is picked on (core’s snapOnRow): the line snaps on its timeline’s axis, a map on its value’s axis, brought back exactly through the map’s inverse. The flag follows the pointer, read again once per animation frame, and hangs from a pole standing at the position like a flag, opening right of the pole, its edge touching it (flagPlace); it never changes sides: near any border of the canvas it is shifted by exactly what keeps it on screen, the pole staying at the position (onScreen); the pole rises above the line, its maps and its cross-section line. Every value is core’s timestamp; the canvas evaluates no map and keeps no value. A measure view needs the expanded rows of its MeasureMap, which the canvas asks of the server through its measureMaps prop, once per manifest version (MeasureKnotsSource); without them its row says so. MatchClaim tables#

A block’s MatchClaims are read from its package’s MatchClaim table, a Parquet file beside the manifest, which the manifest names as a resource of type MatchClaim (MatchClaims and potential MatchStamps). The editor holds at most one per block (MatchClaimTables, matchClaims.svelte.ts, owned by the workspace session): the server’s answer for the table, the file it was read from, its name and whether a belief has changed it. Tables are held for the session only, never written to the workspace file, and dropped when another workspace is opened. Every other resource a manifest names, a Projections table among them, is neither read nor dropped. Matches need a running server: a MatchClaim table is read through tilie-server, which checks it against the block’s manifest (POST /api/match-claims, The server), and only once the server has answered its health check. The session hands the tables two facts apart: whether the server computes (computes, it answered its health check), which is all that reading a table, recording a belief and answering provenance and events need, and whether it offers files (files, the editor’s fileHost, given where the health answer does not say "files": false), which only looking for a table beside a manifest’s file and locating a manifest need. A public server computes and offers no files. While none is given, or the one given has not answered, nothing is read and nothing asked: no table, no MatchClaim’s provenance or events (provenance and events give null), no belief recorded (believe answers unavailable’s text), and MatchClaimTables.unavailable says why, in plain text: “Matches need a running tilie-server. To start it, run uv run tilie –static-dir ../app/packages/editor/dist in the repository’s server folder (or tilie, once installed with pip install tilie-server), then open the editor at the address it prints.” (HOW_TO_START_A_SERVER); after a failed health check the failure comes first, as the header’s offline mark gives it (“The server did not answer the health check (<message>).”). That text is the hint and title of the Matchstamp tab’s Load MatchClaims… and belief buttons, which it disables, and what an expanded MatchClaim shows in place of its events and, under its Provenance button, of its provenance. Show matches posts HOW_TO_START_A_SERVER alone, as information, which stays for its reading time (infoDurationOf); a failure of the health check is shown by the header’s offline mark and those controls. While the server given has neither answered nor failed its health check (MatchClaimTables.waiting), nothing is posted; once it answers or fails with Show matches on, the editor follows the outcome as a press of M then would (checkHealth): answered, it loads the named tables; failed, the information is posted once.

Where a manifest came from. A block may know a place from which the files its manifest’s resources name, at locations relative to the manifest, can be read (ManifestSource): the folder of the manifest’s file on a server that offers files, where the workspace states the block’s location (The workspace view), read through GET /api/files (serverSource), which serves nothing in a hidden folder (a table there is warned of as “<location> could not be read from where the manifest came from: …”), or, in a test build, the Fixture menu’s table fixture served beside its table. A manifest picked in the browser, or one whose location the workspace does not state, has none, since a browser gives no access to the files beside a picked file.

Loading. A table is loaded in three ways.

  • Turning Show matches on (onmatches, Show matches) loads, block after block in the canvas’s one order of blocks (the arranger’s blocksInOrder), the MatchClaim table every block’s manifest names that is not loaded (loadNamedTables, MatchClaimTables.loadNamed): a table the manifest names by an absolute http or https URL fetched from that URL by the browser, any other from where the manifest came from, where the table lies there; either without a question, else as the user chooses it in one question per block (after a URL that cannot be fetched, the fetch’s message shown, the question of Load MatchClaims…); while no server can read a table nothing is asked: where no block holds a table, or a block’s manifest names a table the block does not hold, the message bar gets one information, HOW_TO_START_A_SERVER, in place of every question, none while the health check has not answered yet, its outcome followed then (MatchClaim tables); where no block holds a table once the tables are loaded, the information “No MatchClaim table loaded.” (NO_MATCHES).

  • Load MatchClaims… in the Matchstamp tab asks for the MatchClaim table of the block it shows (choose) in the same dialog, a table without a location where the manifest names none.

  • Import manifest… and Open… in the browser pick several files at once, manifests or a workspace with .parquet tables beside them (openFiles, withMatchClaimTable, readTables): each table is a MatchClaim table, taken by the block whose manifest names a MatchClaim table of its file name, else by the only block, else not loaded, with the warning “<file> was not loaded: no manifest picked with it names it.” Open… opens one workspace file at a time and warns about any other.

The user chooses in the tables dialog (TablesDialog.svelte, on the dialog shell), one question per block, which a URL field answers in every case (a relative URL is read relative to the page):

  • with the server’s files and no location of the manifest, “Where is <manifest>?”: Locate… opens the file dialog on manifest files; the file chosen becomes the block’s location, so the table is read from beside it now and whenever the workspace is opened again, and a folder not holding it is a warning (”<url> is not beside <file>.”);

  • with a location whose folder does not hold the table, “<url> is not beside <manifest>, at <path>”: Locate… opens the file dialog on MatchClaim tables, starting beside the manifest, for the session;

  • for Load MatchClaims…, “Load the MatchClaim table of <manifest>”: Locate… opens the file dialog on MatchClaim tables, starting beside the manifest where its location is known;

  • without the server’s files, wherever a manifest names a table by no http or https URL and no served example holds it, and for Load MatchClaims…, “Load the MatchClaim table of <manifest>”: Locate… opens the browser’s picker for one .parquet file (openFiles), whose bytes are the table, posted to the server as any other; nothing is kept of where it lies, so it is picked again after the workspace is reopened, and no manifest is located.

Locate… answers at once; Load takes an entered URL; Cancel and Escape take nothing. A URL or a path that cannot be read is an error. A table loaded through the dialog or from where the manifest came from is confirmed in the message bar (“Loaded the MatchClaims of <file>.”). A table the server refuses is not held; the error lists its problems by row, or says that the server did not answer or cannot read MatchClaim tables (501).

Reading on demand. The Matchstamp tab asks the server for what it shows of the MatchClaims of the MatchStamp it shows, and only of those: their provenance (POST /api/match-claims/provenance), once per table and list of claims, and, once one of them is expanded, their events (POST /api/match-claims/events), once per table and list of claims; each promise is kept while its tables are held, and a failed one is let go, so asking again asks the server again. A belief the user records goes to the server with the table (POST /api/match-claims/belief), which answers the new file; the editor reads that file through POST /api/match-claims again and holds it in place of the old one, marked changed. A changed table is kept only by Save MatchClaims…, which saves the file under the table’s name, through the same file access as a workspace; Save saves the workspace alone. New workspace, Open… and a workspace example, which drop every table, first ask about a table that a belief changed and that is not saved, in the one question that also names unsaved changes of the workspace (The header and the File menu); Load MatchClaims… over a changed table asks the same of that table in the tab. The editor holds one indexesOf for the canvas, the drawer and the Matchstamp tab (blockIndexes): a block’s anchor indexes under its MatchClaim filter, for the user as believer, under its manifest’s @context, with the user’s belief per MatchClaim, by which the canvas colours the anchors.

The user#

The editor has one user, the person viewing it (core’s User {name, orcid}, workspace/user.ts): a name, which the store records as the author of every command (setAuthor), and an optional ORCID iD, which names the user in the provenance of what they state. DEFAULT_USER is “Gerhardt”, the store’s default author, without an iD. readOrcid(text) reads an iD or its address (https://orcid.org/…, http://orcid.org/…, orcid.org/…), white space trimmed and its check character verified (ISO 7064 MOD 11-2), as the bare iD, else null. statedBy(user) is the IRI stated_by names the user with: https://orcid.org/<iD>, else, since a name is no IRI, the tag IRI tag:timetoalign.github.io,2026:tilie:user: followed by the encoded name (USER_TAG); the editor’s own proposals are stated by TILIE_AGENT, https://github.com/TimeToAlign/TimeLineEditor.

The editor holds the user in UserState (user.svelte.ts), read once at mount from the browser’s localStorage under the storage option’s userKey (tilie.user on the standalone page) as JSON {name, orcid}; a stored text that is not that, no key, or storage that is unavailable gives the default user, and a change then holds until the editor goes. The user belongs to the viewer: no setting, command or workspace holds it, and a workspace shows it only as the author of its journal entries, as stated_by of the pairs the user stated and as the actor of the user’s beliefs.

Type profiles#

A type profile is a type of thing of the viewer’s own (profiles/profiles.ts): an id and a label, its “is a” alternatives per catalogue system, each a statements fragment without slots, the verbs usually stated of such a thing, in the order the composer offers them first, and the systems its values are preferably looked up in. Beside the profiles stand the viewer’s lookup services, {id, name, endpoint}, each the system service:<id>, offered wherever a verb’s fallback pattern takes a thing. The catalogue the Metadata tab offers is TimeToShare’s with the profiles (withProfiles): a profile of a catalogue type’s ID replaces that type, the others come before the catalogue’s types, so a profile’s verbs lead where its “is a” is stated. Profiles and services are application settings of the viewer, never part of a workspace or a manifest. They travel as a canonical YAML file, profiles.yaml, {profiles, services}, read by readProfiles, which names the path of the first problem. The editor holds them in ProfilesState (profiles.svelte.ts), read from localStorage under the storage option’s profilesKey (tilie.profiles on the standalone page) as that file’s text and written there on every change; a stored text that is no profile file gives none, and without the key storage is never touched and the profiles hold until the editor goes, so an embedded editor stores none unless its host names a key.

File, Type profiles… opens the “Type profiles” dialog (ProfilesDialog.svelte, a native modal dialog) over a draft: it lists the profiles, adds one, empty or from a catalogue type (“New profile from”), edits the selected one (ID, Label, “Is a” per system from the catalogue’s patterns, Verbs with Move up, Move down and Remove, “Look values up in”), deletes one, and lists, adds, edits and deletes services, a service’s new ID or its deletion followed in every profile’s lookup systems. Import YAML… replaces the draft with a profile file, Export YAML… writes the draft as one; Save checks the draft and makes it the editor’s profiles, Cancel and Escape drop it.

The combobox#

The composer’s fields are the editor’s own combobox (src/combobox/, decisions/0027), after the WAI-ARIA Authoring Practices example “Editable Combobox With List Autocomplete”: typing never selects an option; the focus stays in the text field, which names the active option through aria-activedescendant; the listbox holds the options alone, and a message for an empty result lies beside it; a visually hidden status region announces the count or a running search. Its keys are one pure table (keys.ts): the arrows open the list and move the active option, Enter picks it, Escape closes a shown list and, with none shown, clears the text; a key the table handles is consumed, so an Escape that closes the list never reaches the drawer. An option is picked on the primary button’s release, the press in the list cancelled so that the field keeps the focus. The options, the loading flag and the text belong to the field’s user: a search is driven through a function binding of the text, and an active option that a shrinking list no longer holds counts as none.

Not yet wired#

Two designs are kept as tested code that nothing but its tests uses.

Deleting a segment, a child of a segment line, leaves a gap in its line, and is refused. The design of closing that gap is kept as two modules:

  • core’s manifest/segments.ts, the gap arithmetic: for a segment S of length l in the segment line Λ, left extends (the segment before S grows by l), right extends (the segment after S starts where S started and grows by l), left shifts (Λ’s offset grows by l and Λ shortens by l, so the later segments keep their place in the parent) and right shifts (every later segment moves back by l and Λ shortens by l); extending needs an unlocked neighbour on that side, shifting needs Λ unlocked, and shifting left needs Λ to be a child; a way is permitted only when the manifest it leaves passes the schema and the invariants; an unknown length stays unknown (segmentGaps lists the permitted ways); core’s main entry exports only the GAPS table and the Gap types, which the menu reads;

  • the arranger’s GapMenu.svelte, the menu that will offer those ways: a form named “Delete segment ” with one fieldset, “Closes the gap”, of four radio buttons, each named after the neighbour that moves, a disabled one titled with why, the first permitted one chosen, then OK and Cancel.

The anchor index (MatchClaims and potential MatchStamps) holds two parts that Show matches does not draw yet, both exported from core’s main entry: the matched spans (AnchorIndex.spans, MatchedSpans: per interval MatchClaim passing the mask, in row order, its start anchor and its end anchor) and the segments of two timelines (segmentsOf(index, first, second), Segments: the anchors between them in the first timeline’s order and, per gap between neighbouring anchors, whether a matched span covers it, built on demand in one sweep; one timeline twice is refused). They are kept for tinting the areas between the start and the end of two matched intervals and the gaps between neighbouring anchors that no matched interval covers, and for the anchors a MatchStamp inferred between stated ones interpolates from.

The schema contract#

@timetoalign/timetoshare supplies the types generated from its one schema document, the validator of the current schema version, unknownKeys and manifestWarnings (which core reads through its file value), the description specification’s statement functions (withPair, withoutPair, withCandidate, withBelief, candidates, rectangles, samePair, expandIri, usedPrefixes, withContext) with FORMAT_CONTEXT and its catalogue of identifier systems, verbs and types (defaultCatalogue, recognize), the complete and minimal forms, the canonical order, the lossless reader and the writer of its serializations (canonical YAML, pretty JSON and RFC 8785 JSON), the manifest fixtures with their minimal twins, the rejection and warning cases, and the two drawing fonts with their one metrics table. It migrates nothing, and neither does TiLiE; the schema repository, not TiLiE, is the source of truth, and timetoalign proves conformance in its own tests.

Core is typed through the package’s typed forms: completeManifest(value: LosslessManifest): CompleteManifest, minimalManifest and canonicalManifest, where LosslessManifest is the schema’s type with every number a JsonNumber and CompleteManifest the same with every defaulted key present. parseJson throws JsonSyntaxError with the offset, line and column of the fault, which the inspector shows. The validators of both languages raise SchemaValidationError with a stable code naming the rule broken (not_a_manifest, schema, representation, finite_number, duplicate_id, dangling_target, conversion_map, child_unit, child_offset, child_end, segment_line, manifest_id, group_member, order_key, display_order, band_order, timestamp_timeline, timestamp_coordinate, match_timestamp, alignment_anchor, matchstamp, resource_timeline, resource_type, calendar_date, and for statements context, reserved_key, iri, element_value, provenance_duplicate, belief_actor), and refuse a number that is not finite at its path; core reports every such refusal as schema_violation with the validator’s path and message.

Until schema 0.2.0 is published on npm, every application package that uses the schema takes it through a link: to the js/ directory of the TimeToShare checkout beside this repository (link:../../../../TimeToShare/js), built there with pnpm build; the link: then gives way to @timetoalign/timetoshare ^0.2.0, and a later schema version not yet released is tried through the same link: (AGENTS.md). The editor’s tests let Vite transform the package (server.deps.inline), since the Fixture menu reads its fixtures as text.

The server#

tilie-server uses FastAPI and uvicorn (decisions/0008). It runs in one of two modes, which its settings carry as data and one guard, OriginGuard, enforces. A local run (tilie) binds only 127.0.0.1, generates a random token for each run, accepts only the Host 127.0.0.1:<port> or localhost:<port> and an Origin of http:// and one of them, and requires Authorization: Bearer <token> on every /api/ route. tilie hands the token to the browser in the URL fragment (http://127.0.0.1:<port>/#token=<token>), which a browser never sends to a server, so the token never reaches the access log; the page reads it, removes it from the address bar with history.replaceState, and keeps it in memory only, so a reload loses it. A public run (tilie --public-host <host>, repeatable, decisions/0038) binds 0.0.0.0, accepts only a Host equal to one of the given hosts as given and an Origin, where one is sent, of https:// or http:// and one of them, checks no token, and does not route the file routes (404); it refuses with 413 a request body over 16 MiB (body larger than 16 MiB, at once where Content-Length says so, else as soon as the bytes received pass it, before the route runs) and a posted MatchClaim table of more than 1,000,000 rows or over 128 MiB uncompressed (the sum of its row groups’ total_byte_size), read from its Parquet footer before any row; at most two computations on a posted body run at once, further ones waiting while health and the editor’s own files are never held behind them, and uvicorn holds at most 32 connections. It opens no browser and prints tilie: public mode on port <port>, answering as <hosts>; no token, no file access on standard error; --check, --root and --no-browser are refused with it. A foreign Host is refused with 403 forbidden host, a foreign Origin with 403 forbidden origin, in either mode, and every response carries Referrer-Policy: no-referrer. The server serves the built app from its wheel and implements GET /api/health, whose member files is true in a local run and false in a public one, POST /api/measure-maps, POST /api/match-claims, POST /api/match-claims/provenance, POST /api/match-claims/belief, POST /api/match-claims/events, POST /api/flows, POST /api/flows/flow-maps, /api/reconcile/musicbrainz and, in a local run, GET /api/folders, GET /api/files and PUT /api/files. POST /api/measure-maps reads the posted manifest with timetoshare’s loads (YAML or JSON, any serialization of the format; the editor posts the minimal canonical YAML of the manifest file, its IDs written #id) and converts it with with_bare_ids, answers each entry of its measure_maps, in order, with its rows as timetoalign’s MeasureMap.expand_rows expands them, the quarter-note values (qstamp, nominal_length, actual_length, offset_within_measure) as Fraction pairs, a measure’s volta as the map writes it (a pass from 1, or a non-empty list of distinct passes; the client keeps 1 and [1] apart), and every ID bare, and refuses with 422 and {detail: {path, message}} a body that is no UTF-8 text, no YAML or JSON, no object, writes an ID without # (at timetoshare’s path and message) or holds a measure map timetoalign refuses. POST /api/match-claims takes a package’s manifest file and its MatchClaim table in one body (decisions/0030): the manifest’s length in bytes as a little-endian 32-bit integer, the manifest’s text in any serialization, then the table’s Parquet bytes. It checks the table against the manifest with timetoshare’s match_claim_problems, reads it in the package form, each coordinate cell’s number written once, in the member its number type names, with timetoalign’s MatchClaimField.from_package_table, and answers in the same framing: a compact JSON header (format tilie-match-claims, version 1, the rows, the timelines with their number types, the actors, each the IRI timetoalign compares it as, the belief words and every array’s offset and length) followed by the columns of decisions/0029 and every row’s beliefs as little-endian arrays, each starting at a multiple of eight bytes from the answer’s start; it filters nothing. A body it cannot split or read, and a table the check refuses, are answered 422 with {detail: {path, message}}, a refused table with the check’s problems besides (each {code, row, column, message}); without timetoshare’s table checks or timetoalign’s MatchClaim table the route answers 501. Two routes read a MatchClaim’s provenance on demand and record a belief (decisions/0031); the server stays stateless, so each takes the whole body of POST /api/match-claims again, behind a little-endian 32-bit length and a JSON object of parameters (a parameter unknown or missing is refused with 422 at its name), and checks the table against the manifest as that route does. POST /api/match-claims/provenance takes {claims}, a list of MatchClaim IDs, and answers JSON {claims: [{id, provenance}]} in the order asked, each entry the MatchClaim’s provenance entry as TimeToShare defines it (its row’s own, else the one the file states for every row, read through timetoalign’s Provenance), its absent members left out, each belief’s actor the IRI timetoalign compares it as, or null; an ID the table lacks is refused at ["claims", i]. POST /api/match-claims/belief takes {claim, actor, belief, date} and answers the new table, application/vnd.apache.parquet: the manifest is read as timetoalign’s AlignmentBundle, the table’s MatchClaims are added to it, and the belief is recorded by its add_match_claim_belief (an actor’s earlier belief replaced in place, every other actor’s standing), the server building no provenance itself; the posted table takes the provenance column of the package table timetoalign writes (package_table), a provenance the file stated for every row now stated by each row, every other column and row kept, columns the table type does not declare and the file’s other metadata included; the new table passes timetoshare’s table checks against the manifest and its succession_problems against the posted table (422 with every problem otherwise) before it is answered, and nothing is stored on the server. Both answer 501 where the installed timetoalign cannot read provenance. POST /api/match-claims/events (decisions/0032) takes the provenance routes’ parameters, {claims}, then the body of POST /api/match-claims unchanged; it checks the table against the manifest as that route does and answers JSON {claims: [...]} in the order asked, each {id, timeline_a_id, event_a_id, event_a_name, timeline_b_id, event_b_id, event_b_name, certainty, is_synchronous} as timetoalign reads the MatchClaim table (null for a side without an event or an event without a name). An ID the table lacks is refused at ["claims", i], and the route answers 501 where the installed timetoalign cannot read MatchClaim tables. The table route and the two provenance routes read the manifest as an AlignmentBundle, so a manifest timetoalign refuses is answered 422 with its message, and every actor is the IRI it stands for under the manifest’s @context, every prefix the context declares (timetoalign’s belief_context and actor_iri): a compact and an absolute spelling of one actor are one actor, as the editor compares them; a belief is stored with its actor as the request spells it, and a stated_by is answered as written. /api/reconcile/musicbrainz answers the Reconciliation Service API 0.2 of the W3C Entity Reconciliation Community Group for MusicBrainz (decisions/0028): GET gives the service manifest; queries=<JSON> (GET, or a POST form) reconciliation queries; extend=<JSON> data extension with the properties name and sameAs, the IRIs timetoshare’s catalogue recognises among an entity’s URL relationships; GET …/suggest/entity entity suggestions; GET …/properties the properties a type can be extended with. An identifier is <type>/<mbid>, so the identifier space http://musicbrainz.org/ followed by it is the entity’s IRI. One MusicBrainz client per application (tilie_server.musicbrainz) keeps MusicBrainz’s rules of use for every request the editor causes: one request a second, the next starting at least a second after the last, under a User-Agent naming the server, its version and its repository and no person, each answer kept for the run (the 1,024 least recently used), so a viewer’s repeated lookups reach MusicBrainz once; requests use the standard library’s urllib in a thread and form bodies are read with parse_qs. A malformed request is refused with 422 and {detail}, a failure of MusicBrainz is 502, and a request needing timetoshare’s catalogue answers 501 where the installed timetoshare has none. Three routes serve TimeLineEditor’s own files below the run’s root folder, the user’s home folder unless tilie --root <folder> names another (decisions/0033): GET /api/folders?path= lists a folder, its subfolders, then its files of the kinds the editor reads (workspace, manifest and table files: .tle.json, .yaml, .yml, .json, .parquet, in any case), each sorted by code point; GET /api/files?path= answers the bytes of a file of those kinds; PUT /api/files?path= writes the request’s body into a workspace file (.tle.json, in any case) and no other, through a new file beside it that then takes its place (os.replace), a replaced file keeping its mode. A path is POSIX and relative to the root, "" the root itself, and resolved with every symbolic link followed. No route lists, reads or writes a path with a hidden segment below the root (a name starting with .), as asked or as resolved, and a file’s kind holds for the name asked and for the file a link leads to. A listing leaves out hidden names and every symbolic link leading out of the root, into a hidden place, to nothing, in a loop or to a file of another kind; a link inside the root is listed as what it leads to. Refusals answer {detail: {path: ["path"], message}}: 400 “path outside the root” for an absolute path, a NUL character or a path resolving outside the root; 403 “hidden files are not served”; 400 “the links at the path form a loop”; 400 “a file’s path does not end in /” where a file route’s path ends in /; 403 “TimeLineEditor reads only workspace, manifest and table files” for a read, and 403 “TimeLineEditor writes only workspace files” for a write, whose name or resolved target is of another kind, checked before the file’s existence, so that no request learns whether a file of another kind exists (a folder asked of GET /api/files under a name of no such kind is refused so too); 404 “no such folder” or “no such file”; 409 “a folder lies at the path”; 403 “permission denied” for a file or folder the user may not read, list or write, a file the user may not write never replaced. tilie serves on port 8000 and opens the browser (--no-browser does not). A browser keeps what a page stores (the drawer’s state, the user, the type profiles) by origin, port included, so one port keeps it from run to run; while port 8000 is taken tilie serves on a free port and prints a notice saying so on standard error, and --port chooses the port, taken as given. --root names the root folder of the file routes, an existing folder. tilie --check verifies an installation (--static-dir checks a development build). The server takes timetoshare through timetoalign’s requirement and imports it inside the route, so it starts without it; an installed timetoalign without the measure-map expansion, or a timetoshare without the reader, answers that route with 501; no timetoalign release carries the expansion yet, so the server is installed against the timetoalign checkout beside this repository, and the timetoshare package that timetoalign checks statements with against the TimeToShare checkout beside it (AGENTS.md).

The flow routes (routers/flows.py). A manifest states where a timeline’s flow jumps and breaks; in which order its stretches are then played is computed by timetoalign alone, and the server stores nothing. Both routes take the body of the MatchClaim provenance routes without a table: a JSON object of parameters behind its length in bytes (a little-endian 32-bit integer), then the manifest file’s text, in any serialization timetoshare reads, to the end of the body; timetoalign reads the manifest whole, checking it as a manifest reader does, before the timeline is looked for. POST /api/flows takes {timeline, convention?, believer?}: timeline a bare ID at any depth, convention one of default, single and printed (all three without it), believer the actor whose beliefs decide which flow-control elements are read (without it, the elements no actor rejects). It answers JSON {timeline, number_type, atomic_sections, conventions}: the atomic sections in the order of the axis, each {name, start, end}, and for each convention asked for its runs (pairs of coordinates on the timeline’s own axis, as its number type writes them) and the sections they cover; default also has findings, each {kind, coordinate, end?, message}, kind one of flow_ends_at_break, never_played, endless_loop, ambiguous_targets and undetermined_target, end only for a stretch never played. A timeline without flow control is one section and one run. POST /api/flows/flow-maps takes {timeline, believer?, actor?, date?} and answers, as application/json, the manifest file’s text with that timeline’s flows written (for a child timeline its atomic sections on the child and its FlowMaps on its root) by timetoalign’s create_flow_maps_from_flow_control, in the minimal form: its atomic sections as regions, typed as such in their statements, and its FlowMaps of the three conventions. A manifest holding flows written before is written again: a region whose stretch is still an atomic section keeps it, a new stretch gets a new region, the FlowMaps keep their IDs, and a region whose stretch is none any more stays, rejected by the belief false of actor (who asks) on date (YYYY-MM-DD, the server’s day without one); without an actor that case is refused. A refusal answers 422 with {detail: {path, message}}, a parameter’s at its name and at ["timeline"] where the manifest lacks the timeline or timetoalign does not write its flows (a length not known, flow control that cannot be played, a section to reject and no actor); a timetoalign that cannot play flow control answers 501, and every other route still answers. The client’s flows(manifest, timeline, {convention?, believer?}) and withFlowMaps(manifest, timeline, {believer?, actor?, date?}) send these requests and check their answers.

create_app(settings) composes the application from one frozen ServerSettings (host, port, token, static_dir, root, resolved once, and the mode: public_hosts, files, body_limit, table_rows, table_bytes and computations, each None or empty in a local run; allowed_hosts() and allowed_origins() give the guard its hosts and origins, and a settings value without a token that serves files is refused), the security middlewares (OriginGuard, the Host, Origin and token guard, which checks a token only where the settings hold one; the referrer policy; and, where a limit is set, the body limit inside the guard), the health, measure-maps, match-claims, MatchClaim provenance, MatchClaim events, flows and MusicBrainz routers, the file router where files is set, the one MusicBrainz client of the run (create_app(settings, musicbrainz=…) takes another, as the tests do to replay recorded answers) and the static files. Performance#

A drag costs script time on every pointer move in proportion to the number of nodes, culled or not, because Svelte Flow re-adopts its node list on each move: about 1.2 ms per move at 100 blocks, 2.4 ms at 300 and 5 ms at 900 in headless Chromium. Culling reduces what is mounted (42 of 300 nodes in a zoomed view) but adds its own cost to pan and zoom (3.1 ms per wheel event at 300 blocks against 0.4 ms without), so it starts above 500 blocks. One node per block, a complete projection, reused node objects, memoized drawings and a preview that replaces one node keep a gesture from doing any work of TiLiE’s own beyond the previewed block.

pnpm e2e:budget checks, for a workspace of 300 blocks in Chromium, from the DevTools Protocol’s script time: dragging a block at most 3 ms of script per pointer move; panning and wheel zoom at most 1 ms per move or event; one command at most 1 ms; one block’s redrawing within one frame (16.7 ms). Opening a workspace costs reading it and replaying its journal; the store’s decision record holds the figures. Heavy data is intended to stay out of the DOM, in WebGL panes and columnar tables, with parsing that could block interaction in workers.

Dependencies and deployment#

Each dependency requires an ADR under decisions/, in the MADR structure, weighing writing it ourselves, naming the audited version and a review date (decisions/0001); the dependency policy, ranges in manifests, exact development pins, lockfiles and the 14-day release window, is stated in AGENTS.md.

Install the current product with pip install tilie-server, then run tilie. The wheel contains the built browser app, so starting it needs neither Node.js nor a download. The container image (Dockerfile at the repository root) serves the editor and the server in public mode on a hosting platform (the README, decisions/0038). Planned deployment forms are a hosted browser app talking to a user-run local server, a desktop wrapper and collaborative hosting.
