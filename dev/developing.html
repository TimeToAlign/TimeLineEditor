Developing TimeLineEditor#

This guide is for people who change the code. It says how the repository is laid out, what the words mean, how a change travels through the layers, and where a new thing goes. The rules every contributor holds are in AGENTS.md; the public code model is in architecture.md; each decision that brought a dependency in is an ADR under decisions/. The commands to install, test, build and run are the table in AGENTS.md; this guide does not repeat them.

Two repositories, one wall#

TimeLineEditor holds the editor (app/, a pnpm workspace of TypeScript packages, Svelte 5 and Vite) and the server (server/, FastAPI, installed from PyPI as tilie-server, whose wheel ships the built editor), run by its user on their own computer or as a public instance. The manifest format the editor reads and writes is specified by the TimeToShare repository: the JSON Schema, the generated TypeScript types and validator, the Python validator, the fixtures, the drawing specification docs/drawing.md and the two drawing fonts with their metrics table. Setting up both checkouts is described in CONTRIBUTING.md. The editor never specifies the manifest; where the editor needs a word the schema does not have, the schema is changed first.

The three layers, named by one word each#

  • A manifest is one TimeToShare manifest: root timelines with exact coordinates, children at exact offsets, conversion maps, regions, flow control; optionally groups, measure maps, an id, a name, statements (the manifest, every timeline and every region state what they stand for in property–value pairs with their provenance, under an inline JSON-LD @context), resources (the files that come with it, such as its MatchClaim table), and graphical, the styling of its own drawing with the display orders of its groups, the band orders of its timelines, its named timestamps (groupstamps among them), its named MatchStamps and its elisions, the stretches of timelines its drawing leaves out. A manifest knows nothing about a canvas.

  • A block is one manifest placed on the canvas with its placement: x, y, scale, length, spacing (the gap between the items of its drawing), margin (the empty space above its first item and below its last), the requested width and height, and places, the stated places of its items and the stated sizes, spacings and margins of its members’ cards, a member’s card set through its group, and, where it has one, its arrangement, which places its items in an order instead of at their places. A block’s styling is its manifest’s graphical; the block has no style layer of its own.

  • The workspace is the canvas: its name, its blocks (and, where it has one, how they are arranged), its default style rules, the settings (outside the journal), and the command journal that produced them with the sessions of its editors. It is one pretty JSON file, written as TimeToShare’s schema package writes JSON, by default <name>.tle.json after its name, format version 8; the manifests it holds are written there in their complete form with bare IDs, whatever file form each is exported in.

No other noun names any of these. “Document”, “record” and “envelope” name nothing here.

The containers of a drawing#

A manifest and its drawing nest the same four containers, each drawn by a container of its own (TimeToShare’s README, “Containers”, states the format’s side):

Semantic level

Graphical container

What it draws

bundle

block

the manifest; a block’s whole drawing

group

frame

a group, with its title and its members’ cards

timeline

card

a timeline drawn as a root: an ungrouped root’s card lies in the block, a member has one in the frame of each group that has it as a member; a virtual timeline is drawn the same way, in its groups’ frames or, placed by the block, in the block

facet

band

one of a timeline’s five facets, markers, conversion_maps, children, regions and flow_control, at the timeline’s line

All four are boxes, drawn as rectangles with rounded corners; “box” is the generic word and names none of them. A child timeline is content of its parent’s children facet, drawn as a line in the children band, never in a card of its own unless a group has it as a member. A band draws nothing of its own; it is the unit that holds and orders what its timeline has of its facet, placed by the timeline’s band order in graphical.order. Manifest content and addresses take the semantic words, the drawing’s containers the graphical ones.

One card stands apart: a root that contains a group member is drawn whole as an ancestor card behind the frame of that member’s group (TimeToShare’s drawing specification, section 8.8; shown by default, hidden by a style rule). It is a card held by the block with no place (its key {item: G, member: R}, core’s holding "ancestor"), no bands and no places entry; layout records it only when displayed, with axes for its lines marked ancestor. The arranger tells it apart with isAncestorCard and raises it over the frames while a timeline editor is open on its root or on a descendant of it that no member card below the root draws, where the root is in no group or is a member drawing an ancestor card itself (raisedRoot, the one decision that the drawn order, the pointer, the places of coordinates, the marquee and the focus all read, through one stacking, stackingOf). The Elisions tab’s timeline block hides it (the mode Hidden, a display of none on the root’s cards) or chooses which elisions it draws.

Elisions follow the containers. A card elision, on a timeline in no group, is drawn by the cards that draw its root whole, the root’s own card where it is ungrouped and its ancestor cards; a group elision, on a timeline that lies in a group, is drawn across that group’s frame, held by the frame, and carried into the ancestor cards behind it. Which elisions a card or a frame draws is a style property of that container (elision-mode and its companions, in one rule on the root’s card address or on the frame’s), never a setting of the workspace, so an export draws what the canvas shows (decisions/0035). Layout records what each such container decides in Drawing.elisions, and the arranger’s cardAxis.ts is the one map between its abscissas and the coordinates of its timelines.

Arrangements are placement, never part of the manifest (decisions/0036). A block’s arrangement takes its items, frames and ungrouped roots’ cards, in an order: one under another, or side by side in one row, where every root that a group hangs on (a member of the group lying inside it) is drawn once, by one ancestor card, the once card, behind every group hanging on it. Core owns the rules (manifest/arrangement.ts: the anchors, rowOf, the refusal and its reason), layout places what they give (block/arrangement.ts, and packed, the one packing function), and the arranger reorders: the workspace’s arrangement packs the blocks, an arranged level turns move into a drag that reorders, and orderAt is the one rule of where a content drops. The once card’s line changes scale only between two groups, in a gap of origin arrangement or at a seam, a jump of its axis that leaves out nothing and is no element; seams also lie where an ancestor card’s member meets its frame’s borders.

The code treats the four kinds through one model:

  • core’s registry (manifest/containers.ts) says what each kind is, the level it draws, what holds it, which place members it may state in a block’s places, and the schema element type it is styled as (block, frame, card, band); containersOf lists every container of a manifest with its holder;

  • layout records every container of a drawing as one DrawnBox in Drawing.boxes, with its kind, its place in places, address, holder, rectangle and the range of elements it drew; the arranger and the editor read that list and derive nothing from element order;

  • the arranger’s level table (containers.ts) says, per level of the canvas (the arranger, the bundle editor, the group editor, the timeline editor, the flow-control editor), what may be moved, reordered, resized, removed and opened, and how each kind of box resizes.

“Item” is a role (a frame or a block-held card is placed freely in the block); “member” is a fact about a timeline (a group names it); neither is a kind.

IDs and addresses#

Every element of a manifest has an ID, unique across the manifest and kept verbatim: any non-empty string without control characters, with no reserved word (TimeToShare’s README, “Identity”). TiLiE never parses an ID, and names a timeline, group or manifest without a name by its whole ID. Where core creates an element itself (the first segment of an empty segment line, the default timestamps, a named timestamp, an elision, a named MatchStamp with its anchors, and a group, region, conversion map, flow-control element, FlowMap or virtual timeline added without an ID), it mints the ID as timetoalign does: the type prefix, _ and 13 random base-36 characters (timestamp_3b7gu4iwqkg9i). dispatch mints these IDs before the command is applied and journals them in the command’s ids, so a replay reads them and mints nothing; a minted ID is never renamed or reissued. Any other element a command adds (a timeline, a child, a measure map) carries its ID.

An address names anything a command can target, and it is an object, never a string composed from IDs: {} the workspace, {block} a block, and {block, id?, facet?, part?} an element of the block’s manifest, its members those of the manifest’s own address (TimeToShare’s drawing specification, section 2.2): {block, id: X} a timeline, group, measure map, conversion map, region, flow-control element, FlowMap, virtual timeline, named timestamp (its wedge), named MatchStamp, alignment anchor (its line) or stored elision (every gap drawn of it); {block, id: T, part: ["elision"]} the gaps a card derives on T, which have no ID; {block, id: T, facet: F} the band drawing facet F of timeline T; {block, id: T, part: ["card"]} every card of T, a virtual timeline’s too; {block, id: V, facet: "regions", part: [k]} the chunk k (from 0) that the virtual card of V draws, or the switched card of T with T in place of V, the one address where facet stands with part, its part beginning with the chunk index; {block, part: ["name"]} the manifest’s title; and a part of an element its address with start, name, label, id, coordinate, line or ellipsis appended to part (a named timestamp’s line, a groupstamp’s across every frame drawing it included, is {block, id: X, part: ["line"]}). An element drawn more than once, a member’s cards in several frames among them, keeps one address in every drawing of it, so a rule or a selection reaches every drawing. Two addresses are equal when they are equal as whole objects, and addressKey(address) is an address’s one string form, the only key a map or a Svelte keyed block takes for it. Core’s resolveAddress(view, address) is the one place that decides what an address names, returning a discriminated record (workspace, block, title, frame, timeline, card, band, entry, measure map, timestamp, elision, MatchStamp, anchor, FlowMap, virtual timeline, virtual card, virtual band, chunk, part); a part names something only where it is drawn. Nothing reads an ID or an address out of a string. A statement has neither an ID nor an address: it is changed through the element holding it, by one set of that element’s whole statements or provenance.

How a change travels#

  1. A gesture or a form edit in the arranger or the inspector builds one command: add, set, remove or style, aimed at addresses. One completed gesture is one command; a choice that needs several (a frame’s flow switch) goes through Store.edit, which makes them in the editor of their target as one closed session, so one undo step. A preview shows drawingOf(preview(view, command)), the command applied without the schema check, so a preview can never differ from what the release stores.

  2. The store parses the command, applies it through the pure reducer (apply in core), refuses it with a typed {code, message, path} when a rule is broken (the path starts with the addressKey of the target at fault, an added value’s problems under the address it goes into, never with a bare block id), runs the side-effect guard when an editor is open, and journals it with its author and time. Undo and redo move by steps over the journal; a closed editor session undoes by runs.

  3. The view is an immutable value; every component reads it through the arranger’s liveStore adapter, never through a copy.

  4. The projection turns the view into one Svelte Flow node per block; each node renders its block’s drawing, draw(manifest, placement, layers, metrics) from layout, memoised by the identity of its inputs, as the element descriptors the writers produce. The same descriptors become the SVG and TikZ exports, so the screen and the file agree element for element.

  5. Saving writes the store’s snapshot (every open editor folded as Done would fold it) through core’s canonical writer; opening reads the file, checks its structure, and createStore replays the journal to verify that it reproduces the snapshot, refusing a file whose journal does not.

Where a new thing goes#

  • A new settable property of an existing target: a row in the capability table commands/set.ts with its reader in values.ts; a test case per accepted and refused value; the README section of the commands.

  • A new command kind does not exist: the four commands are the whole vocabulary (AGENTS.md). A new editable thing is a new target kind or a new property.

  • A new target kind (something addressable): the resolver (workspace/address.ts) learns to name it, the registry learns its container facts if it is a container, commands/set.ts gets its table, commands/add.ts and commands/remove.ts their branches, and manifest/contains.ts what it contains, which the side-effect guard reads.

  • A new container kind: a row of core’s registry CONTAINERS (its level, its element type, the place members it may state by holder); layout’s DrawnBox records for it; the arranger’s RESIZING row and its rules in the level table, which fieldsOf turns into the inspector’s Properties fields. The drawing specification names it first.

  • A new element of the drawing: drawing.md first (the specification and its fixtures pin the drawing; a rule the code applies that the specification does not state is a defect), then layout’s element type, its band or module, its bounds in geometry.ts, its descriptors in the writers, a hand-derived fixture beside the emitted ones. pnpm draw (the writers’ script, AGENTS.md) draws any manifest file to SVG and TikZ as the fixtures are drawn, to look at a case before it becomes one.

  • A new gesture: a strategy in the arranger’s gestures/ for its pointerGesture engine (what a press takes, the value a point gives, the preview, the command on release) and a row property in the level table saying where it is offered; never a fifth pointer machinery.

  • A new inspector panel: a Svelte component over a plain state module (<name>.svelte.ts) with the logic unit-tested without the component, a tab in tabs.ts, words in words.ts.

  • A new area of a stamp tab (Timestamp, Matchstamp): a section component in the editor’s inspector/stamps/, beside the name header (StampName), the Coordinates both tabs share (StampCoordinates), the display toggles (DisplayToggles), the wedge’s rotation (WedgeRotation), the Timestamp tab’s Properties (TimestampProperties), a MatchStamp’s Lines, MatchClaims, Update and Remove and MatchClaim filter (MatchstampLines, MatchClaimList, MatchstampActions, MatchClaimFilter) and the coordinate granularity (CoordinateGranularity), all inside StampPane, which holds their shared look. The section reads what its tab hands it and builds its commands with the tab’s readers (timestamp.ts, matchstamps.ts, matchstampLines.ts); what both tabs show is one section, never two.

  • A new schema rule: the rule in both validators (js/src/validate/*, python/src/timetoshare/validation/*, the same module and the same order), a stable code in both diagnostics modules’ ValidationCode, a shared rejection fixture that pins the path and message in both languages, and the README’s rule table.

  • A new lookup system: an ADR on its access rules first (where it is asked from, its rate limit and identification, what leaves the browser); a module in the editor’s src/lookup/ behind the one Lookup, its kinds in kinds.ts, and answers recorded once for its tests. A service whose rules of use a browser cannot keep is asked only through the server, which keeps them for every request of the run.

  • A new viewer setting (who the user is, type profiles): the browser’s localStorage under a key the host passes in storage, never a workspace setting, a command or a manifest member.

  • A new dependency: an ADR first, always weighing “write it ourselves” (AGENTS.md, Design Rules).

Testing#

Every package carries tests/README.md, written before its tests. It states what proves each behaviour, which values the package owns and derives, and where the oracle is: the drawing fixtures of TimeToShare (byte-equal SVG and TikZ), the specification’s arithmetic, or another package. It names no internal function. Values are exact; no thresholds, no approximate matchers. Tests reach a package through its public behaviour: what a press at a point hits, which command a drop produces, what a drawing contains. Small runs (one package’s vitest --project, one Playwright spec, one package’s tsc) run freely and in parallel; the whole suite, e2e:all, the build, the budgets and the screenshots are heavy and run one at a time. The end-to-end specs read state through the test hook window.__tilie, present only in development and test builds, never through pixels, except the screenshot project.

Embedding#

Editor.svelte is the whole editor as a component with options: the server ({url, token} or none), the storage slot and keys (or none: the user and the type profiles are kept under userKey and profilesKey only where the host names them), whether the Metadata tab may look values up (lookup), the shortcut scope ("editor" or "window"), the notices URL, and onchange and onselect callbacks. It styles only its own root, names its ids per instance and reaches the page only through those options. App.svelte is the one page that owns a document: it writes the title, styles html and body, takes the server’s token from the URL fragment and binds the window shortcuts. embed.ts exports mount(element, options), a framework-free entry over Svelte’s mount/unmount, built by pnpm build:embed into dist-embed/ (tilie.js, tilie.css, the fonts). A host page loads the stylesheet, imports mount, and hands it one element with a definite height.

Releasing#

A TimeToShare version is a schema version, released by release-please on main and published to npm and PyPI by trusted publishing. TimeLineEditor is released as tilie-server to PyPI, by release-please and trusted publishing as well; while the editor builds against an unreleased schema version it links the sibling checkout and is not published. scripts/check_versions.py checks a tag against the declared versions.
