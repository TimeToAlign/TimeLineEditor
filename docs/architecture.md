# Architecture

## Overview

TimeLineEditor (TiLiE) is a browser editor and visualization tool for musical timelines represented by `timetoalign`. It works with three layers, each named by one word only:

- a **manifest** is one TimeSkeleton manifest as the `timeskeleton` schema (version 0.7.0) describes it, of the one shape every manifest has, `{timeskeleton, timelines, id?, name?, meta?, groups?, graphical?}`, whatever it holds: one root timeline, several, a group, or several groups beside ungrouped timelines. Every manifest is an alignment bundle. A timeline has exact coordinates, children at exact offsets, conversion maps, regions, flow control, measure maps and metric hierarchies, and a `length` that may be `null`, not known; `graphical` holds the style rules of the manifest's own drawing, its groups' display orders and its timelines' band orders. Every timeline, group, conversion map, region, break, jump and marker has an `id`, and so may the manifest; a `name` is what is displayed. A manifest with an `id` or a `name` is a named bundle, one with neither an anonymous bundle. A manifest knows nothing about where, or how often, it is shown;
- a **block** carries one manifest, entirely its own, together with its placement, the other inputs of its drawing: position, scale, graphical length, vertical spacing, requested box size, and the stated places of its drawing's containers. A block's styling is its manifest's `graphical`; the block has no style layer of its own;
- a **workspace** is the canvas: its blocks, their order, the workspace settings, and the command journal that produced them, with the sessions of its editors. Block ids are the workspace's namespace, and an address names anything in it.

TiLiE is local-first: the browser communicates with a local Python server, which is meant to own loading through `timetoalign`, heavy computation and exports, while the browser keeps a small editing core. This keeps the library's capabilities close to the operations that need them and interaction responsive without a request per keystroke.

## What exists today and what is planned

`tilie-server` starts a FastAPI server on loopback, serves the built editor, and answers authenticated `GET /api/health`. The editor is one component that the standalone page mounts and that any other page can mount through a framework-free entry ([Embedding](#embedding)). It is a header and the canvas, with an inspector drawer on the right. It opens, saves, imports and exports files, autosaves in the browser, and inspects and edits the selected block's manifest ([The editor](#the-editor)); a block, a group or a timeline opens in an editor of its own on the canvas ([Editors](#editors)); everything it changes goes through the commands of `core`.

Loading sources through the server, raster exports, a generated API client, collaboration over a network and automation endpoints are not implemented. Sections and sentences that say "planned" describe the intended architecture, so that additions have explicit boundaries; everything else describes behaviour that exists.

## The container hierarchy

A block's drawing is one **box**, the manifest's box. Inside it every group is drawn as a **frame**, every timeline drawn as a root as a **card**, and every timeline, root or child, has five **bands**, one per feature it can hold: `markers`, `conversion_maps`, `children`, `regions` and `flow_control`. A band draws nothing; it is the unit that holds and orders what its timeline has of that kind. These four kinds are one hierarchy, and every package reads it through one model:

| Kind | Level | Styled as | Held by | Its entry in the block's `places` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| box | bundle | `block` | nothing | none: the block's own placement places it |
| frame | group | `group` | the box | under the group's id: `x`, `y`, `length`, `scale`, `spacing`, `width`, `height` |
| card | timeline | `card` | the box, for a root in no group; its group's frame, for a member | held by the box: as a frame's; held by a frame: only `width`, `height`, `spacing` |
| band | feature | `band` | the card of the member drawing that draws its timeline | none: its place is its timeline's band order |

The **level** is the semantic word a kind stands for; the schema's element types (`block`, `group`, `card`, `band`) remain the words style selectors use. "Item" is the role of a frame or of a card the box holds, each placed freely in the box; "member" is a fact about a timeline, that a group names it; neither is a kind.

`core` holds the registry (`CONTAINERS` in `manifest/containers.ts`): for each kind its level, its element type and the place members it may state by how it is held. `containersOf(manifest)` lists every container of a manifest with its holder, memoized per manifest, and `placeMembersOf` answers what a container may state. A block's `places` maps the id of a group or of an ungrouped root (a root that no group names and that holds no member) to its stated place, and the id of a member to its card's stated `width`, `height` and `spacing`; it is the drawing's `places` (timeskeleton's drawing specification, sections 1 and 8.6). After every command each touched block drops the keys that name no container and cuts an entry to what the registry allows its container, so a command never fails for a stale key: a root that becomes a member keeps only its card's size, a member that leaves its group keeps it as an item.

A timeline's **band order** is its five band addresses and its own id, top to bottom (section 6.4). Its **own** band order is its entry of the manifest's `graphical.order`; its **effective** one, the one it is drawn with, is its own, else its parent's effective order band by band, else the default (children and conversion maps above the line, markers, regions and flow control below); its **inherited** one is the effective order it would have without an entry (core's `bandOrderOf` and `inheritedBandOrder`). A group's display order is its entry of `graphical.order`, else its `members` (`displayOrder`).

Layout records every container of a drawing as one `DrawnBox` in `Drawing.boxes` ([Layout and writers](#layout-and-writers)); the arranger and the editor read that list and derive nothing from elements or descriptor order. The arranger's level table says what each level of the canvas may do with each kind ([Levels and permissions](#levels-and-permissions)).

## Addresses

An address names anything a command can target: `workspace`, a block id, or `<block id>/<element address>`. Inside a manifest one rule holds in every manifest: a timeline and a group are addressed by their bare ids; every other element (a conversion map, region, break, jump or marker) as `<root>/<id>`, `<root>` being the root timeline whose tree holds it; a card as `<timeline>/card`; a band as `<timeline>/<kind>_1` (`score/regions_1`); a drawn part of an element appends `/start`, `/end`, `/name` or `/label`; and the manifest's title is `name` (`TITLE_ADDRESS`), whether or not the manifest has an id, so giving a manifest an id changes no address; a title exists only for a named manifest, so on an unnamed one `name` names nothing (`unknown_target`). These words are reserved, and so is every id matching `^(markers|conversion_maps|children|regions|flow_control)_[1-9][0-9]*$`, so no address is ambiguous.

`resolveAddress(view, address)` in core is the one place that decides what an address names. It returns a record of one kind: `workspace`, `block`, `title`, `frame`, `timeline`, `card`, `band`, `entry` (with its kind and index) or `part` (with its owner and word), carrying the block, the located timeline and the container where there is one, or a `CommandError`. `resolveElement(manifest, element)` does the same within one manifest, and `elementOf` folds parts and bands away to the element they belong to. The builders `qualify(block, element)`, `cardAddress(timeline)`, `partAddress(owner, word)` and `elementAddress(root, id)` are the only code that builds an address, and no package splits, slices or suffix-tests one. `<block>/<manifest id>` resolves to the block for every reader, and a command aimed at it is refused with `unknown_target`: the manifest's own id names no target. `indexOf(manifest)` is what every reader of a manifest needs once, memoized per manifest: its containers, the ancestry of its timelines, the owner of every element address and its drawn elements.

A block id is ten characters from `0-9a-z`, drawn from the platform's cryptographic random source (`mintBlockId`). Element ids live inside a manifest and repeat freely across blocks; an id a manifest carries is kept, and `nextId` mints `{prefix}_{N}` (`clt_1`, `region_1`) for an element that has none, following timetoalign's scheme.

## The core package

`@timetoalign/tilie-core` is framework-free: no DOM, no Svelte, no network. Its main entry holds what a host and the packages above core use: manifest and workspace I/O, the commands and `preview`, the store, the resolver, the registry, the band and order helpers, the error types and the domain types. `@timetoalign/tilie-core/drawing` holds the drawing's primitives for layout and the writers (the elements a manifest draws, the selector test, the member drawings); `@timetoalign/tilie-core/numbers` the exact arithmetic. The package needs Node 22.18 or later, as `@timetoalign/timeskeleton` does.

### Numbers

`Rational` is an exact fraction over `BigInt`, always normalized, built from a `[numerator, denominator]` pair, a `bigint`, a safe integer or a decimal integer string, never from a JavaScript float. A `Real` is a value as Python's numeric tower holds it, an exact `Rational` or a float, combined and compared as the tower does (a sum involving a float is a float, a comparison is exact). `fromFloat` returns the exact binary value of a float and serves arithmetic that must be exact on a float's value, such as the writers' page transform; it never re-expresses a coordinate. A `Coordinate` is `{unit, numberType, value}` whose value is what the axis's number type declares: a `bigint` for `int`, a `number` for `float`, a `Rational` for `fraction`. A value on its timeline's own axis is bare, read and written as timetoalign's `number_from_json` and `number_to_json` do: an `int` axis reads a JSON integer, or an integral JSON number as that integer; a `float` axis reads a JSON number, or an integer as the float it equals exactly; a `fraction` axis reads only a pair; every integer is int64-bounded. Arithmetic and comparison work within one axis only and throw `AxisMismatchError` across units or number types.

### The manifest value, load and save

A manifest held by `core` is always in the complete form, every key with a default present. Its types are read-only views of the schema package's generated complete forms: `Manifest` of `CompleteTimeSkeleton`, holding root timelines (`TimelineManifest`, whose `length` is a value or `null` and whose `children` carry their `offset`) and groups, its `meta` a JSON object. A value on a timeline's own axis (`length`, `offset`, region `start` and `end`, break and marker `coordinate`, jump `from_coordinate` and `to_coordinate`) is bare (`AxisValue`). Every number is a `JsonNumber`: a plain `number`, a `bigint` beyond 2^53, or a `JsonFloat` for an integral float, so that `600.0` is written back as `600.0`. The `graphical` block is the one exception: its declarations hold canvas numbers, so it is read plain at the schema boundary, a declaration's `2.0` stored as the double `2` and an integer beyond 2^53 refused with `invalid_value` unless a double holds it exactly; a `style` command's `set` and the workspace settings' `graphical` are read the same way. A key the schema does not declare is kept where it was found: values are spread, never rebuilt from known fields, so unknown keys survive every command and are written back. `core` reads, orders and writes no JSON of its own: the reader, the canonical order and the standard writer are the schema package's.

`normalizeManifest(value)` is the one path from an unknown value to a manifest, wherever it comes from (a file, a workspace, a command): validate with timeskeleton's `validate`, complete, put in canonical order, apply the segment locks, check the invariants root by root, complete the styles. It returns the manifest or a `LoadError`, and holds no type assertion: the validator's assertion types the value. A command that has already checked a fragment continues from completion, so a fragment is validated once; a value canonical order cannot order is refused with the schema's first problem. `loadManifest(text)` parses the text with timeskeleton's `parseJson` and normalizes it; its problems are `invalid_json` for text that is not JSON (at its line and column), the first `schema_violation` with the path the validator reports, or every invariant violation of a schema-valid manifest; unknown keys are never problems. A value declaring another version than 0.7.0 is refused at `["timeskeleton"]`; there is no migration. `saveManifest(manifest, {minimal?})` checks the manifest the same way, throwing `InvalidManifestError` with every problem, and writes it with the schema package's `dumps` in the complete or the minimal form, byte-identical to what the Python writer produces. An entry a command adds or replaces (a map, region, break, jump or marker) is checked by the same schema inside its timeline before it is stored.

### Commands

Every edit is a command, a plain JSON object `{type, ...}` carrying its author and time once journalled, and there are four: `add`, `set`, `remove` and `style`. Each is aimed at addresses, and the kind of its target, resolved once, decides what it does. `apply(view, command)` is a pure reducer: it never modifies its input and returns either the new view or a `CommandError` `{code, message, path}`. It parses the command, dispatches it by type and target kind, applies it atomically (one refused change refuses it whole), and settles every block the command touched: each touched manifest is checked by the schema and the invariants once, and its block's `places` fitted to its containers.

- `add {into, <kind>: value}` adds a `block` into `workspace`; a root `timeline` (completed, the locks of its place applied, its id not taken; a root may carry an `offset`) or a `group` (`{id, name?, meta?, members}`, its members existing timelines, under the identity rules) into a block; a `child`, `region`, `break`, `jump`, `marker` or `map` into a timeline. The value carries its id.
- `set {changes: [{target, values: {...properties}}]}` sets properties of blocks and elements; the properties are nested under `values`, so none collides with `target`. One gesture is one command, so moving five blocks is one `set` with five changes.
- `remove {targets}` removes blocks and elements, mixed, every target resolved before anything is removed; a block goes with every block whose `parent` chain reaches it. A removed timeline takes along its subtree; its id from every group's `members` and every display order; its own and its descendants' band orders; every conversion map whose `source_id` or `target_id` names a removed timeline; every style statement entry addressing a removed element, a statement left with none going too, and every statement whose `select.within` names it. A group left without members stays. A removed group goes alone: its members stay, roots ungrouped and children in their parents. Removing the last root leaves an empty box. A segment, a child of a segment line, whose removal would leave a gap in its line is refused with `segment_not_removable` ("Removing segment S of line L is not supported yet"); one that goes with its line, an ancestor or its block is removed with it.
- `style {select, set, name?, blocks?}` puts a style statement into the manifest of each listed block, or of every block with an element the selector matches. With `name`, the declaration becomes a named style in that manifest's `styles`. A statement whose selector equals an existing one is merged into it; a property set to `null` is removed, and a statement left empty is dropped.

What each target admits is one capability table in code (`SETTABLE` for `set`, beside the tables of `add` and `remove`):

| Target | Address | `set` | `add` into it | `remove` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| workspace | `workspace` | `grid {size, snap}`, `blockDefaults {scale, length, spacing, width, height}`, `graphical` (the default statements) | `block` | no |
| block | `<block>` | `x`, `y`, `index`, `scale`, `length`, `spacing`, `width`, `height`, `manifest` (whole) | `timeline`, `group` | yes |
| timeline (root or child) | `<block>/<id>` | `name`, `length` (a value, or `null` for not known), `locked`, `meta`, `order` (its band order), `parent`, `group`; a child also `offset`; an ungrouped root also `place`, and a group's member a `place` of only `width`, `height` and `spacing` | `child`, `region`, `break`, `jump`, `marker`, `map` | yes |
| group | `<block>/<id>` | `order` (its display order), `place` | none | yes |
| region | `<block>/<root>/<id>` | `name`, `start`, `end`, `meta` | none | yes |
| break | `<block>/<root>/<id>` | `coordinate`, `control_type`, `condition`, `repeat_count`, `label`, `name`, `meta` | none | yes |
| jump | `<block>/<root>/<id>` | `from_coordinate`, `to_coordinate`, `control_type`, `condition`, `repeat_count`, `label`, `meta`, and `target` (a marker id of the same timeline, or `null`) | none | yes |
| marker | `<block>/<root>/<id>` | `name`, `coordinate` | none | yes |
| conversion map | `<block>/<root>/<id>` | `map` (the payload, whole, its `id` unchanged) | none | yes |

A group's `order` is its display order, every member once, or `null` to delete the entry (`invalid_order` otherwise). A timeline's `order` is its band order, or `null` so that it inherits again; any other value is refused with `invalid_order` and the first problem in the terms of timeskeleton's validators, such as `"score/regions_1" is missing from the band order of "score"`. `place` merges into the block's `places` entry of an item or a member's card: a member given a value is stated, one given `null` is no longer stated, and a place left empty is deleted; a member's card given `x`, `y`, `length` or `scale` is refused with `not_an_item`, and so is a timeline drawn in no card of its own.

**Where a timeline lies.** Two properties change a manifest's structure; no gesture sends them yet. `parent` is `null`, making the timeline a root, or `{timeline, offset}`, making it a child of that timeline at that bare offset, its subtree with it, by the rule that places every child (it may grow an unlocked parent, as below). Refused: `unit_mismatch` for a parent of another unit; `segment_structure` when the moved timeline is a segment or a segment line, or the new parent a segment line; `cycle` for the timeline itself or one of its descendants; a locked parent that would have to grow, as for any child. `group` is a group id, adding the timeline as that group's last member, or `null`, taking it out: `already_member` when it is a member of another group, `nested_member` when it would hold or lie inside another member of that group. Ids never change; a moved timeline keeps its band order and its card's stated size, and settling reshapes its `places` entry for its new holder.

A command is atomic. Ids are never settable, nor a timeline's `unit`, `number_type`, `measure_map` or `metric_hierarchy`, nor a root's `offset`. A `set` that changes nothing, a value written equal to the stored one or values that undo each other, is refused with `invalid_value` ("the command changes nothing"); the values written are compared path by path, and a block where a change reached past its path (a whole manifest, a move, an offset, a group) is compared whole. A position on an element's own axis is a bare value, normalized as its axis's number type selects: an integer on a `float` axis is stored as that float, an integer on a `fraction` axis or a coordinate dict is refused with `invalid_coordinate`, and a pair is reduced to lowest terms. The caller mints the id of what it adds, so that replaying a journal is deterministic: a block id with `mintBlockId`, an element id with `nextId`; an id already held in its scope is refused with `duplicate_id`.

`preview(view, command)` is the reducer without the schema check, the invariants and the no-op refusal: what a gesture shows before its release, so a preview can never differ from what the release stores ([The gesture engine](#the-gesture-engine)).

A refusal's path starts with the address of the target at fault, followed by the property or kind at fault (`[<address>, "length"]`), or by `"manifest"` and a location in the manifest the command would leave; only a malformed command names the malformed member of the command itself. The codes are stable strings, exported as `ErrorCode`:

- reading: `invalid_json`, `invalid_command`, `invalid_coordinate`, `invalid_value`, `schema_violation`;
- the workspace file: `unsupported_format`, `unsupported_version`, `unknown_member`, `missing_member`, `unknown_session`, `unreferenced_session`, `session_referenced_twice`, `journal_mismatch`;
- the workspace: `invalid_block_id`, `key_mismatch`, `unknown_parent`, `parent_cycle`, `invalid_index`, `duplicate_index`, `non_finite_number`, `non_positive_number`, `invalid_order`, `unknown_place`;
- addressing: `invalid_address`, `unknown_target`, `unknown_property`, `wrong_target_kind`, `id_not_settable`, `not_removable` (only `workspace`), `duplicate_id`, `not_an_item`;
- structure: `unit_mismatch`, `segment_structure`, `cycle`, `already_member`, `nested_member`;
- editors: `no_editor`, `not_inside`, `side_effect`;
- lengths and Delete: `child_exceeds_parent`, `locked`, `length_below_content`, `segment_end_fixed`, `segment_not_removable`;
- locks: `segment_never_locked`, `unlocking_not_supported`;
- manifest invariants: `number_type_not_allowed`, `region_end_before_start`, `map_source_unit_mismatch`, `metric_hierarchy_without_measure_map`, `negative_child_offset`.

### Invariants

The manifest invariants are timetoalign's, checked on every load and over the manifest every command would leave. The unit decides a timeline's class, and a unit admits its number types; every value on a timeline's own axis reads as that axis's number type; a region does not end before it starts; a map's source unit is the timeline's unit; a metric hierarchy needs a measure map. That ids are unique in their scope, that a jump's `target` names a marker of its timeline, that a child has its parent's unit and lies within its parent's known length, and that a segment line's segments are contiguous, are timeskeleton's rules, checked by its validator. A negative child offset is valid in the schema but not supported yet: core refuses it with `negative_child_offset` at the child's `offset` and the message `A negative child offset is not supported yet: child '<id>' has offset <offset>.`; a root's offset may be negative. The catalogue in `app/packages/core/tests/README.md` lists every row.

**Locking.** `locked` states whether a timeline's length is fixed; it defaults to `false`. A segment is never locked: its segment line's lock governs it, so reading a manifest unlocks every segment, and a segment is written with `false`. A segment line that is a child, a segment or a group member is locked, and is written with an explicit `true`, in the minimal form too. A `locked` that contradicts its place is refused: `true` on a plain segment with `segment_never_locked` ("A segment is never locked: lock its segment line '<id>' instead."), `false` on such a segment line with `unlocking_not_supported`. A locked timeline's length cannot be set (`locked`). An unlocked timeline's length may be set, never below the farthest point a child reaches (`length_below_content`) and never past its parent's known length (`child_exceeds_parent`); a segment line fixes its segments' ends, and a length that would move one is refused with `segment_end_fixed`; a length of `null` is always taken. A child added into, or moved within, an unlocked timeline past that timeline's known end grows that timeline, and only that timeline, to the child's end, provided the grown length passes the same checks; a locked timeline refuses the child with `child_exceeds_parent`. A parent never grows because a child grew, as in timetoalign.

**Unknown length.** A `length` of `null` means that the length is not known, on any timeline. It is a fact the manifest states, never inferred and never a violation; every check that needs an end runs where the end is known and is skipped where it is not. A child of unknown length reaches its offset, so adding it past the end of an unlocked timeline of known length grows that timeline to the offset.

Two rules go beyond what the library enforces, because the editor addresses and stores values directly: a value on a timeline's own axis must already carry the axis's number type, and a parent grows only to a value its own number type holds exactly. A child shares its parent's unit but may have another number type; its end, offset plus length, follows Python's numeric tower.

### The workspace view

A workspace is held as a `View`, an immutable plain value: `meta` (`format: "tilie-workspace"`, `version: 4`), `settings` and `blocks`, a map from block id to block. The settings are `grid {size, snap}` (8 units, snapping), `blockDefaults` (scale 1, length 400, spacing 1, no requested box size) and `graphical`, the workspace's default style statements. A block has an `id`; a `parent`, the canvas group holding it, `null` in this version; an `index`, its order key among the blocks of the same parent; and the inputs of its drawing besides the manifest's own: `x` and `y` (the canvas position of its drawing's place origin), `scale`, `length`, `spacing`, `width` and `height` (each `null` for the content's own size), `places` and `manifest`. `emptyWorkspace()` is the empty one. A view's problems, by block, are a block stored under another key, a malformed id, a missing parent or a cycle of parents, a malformed or shared order key, a canvas number that is not finite or a placement number that is not positive, a malformed place or one that names no container (`unknown_place`), and every schema or invariant problem of a block's manifest.

Sibling order is a fractional index, a string key that compares with ordinary string comparison, so that a block moves between two others by taking a key between theirs (`indexBetween`, `indicesBetween`, `isIndex`), compatible with Rocicorp's `fractional-indexing` 4.0.0 and written in `core` ([decisions/0016](../decisions/0016-fractional-indexing.md)).

### The store and its journal

`createStore(workspace, {author?, clock?})` holds a workspace, `{view, journal, sessions}`, and returns `view`, `journal`, `sessions`, `editing`, `entries`, `listed`, `changes`, `openings` (the count of every `open`), `canUndo`, `canRedo`, `dispatch(command)`, `undo()`, `redo()`, `open(target)`, `done()`, `cancel()`, `snapshot()`, `setAuthor(name)` and `subscribe(listener)`. `dispatch` stamps the command with its author (by default `Gerhardt`) and its time (ISO 8601, UTC, from the injected clock), applies it, runs the side-effect guard while an editor is open, and appends `{command, author, time}`; it returns `null`, or the `CommandError` of a refused command, which leaves the store unchanged.

**The journal is the authority.** Replaying the journal from the empty workspace reproduces the workspace. `createStore` verifies this before it returns: it replays the journal to its end and compares the result's `settings` and blocks with the workspace it was given, and throws `JournalMismatchError {entry, path}` naming the first differing path when they differ, an empty journal beside a non-empty view included. The editor shows that error as it shows a refused file. `readWorkspace` does not replay; reading and verifying are two costs ([Performance](#performance)). The decision record is [decisions/0019](../decisions/0019-workspace-store-and-journal.md).

**Undo by steps.** A change is one command at any depth, owned by the innermost session whose own entries hold it, the session of the editor it was made in, or by none for a command of the main journal. A change that the level in view owns itself is a step of its own; a closed session's changes go by runs, a step being a maximal run of consecutive changes owned by one closed session. `undo` reverts the latest applied step, `redo` re-applies the earliest undone one, so redo mirrors undo step for step, and a new command discards the undone changes. So a closed session holding `a1`, then a closed session of `b1` and `b2`, then `a2` is undone in three steps, `a2`, then `b1` and `b2` together, then `a1`. Views are kept per change with structural sharing; an undo past the views the store holds applies the changes from the latest view it holds, or from the empty workspace at the start of a loaded journal.

**Editor sessions.** Every editor is a **session**: the commands made in it between its opening and Done or Cancel. The store keeps one level per open editor above the main journal; the open editors are `editing`, outermost first, each `{session, target, kind, earlier}`: its session id (ten characters from `0-9a-z`), its target (a block id, `<block>/<group>` or `<block>/<timeline>`) and its kind (`bundle`, `group` or `timeline`; any other address has `no_editor`). `entries`, `listed`, `dispatch`, `undo` and `redo` act on the innermost level: undo inside a session stops at its start.

- `open(target)` opens an editor; with one open, the target must lie inside the innermost target and not be it (`not_inside`). When the last entry holding an applied change on that level is the session of the same target, that session is **unfolded**: taken off the level, keeping its id, its changes undoable and redoable again. Otherwise a fresh session opens, and `earlier` names the last session of the same target, listed read-only in the Journal tab.
- `done()` closes the innermost editor; a session holding entries is stored under its id in `sessions`, and `{session, author, time}`, stamped at Done, is appended to the level below. A session left without entries records nothing.
- `cancel()` closes the innermost editor and restores exactly the state at its opening.
- `changes` counts what Cancel would discard: with `o` the changes applied at opening, flattened at every depth, `c` those now and `p` the length of their common prefix, `(o − p) + (c − p)`.
- `snapshot()` returns the workspace as Save writes it, every open editor folded innermost first as Done would fold it, writing only applied changes.

### No side effects

An editor changes only what it edits. While an editor is open, `dispatch` compares the view a command would leave with the view before it and refuses the command with `side_effect` ("`<what>` lies outside `<target>`: an editor changes only what it edits") at the path of the first difference outside the innermost editor's target, in this order: a workspace setting; another block; for a group or timeline editor, its block's `scale`, `length`, `spacing`, `index` or `parent`; a key of the block's `places` that the target does not contain, except that a member's editor may change its group's `length`, `scale`, `width` and `height` and the `width` of every sibling's card (they are shared, or kept in proportion by a stretch); and the manifest outside the target, compared with the target reduced to its id and with only the style statements that match an element outside it. The command's change set, the blocks and settings it touched and the paths it wrote, narrows the comparison to them, with the same result as comparing everything: where every path lies inside the target and no change reached past its path, or the manifest is unchanged, the manifest is not compared at all. An entry of `graphical.order` lies inside when the target contains its group or timeline, and a `style` statement selecting bands lies inside when every band it matches belongs to a contained timeline. A block's `x`, `y`, `width` and `height` are the container adapting, never a side effect; removing or renaming the edited element is refused at the target. The bundle editor contains its whole block.

What a target contains is `contains(manifest, container, element)`: a timeline contains itself, everything that belongs to it or to a descendant, its bands among them, and their parts, but no ancestor line; a group contains itself, its title and whatever its members contain.

### The workspace file

A workspace is saved as one JSON file, `<name>.tle.json`: `{meta, settings, blocks, journal, sessions}`, every manifest embedded whole, never the canvas engine's state, so a text diff of two files shows only real changes. `writeWorkspace(workspace)` writes `{view, journal, sessions}` with timeskeleton's `dumps`; Save and the autosave write `writeWorkspace(store.snapshot())`. A journal entry is exactly `{command, author, time}` or `{session, author, time}`, and a session exactly `{target, entries}`, so sessions nest; every session is reached exactly once from the journal (`unknown_session`, `unreferenced_session`, `session_referenced_twice`), and one without entries is refused with `invalid_value`. `readWorkspace(text)` reads the file with timeskeleton's lossless `parseJson` and returns the `Workspace` or a `LoadError` listing every problem at its path in the file; a file of another `format` or `version` (such as "workspace version 3 is not supported; this editor reads version 4 only") is refused for that alone. There is no migration. `writeWorkspace(readWorkspace(text))` returns the text it read, byte for byte. A block's manifest is a TimeSkeleton manifest that timetoalign reads with `AlignmentBundle.from_dict`, or with `Timeline.from_dict` or `TimelineGroup.from_dict` when it holds one root or one group.

### Autosave

The editor keeps the last text of a workspace in the browser's IndexedDB, in the slot its `storage` option names ([decisions/0018](../decisions/0018-indexeddb-autosave.md)). Every change is written one second after the last, and a pending write is made at once when the page is hidden or the editor is destroyed; the text is `writeWorkspace`'s, deferred to the write so the command path never serializes. One editor at a time writes a slot, holding the Web Lock `tilie-autosave/<slot>`; a second one shows "Autosave of this workspace is active elsewhere, so changes here are not autosaved." When the editor opens and the slot holds anything but an empty new workspace, it asks "Restore the unsaved workspace from …?", with the time of the workspace's last journal entry; Restore opens it, untitled and marked unsaved, and Discard empties the slot. A saved text whose journal is refused shows "The autosaved workspace cannot be restored." with its problem. A failing autosave is reported once, in a message, as an `AutosaveError` whose `code` is `indexeddb_unavailable` or `indexeddb_failed`, and editing goes on.

## Layout and writers

The drawing of a manifest is specified in the schema repository, not here: timeskeleton's [drawing specification](https://github.com/TimeToAlign/timeskeleton/blob/main/docs/drawing.md) defines the `graphical` block, arithmetic, default styles, font, layout, element descriptors, page, and SVG and TikZ profiles completely enough that another implementation writes the same text byte for byte. The specification, together with the SVG and TikZ fixtures of a schema version, pins the drawing; there is no drawing schema, and a drawing's JSON form is internal.

### The drawing

`@timetoalign/tilie-layout` exports `draw(manifest, placement, layers, metrics)`, a pure deterministic function implementing sections 1 to 8 of the specification for every manifest. The placement supplies the graphical length of every item without one of its own, the vertical spacing, the requested box size and the stated places; the layers are the ordered `Graphical` blocks of the cascade, from lowest to highest precedence: the specification's defaults, the workspace settings' `graphical`, and the manifest's own; the metrics are the advance widths of the bundled drawing font. The returned `Drawing` holds:

- `width`, `height` and `origin`, the box-local position of the place origin `(Ox, Oy)`;
- `elements`, every drawn element in descriptor order;
- `boxes`, every container as a `DrawnBox`, the box first, then in drawing order: its `kind`, its `key` in `places`, its `address`, its holder's address, its rectangle in the drawing's coordinates, `displayed` (whether its own rectangle is drawn: always for the box, never for a band), and `range`, the half-open range of `elements` it emitted (a frame or card its own rectangle when displayed, then everything drawn as part of it; a band none). An item carries its place with the defaults applied and as stated; a card its drawn size before the scale, its vertical stretch and its stated `width`, `height` and `spacing`; a band its timeline, kind, signed **level** (1, 2, … above its timeline's line, −1, −2, … below), the number of displayed elements it holds directly, and `line`, the ordinate of its timeline's line, which tells apart the records of a timeline drawn twice. Only a band that draws an element is recorded, on a timeline whose line is displayed;
- `axes`, one `DrawnAxis` per line drawn: the timeline's address, its `line`, the abscissas `xStart` and `xEnd` of its semantic start and end (not of a circle, chevron or end mark; an ancestor line clipped to its member), its `unit` and `numberType`, the exact own-axis values `start` and `end` there (`end` null where the length is not known) and the conversion maps the drawing displays. `coordinateAt(axis, x)` gives the coordinate at an abscissa, computed exactly and rounded once into the axis's number type, or `null` outside the axis or past an unknown end;
- `unmapped`, the code points measured with the font's fallback advance.

Every manifest is drawn the same way: a grey box that opens with the manifest's title (its `name`, else its `id`; an anonymous bundle has none); each group a frame, a rounded rectangle holding the group's title and its members' cards one under the other in display order; each timeline drawn as a root in its own card, a raised rounded rectangle with a shadow offset to the right and down. The box has one horizontal layout: every item starts its lines after one name column and ends them with one right margin, and the members of a group share their frame's graphical length and scale. An item's scale scales everything in it about its top left corner. The title lies at the box's top left corner, above every item; an item placed higher grows the box upward. Within its card a timeline is drawn with its line and its five bands, stacked in its band order; each child is drawn whole in a row of its parent's children band, and the children bands of the lines of one row are laid out together; the defaults hide the markers, regions and flow control bands of every timeline in a card but the card's own. A segment line draws its segments on its own line, separated by boundaries. A top-level timeline ends with an arrowhead (open for a segment line) or a filled circle when locked; a child ends with an open diamond. A root of unknown length is scaled to its **reach**, the farthest coordinate its content reaches, and a line whose end is open runs on into three chevrons. A member that is a child also draws its ancestors above its line as **ancestor lines**, clipped to the member, running on into a double chevron where the ancestor reaches beyond it. `display: none` removes an element from the drawing and its layout; section 2.4 states every way a hidden element hides others.

### The pipeline

`draw` (`box.ts`) orchestrates four stages over one item model, in which a frame with its cards and a box-held card (a frame-less item with one card) take one code path: the places of every item, stated or default, and the box's place origin (`box/places.ts`); the box's one horizontal layout over all its items (`box/horizontal.ts`); each item laid out in its own coordinates (`box/items.ts`); and the shift into the box with the `Drawing`'s records (`box/emit.ts`, with the axes from `box/axes.ts`). A member drawing (`member.ts`) takes one timeline as the root: `traversal.ts` walks its timelines once into an index of children by parent and sources by line, fixes the axis (the length, or the reach, memoized per drawing) and every timeline's end; `timeline.ts` lays out every line with its bands, one module per kind of band filling them (`markers.ts`, `maps.ts`, `children.ts`, `regions.ts`, `flow.ts`); `rows.ts` lays out the rows of children bands at every depth, and `ancestors.ts` the ancestor lines. Line ends, chevrons, the brace and every element's bounds are `geometry.ts`'s, which the writers share. The style cascade (`Cascade`, `INITIAL_STYLE`, `DEFAULT_LAYER`) decides each property per element by the specification's layer order and statement rank, the statements bucketed by the selector members that discriminate them; text widths come from the font's advances, measured once per distinct text. Layout knows nothing about a page.

### The writers

`@timetoalign/tilie-writers` exports `toDescriptors(drawing)`, `writeSvg(placed)` and `writeTikz(placed, options)`. `toDescriptors` returns the ordered SVG-shaped descriptors that the canvas and both writers share; every descriptor carries its `source`: the index of the drawn element it renders, that element's type and address, and its role (`primary`, or `shadow` for a card's shadow). `placed` is a list of `{drawing, x, y, scale}`; the writers find the bounding box of all placed drawings, add a margin of 16 on every side, translate each drawing to the page and apply its scale. `writeSvg` returns one SVG page; `writeTikz` a complete LuaLaTeX document, or with `standalone: false` the `tikzpicture` alone, `fontDirectory` naming the directory of `DejaVuSans.ttf`. Both round the exact value of every double to four decimal places, ties to even.

`pnpm -C app/packages/writers review`, from the repository root, draws every manifest fixture of the installed schema version (not the minimal twins nor the corpus ones), the resize and place cases and the manifests whose application layer shows a band the defaults hide, which are timeskeleton's drawing fixtures, and writes SVG and PNG files plus `index.html` under `app/packages/writers/review/`, rasterized with `rsvg-convert --zoom 2` under a fontconfig that exposes only the bundled font ([decisions/0017](../decisions/0017-rsvg-convert-for-review-renders.md)). `emit-drawings` writes those fixtures into a timeskeleton checkout and never rewrites the hand-derived figures. The application exposes no PNG, JPG, PDF or `.drawio` export.

## The canvas

`@timetoalign/tilie-arranger` builds the canvas on [Svelte Flow](https://svelteflow.dev) (`@xyflow/svelte`), which supplies pan and zoom, node dragging, the selection rectangle, keyboard focus and culling ([decisions/0020](../decisions/0020-svelte-flow.md)). It has two levels: at the workspace level, the **arranger**, blocks are placed freely; inside a block, its groups and timelines are arranged in **editors**, modes of the same canvas. The drawing comes from the manifest, never from an editable drawing surface: a person edits the manifest through commands, never by dragging an arrow endpoint, so a musical relation keeps its meaning whatever the display. Every rule is plain TypeScript tested without a browser; the Svelte components connect those rules to Svelte Flow.

### One node per block

`project` returns one Svelte Flow node per block, in index order. A node's id is the block id; its `position` is the block's `x` and `y`, the canvas position of the drawing's place origin, less the drawing's origin times the scale (`nodePosition`), so a box that grows left or up moves nothing else. Its `width` and `height` are the drawing's box times the scale, given again as `measured` with `handles: []`, so Svelte Flow never measures a node; one it had to measure would be mounted even outside the view. `selected` follows the selection, `zIndex` is the block's rank by `index`, and `data` carries the block, its drawing and descriptors, its selected elements, the open editor and the addresses inside the edited element. With an editor open, every other node is neither selectable nor draggable and carries `tilie-outside`. A node whose inputs are unchanged is returned as the same object, so a change to one block re-renders one node.

`drawingOf(block, settings, metrics)` draws a block with the layers `DEFAULT_LAYER`, the workspace's `graphical` and the manifest's own; drawings are memoized per block object and recomputed only when the settings or the metrics change identity, descriptors per drawing. Neither is stored.

`BlockNode` renders the descriptors as Svelte elements inside one `<svg>` per node, whose `viewBox` is the drawing's box; every attribute is the text the SVG writer writes, so the node's element tree equals the exported SVG's. Text is rendered as text, never parsed as markup. Every drawn element carries its address in `data-address`. The drawings use the DejaVu Sans file bundled by timeskeleton, under a family name of its own, and are mounted once that face has loaded.

**Marks.** The node draws marks after the drawing, never part of it, its descriptors or an export, and never stored. Every mark is one overlay element in one vocabulary: `.tilie-mark`, its kind in `data-mark` (`selection`, `hover` or `holding`), the address it marks in `data-marks` (none for the block's own box) and the kind of box in `data-box` (`box`, `frame`, `card` or `band`; none for a boundary's `line`). A box's mark copies the outline of its own rectangle (a card's, not its shadow's). The last drawn card or frame under the pointer inside the edited element is hovered, the block's own box never, and a segment boundary under the pointer is hovered in the accent colour; a selected box shows its selection mark and no hover mark. A hover change adds or removes one mark and touches no drawn element. Every other selected element carries `data-selected`. Every box shows a **holding mark** whenever the selection holds an element drawn as part of it other than itself, read from the boxes' ranges, never from where a box lies: 3 px dashed on a card, 2 px dashed on a frame, 1 px dotted on the block, whose node also carries `data-contains-selection`, measured on screen. In a timeline editor the hovered and the selected bands of the edited timeline are marked the same way (`data-box="band"`). Stroke widths are divided by the zoom.

**The box reader.** `boxes.ts` is the one reader of `Drawing.boxes` and of the descriptors' sources. A press, a hover and Enter are classified from the drawing: a press reads the hit descriptor's `source`, never the DOM target's form, and Enter takes its target from the drawing by address. It decides what a press hits (a box, a band or another drawn element), what it selects, what is hover-marked, and which boxes hold part of the selection; while an editor is open, only what lies inside the edited element. Which editor a double-click opens is decided from the same hit.

### Levels and permissions

One table in code, `LEVELS`, holds every rule of each level: `move`, `nudge` (the arrow keys, on the arranger only), `reorder`, `bands`, `marquee`, `itemsFree`, `frameWidthPinned`, `remove`, `order`, `open`, `lock` and `resize`. `rulesOf(editing, {locked, readonly})` is the one answer to whether something may run: a locked canvas turns off `move`, `nudge`, `bands` and `resize` and keeps the rest; a read-only canvas turns off everything that writes, opening an editor included, and still selects, pans and zooms. How each kind of box resizes is a second table, `RESIZING`, keyed by kind with a variant for a card held by a frame, naming the levels where it resizes and what each mode writes (`resizingOf(box)`). From the two tables, `fieldsOf(box, level)` lists the place members a Properties field shows for a container at a level, in core's place order, each with whose place it writes and whether the level edits it.

| Level | Moves | Resizes | Reorders | Marquee selects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Arranger | blocks | every block, frame and card | blocks among their siblings (z-order) | blocks |
| Bundle editor | groups' frames and ungrouped cards, freely, alone or with every other one in the selection | the edited block and every frame and card in it | none | elements |
| Group editor | none | the edited frame and its members' cards; the frame keeps its width | the member cards | elements |
| Timeline editor | none | the edited card, a root's or a member's; nothing for a child, which has no box | the bands of the edited timeline | elements |

In the arranger and the bundle editor items lie freely (`itemsFree`): an edit of one item's place, by gesture or form, also states the drawn `x` and `y` of every other item that states none, the frame holding a resized member's card excepted, so that nothing else moves. In the group and timeline editors an edit states only the places it changes, and an item below may follow it down, its container adapting.

### The gesture engine

Every drag inside a drawing (moving an item, reordering a frame's cards, moving a band) and every resize handle is one engine, `pointerGesture(strategy, options)`, an attachment driven by a strategy: `item`, `held`, `band` or `resize` (`gestures/`). The strategy says whether it takes a press, what value the pointer stands for, how that value is previewed and which command stores it; the engine owns everything else once. A press the strategy takes marks its element `nodrag nopan` before Svelte Flow reads it, so neither a node drag nor a pan starts, on a mouse and a touch screen alike, and captures the pointer; a press it refuses is the canvas's (a click, a pan, a marquee). A press released within 4 px (`CLICK_DISTANCE`, Svelte Flow's `paneClickDistance`) is a click and selects; past it the gesture starts, selecting the dragged box unless it is an item in the selection in the bundle editor, and every move whose value differs is previewed; the release dispatches one command and withdraws the preview; the click a browser fires after a drag is swallowed. Escape and a cancelled pointer end a started gesture without a command. Every gesture's preview is the command applied, the block drawn as `drawingOf(preview(view, command))`, and only that block's node is replaced; the projection never runs again for a preview, and a gesture can never show what the release would not store. Blocks are dragged by Svelte Flow itself, snapped by the canvas ([Numbers on the canvas](#numbers-on-the-canvas)).

The canvas reads the store through `liveStore(store)`, the one reactive adapter, which the editor makes once per store and passes to the canvas, the inspector and its editing bar: each getter (`view`, `editing`, `entries`, `listed`, `journal`, `sessions`, `changes`, `openings`) reads the store again after every change it reports, so no component keeps a copy. Undo and redo are exact, because the canvas shows nothing between two commands that the next command does not store or discard. Each gesture is one command:

- a drag of n blocks is one `set` with n changes `{x, y}`; a keyboard move (an arrow key one grid step, Shift and an arrow key eight) is one `set`;
- a resize of a block, a frame or a card is one `set`;
- a change of block order is one `set` of `index`; Delete is one `remove`;
- in the bundle editor, a move of one item, or of every item in the selection by the same travel, is one `set`;
- in the group editor, a drop of a member card is one `set` of the group's `order`, or nothing when the order is unchanged;
- in the timeline editor, a move of a band, by drag or by key, is one `set` of the timeline's `order`.

Opening an editor, Done and Cancel are store operations, as undo and redo are, never commands. While a block, or a frame or card in it, is dragged or resized, the canvas reports the placement it previews (`onlive`), which the inspector's fields follow.

### Numbers on the canvas

Canvas numbers are doubles, and the positions Svelte Flow reports carry binary noise. Before a number is stored it is quantized: a position to the grid when the workspace snaps to it, every other number (and every position without snapping) to 1/2048 unit, the bundled font's units per em, so that at an integral font size and scale every text-derived edge and plot origin is stored exactly. While blocks are dragged, Svelte Flow snaps them to the grid and the canvas aligns the dragged block horizontally: its left edge or plot origin moves onto the nearest left edge or plot origin of another block within 6 screen pixels, and a vertical guide is drawn there while the alignment holds; the other dragged blocks move by the same offset.

### Resizing

Every box is resized the same way: a block, a frame, an ungrouped root's card and a member's card each offer three modes wherever their level resizes them.

- **Scale** sets `scale`: everything grows or shrinks with its proportions, text included. Only the four corner handles are offered; the new scale is the larger of the two axis ratios, never below 1/8.
- **Box** sets `width` and `height`: the box changes and the content keeps its place at the top left. A request at or below the content's own extent stores `null` (a place's member unstated), so the box follows the content when it grows.
- **Stretch** sets `length` from the left and right handles, `spacing` from the top and bottom ones and both from a corner: lengths and distances stretch, while markers, line widths and text keep their size. The content grows in piecewise-linear steps, as the drawing specification derives them, so the values that put the dragged edge under the pointer are solved for numerically, each candidate value drawn once; the floors are 8 for `length` and 1/4 for `spacing`. A box enlarged beyond its content keeps its proportion to the content while it stretches, and a request no larger than the content becomes `null`.

In every mode the dragged edge ends under the pointer and the opposite edge stays put; a left or top handle also stores the new `x` or `y`. A block's stretch scales every place it states with it, `x`, `length` and `width` by the ratio of the lengths and `y` and `height` by that of the spacings (`blockStretchPlaces`). A frame or card resizes by changing the block's `places` (section 8.6). A frame or a box-held card: Scale sets its `scale` from a corner; Box its `width` and `height` from any of eight handles; Stretch its `length` and `spacing`, every stated width or height of it and of the cards it holds following its content in proportion. A member's card: Scale sets its group's `scale`; a horizontal Stretch its group's `length`, the stated widths of the frame and of every member's card following; a vertical Stretch the card's own `spacing`, the stated heights of the card and its frame following; Box the card's own `width` and `height`; as its frame decides where it lies, it scales from its bottom right corner and sizes from its right and bottom edges. In the group editor the frame keeps its width: a horizontal Stretch of the members runs from the right only, states the frame's `width` at its drawn width and stops where the cards would pass it. Sizes are in the item's own coordinates, before its scale, quantized to 1/2048 unit.

When exactly one box that the level resizes is selected, a toolbar centred 10 px above it offers Scale, Box and Stretch, also the keys 1, 2 and 3; it is placed by the rule of Svelte Flow's `NodeToolbar` (`getNodeToolbarTransform`) applied to the box, so it follows the box through every move, pan and zoom. The pressed button shows its mode's handles. The handles are the arranger's own, since a box's size during a resize is whatever its drawing measures at the previewed placement. The mode is the viewer's own and stays when the selection changes.

### Selection, marquee and deletion

The selection is a set of addresses: block ids and `<block>/<element address>`. It alone decides what the canvas shows as selected; Svelte Flow's own flags are reset to it. A selected address is marked wherever it is drawn, so a timeline drawn both as an ancestor line and as a member is marked on both lines.

- A click on a drawn element selects that element only. A press on a box selects that box: a card (or its shadow) selects `<block>/<timeline>/card`, a frame outside its cards the group, the block's own area outside its frames and cards the block.
- In a timeline editor, a click on an offered band selects it, `<block>/<timeline>/<kind>_1`; a selected band counts as its timeline's line for the contains-selection marks, and a selection rectangle never selects a band.
- Shift and a click add the address to the selection, or remove it. A click on the empty canvas clears the selection; Shift and a click there leave it.
- Escape cancels a gesture in progress; with none it clears the selection, and with nothing selected it closes the innermost editor.
- A focused block is selected with Enter or Space. A drag on a block, on one of its elements included, moves the block, first selecting it alone when neither it nor an element of it is selected.

A plain drag on the empty canvas pans; Shift and a drag draw a selection rectangle, which replaces the selection with the blocks wholly inside it (in an editor, the elements). The Marquee button ("Add to selection (marquee)") switches to an additive mode, which is also how a touch screen selects several things: a drag on the empty canvas adds what lies wholly inside, and every click, Enter or Space toggles its address.

Delete or Backspace removes exactly the selected addresses as one `remove`, blocks and elements mixed; a selected part stands for its element. While elements are selected, what a Delete would take with them is marked with `data-affected`. A band is never deleted: its address is left out, and a selection of bands alone is refused with "A band is not deleted; select its elements to delete them." A selection holding a manifest's title is refused whole, since a title goes only with its block. A segment that would leave a gap, or a part of one, is dispatched like any target and refused whole by core ("Removing segment S of line L is not supported yet"). Refusals are shown in a status region that screen readers announce.

### Editors

An **editor** is a mode of the canvas on one element, never a pane or a second canvas. There are three kinds, chosen by the box that is hit, never by what it holds: the block's own box opens the **bundle editor**, a group's frame the **group editor**, a timeline's card or line, a root's or a child's, the **timeline editor**. A double-click, a double tap (two taps within 300 ms and 10 px) or Enter on one selected box or timeline opens its editor; a double-click on another element inside a card or frame opens the innermost card or frame under the pointer, a child's line the child's editor, and a band opens nothing. Inside an editor, every box or timeline inside the edited element opens its own editor on top; a double-click on the edited element does nothing, and one outside it closes the innermost editor, as Done does. The open editors, outermost first, form the **editing path**, the store's `editing`, one source for the canvas, the inspector and the test hook.

Opening an editor animates the view in about one second (`FOCUS_DURATION`; at once under reduced motion) so that the edited element is centred left of the inspector drawer with a 10 % margin, the zoom between 0.05 and 4; closing animates back to where that level was entered from; a change of the canvas's size with an editor open centres the element again. The view follows the editing path through the store's subscription (`editorFocus.svelte.ts`). Everything outside the edited element is faded to 0.3 and inert: nothing there is hovered, selected or moved, and a drag there pans. After every change of the store, an editor opening or closing included, the editor's session prunes the selection by one rule (`existing`): it keeps an address that still resolves and, in an editor, lies inside the edited element (the block itself in the bundle editor), the edited element and its card excepted; a selected band is kept while its timeline's editor is the innermost open editor of its block. Inside, elements are selected by click and by marquee (`elementsInRect`).

In the bundle editor an item is in the selection when the selection holds its frame or card, a member's card in its frame, or another element its group or root contains by address; a drag on such an item moves every item in the selection by the same travel, the pressed item's place quantized, and keeps the selection; a drag on another item selects and moves it alone. In the group editor a dragged member card previews the order a drop would give, before the first card whose middle lies below the pointer (`memberOrderAt`), the other cards sliding apart (`slide`) only while a member drag previews, and at once under reduced motion.

An editor has no side effects ([No side effects](#no-side-effects)); a refused command leaves everything as it was, and the status region names what lay outside. Done keeps the editor's changes as one entry of the level below; Cancel restores everything as it was when the editor opened. Both are the inspector's.

### Bands

In the timeline editor the bands of the edited timeline, a root's or a child's, are arranged (`bands.ts`). A band is offered only in the timeline editor of its own timeline while that editor is the innermost open one, and only while it is recorded; the arranger, the bundle editor and the group editor offer none. The **visible** entries are the timeline's recorded bands and its own id, top to bottom, a band's position being the middle of its rectangle and the id's its line. A band dropped at ordinate `y` moves, in the effective band order, to just after the last other visible entry above `y`, or before the first when none is (`bandDrop`), read against the drawing at the press; the line is an entry like a band, so a band dropped across it changes sides, and a release in place writes nothing. Either move is one `set` of the timeline's `order`, its value the new band order, or `null` when that equals the inherited order; moving a timeline's bands also moves those of every descendant without an entry of its own. A press on an offered band is hit where it lies in the band's rectangle, edges included, after every drawn element.

| On an offered band | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Hover | The band under the pointer is marked |
| Click, tap | Selects the band; Shift and a click add it or remove it |
| Drag, mouse or one finger | Previews the band's new place, the other bands sliding apart; the release stores it in one `set`, a release in place nothing; on a locked canvas a drag pans |
| Double-click, double tap | Nothing: no editor opens and none closes |
| Enter, with the band selected | Nothing |
| Ctrl+↑, Ctrl+↓ (Cmd on macOS), with exactly one band selected | Steps the band past the next visible entry, the line included, in one `set`; nothing at the top or bottom or on a locked canvas |
| Escape, during a drag | Withdraws the preview and stores nothing |
| Delete, Backspace | Never deletes a band; a selection of bands alone is refused |

### Order, keys and the lock

The order of blocks is their `index`, projected as `zIndex`; selecting a block does not raise it. Ctrl and `]` (Cmd on macOS) move the selected blocks one place forward past the next unselected block, Ctrl and `[` backward, with Shift to the front or back; only the moved blocks receive new indices.

The canvas reads its keys before Svelte Flow does, from one table (`keys.ts`) whose rows each ask `rulesOf` whether they may run; keys typed into a text field are left alone. The canvas is one tab stop (an ARIA `application` named "Arranger"); its keys apply only while the focus is inside it. The file shortcuts and undo and redo are the editor's ([The editor](#the-editor)); they are listed here because they work on the canvas too.

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Tab, Enter or Space | Focus a block, select the focused block |
| Enter, with one box or timeline selected | Open its editor |
| Double-click, double tap | Open the editor of the timeline line or innermost box under the pointer; outside the edited element, close the innermost editor |
| Wheel, Shift and the wheel | Pan vertically, horizontally |
| Ctrl and the wheel (Cmd on macOS), a pinch | Zoom |
| Arrow keys, Shift and an arrow key | Move the selected blocks one grid step, eight (on the arranger) |
| Ctrl+↑, Ctrl+↓ (Cmd on macOS) | Move the one selected band (in its timeline editor) |
| Delete, Backspace | Remove the selected addresses |
| `+`, `-`, `0` | Zoom in, zoom out, fit every block into view |
| Ctrl+Z; Ctrl+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y (Cmd+Z, Cmd+Shift+Z on macOS) | Undo, redo |
| Ctrl+`]`, Ctrl+`[`, with Shift | Forward, backward; front, back |
| 1, 2, 3 | Resize mode Scale, Box, Stretch |
| L | Lock or unlock the canvas |
| Escape | Cancel a gesture; otherwise clear the selection; otherwise close the innermost editor |

Svelte Flow pans on the wheel and zooms only with Ctrl or Cmd held; a touchpad pinch, reported as a wheel event with Ctrl, zooms too. Outside macOS, Chromium and Firefox already report a Shift wheel as horizontal travel, which Svelte Flow would discard, so the canvas pans it itself at Svelte Flow's speed (`gestures/wheel.ts`).

The Lock button, or L, locks the canvas: a drag on a block pans instead of moving it, the arrow keys move nothing, a drag on a band pans and the band keys move nothing, and the resize handles are hidden with the mode buttons disabled. Selecting, deleting, reordering, panning and zooming stay available. A read-only canvas offers no lock button.

### Overlays, culling and per-viewer state

The resize handles and the alignment guides lie in one overlay layer in front of the nodes and keep their screen size at every zoom. The canvas controls (zoom in, zoom out, fit, Lock, Marquee) are a vertical toolbar of icon buttons at the bottom left, each with an accessible name and a tooltip naming its key. Above 500 blocks only the blocks in view are mounted (Svelte Flow's `onlyRenderVisibleElements`); below that count culling costs more than it saves ([Performance](#performance)).

The selection, the viewport, the lock and the resize mode belong to the viewer: the editor holds them, and they are never stored in the workspace or its file. The open editors are the store's: a file saved with an editor open holds its session folded, and a reopened file opens no editor. A newly opened workspace is fitted into view. Svelte Flow's attribution link is hidden (`proOptions.hideAttribution`, which its MIT licence permits); the About dialog credits it.

### The canvas's modules

`Canvas.svelte` composes the canvas from modules that hold one concern each: the nodes with the previewed block (`nodes.svelte.ts`), the view following the editing path (`editorFocus.svelte.ts`), what a press, click, double-click and Enter do as pure functions of what was pressed (`selectionRules.ts`, driven by `gestures/canvas.ts`), the key table (`keys.ts`, which also exports `isTextInput` and `within` for the editor), the block drag with its snapping (`blockDrag.ts`), the Shift wheel (`gestures/wheel.ts`) and the styles (`canvas.css`, scoped under `.tilie-canvas`). The open editor and the one split of a qualified address that every module reads live in a leaf module, `editing.ts`. `BlockNode.svelte` gives each box its gesture once (`gestureOf(box)`) from the level's rules and the box's kind. The package exports `Canvas`, `liveStore`, `addCommand`, `drawingOf`, `resolveSelection` (the selection as subjects, each with its container kind, its `DrawnBox` and its resolved address), `blockOf`, `existing`, `isTextInput`, `within`, the inspector's readers (`boxOf`, `isItem`, `isHeld`, `fieldsOf`, and `shownValues`, a box's place stated, else drawn), Svelte Flow's `Viewport` type, and `placeEdit(view, selection, property, value, level, metrics)`, the inspector's one call: the `set` a Properties field writes, by the same rules a gesture applies, or `null`, or a typed refusal.

## The editor

`@timetoalign/tilie-editor` is the application around the canvas. Every change it makes to a workspace is a command dispatched to the store, so undo, redo, the journal and the autosave cover it as they cover a gesture.

### The component and the page

`Editor.svelte` is the whole editor for one workspace: header, canvas, inspector and dialogs. Its props are the embedding options ([Embedding](#embedding)) and `testHook`; it exports `text`, `load`, `selection`, `select`, `focus` and `flush`. It styles only its own root (`.editor`, `container-type: inline-size`, the drawer `clamp(22rem, 32cqw, 40rem)` wide and at most `60cqw`), names every DOM id per instance with `$props.id()`, takes its shortcuts on its root unless `shortcuts` is `"window"`, and reaches outside itself only through its options. Its parts are modules: the open workspace with its store, file name, handle, unsaved state, open, save, import and export (`workspaceSession.svelte.ts`, where a journal that does not reproduce its workspace becomes a refusal coded `journal_mismatch`), the autosave with its lock and recovery (`autosaving.ts`), the drawer (`InspectorDrawer.svelte`), the Fixture menu of test builds (`FixtureMenu.svelte`), one `SHORTCUTS` table driving both the keys and the menu's labels, and the message area with its messages (`messages.ts`). The drawer renders the inspector only while it is open; the unapplied text, the drafts and the chosen tab live in the drawer and survive closing. The header shows the title the page gives it, a snippet, else a plain name that is no heading, so an embedded editor adds no heading to its host. The health check is the client's `connect(server).health()`, made once when a server is given; an answer that is not a name, a version and a `timetoalign` version, each a string, fails as an answer outside 2xx does.

`App.svelte` is the standalone page `tilie` serves, and the one host that owns a document: it writes the title (`• ` while unsaved, then the file name and "– TimeLineEditor"), styles `html` and `body`, takes the server's token from the URL fragment (`page.ts`), gives the header the page's one level-1 heading, and mounts the editor with `server: {url: "api/", token}`, the storage slot `current` and `tilie.inspector.open`, window shortcuts, autofocus and the test hook.

### The header and the File menu

The header holds the title, the File menu, the open file's name (cut with an ellipsis where the header is narrow), a quiet "offline" mark while the server's health check fails (its reason in the tooltip and in text for screen readers), and the About button. The file name starts with `•` while the workspace has changes it was not opened or saved with; undoing back to the saved state removes the mark. The File menu is a WAI-ARIA menu button.

| Item | Shortcut | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New workspace | | An empty workspace named `untitled.tle.json` |
| Open… | Ctrl+O (Cmd+O) | A `.tle.json` file, read into a new store, its journal verified |
| Save | Ctrl+S (Cmd+S) | The workspace written back into the file it was opened from or last saved to |

Where the browser has the File System Access API, the editor opens and saves through its pickers and keeps the file's handle; elsewhere it opens through a file input and saves by download ([decisions/0023](../decisions/0023-file-access-written-in-editor.md)). A file that cannot be read, a journal that does not reproduce its workspace included, leaves the open workspace as it is and shows a dismissible message listing the problems, each at the JSON pointer of its place. Undo and redo leave a text field to the browser's own undo; after either, as after every change, the selection is pruned ([Editors](#editors)).

### The inspector

The inspector drawer opens from the `«` tab at the canvas's right edge and closes with its `»` button; with a `storage` option the browser remembers whether it was open. It also opens when the first editor opens. It shows the block the whole selection lies in, with a WAI-ARIA tab list: Editor, present only while an editor is open, then Properties, Manifest and Journal. Properties is selected when the editor opens and Editor whenever an editor opens (the store's `openings`); leaving the last editor while Editor is selected selects Properties; otherwise the chosen tab is kept. Only the shown tab's panel renders its content. With no block selected and no unapplied text, Properties and Manifest show the hint "Select a block to inspect its manifest." The arrows, Home and End move between tabs; with Alt, Ctrl or Meta held these keys are left to the browser.

- **The editing bar.** While an editor is open, a bar (a group named "Editing") holds a green **Done** button, a red **Cancel** button and the editing path, a list named "Editing path" of the open editors' names joined by " > ": a bundle's `name`, else its `id`, else "Anonymous bundle", and a group's or timeline's `name`, else its id. Cancel closes at once when the session holds no change to discard; otherwise the shared confirmation dialog asks "Are you sure you want to discard 1 change?" (or "n changes?"), No focused. While the drawer is closed, Escape on the canvas still gives Done.
- **The inspected thing.** Beside the heading, the inspector names what is selected by kind and name, or id: "Bundle `<name>`" or "Anonymous bundle"; "Group `<name>`"; "Timeline `<name>`"; "Card of timeline `<name>`"; a part by its element, as "Start of timeline `<name>`" or "Title of bundle `<name>`"; a region, map, break, jump or marker by its kind; a band as "`<Kind>` band of timeline `<name>`" (Markers, Conversion maps, Children, Regions, Flow control). Several things are "N selected". The words live in one module (`words.ts`), read from core's resolver.
- **Editor.** Names the innermost editor, "Bundle editor: `<name>`", "Group editor: `<name>`" or "Timeline editor: `<name>`".
- **Properties.** A form for the geometry of what is selected, never the block's when something inside it is, by the role of each selected subject, its fields the arranger's `fieldsOf` in core's place order: a block's `x`, `y`, `length`, `scale`, `spacing`, `width` and `height`; an item (a frame, or a box-held card, selected by the box, the timeline's line or a part of it, or a frame's title) the same members of its place; a card held by a frame its group's `length` and `scale` ("group length", "group scale") and its own `spacing`, `width` and `height`. A field shows the stated value, else the drawn one (the arranger's `shownValues`, which `placeEdit` reads too), rounded to at most two decimals. Several selected things of one role are edited together in one `set`; a field whose values differ shows "mixed". A selection of different roles, or of an element without a box, shows "Select a block, a frame or a card to edit its geometry." One selected band shows read-only rows: "Band", "Level" (signed, `−` for negative) and "Elements". A field accepts decimal text only (`0x10` and `Infinity` are refused); typed text is applied as one `set` on Enter or blur, and Escape discards it; an empty `width` or `height` is `null`. Every field writes the arranger's `placeEdit`, so a field follows its level's rules as a gesture does: a `length` or `spacing` writes what a Stretch to that value writes. A step (spin button, Up and Down, a wheel over the focused field) is one `set` of its own: 1 for `x`, `y`, `length`, `width` and `height` and 0.1 for `scale` and `spacing`, from the exact value or typed text, giving the exact decimal (0.2 + 0.1 gives 0.3). During a gesture on the selected block the fields follow its preview. A draft (typed and not committed) and a refusal belong to the containers they were typed for in the manifest they were typed against: they reappear while the form edits those containers, and go when the manifest is replaced. In a group or timeline editor the block's `scale`, `length` and `spacing` are disabled; an item's `x` and `y` are disabled everywhere but in the bundle editor.
- **The manifest as text.** The Manifest tab holds the format, the Include styling and Minimal checkboxes, the text, Apply and Revert, the problems and the warnings. The text is JSON (timeskeleton's standard writer), YAML or TOML; YAML ([decisions/0024](../decisions/0024-yaml-library.md)) and TOML ([decisions/0025](../decisions/0025-toml-library.md)) are loaded the first time they are chosen. Numbers stay exact in all three. Apply, or Ctrl+Enter (Cmd+Enter), reads the text and dispatches one `set` of the block's `manifest`; a text that stands for the stored manifest dispatches nothing. A refused text stays as typed with its problems: a syntax error at its line and column (from the JSON reader's position), any other problem at its JSON pointer. While the same block stays selected, an applied YAML or TOML text is patched in place, so comments are kept. Apply and Revert are enabled exactly while the text differs from the text shown; Revert shows the stored manifest. While the text holds an edit the format and options cannot change ("Apply or Revert to switch"); a change of the manifest from elsewhere does not replace it, Apply still applies it to the block it was written for, and a notice says the manifest changed. An unapplied text survives a visit to another tab. Include styling, unchecked by default, leaves `graphical` out of the text and refuses a typed `graphical` member ("the styling is excluded; check Include styling to edit graphical"). Minimal shows the minimal form, read back in its complete form. The keys the schema does not declare are listed as warnings, "Unknown key `<key>` at `<pointer>`", following the stored manifest. TOML has no null: a member that may be null and that the schema requires or defaults to `null` is left out when null and read back as null, found by walking the schema along the value; a manifest holding any other null cannot be shown as TOML, and the inspector names that null's path.
- **Journal.** One row per entry of the level shown, newest first, each with its time (`HH:MM:SS`, UTC), its author and a text: "Added `<kind>` `<id>` to `<into>`", "Set `<properties>` of `<target>`" (" and N more"), "Removed `<targets>`", "Styled `<selector>`", and for a closed session "Edited `<kind>` `<name>` (n changes)", or "Edited `<address>` (n changes)" once its target is gone. On the arranger it lists the main journal; in an editor its session's entries, then the read-only rows of the earlier session of the same element, greyed below a heavier rule. A session's row has a disclosure button listing its entries; a partly undone session reads "(n changes, k undone)". Undone entries stay listed, grey and marked "undone" for screen readers, until redone or replaced; they are not saved.

Problems are rendered by one list, shared by the inspector and the message area.

The About dialog shows the editor's version, the server's name and version with its `timetoalign` version ("offline" with the reason, or "no server" when the editor has none), the timeskeleton package and schema version, the workspace packages, and the third-party software in the bundle with its licence and copyright lines. ## Embedding

The editor runs in any page, not only in the one `tilie` serves. A host page loads the stylesheet, imports `mount` and hands it one empty element with a definite height:

```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="tilie/tilie.css">
<div id="timelines" style="height: 600px"></div>
<script type="module">
  import { mount } from "./tilie/tilie.js";
  const editor = mount(document.getElementById("timelines"), { shortcuts: "editor" });
</script>
```

`mount(element, options)` refuses an element that has children or already holds an editor, marks it `data-tilie`, and returns a `TimeLineEditor` handle: `text()` (the workspace as Save writes it), `load(text, name?)` (open without asking, returning the reader's refusal or `null`), `selection()`, `select(addresses)`, `focus()` and `destroy()`, which writes the pending autosave, then removes the editor. Every option may be left out:

| Option | Default | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `workspace` | the empty workspace | The workspace file text to open |
| `server` | none: no request, About says "no server" | `{url, token}`: the API's base URL ending in `/` and the bearer token, or `null` for no header |
| `storage` | none: no autosave, the drawer not remembered | `{slot, inspectorKey}`: the IndexedDB slot (its lock `tilie-autosave/<slot>`) and the `localStorage` key of the drawer |
| `shortcuts` | `"editor"` | Where the file and undo shortcuts are taken: inside the editor, or anywhere in the window |
| `autofocus` | `false` | Whether the canvas takes the focus at mount |
| `noticesUrl` | beside `tilie.js` | The licence texts the About dialog links to |
| `onchange` | | Called with `{fileName, dirty}` after every change and every load; the text is `text()` |
| `onselect` | | Called with the selected addresses whenever the selection changes |

An embedded editor never touches its host's document: no title, no global style, no `location` or `history`, no window listener unless asked, no request without `server`, no storage without `storage`, no test hook. Two editors on one page share no id and no selection. `pnpm build:embed` builds `src/embed.ts` into `app/packages/editor/dist-embed/`: `tilie.js` (its exports kept), `tilie.css` (every style), `assets/` (the lazily loaded codecs and the font, by relative URL) and `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.txt`. Library mode is not used, since it would inline the font into the stylesheet. Inside a TypeScript workspace the same entry is `@timetoalign/tilie-editor/embed`.

**Planned.** `tilie --allow-origin <origin>`, repeatable and exact, so that a page on another origin may call a running server: an allowed origin's preflight answered without the token check, and responses carrying `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` and `Vary: Origin`, never credentials, the Host check unchanged. A Jupyter widget mounting the bundle with the kernel's server and token. The bundle served by `tilie-server` to foreign pages. A read-only viewer of one block, built on the canvas's `readonly`, which already writes nothing. How a WebSocket handshake carries the token, which a browser cannot send as a header, is decided with the command channel; the guard already closes a handshake from a foreign host or origin.

## Not yet wired

Deleting a segment, a child of a segment line, leaves a gap in its line, and is refused. The design of closing that gap is kept as two tested modules that nothing else refers to:

- core's `manifest/segments.ts`, the gap arithmetic: for a segment S of length l in the segment line Λ, **left extends** (the segment before S grows by l), **right extends** (the segment after S starts where S started and grows by l), **left shifts** (Λ's offset grows by l and Λ shortens by l, so the later segments keep their place in the parent) and **right shifts** (every later segment moves back by l and Λ shortens by l); extending needs an unlocked neighbour on that side, shifting needs Λ unlocked, and shifting left needs Λ to be a child; a way is permitted only when the manifest it leaves passes the schema and the invariants; an unknown length stays unknown (`segmentGaps` lists the permitted ways); core's main entry exports only the `GAPS` table and the `Gap` types, which the menu reads;
- the arranger's `GapMenu.svelte`, the menu that will offer those ways: a form named "Delete segment <id>" with one fieldset, "Closes the gap", of four radio buttons, each named after the neighbour that moves, a disabled one titled with why, the first permitted one chosen, then OK and Cancel.

## The schema contract

`@timetoalign/timeskeleton` supplies the types generated from its one schema document, the validator of the current schema version, `unknownKeys`, the complete and minimal forms, the canonical order, the lossless reader and the standard writer, the manifest fixtures with their minimal twins, the rejection and warning cases, and the drawing font with its metrics. It migrates nothing, and neither does TiLiE; the schema repository, not TiLiE, is the source of truth, and `timetoalign` proves conformance in its own tests.

Core is typed through the package's typed forms: `completeManifest(value: LosslessTimeSkeleton): CompleteTimeSkeleton`, `minimalManifest` and `canonicalManifest`, where `LosslessTimeSkeleton` is the schema's type with every number a `JsonNumber` and `CompleteTimeSkeleton` the same with every defaulted key present. `parseJson` throws `JsonSyntaxError` with the `offset`, `line` and `column` of the fault, which the inspector shows. The validators of both languages raise `SchemaValidationError` with a stable `code` naming the rule broken (`not_a_manifest`, `schema`, `representation`, `finite_number`, `duplicate_id`, `dangling_target`, `child_unit`, `child_offset`, `child_end`, `segment_line`, `reserved_id`, `manifest_id`, `group_member`, `order_key`, `display_order`, `band_order`), and refuse a number that is not finite at its path; core reports every such refusal as `schema_violation` with the validator's path and message.

Every application package takes schema 0.7.0 through a `link:` dependency on the `js/` package of a timeskeleton checkout beside this repository (`../timeskeleton/js`), built there with `pnpm install` and `pnpm build`, until that version is released ([`AGENTS.md`](../AGENTS.md#dependency-policy)).

## The server

`tilie-server` uses FastAPI and uvicorn ([decisions/0008](../decisions/0008-fastapi-and-uvicorn.md)). It binds only `127.0.0.1`, generates a random token for each run, rejects foreign Host and Origin headers, and requires `Authorization: Bearer <token>` on every `/api/` route. `tilie` hands the token to the browser in the URL fragment (`http://127.0.0.1:<port>/#token=<token>`), which a browser never sends to a server, so the token never reaches the access log; the page reads it, removes it from the address bar with `history.replaceState`, and keeps it in memory only, so a reload loses it. Every response carries `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`. The server serves the built app from its wheel and implements `/api/health`; `tilie` selects a free port and opens the browser (`--port`, `--no-browser`), and `tilie --check` verifies an installation (`--static-dir` checks a development build).

`create_app(settings)` composes the application from one frozen `ServerSettings` (`host`, `port`, `token`, `static_dir`), the two security middlewares (the Host, Origin and token guard, and the referrer policy), the health router and the static files. It serves no generated OpenAPI document: `/api/openapi.json` is 404, and a committed document is planned with the API it describes. SIGINT and SIGTERM stop the server gracefully with exit code 0, also when it was started with SIGINT ignored, as a background job of a non-interactive shell is.

Planned: loading any supported source through `timetoalign` loaders; conversion-map batches; unfolding and traversal diagnostics with fix suggestions; validation; exports; a committed OpenAPI document with a generated TypeScript client; a GitHub token proxy; and the command layer with an MCP server and a command channel for live sessions as the contract for automation.

## Testing and quality

Tests are README-first: every `tests/README.md` states what proves each behaviour, which values its package owns and derives, and where the oracle is; tests follow it, with exact expected values and no thresholds. Fixtures flow from Python to TypeScript, so both sides work from the same examples: `core` loads and saves every 0.7.0 manifest fixture of the schema package byte for byte, loads each minimal twin to its fixture, and refuses every shared rejection case at its pinned path; layout and the writers derive their expected drawings from the specification and equal its SVG and TikZ fixtures byte for byte, the hand-derived figures included; a component test renders the block node for every drawing fixture and compares its element tree with the fixture's SVG. Property tests with fast-check run generated command sequences from every manifest fixture and check that accepted commands keep every manifest valid, refused ones leave the view unchanged, undo and redo restore every intermediate view and mirror each other step for step, Cancel restores its opening, the journal replays to the snapshot, and a workspace file writes back to its own bytes; on the canvas, that projections reuse node objects, that every gesture's command is accepted, and that stretches are monotonic and reversible.

The workspace store's budgets, run alone by `pnpm test:budget`, bound reading and writing a 300-block workspace with a 1,000-entry journal at 250 ms each and one command at 1 ms; the same test prints "open and verify", reading that workspace and creating its store, journal replay included, as a measured figure without a limit ([decisions/0019](../decisions/0019-workspace-store-and-journal.md)). Layout's redraw cost is pinned by counting, not timing: on a synthetic manifest of 1,000 elements, every element is resolved by the cascade once, every statement tested once per element, and the font's advances read once per distinct text. The tests that need a production build, the attributions and notices file among them, run alone by `pnpm test:build`.

The canvas and the editor are tested end to end with Playwright in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit and on an emulated touch screen ([decisions/0021](../decisions/0021-playwright.md)), in the pinned Playwright image through rootless podman; `app/e2e/README.md` states what each spec proves. Expected values come from the store through the test hook `window.__tilie`, never from pixels, except the zero-tolerance screenshots, made only inside that image. The hook (`load`, `store`, `selection`, `select`, `viewport`, `setViewport`, `locked`, `mode`, `health`, `text`, `editing`, `open`, `done`, `cancel`, `boxes`, a block's container records, and `bands`, a block's recorded bands in client pixels) and the header's Fixture menu exist only where the page asks for them in a development build or a build made with `VITE_TILIE_TEST_HOOK=1`; a production build, and so the wheel, contains neither, nor the fixture files, and an embedded editor never installs them. The workspaces the suite opens are written from the schema package's manifest fixtures through the store's commands by `pnpm e2e:fixtures`, never by hand. An embed spec mounts two editors into a foreign host page built in embed mode. axe-core checks the page for accessibility violations ([decisions/0022](../decisions/0022-axe-core.md)). The gates are Biome, Vitest, fast-check, dependency-cruiser, svelte-check, Playwright, axe-core, LuaLaTeX, Ruff and pytest.

Known gaps: the editor's component tests do not check CSS layout or mobile rendering (the end-to-end tests check the drawer's positions and the phone header), nor a fragment carrying an empty token; the File System Access path is tested against stubbed pickers only, the end-to-end tests taking the file-input and download path; the server's static-file tests do not exercise traversal.

## Performance

A drag costs script time on every pointer move in proportion to the number of nodes, culled or not, because Svelte Flow re-adopts its node list on each move: about 1.2 ms per move at 100 blocks, 2.4 ms at 300 and 5 ms at 900 in headless Chromium. Culling reduces what is mounted (42 of 300 nodes in a zoomed view) but adds its own cost to pan and zoom (3.1 ms per wheel event at 300 blocks against 0.4 ms without), so it starts above 500 blocks. One node per block, a complete projection, reused node objects, memoized drawings and a preview that replaces one node keep a gesture from doing any work of TiLiE's own beyond the previewed block.

`pnpm e2e:budget` checks, for a workspace of 300 blocks in Chromium, from the DevTools Protocol's script time: dragging a block at most 3 ms of script per pointer move; panning and wheel zoom at most 1 ms per move or event; one command at most 1 ms; one block's redrawing within one frame (16.7 ms). Opening a workspace costs reading it and replaying its journal; the store's decision record holds the figures. Heavy data is intended to stay out of the DOM, in WebGL panes and columnar tables, with parsing that could block interaction in workers.

## Dependencies and deployment

Each dependency requires an ADR under [`decisions/`](../decisions/), in the MADR structure, weighing writing it ourselves, naming the audited version and a review date ([decisions/0001](../decisions/0001-record-architecture-decisions.md)); the dependency policy, ranges in manifests, exact development pins, lockfiles and the 14-day release window, is stated in [`AGENTS.md`](../AGENTS.md#dependency-policy).

Install the current product with `pip install tilie-server`, then run `tilie`. The wheel contains the built browser app, so starting it needs neither Node.js nor a download. Planned deployment forms are a hosted browser app talking to a user-run local server, a desktop wrapper and collaborative hosting.
