# Architecture

## Overview

TimeLineEditor (TiLiE) is a browser editor and visualization tool for musical timelines represented by `timetoalign`. It works with three layers, each named by one word only:

- a **manifest** is one TimeToShare manifest as the TimeToShare schema (version 0.2.0) describes it, of the one shape every manifest has, `{timetoshare, timelines, "@context"?, id?, name?, meta?, groups?, measure_maps?, resources?, flow_maps?, virtual_timelines?, statements?, provenance?, graphical?}`, whatever it holds: no timeline, one root timeline, several, a group, or several groups beside ungrouped timelines. Every manifest is an alignment bundle. Its **statements** say what the manifest itself, every timeline and every region stand for: `statements`, property–value pairs about that thing (a work, a recording, an edition, a performer), and `provenance`, the annotations of those pairs (who stated one, when, how, on what basis, and who believes it, `true`, `unlikely` or `false`), under an inline JSON-LD `@context`, as TimeToShare's [description specification](https://github.com/TimeToAlign/TimeToShare/blob/main/docs/description.md) defines them; the drawing does not read them. A timeline has exact coordinates, children at exact offsets, conversion maps, regions and flow control, and a `length` that may be `null`, not known; its flow control is a list of elements in five roles, each at a coordinate: a jump, whose destinations (`targets`) each go `to` a jump target, a region, a child or the timeline itself, or `by` a distance, a jump target, a break, a section break and a barline; an element some actor believes `false` in its `provenance` is rejected and drawn faded, and which elements a flow the editor asks for reads is decided by the editor's user (core's `flowReading`): by the user's own belief where they recorded one (every belief but `false` reads it), else by the block's belief filter, the MatchClaim filter's belief states, as for a MatchClaim the user holds no belief in (the element is read when the filter keeps `undecided`); the manifest's `flow_maps` and `virtual_timelines` hold the orders in which a root's stretches are played and the timelines they make; the measure maps its conversion maps read belong to the manifest, not to a timeline; `graphical` holds the style rules of the manifest's own drawing, its groups' display orders, its timelines' band orders, its named timestamps (a named position of a timeline, drawn as its `display` lists: a wedge, stacked labels of its name, ID and coordinate, and a cross-section line, which a groupstamp's runs across every member card of its group's frame) and its named MatchStamps (alignment anchors, each joining two match timestamps on two timelines, with the IDs of the MatchClaims behind them) and its elisions (each a stretch of one timeline left out of the drawing, drawn as a narrow gap holding one ellipsis, with a `name`, a `width` and a `display`; an elision on a timeline that lies in a group is that group's and elides its frame, every other one elides the cards that draw its timeline's root whole; [Elisions](#elisions)); `resources` lists the files that come with the manifest, such as its MatchClaim table, and the timelines each concerns. Every timeline, group, measure map, conversion map, region and flow-control element has an `id`, unique across the manifest, and so may the manifest; a `name` is what is displayed. A manifest with an `id` or a `name` is a named bundle, one with neither an anonymous bundle. A manifest knows nothing about where, or how often, it is shown;
- a **block** carries one manifest, entirely its own, together with its placement, the other inputs of its drawing: position, scale, graphical length, spacing (the gap between the items of its drawing), margin (the empty space above its first item and below its last), requested size, the stated places of its drawing's containers and, where it has one, its **arrangement**, which places its items in an order instead of at their places ([Arrangements](#arrangements)). A block's styling is its manifest's `graphical`; the block has no style layer of its own;
- a **workspace** is the canvas: its name, its blocks, their order and, where it has one, their arrangement, its default style rules, the workspace settings, and the command journal that produced them, with the sessions of its editors. Block ids are the workspace's namespace, and an address names anything in it.

TiLiE is local-first: the browser communicates with a local Python server, which is meant to own loading through `timetoalign`, heavy computation and exports, while the browser keeps a small editing core. This keeps the library's capabilities close to the operations that need them and interaction responsive without a request per keystroke.

## What exists today and what is planned

`tilie-server` starts a FastAPI server, on loopback for its user (`tilie`) or publicly for the hosts it is named for (`tilie --public-host`), serves the built editor, and answers, authenticated by the run's token in a local run, `GET /api/health`, `POST /api/measure-maps`, which expands a manifest's measure maps through `timetoalign`, `POST /api/match-claims`, which checks a package's MatchClaim table against its manifest and answers its columns, `POST /api/match-claims/provenance` and `POST /api/match-claims/belief`, which read the provenance of given MatchClaims and record a belief in a new table, `POST /api/match-claims/events`, which answers the events on both sides of given MatchClaims, `POST /api/flows`, which plays a timeline's flow control through `timetoalign`, `POST /api/flows/flow-maps`, which answers the manifest with that timeline's flows written, `/api/reconcile/musicbrainz`, MusicBrainz as a Reconciliation Service API endpoint, and, in a local run only, `GET /api/folders`, `GET /api/files` and `PUT /api/files`, which list and read the workspace, manifest and table files below the server's root folder and write workspace files there, never a path through a hidden folder or to a hidden file ([The server](#the-server)). The editor is one component that the standalone page mounts and that any other page can mount through a framework-free entry ([Embedding](#embedding)). It is a header and the canvas, with an inspector drawer on the right. It opens, saves, imports and exports files, through the server's file routes in its own file dialog where a server offers them and through the browser's pickers otherwise, autosaves in the browser, inspects and edits the selected block's manifest and the statements of its bundle, timelines and regions, looking their values up in public services, and reads the MatchClaim table a block's manifest names through the server to show its potential MatchStamps on the canvas and name, update and remove MatchStamps from them ([The editor](#the-editor)); a block, a group or a timeline opens in an editor of its own on the canvas, and a root's flow control and FlowMaps in the flow-control editor, where its flow-control elements are edited and its FlowMaps composed and dragged out as virtual cards ([Editors](#editors), [The flow-control editor](#the-flow-control-editor)); a virtual timeline is drawn as a card of its own, and a root's card may be switched onto one of its flows ([Virtual and switched cards](#virtual-and-switched-cards)); everything it changes goes through the commands of `core`.

Loading sources through the server, raster exports, a generated API client, collaboration over a network and automation endpoints are not implemented. Nor is editing flow control in the editor: it shows a selected flow-control element read-only, a jump's destinations as rows that select what they lead to, and records the user's belief in it; its name and label are set as any element's, and how flow control is drawn is toggled in the properties (below), in the root's flow-control editor as outside it; an element is added, moved, retargeted or relabelled only through core's commands and the Manifest tab. A root's computed flows are shown and written only in its flow-control editor, which asks the server for a convention's findings (the client's `flows`) and writes the flows of its flow control with Recompute (`withFlowMaps`), each under the reading the Properties tab states (`readingOf`); nothing else of the editor computes flows. A virtual timeline carried `through` a group is evaluated by timetoalign alone: core gives it the axis and played length of the FlowMap's own root, as timetoalign does, but carries no position through the group, so its card is drawn as its line alone, as an unresolved virtual timeline is, the coordinate flag reads no written position on it and no card is switched onto it; one carried through a conversion map keeps the length it states. Sections and sentences that say "planned" describe the intended architecture, so that additions have explicit boundaries; everything else describes behaviour that exists.

## The container hierarchy

A manifest nests its content in four containers, and its drawing draws each of them as a container of its own ([TimeToShare's README, "Containers"](https://github.com/TimeToAlign/TimeToShare#containers)): semantically, the **bundle** (the manifest) holds **groups** and timelines, a group has timelines as its members, and every **timeline**, root or child, has five **facets**, `markers`, `conversion_maps`, `children`, `regions` and `flow_control`; graphically, the **block** draws the bundle, a **frame** a group, a **card** a timeline drawn as a root, and a **band** a facet, at its timeline's line. All four graphical containers are **boxes**, drawn as rectangles with rounded corners; "box" is the generic word and names none of them. A child timeline is content of its parent's `children` facet, drawn as a line in its parent's children band, never in a card of its own unless a group has it as a member. A band draws nothing; it is the unit that holds and orders what its timeline has of its facet. Manifest content and addresses take the semantic words, the drawing's containers the graphical ones, and every package reads the hierarchy through one model:

| Kind | Level | Held by | Its entry in the block's `places` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| block | bundle | nothing | none: the block's own placement places it |
| frame | group | the block | `places[G]` under the group's ID `G`: `x`, `y`, `length`, `scale`, `spacing`, `margin`, `width`, `height` |
| card | timeline | the block, for a root or a virtual timeline in no group; for a member, a virtual one included, the frame of each group that has it as a member, one card in each | held by the block: `places[T]` under the root's or the virtual timeline's ID, as a frame's; held by the frame of group `G`: `places[G].cards[T]`, only `width`, `height`, `spacing`, `margin` |
| band | facet | the card of the member drawing that draws its timeline | none: its place is its timeline's band order |

Each kind is styled as the schema element type of its own name (`block`, `frame`, `card`, `band`), and a band also by its facet (the selector member `facet`). "Item" is the role of a frame or of a card the block holds, each placed freely in the block; "member" is a fact about a timeline, that a group names it; neither is a kind.

A root that contains a member, a child timeline some group names, is drawn as that member's ancestor lines and may also be drawn whole as an **ancestor card**: a card held by the block behind the frame of the member's group, at the proportions of the root's own axis, its line continuing the member's ancestor line of the root (the drawing specification's element type `ancestor_card`, section 8.8). The defaults show it; a rule setting its `display` to `none` hides it. An ancestor card has no place (its key `{item: G, member: R}` names none, `Holding` `"ancestor"`), no bands of its own and no entry in `places`; only a displayed one is recorded, as a `DrawnBox` carrying `ancestor {group, member, groups}` (`groups` every group whose frame it lies behind: `[group]`, or, for a root drawn once behind a row of groups side by side, every group hanging on it in the row's order, `group` the first; [Arrangements](#arrangements)), its lines as `DrawnAxis` records carrying the same `ancestor`, after every item's lines, and the block grows to hold it.

A **virtual timeline**, an entry of the manifest's `virtual_timelines` (a FlowMap or a conversion map applied to a timeline), lies in no tree and is drawn as a card of its own, a **virtual card** (section 8.10; [Virtual and switched cards](#virtual-and-switched-cards)): in the frame of each group listing it as a member, in display order, and, where it is in no group, held by the block as an item, after the ungrouped roots in name order, drawn only where the block's `places` hold its ID; taking that entry away hides the card and keeps the virtual timeline. Its card holds its five bands, as every card does (`FACETS`), which hold nothing of its own but its named timestamps; its line is a segment line of its FlowMap's chunks, and each chunk's **window**, five bands `{id: V, facet, part: [k]}`, shows what the chunk's sources hold. Core's registry lists a block-held card for every virtual timeline in no group, placed or not, since a manifest does not know the block's `places`; layout draws and records only the placed ones.

`core` holds the registry (`CONTAINERS` in `manifest/containers.ts`): for each kind its level, its element type and the place members it may state by how it is held. `containersOf(manifest)` lists every container of a manifest with its holder, memoized per manifest, and `placeMembersOf` answers what a container may state. A block's `places` maps the ID of a group or of an ungrouped root (a root that no group names and that holds no member) to its stated place, a group's entry holding in its `cards`, under a member's ID, the stated `width`, `height`, `spacing` and `margin` of the member's card in that group's frame; it is the drawing's `places` (TimeToShare's drawing specification, sections 1 and 8.6). After every command each touched block fits its `places` to the drawing, so a command never fails for a stale entry: an entry that placed a timeline's card before the command and places nothing after it moves to that timeline's one card (a root that became a member of `G`, from `places[T]` to `places[G].cards[T]`; a member that left its group, back) unless that card's entry is stated already, and a timeline drawn in several cards after the command gets no moved entry; every other entry that places no container goes; each entry is then cut to what the registry lets its container state as it is held, so a root that becomes a member keeps only its card's size, spacing and margin.

A timeline's **band order** lists `line`, its own line, and its five facets, each standing for the band drawing it, top to bottom (section 6.4). Its **own** band order is its entry of the manifest's `graphical.order`, which lists `line` and any of the facets; its **inherited** one is its parent's effective order, else the default (children and conversion maps above the line, markers, regions and flow control below); its **effective** one, the one it is drawn with, is its own completed against the inherited one, else the inherited one: the stated entries keep their order, and every facet the entry leaves out keeps the side of `line` it has in the inherited order and lies beyond the stated entries on that side, in the inherited sequence (core's `bandOrderOf` and `inheritedBandOrder`); a virtual timeline, which has no parent, completes its own entry against the default. A group's display order is its entry of `graphical.order`, else its `members` (`displayOrder`).

Layout records every container of a drawing as one `DrawnBox` in `Drawing.boxes` ([Layout and writers](#layout-and-writers)); the arranger and the editor read that list and derive nothing from elements or descriptor order. The arranger's level table says what each level of the canvas may do with each kind ([Levels and permissions](#levels-and-permissions)).

## IDs and addresses

Every element of a manifest, timeline (root or descendant), group, measure map, conversion map at any depth, region, flow-control element, FlowMap and virtual timeline, has an ID, and so may the manifest itself; so does every named timestamp, named MatchStamp, alignment anchor and resource; every ID is unique across the manifest ([TimeToShare's README, "Identity"](https://github.com/TimeToAlign/TimeToShare#identity)). A statement has neither an ID nor an address: statements and provenance are members of the element holding them, changed through that element ([Commands](#commands)); a statement's value names an element of the manifest by an IRI reference to its ID (`#cpt_1`). An ID is any non-empty string without control characters, `/`, spaces, colons and URIs included, and no word is reserved; TiLiE keeps it verbatim and never parses it, so a timeline, group or manifest without a `name` is named by its whole ID. Where core creates an element itself, the segment that covers an empty segment line ([Invariants](#invariants)), it mints its ID as timetoalign does, the type prefix, `_` and 13 random base-36 characters (`clt_3kq9x0m2ztb7a`), as it mints the IDs of the default timestamps and of every named timestamp, elision, named MatchStamp with its anchors and match timestamps, and group, region, conversion map, flow-control element, FlowMap and virtual timeline without an ID that a command adds ([Commands](#commands)), each with its prefix: `region`, `cmap`, the flow-control element's role (`jump`, `jump_target`, `break`, `section_break`, `barline`), `flow` for a FlowMap and, for a virtual timeline, that of its unit's timeline class (`clt`, `dpt`, …); a command that adds any other element, a timeline, a child or a measure map, carries its ID. The minter is core's `createIdMinter` (`workspace/ids.ts`): a seed, 64 random bits of the platform's cryptographic source for each store unless a test gives one, and the algorithm of TimeToShare's minted-ID test vectors (`fixtures/ids/minted_ids.json`), which its tests check, so that it computes the IDs timetoalign computes from the same seed. A minted ID is never renamed or reissued; one the manifest already holds is refused by the schema check (`schema_violation`). IDs repeat freely across blocks, each manifest being its own. A block ID is ten characters from `0-9a-z`, drawn from the platform's cryptographic random source (`mintBlockId`).

An address names anything a command can target. It is an object, never a string composed from IDs: `{}` the workspace (`WORKSPACE`), `{block}` a block, which draws its bundle, and `{block, id?, facet?, part?}` an element of the block's manifest, whose members are those of the manifest's own address, the drawing specification's (section 2.2):

| Element | Address in its block `B` |
| --- | --- |
| a timeline, group, measure map, conversion map, region, flow-control element, FlowMap or virtual timeline with ID `X` (a virtual timeline also its line), the wedge of a named timestamp, a named MatchStamp or the line of an alignment anchor with ID `X` | `{block: B, id: X}` |
| the band drawing the facet `F` of timeline `T`, or of virtual timeline `V` | `{block: B, id: T, facet: F}` |
| every card of timeline `T`: an ungrouped root's, one in the frame of each group that has `T` as a member, or an ancestor card behind the frame of each group having a member below `T`; every card of virtual timeline `V`, block-held or in a frame | `{block: B, id: T, part: ["card"]}` |
| chunk `k` (from 0) of the FlowMap a virtual card of `V` draws, or a switched card of root `T` draws ([Virtual and switched cards](#virtual-and-switched-cards)): its segment of the line; its label, its leader and its start boundary | `{block: B, id: V, part: [k]}`; the same with `[k, "label"]`, `[k, "leader"]` or `[k, "start"]` as `part` (`T` in place of `V` on a switched card) |
| the band of facet `F` in the window of chunk `k` of a virtual card of `V`, where the chunk's source is drawn (a switched card's chunks have no window) | `{block: B, id: V, facet: F, part: [k]}` |
| a stored elision with ID `E`: every gap drawn of it | `{block: B, id: E}` |
| the gaps a card derives on timeline `T` (they have no ID) | `{block: B, id: T, part: ["elision"]}` |
| a part of an element: a segment's boundary, a name, a label, a named timestamp's labels and its cross-section line, an alignment anchor's label (`name`), an elision's ellipsis and labels | the element's address with `start`, `name`, `label`, `id`, `coordinate`, `line` or `ellipsis` appended to its `part`, such as `{block: B, id: S, part: ["start"]}`, `{block: B, id: X, part: ["coordinate"]}` or `{block: B, id: T, part: ["elision", "ellipsis"]}` |
| the manifest's title (`TITLE_ADDRESS`) | `{block: B, part: ["name"]}` |

`facet` stands only with `id`, and with `part` only where the part begins with a chunk index: an item of `part` is a `PartItem`, a part word or, at its head, a `ChunkIndex`, an integer from 0 (refused otherwise with "a facet stands with a part only where the part holds a chunk index"). The manifest's title is `{part: ["name"]}` whether or not the manifest has an ID: the manifest's own ID addresses nothing, so giving or changing it changes no address, and a command aimed at `{block, id}` with the manifest's ID is refused with `unknown_target`; a title exists only for a named manifest, so on an unnamed one `{part: ["name"]}` names nothing (`unknown_target`). An element drawn more than once (a member in several frames with everything it draws, an ancestor line above several members) keeps one address, which every drawing of it carries; so the cards of one timeline share theirs, a style rule selecting an element styles every drawing of it, and selecting it marks every drawing of it.

`resolveAddress(view, address)` in core is the one place that decides what an address names. It returns a record of one kind: `workspace`, `block`, `title`, `frame`, `timeline`, `card`, `band`, `entry` (with its kind and index), `measure_map`, `timestamp`, `elision` (a stored one, with its timeline), `matchstamp`, `anchor` (with its MatchStamp), `flow_map`, `virtual_timeline`, `virtual_card`, `virtual_band`, `chunk` (with its index, its FlowMap and the line drawing it, `ChunkFound`), `chunk_band` (the same with the facet of the chunk's window) or `part` (with its owner and word), carrying the block, the located timeline and the containers where there are any (a group's frame, every card of a timeline, every drawing of a band), or a `CommandError`: `invalid_address` for a malformed address, `unknown_target` for one that names nothing, each at `[<address key>]`. A part resolves only where it is drawn: an address of a part the element's drawing does not draw names nothing (`unknown_target`); the address of a card's derived gaps, which depend on the view and the cascade that core does not read, resolves, as a part of its timeline, wherever an ancestor card may derive one on that timeline (`defaultElisionTimelines`), drawn or not. `resolveElement(manifest, element)` does the same within one manifest. Addresses are compared as whole objects; `addressKey(address)` is an address's one string form, the only key a map or a Svelte keyed block takes for it, and no package writes an address as any other string or reads anything out of its key. `indexOf(manifest)` is what every reader of a manifest needs once, memoized per manifest: its containers, the ancestry of its timelines, the timeline that owns every timeline and entry ID, and the elements of its drawing.

## The core package

`@timetoalign/tilie-core` is framework-free: no DOM, no Svelte, no network. Its main entry holds what a host and the packages above core use: manifest and workspace I/O, the commands and `preview`, the store, the resolver, the registry, the band and order helpers, the error types and the domain types. `@timetoalign/tilie-core/drawing` holds the drawing's primitives for layout and the writers (the elements a manifest draws, among them each timeline's named timestamps, `namedTimestamps(manifest, timeline)` in canonical order, memoised per manifest, the selector test, the member drawings); `@timetoalign/tilie-core/numbers` the exact arithmetic. The package needs Node 22.18 or later, as `@timetoalign/timetoshare` does.

### Numbers

`Rational` is an exact fraction over `BigInt`, always normalized, built from a `[numerator, denominator]` pair, a `bigint`, a safe integer or a decimal integer string, never from a JavaScript float. A `Real` is a value as Python's numeric tower holds it, an exact `Rational` or a float, combined and compared as the tower does (a sum involving a float is a float, a comparison is exact). `fromFloat` returns the exact binary value of a float and serves arithmetic that must be exact on a float's value, such as the writers' page transform; it never re-expresses a coordinate. A `Coordinate` is `{unit, numberType, value}` whose value is what the axis's number type declares: a `bigint` for `int`, a `number` for `float`, a `Rational` for `fraction`. A value on its timeline's own axis is bare, read and written as timetoalign's `number_from_json` and `number_to_json` do: an `int` axis reads a JSON integer, or an integral JSON number as that integer; a `float` axis reads a JSON number, or an integer as the float it equals exactly; a `fraction` axis reads only a pair; every integer is int64-bounded. Arithmetic and comparison work within one axis only and throw `AxisMismatchError` across units or number types.

### Values on timelines

A conversion map is evaluated as timetoalign evaluates it. `compileMap(map, context)` (`maps/compile.ts`) reads a map's parameters once per map object and returns an evaluator, a closure over those parameters; every evaluation computes its result anew on Python's numeric tower, exact where the position and the parameters are exact, a float as soon as a float takes part, and where timetoalign decides on doubles (a TableMap off its exact path, a PiecewiseMap's interval, a measure view's measure) so does the evaluator, by bisection over a `Float64Array` of the map's own parameters. No value is cached. A measure view reads the expanded rows of its MeasureMap from `context`, as the server's measure-maps route answers them, and without them gives nothing and says so. `express` (`maps/express.ts`) writes a result on the map's target unit in that unit's number type, as timetoalign's `express_as` does, an int rounded half to even. A **timestamp** (`maps/timestamp.ts`) is the cross-section of a timeline, its **anchor**, at a position exact on the anchor's own axis: everything a cross-section line drawn there crosses. A `CrossSectionSpec` names the anchor and its **members**, the timelines its timestamp holds, each a `TimelineAxis` (its unit, number type, length and drawn maps, in drawing order; `timelineAxisOf`; and, where a reader of the canvas gives them, its `ordinate`, the ordinate of the drawn line at which the reading crosses it, and its `mapOrdinates`, by map ID the ordinate of each of its maps' drawings in that card) at its exact `offset` from the anchor's zero: by the canvas's convention first the anchor's ancestors, parent first (`ancestorMembers`: an ancestor's coordinate is its descendant's plus the offsets up to it, and it carries no map; an ancestor's span holds its descendant's, so it is crossed wherever the anchor is), then the timelines its line may cross (the anchor's segments and its displayed descendants drawn with a line of their own, with the segments of each, in traversal order); nothing marks a member as a segment, a segment is a member like any other. A member read by a group (the other members of a groupstamp's group, [Show coordinates](#show-coordinates)) also has a `scale`, its drawn end over that of the member holding the anchor, since the members of a group start and end together: its coordinate at an anchor position `p` is `(p − offset) × scale`, and its span ends at `offset + length / scale`. `timestampAt(spec, position, knots)` gives the anchor's part, then the part of each member whose closed span `[offset, offset + length]` holds the position (a boundary two contiguous members share is crossed by both), each with its coordinate in its number type and each drawn map's value, which every reader shows without computing it again. A **row** (`Row {timeline, map}`) is one of those values: a member's own coordinate (`map` `null`) or one of its maps' values. `rowAxis(spec, row, knots)` reads a row both ways, forward from an anchor position to the row's value and back from a value to the anchor position where the row takes it; it is the one path by which anything evaluates or inverts a map, so the values shown, the position a pointer picks on a row (`snapOnRow`) and the position an edited value gives (`positionFor`) never disagree. A **pin** (`Pin`) is a timestamp held for editing: its spec, the expanded MeasureMap rows it is read with (or why they are not at hand), the timestamp, and `named`, the named timestamp it shows, or `null`; `pinAt(pin, position)` reads it anew at another position. `positionFor(pin, row, value)` gives the anchor position at which a row takes a typed value: a coordinate's at its member's offset plus the value, a map's value inverted over its member's span and settled on the anchor's axis (a float on a fraction axis to the nearest multiple of `1/denominator`, any value on an int axis to the nearest integer); a row the timestamp does not show, a value no position gives, a position outside the timeline's `[0, length]` and one outside the anchor's (an ancestor's value beyond the anchor's span) are refused with `invalid_value`. `timestampRows(pin)` lists the rows as every reader shows them (the parts top to bottom by their timelines' `ordinate`, a part without one last, the timestamp's own parts keeping the anchor first; per part its rows top to bottom as drawn, its coordinate at its `ordinate` and each map at its entry of `mapOrdinates`, a row without one after those with one, so a part without ordinates lists its coordinate, then its maps; each row with its label, its value or the text shown instead, and whether an edit moves the timestamp: a coordinate always, a map while it is invertible over its timeline's span), and `timestampJson(pin)` writes the timestamp as one JSON value: the named timestamp's members as the manifest holds them, or `{timeline, coordinate}` for an unnamed one (the anchor's coordinate as a bare value), followed by `mapping`, keyed by ID in part order, each crossed timeline but the anchor its coordinate dict and each map its value (`null` where it gives none). The inverse is `maps/invert.ts`'s: `invertible(map, context)` says whether a map is strictly monotone, decided exactly on its own parameters, over the span the context names (a LinearMap whose scalar is not 0, a `linear` TableMap and a PiecewiseMap rising or falling strictly over what the span reaches, a ChainMap whose every link is), and `invert(map, value, context)` gives the position, exact on exact operands and a float as soon as a float takes part, or `null`; a `previous` TableMap, a ConstantMap, a CombinationMap and the four measure views are never invertible.

A position is picked on the **row the pointer hovers**, by `snapOnRow(spec, row, p, h, knots)`: `p` is the pointer's distance in units from the anchor's zero and `h` half a device pixel in units. On every row of a member of an `int` axis (samples, ticks, pixels), its coordinate's and its maps' alike, the picked value is the integer nearest to `p` minus its offset, a tie going up, so such a timeline steps by 1 whatever the granularity and no map's value axis decides its step. On a coordinate row of any other member the picked value is the simplest of the member's number type within `h` of `p` minus its offset; on a map's row of any other member it is the simplest of the map's target number type (the value's own kind where the map names no target unit) within `hv` of the map's value at `p`, `hv` being half the spread of its values at `p − h` and `p + h`, and that value is brought back to the anchor's axis exactly through the map's inverse; a map without an inverse there, or giving no number, falls back to its member's own coordinate. The result is held to the member's span within the anchor's `[0, length]`. The simplest value is `snapValue(value, reach, numberType, granularity)` (`number/grid.ts`), never finer than the workspace's granularity, which is a cap: on a fraction axis the nearest `k/d` for each divisor `d` of `denominator`, ascending; on a float axis the value rounded to 0, 1, … `decimals` places; on an int axis the nearest multiple of each power of ten from the largest not above `2·reach` down to 1. The first within reach wins; where none is, the finest is taken (the nearest multiple of `1/denominator`, `decimals` places, the nearest integer), a tie going up each time; `clampValue` holds a value to a span, below only where the length is not known. Every comparison is exact on the doubles' exact values (`fromFloat`), so the position is exact and no float constructs it; zooming in reaches finer values by itself, since a device pixel then covers fewer units. `number/display.ts` gives a coordinate's two texts and its coordinate dict. The **raw** text, `rawText(value, numberType, unit)`, is the value as Copy writes it (`copyValue`: a fraction `n/d`, or `n` when the denominator is 1, a float its shortest round-trip `repr`, an int its plain digits), a space and the unit's schema name (`219/16 quarters`, `0.1 seconds`, `514041 samples`). The **pretty** text, `prettyText(value, numberType, unit, decimals?)`, is the number, a space and its unit's **pretty unit**, `prettyUnit(unit)`, a symbol, an abbreviation, else the unit's name: `♩` for quarters, `𝅝` (U+1D15D) for whole notes, `ms`, `min`, `smp`, `ticks`, `px`, `pt`, `m`, `cm`, `mm` and `in`, any other unit its name; a fraction is mixed and its digits never grouped (`13 11/16 ♩`), an int is grouped by three with single spaces (`514 041 smp`), so only on an int axis, a float is its `repr`, and a value without a unit is its number alone. Seconds are a clock with no pretty unit after it (`prettyUnit` gives `s`, shown beside a field): hours `h` only when not 0, minutes `′` when not 0 or after hours, seconds `″` always, a part after a shown one padded to two digits, and decimals only where the seconds are not whole (`42.5″`, `1′15.5″`, `1h02′05″`, `0″`, the fraction 121/2 as `1′00 1/2″`, the float `1e-05` as `0.00001″`); the split is exact, on the rational or on the decimal digits of the float's `repr`, never in float arithmetic. Without `decimals` the pretty text is exact. With `decimals` it is rounded: a float to `decimals` places as Python's `round` rounds it, then spelled; a fraction mixed while its denominator is at most 2520, else its exact decimal where that ends, else `≈`, before the sign, and 12 decimal places; an int as it is; seconds split after rounding. The coordinate flag rounds with the granularity's `decimals`, and a drawing's coordinate label is the pretty text rounded so with `decimals` 3 (layout's `coordinateText`, the drawing specification's section 5.2). `coordinateDict` gives the coordinate dict `{unit, number_type, value}`. `parseAxisValue(text, numberType)` (`number/parse.ts`) reads typed text as the exact value it spells, never through a JavaScript float: on a fraction axis an integer, `n/d`, a mixed fraction `a b/c` or a decimal, on a float axis a decimal (`0.1`, `.5`, `1e-07`), on an int axis digits, plain or grouped by three with single spaces, each with an optional sign; other text, a zero denominator and an exponent beyond ±1000 are refused with `invalid_value`, and whether a negative value is allowed is the caller's to say.

### The manifest value, load and save

A manifest held by `core` is always in the complete form, every key with a default present. Its types are read-only views of the schema package's generated complete forms: `Manifest` of the package's `CompleteManifest`, holding root timelines (`TimelineManifest`, whose `length` is a value or `null` and whose `children` carry their `offset`), groups, measure maps and its own `statements` and `provenance`, its `meta` a JSON object; a timeline and a region hold their own `statements` and `provenance` likewise, kept as given (`manifest/statements.ts`). `statementHolders(manifest)` walks the elements that may hold statements once, in subject order, the manifest first, then root by root the timeline, its regions in their order and its children depth first, each with its path, and `statementHolder(manifest, id)` finds one; `skeletonOf(manifest)` is the manifest's statements alone at the paths they have in it, with the full context, memoised per manifest: the schema package's statement functions (`withPair`, `withCandidate`, `withBelief`, `withoutPair`, …) run on that skeleton, never on a held manifest, whose lossless numbers a structural copy would lose. **The `@context`.** A held manifest keeps of its `@context` only the prefixes it declares itself, those that are no format term and no prefix of TimeToShare's catalogue with the same namespace, and only while its statements and provenance use them; with none left it has no `@context` (`withStatementContext`), so a workspace file does not repeat the format's terms in every manifest. The full context, the format's terms and every prefix the statements use, each namespace from the manifest's own declaration else the catalogue's (`withFullContext`), is added where a manifest leaves core: `manifestFileValue(value)` is that one boundary, the value with every ID written `#id` and the full context, which `saveManifest` writes, the schema check, the warnings and the unknown keys read, and the Manifest tab shows. A value on a timeline's own axis (`length`, `offset`, region `start` and `end`, a flow-control element's `coordinate`) is bare (`AxisValue`). Every number is a `JsonNumber`: a plain `number`, a `bigint` beyond 2^53, or a `JsonFloat` for an integral float, so that `600.0` is written back as `600.0`. The `graphical` object is the one exception: its declarations hold canvas numbers, so it is read plain at the schema boundary, a declaration's `2.0` stored as the double `2` and an integer beyond 2^53 refused with `invalid_value` unless a double holds it exactly; a `style` command's `set` and the workspace's `graphical` are read the same way. A key the schema does not declare is kept where it was found: values are spread, never rebuilt from known fields, so unknown keys survive every command and are written back. `core` reads, orders and writes no JSON of its own: the reader, the canonical order and the standard writer are the schema package's.

`normalizeManifest(value)` is the one path from a value with bare IDs (a workspace's manifest, a command's change) to a manifest: validate it through `schemaChecked` (below), complete, put in canonical order, apply the segment locks, check the invariants root by root, complete the styles. It returns the manifest or a `LoadError`, and holds no type assertion: the validator's assertion types the value. A command that has already checked a fragment continues from completion, so a fragment is validated once; a value canonical order cannot order is refused with the schema's first problem. `loadManifest(text)` is the path from a manifest file: it reads the text in any of TimeToShare's serializations with the schema package's `loads`, validates the value as the file writes it, makes its IDs bare and normalizes it as above; its problems are `invalid_json` or `invalid_yaml` for a text that does not parse as the serialization it is read as (the reader's message naming the place), the first `schema_violation` with the path the validator reports, or every invariant violation of a schema-valid manifest; unknown keys are never problems. A value declaring another version than 0.2.0 is refused at `["timetoshare"]`; there is no migration. `saveManifest(manifest, file)` checks the manifest the same way, throwing `InvalidManifestError` with every problem, and writes its file value (`manifestFileValue`: IDs as `#id`, the full `@context`) with the schema package's `dumpsFile` as the manifest file `{minimal, styling, serialization}` says: in the minimal or the complete form, with or without its `graphical` object, in canonical YAML (`yaml`), pretty JSON (`json`) or RFC 8785 JSON (`jcs`), each member not given as for a new bundle (minimal, with styling, YAML), byte-identical to what the Python writer produces. Import reads a file with `readManifestFile`, which is `loadManifest` and also tells how its text writes the manifest, so that it can be written back as it came: minimal when the value equals its own minimal form, with styling when it holds a `graphical` object, and in the serialization the text is in (`jcs` when it is byte for byte the RFC 8785 JSON of its value, `json` for any other JSON, `yaml` otherwise). An entry a command adds or replaces (a conversion map, region or flow-control element) is checked by the same schema where it will lie before it is stored: the manifest with the value in place is completed, ordered and checked whole, as a settled manifest is, so what the value names, a measure view's measure map, an element a statement's value names, is found wherever it lies in the manifest. A timeline it adds is checked first alone, as the one root of a manifest of its own beside the manifest's measure maps, what its elements state set aside, and then with their statements and provenance in place in the whole manifest, so a value may name any element of it.

**IDs in a file.** A manifest file writes every ID and every reference to an ID as `#` followed by the ID (TimeToShare's README, "Written in a file"); a manifest held by `core`, and everything built on it, holds them bare. `loadManifest` reads a file's `#id` into the bare ID with the schema package's `withBareIds`, and `saveManifest` writes the file form with its `withFileIds`. The schema states the file form, so every schema check of a manifest `core` holds goes through one function, `schemaChecked`, which validates its file value (`manifestFileValue`), returns the value as core holds it (bare IDs, the held `@context`) and maps the path of a problem back to the bare manifest (`idFormPath`: a key of `graphical.order` loses its `#`); a file is validated as written, before its IDs are made bare; the invariants and their messages work on bare IDs and quote them so. The workspace file holds IDs bare throughout, its manifests included, and so do the `data-id` attributes of a drawing's SVG.

### Commands

Every edit is a command, a plain JSON object `{type, ...}` carrying its author and time once journalled, and there are four: `add`, `set`, `remove` and `style`. Each is aimed at addresses, and the kind of its target, resolved once, decides what it does. `apply(view, command)` is a pure reducer: it never modifies its input and returns either the new view or a `CommandError` `{code, message, path}`. It parses the command, dispatches it by type and target kind, applies it atomically (one refused change refuses it whole), and settles every block the command touched: in each touched manifest every groupstamp whose timeline is no group's member any more (a group removed, a member gone from its group) is made an ordinary timestamp (`withGroupstampsOnMembers`), so a command ending a membership is never refused for the groupstamps it leaves; then the manifest is checked by the schema and the invariants once, and its block's `places` fitted to its containers.

- `add {into, <kind>: value}` adds a `block` into `workspace`; a root `timeline` (completed, the locks of its place applied, its ID not yet held in the manifest; a root may carry an `offset`), a `group` (`{id?, name?, meta?, members}`, its members existing timelines or virtual timelines, each joining it as `set groups` joins a group), a `measure_map` (`{id, name?, meta?, rows}`) or a `virtual_timeline` into a block; a `child`, `region`, `flow_control` element or `map` into a timeline; a `flow_map` into a root ([FlowMaps and virtual timelines](#flowmaps-and-virtual-timelines)). The value carries its id, except a named timestamp, an elision and a named MatchStamp, whose IDs core mints (below), and a group, a region, a flow-control element, a map, a FlowMap and a virtual timeline, which may leave their `id` out to have one minted ([IDs and addresses](#ids-and-addresses)). A named `timestamp` is added into a timeline (any timeline, a segment included): `{name, coordinate, display?, groupstamp?}`, the name and the coordinate required (`invalid_value`) and nothing else (`unknown_property`), the name non-empty, the coordinate a bare value on the timeline's own axis within `[0, length]` (`invalid_value`, or `invalid_coordinate` for a value of another number type), and `display` a list of display words from `wedge`, `name`, `id`, `coordinate` and `cross_section`, each at most once (`invalid_value`: "'w' is no display word", "'w' is given twice"), stored in that canonical order, the schema's default `["wedge", "name", "cross_section"]` where it is left out, and `groupstamp` a boolean (`readGroupstamp`), `true` only on a group member, a timeline a group lists or a descendant of one (core's `groupsHolding`, `manifest/tree.ts`, over `memberAncestry`, so a group's virtual member is one), else refused with `invalid_value`, and `false` where it is left out (the editor gives `groupstampByDefault`: on a group member, unless the innermost open editor is a timeline editor); each refusal lies at `[addressKey(into), "timestamp", <member>]`; it is given a minted `timestamp_…` ID, which the journaled command carries, so a replay reads it and mints nothing, and written into the manifest's `graphical.timestamps` in canonical order (by `timeline`, coordinate, `id`). Only the coordinate on its own timeline is stored, never a map's value. An `elision` added into a timeline (at any depth, a segment included) is `{start, end, name?, width?, display?}`, the start and the end required (`invalid_value`) and nothing else (`unknown_property`): bare values on the timeline's own axis, the start at or after 0 and the end at or before a known length (`elision_stretch`, worded as TimeToShare's validators word it), the end after the start (`elision_stretch` at `end`); the name non-empty; `width` exactly one of `{ratio}` (above 0, at most 1), `{span}` (a bare value on the timeline's axis above 0, `non_positive_number` otherwise) and `{canvas}` (a finite number above 0), and the standard gap where it is left out; `display` words from `ellipsis`, `name`, `id`, `coordinate` and `continuation`, each at most once, stored in that order, the schema's default `["ellipsis", "name"]` where it is left out. It is given a minted `elision_…` ID and written into `graphical.elisions`. Added into a block, `elision` is a non-empty list of such values, each naming its `timeline` (`elision_timeline` for an ID naming no timeline), given their IDs in list order: one command storing several elisions at once, as the editor stores the gaps a card derived ([The inspector](#the-inspector)). Every problem of an elision lies at `[addressKey(into), "elision", …]` (a list's `i`-th at `[…, "elision", i, …]`), so no elision a command makes reaches the schema check. A root timeline added into a block is given, within the same command, two **default timestamps** (`withDefaultTimestamps`): `origin` at the zero of its number type and, where its length is known, `length` at its length, each with the `display` `["wedge", "coordinate"]`, no groupstamp and a minted `timestamp_…` ID; a child is given none when it is added. A child that joins a group (`set groups`, or an `add` of a group listing it) is given the same two as groupstamps, their `display` `["wedge", "coordinate", "cross_section"]`, so that their lines cross the group's frame and the ancestor lines in its card; a timestamp of it already named `origin` or `length` at that value is made a groupstamp instead, `cross_section` added to its `display`. Only joining gives them: one deleted later stays deleted. Once made they are named timestamps like any other. The editor gives every root of a manifest it imports the same pair when the manifest has no `graphical` object (`importedManifest`), which would state its own timestamps. A named MatchStamp, `matchstamp`, is added into a block: `{name, anchors, match_claims}`, each required (`invalid_value`) and nothing else (`unknown_property`), the name non-empty, `anchors` a non-empty list of alignment anchors in screen order (top to bottom, then left to right), each a pair of coordinates `{timeline, coordinate}` naming a timeline of the manifest and a bare value on its own axis within it, no anchor given twice, and `match_claims` the IDs of the MatchClaims behind it, kept in the order given. It is given a minted `matchstamp_…` ID, each anchor a minted `anchor_…` and the `display` `["line"]`, the middle anchor in screen order (the upper of two middles) `["line", "name"]`; every coordinate becomes the **match timestamp** lying there, a named timestamp with `matchstamp: true`: the one the manifest holds at that exact value of that timeline, else a new one with a minted `timestamp_…` ID, no name, the `display` `["cross_section"]` and `groupstamp: true` where its timeline is a group member, else `false` (a match timestamp taken over keeps its own), so at most one lies on a coordinate and an ordinary named timestamp is never taken for one. The rules of a MatchStamp (an anchor joining two timelines, its anchors one connected set, a claim ID listed once) are the schema's, checked on the settled manifest (`schema_violation`).
- `set {changes: [{target, values: {...properties}}]}` sets properties of blocks and elements; the properties are nested under `values`, so none collides with `target`. One gesture is one command, so moving five blocks is one `set` with five changes.
- `remove {targets}` removes blocks and elements, mixed, every target resolved before anything is removed; a block goes with every block whose `parent` chain reaches it. A removed timeline takes along its subtree; the named timestamps and the elisions lying on it or on its subtree; its id from every group's `members` and every display order; its own and its descendants' band orders; every entry of a style rule's `select.addresses` addressing a removed element, a rule left with none going too, and every rule whose `select.within` names it. A group left without members stays. A removed timeline or region, with every timeline and region removed along with it, is taken out of what every element of the manifest states (`withoutReferencesTo`): a value that is a node naming it (`{id: "#X"}`) goes, as does a provenance entry whose value names it, `#X` leaves every `basis` and `stated_by`, a `service` `#X` goes, and a list, node, container or `provenance` left empty loses its key; a reference into another manifest (`other.yaml#X`) stays. A removed named timestamp takes along every `select.addresses` entry naming it or one of its parts, its labels and its cross-section line, a rule left with none going too. A named MatchStamp goes with its anchors and every match timestamp no other MatchStamp names; a removed timeline or match timestamp takes along every MatchStamp an anchor of which names it, and leaves the `timelines` of every resource; an alignment anchor alone is refused with `not_removable`. A removed group goes alone: its members stay, roots ungrouped and children in their parents. A removed measure map goes alone too. Removing the last root leaves the block empty. A segment, a child of a segment line, whose removal would leave a gap in its line is refused with `segment_not_removable` ("Removing segment 'S' of line 'L' is not supported yet"); one that goes with its line, an ancestor or its block is removed with it.
- `style {select, set, name?, blocks?}` puts a style rule into the manifest of each listed block, or of every block with an element the selector matches; its addresses name bare IDs, as every command does, and a file writes them `#`-first. With `name`, the declaration becomes a named style in that manifest's `styles`. A rule whose selector equals an existing one is merged into it; a property set to `null` is removed, and a rule left empty is dropped.

A `name` set on a block is its manifest's, the title; on a timeline, group, measure map, conversion map, region or flow-control element the element's own; `null` states none. A named timestamp and a named MatchStamp keep a name, so `null` is refused for them (`invalid_value`), except on a match timestamp, whose name is optional: `null` deletes it. The workspace's `name` is a non-empty string (`invalid_value` otherwise). A timeline's `is_segment_line` makes its children its segments, or plain children again; it and its subtree then take the locks their places decide ([Invariants](#invariants)), and a line turned off loses the lock its place gave it for being one: a child or a group member is unlocked, a root that is no member keeps its own `locked`. A group's `order` is its display order, every member once, or `null` to delete the entry (`invalid_order` otherwise). A timeline's `order` is its band order, or `null` so that it inherits again; any other value is refused with `invalid_order` and the first problem in the terms of TimeToShare's validators, such as `'regions' is missing from the band order of 'score'`. `place` merges into the block's `places` entry of an item or of a timeline's one card: a member given a value is stated, one given `null` is no longer stated, and a place left empty is deleted. A member's card is set through its group: a group's `cards`, `{[T]: {width?, height?, spacing?, margin?}}`, merges each member's value into `places[G].cards[T]` by the same rule, a card place left empty and then a group entry left with nothing being deleted; `cards: {}` and a member value of `null` are refused with `invalid_value`, and a timeline that is no member of the group with `unknown_place` (`'T' is no member of group 'G'`). A timeline's `place` sets its card only where it has one card: a timeline drawn in no card of its own or in the frames of several groups is refused with `not_an_item` (`'T' is drawn in the frames of several groups`), and a member's card given `x`, `y`, `length` or `scale`, through either target, with `not_an_item` too (`'T' is a member: its card takes only width, height, spacing and margin`). The card address `{block, id: T, part: ["card"]}` takes no `set` (`unknown_target`). A MatchStamp's `anchors` rewrite its anchors, as an Update of it to the current potential MatchStamp gives them: an anchor joining the same two match timestamps as one it has keeps that anchor's ID and `display`, any other is new; where the anchor showing the name is gone and no kept one shows it, the middle one does; the match timestamps the new anchors need are made, those no MatchStamp names any more go, and the same anchors in another order change nothing.

**Arrangements.** An arrangement is placement, never part of the manifest ([Arrangements](#arrangements)). A block's `arrangement` is `null`, deleting the member, or `{direction, order}`: `direction` `vertical` or `horizontal`, `order` a list of IDs of its groups and ungrouped roots, kept as given, an ID naming no item or named again being valid (`readItemArrangement`). A `horizontal` one is stored as the row the rules give from the order given (core's `rowOf`), so a later reading of the block finds the row it is drawn in; one the rules refuse is refused with `arrangement_conflict`, its message the rules' reason (`"Theme A" and "Theme B" overlap on "Score" (8–16 ♩)`). An edit that later breaks the rules, an offset moved, keeps the stored arrangement, which is then drawn one item under another until the conflict is gone. The workspace's `arrangement`, of its blocks, is `null` or `{direction, columns?}`: `direction` `vertical`, `horizontal` or `matrix`, `columns` a positive integer that only a matrix states (`invalid_value`: "only a matrix has columns"; `readCanvasArrangement`), a matrix without it taking the smallest number of columns whose square holds the blocks (`columnsOf`). Each is one `set`, one journal entry.

**Statements.** What an element states is changed only whole: one `set` of `statements` (an object of property–value pairs) or `provenance` (a list of annotations), or both in one change, on `{block}` (the manifest's own, which, like `name`, are properties of the block's manifest and so no member of an `add` of a block, refused there with `unknown_property`), on a timeline at any depth, a segment included, or on a region replaces that member; `null` removes it. The Metadata tab builds every change with the schema package's statement functions and sends it as one such `set`, so undo takes it back in one step. Core reads the shape alone (`invalid_value`, "statements is an object of property–value pairs, or null", "provenance is a list of annotations, or null") and leaves every other rule to the schema check of the settled manifest, a refusal being `schema_violation` with the schema package's message at the offending value's path in the manifest: "'#nope' names no element of this manifest" at `[<block key>, "manifest", "timelines", 1, "children", 0, "statements", "dcterms:source", 0, "id"]`, "'foaf:maker': the prefix 'foaf' is not declared in @context". A prefix a statement uses is taken from the manifest's own `@context`, else from TimeToShare's catalogue, and the held `@context` follows the statements ([The manifest value, load and save](#the-manifest-value-load-and-save)). A group takes neither member (`wrong_target_kind`). The manifest's own statements lie outside every element of the manifest, so a group or timeline editor refuses to change them ([No side effects](#no-side-effects)).

**Where a timeline lies.** Two properties change a manifest's structure; the Properties tab sends `groups` ([The inspector](#the-inspector)), and no gesture sends `parent` yet. `parent` is `null`, making the timeline a root, or `{timeline, offset}`, making it a child of that timeline at that bare offset, its subtree with it, by the rule that places every child (it may grow an unlocked parent, as below). Refused: `unit_mismatch` for a parent of another unit; `segment_structure` when the moved timeline is a segment or a segment line, or the new parent a segment line; `cycle` for the timeline itself or one of its descendants; a locked parent that would have to grow, as for any child. `groups` is the list of the groups the timeline is a member of, distinct group IDs in any order: the timeline joins each listed group it was no member of, as its last member, and leaves each it was a member of that the list leaves out, so a timeline may be a member of several groups and is drawn in one card in each of their frames. Refused: `invalid_value` for a value that is no list of group IDs or that lists one twice, `unknown_target` for an ID that names no group, `nested_member` when the timeline would hold or lie inside another member of a group it joins. Ids never change; a moved timeline keeps its band order and its card's stated size, and settling reshapes its `places` entry for its new holder.

**FlowMaps and virtual timelines** (`commands/flowmaps.ts`; what they mean, [FlowMaps and virtual timelines](#flowmaps-and-virtual-timelines)). A FlowMap is added into the root it orders, `{id?, timeline?, chunks, name?, canonical?, convention?, provenance?}`, its `timeline` that root (`invalid_value` for another). A virtual timeline is added into a block, `{id?, map, timeline, name?, through?, length?}`, `map` and `timeline` required (`invalid_value`) and its `unit` and `number_type` never given (`unknown_property`): they and its length are computed, through a FlowMap its root's unit and number type and the FlowMap's played length, also `through` a group, as timetoalign reads it; through a conversion map core evaluates, the map's target unit, that unit's default number type and the map's value at the end of the timeline holding it; `through` anything else, the unit and number type of `timeline` and the length the value must state, core carrying no map through it. A given `length` other than the computed one is refused with `invalid_value` naming both ("the length of a virtual timeline is computed from its map: 3054 ♩, not 24 ♩"). Each value is read where it will lie, the whole manifest checked by the schema and TimeToShare's validators, so the root itself or nothing of the root's tree as a chunk, a second canonical FlowMap, a cycle and a FlowMap applied outside its root's tree without `through` are refused as the validators refuse them, `schema_violation` with their message at `[<address key of into>, <kind>, …]` (at `[<FlowMap's key>, "chunks", k]` for a `set`). A FlowMap is **computed** when it states a `convention` and a provenance entry of method `inferred` (`isComputedFlowMap`): a `set` of its `chunks` or `name` is refused with `computed_flow_map` ("FlowMap 'default' is computed from the flow control of 'allegro' and is read only"), and it takes only a new computation's result, `computed: {chunks, provenance}`, both required and the provenance holding an entry of method `inferred` (`invalid_value` otherwise, and on a FlowMap written by hand, whose chunks take `chunks`), its convention, name and canonical kept. Every change of a FlowMap's chunks writes, in the same command, the played length of every virtual timeline playing it, without `through` or through a group, and of every virtual timeline playing one of those as a chunk, each recorded at `[<virtual timeline's key>, "length"]`. `remove` refuses, with `not_removable` naming what still names it, a FlowMap a virtual timeline left applies ("FlowMap 'default' is applied by the virtual timeline 'allegro_default'") and a virtual timeline that another comes from or that a FlowMap names as a chunk, unless they go in the same command; a removed virtual timeline leaves its groups, every display order, its place and every rule addressing it, and a rule whose `flow` names a removed FlowMap or virtual timeline loses its `flow`, so the card is drawn as written. A virtual card is **hidden** by `set {place: {x: null, y: null}}` on `{block, id: V}` (a place every stated member of which goes is deleted, and the card with it) and shown again by a place; `remove` of `{block, id: V}` deletes the virtual timeline and leaves its FlowMap. A named timestamp is added into a virtual timeline, on its own axis, as into a root, and a group or a band order takes a virtual timeline as it takes a timeline.

**The flow switch** is a style rule, no command of its own: `style {select: {addresses: [{id: T, part: ["card"]}]}, set: {flow: F}, blocks: [B]}` switches the cards of root `T` onto `F`, a FlowMap of `T` or a virtual timeline applying one of `T`'s FlowMaps to `T` without `through`, and `flow: null` switches them back. `flow` is read as an ID; the validators' `flow_rule` refuses a named style setting it, a selector other than card addresses alone, a value naming no FlowMap or virtual timeline, and one of another timeline (`schema_violation`). Core's `cardFlow(manifest, T)` is the one reader of the rule, for layout, core's element listing and the canvas alike: the last rule of the manifest's own layer that selects `{id: T, part: ["card"]}` by `addresses` alone and sets `flow`; no other layer gives a card a `flow`, and layout's cascade has no `flow` style.

A command is atomic. Ids are never settable, nor a timeline's `unit` or `number_type`, nor a root's `offset`. A flow-control element is never changed in place: its role, its coordinate and what decides the flow stay as they were added, and it is removed only with its timeline (`not_removable`). A `set` gives it a `name` and a `label`, and changes its `provenance` only by a `belief`: the actor's earlier belief, compared as the IRI it stands for under the manifest's `@context`, is replaced in place, else the belief joins the first entry, else the element gains one entry holding it alone; every entry's paradata and every other actor's belief are kept, so no command erases provenance, and an element is believed again only by the actor who rejected it. `provenance` itself is no property a `set` gives (`unknown_property`). A `set` that changes nothing, a value written equal to the stored one or values that undo each other, is refused with `invalid_value` ("the command changes nothing"); the values written are compared path by path, and a block where a change reached past its path (a whole manifest, a move, an offset, a group) is compared whole. A position on an element's own axis is a bare value, normalized as its axis's number type selects: an integer on a `float` axis is stored as that float, an integer on a `fraction` axis or a coordinate dict is refused with `invalid_coordinate`, and a pair is reduced to lowest terms. Replaying a journal is deterministic because every ID is in it. The caller gives the id of what it adds: a block id with `mintBlockId`, an element id unique in its manifest (`manifestIds` lists the IDs a manifest holds, an elision's among them). Every ID a command creates, the kinds above and the first segment of every segment line it leaves without one, `dispatch` mints with the store's minter before the command is applied and journals in the command's `ids` list, in the order `CarriedIds` (`commands/types.ts`) states for each kind; the reducer is pure and mints nothing, so a replay takes the IDs from that list, and a list too short, or too long where the command is checked, is refused with `invalid_command` at `["ids"]`. `preview` mints with a copy of the store's minter (`store.minter()`), so a preview shows the IDs its dispatch will journal. A given ID the manifest already holds is refused with `duplicate_id`, the value's own ID at `[addressKey(into), <kind>, "id"]`, and a given ID nested inside the value (such as a child's own child) at its location in the manifest the command would leave (`[<block key>, "manifest", "timelines", 0, "children", 0, "children", 0, "id"]`).

`preview(view, command)` is the reducer without the schema check, the invariants and the no-op refusal: what a gesture shows before its release, so a preview can never differ from what the release stores ([The gesture engine](#the-gesture-engine)).

A refusal's path follows one rule: it starts with the key (`addressKey`) of the address of the target at fault, followed by the property or kind at fault (`[<address key>, "length"]`), or by `"manifest"` and a location in the manifest the command would leave. The problems of an `add`'s value lie under the address it is added into, at `[addressKey(into), <kind>, …]`: a block added to the workspace at `[addressKey({}), "block", …]`, its duplicate ID at `[addressKey({}), "block", "id"]`. No path starts with a bare block id; only a malformed command names the malformed member of the command itself. A message quotes an ID as `'x'` and names an address by its words, `addressWords(view, address)`: the workspace, a bundle by its manifest's name or id ("bundle 'Mixed'", "the anonymous bundle"), an element by its kind and its name, else its ID, followed by its bundle unless the view holds that block alone ("timeline 'Score' in bundle 'Mixed'"), a band, a card or a part by its owner ("the regions band of timeline 'Score'", "the line of timestamp 'Downbeat'"), and an address naming nothing by its members ("'gone' in bundle 'Mixed'", "block '0000000009'"). The path keeps the key; the words are for the reader only. The codes are stable strings, exported as `ErrorCode`:

- reading: `invalid_json`, `invalid_yaml`, `invalid_command`, `invalid_coordinate`, `invalid_value`, `schema_violation`;
- the workspace file: `unsupported_format`, `unsupported_version`, `unknown_member`, `missing_member`, `unknown_session`, `unreferenced_session`, `session_referenced_twice`, `journal_mismatch`;
- the workspace: `invalid_block_id`, `key_mismatch`, `unknown_parent`, `parent_cycle`, `invalid_index`, `duplicate_index`, `non_finite_number`, `non_positive_number`, `invalid_order`, `unknown_place`;
- addressing: `invalid_address`, `unknown_target`, `unknown_property`, `wrong_target_kind`, `id_not_settable`, `not_removable` (the workspace and an alignment anchor), `duplicate_id`, `not_an_item`;
- structure: `unit_mismatch`, `segment_structure`, `cycle`, `nested_member`;
- editors: `no_editor`, `not_inside`, `side_effect`;
- arrangements: `arrangement_conflict`;
- FlowMaps: `computed_flow_map`;
- elisions: `elision_timeline`, `elision_stretch`;
- lengths and Delete: `child_exceeds_parent`, `locked`, `length_below_content`, `segment_end_fixed`, `timestamp_exceeds_length`, `elision_exceeds_length`, `segment_not_removable`;
- locks: `segment_never_locked`, `unlocking_not_supported`;
- manifest invariants: `region_end_before_start`, `negative_child_offset`.

### Invariants

The manifest invariants are timetoalign's, checked on every load and over the manifest every command would leave: every value on a timeline's own axis reads as that axis's number type; a region does not end before it starts. That a unit admits a timeline's number type (TimeToShare's one table of units, `UNITS`, refused as `schema_violation` at the timeline's `number_type`), that IDs are unique across the manifest, that a jump destination's `to` and a break's `ends.after` name an element that is there and not rejected, that a measure view's `measure_map` names one of the manifest's measure maps, that a conversion map converts from its timeline's unit and a map another map holds from its holder's source unit, that a child has its parent's unit and lies within its parent's known length, and that a segment line's segments are contiguous, are TimeToShare's rules, checked by its validator; so a removal that would leave a destination's `to` or a measure view's `measure_map` naming nothing is refused. A negative child offset is valid in the schema but not supported yet: core refuses it with `negative_child_offset` at the child's `offset` and the message `A negative child offset is not supported yet: child '<id>' has offset <offset>.`; a root's offset may be negative. The catalogue in `app/packages/core/tests/README.md` lists every row.

**Locking.** `locked` states whether a timeline's length is fixed; it defaults to `false`. A segment is never locked: its segment line's lock governs it, so reading a manifest unlocks every segment, and a segment is written with `false`. A segment line that is a child, a segment or a group member is locked, and is written with an explicit `true`, in the minimal form too. A `locked` that contradicts its place is refused: `true` on a plain segment with `segment_never_locked` ("A segment is never locked: lock its segment line '<id>' instead."), `false` on such a segment line with `unlocking_not_supported`. A locked timeline's length cannot be set (`locked`). An unlocked timeline's length may be set, never below the farthest point a child reaches (`length_below_content`) and never past its parent's known length (`child_exceeds_parent`); a segment line fixes its segments' ends, and a length that would move one is refused with `segment_end_fixed`; a named timestamp lying exactly at the old end follows the end to the new length (`replaceTimeline`), so `length` stays at the end, while any other named timestamp keeps its place, and a length below one is refused with `timestamp_exceeds_length`, naming the first in canonical order; an elision ending at the old end follows the end while the new length lies after its start, and is refused with `elision_exceeds_length` otherwise; any other elision keeps its place, and a length that would leave one ending beyond it is refused with `elision_exceeds_length`; a length of `null` is always taken. A child added into, or moved within, an unlocked timeline past that timeline's known end grows that timeline, and only that timeline, to the child's end, provided the grown length passes the same checks; a locked timeline refuses the child with `child_exceeds_parent`. A parent never grows because a child grew, as in timetoalign.

**Segment lines.** A segment line of known length that an edit leaves without segments is given one: a segment from 0 covering the line's whole length, of its unit and number type, unlocked as every segment is, its id minted with the prefix of its unit's timeline class and carried in the command's `ids` (core's `withSegmentLinesCovered`). This happens before the schema checks every edited value: a manifest set whole (the Manifest tab's Apply), a block or timeline added, and every command's settling. In a manifest set whole, the tree of every root whose lines were covered then takes the locks its places decide, as `is_segment_line` gives them, so a member segment line that Apply gave its first segment is locked; the tree of a root the cover left alone is taken as the text states it. Reading a manifest never applies it, so a file holding such a line is refused as the schema's validator refuses it. A segment line of unknown length is left as it is, since nothing fixes where its segment would end.

**Unknown length.** A `length` of `null` means that the length is not known, on any timeline. It is a fact the manifest states, never inferred and never a violation; every check that needs an end runs where the end is known and is skipped where it is not. A child of unknown length reaches its offset, so adding it past the end of an unlocked timeline of known length grows that timeline to the offset.

Two rules go beyond what the library enforces, because the editor addresses and stores values directly: a value on a timeline's own axis must already carry the axis's number type, and a parent grows only to a value its own number type holds exactly. A child shares its parent's unit but may have another number type; its end, offset plus length, follows Python's numeric tower.

### MatchClaims and potential MatchStamps

A package's MatchClaim table lies beside its manifest, never in it, and core never holds it in a workspace; what it reads of one is the columns the server answers ([The server](#the-server)), in core's `matches/` ([decisions/0029](../decisions/0029-match-claim-columns.md)): the table's typed columns of exact values and their check (`columns.ts`), which builds, once per columns, the map from claim ID to row (`claimRowOf`) that the comparison and the editor read; the belief filter (`beliefs.ts`): its four states, `true`, `undecided`, `unlikely` and `false` (`BELIEF_STATES`, in the order the editor offers them), its default, every state but `false` (`DEFAULT_BELIEFS`), and its check (`checkBeliefFilter`), all TimeToShare's and re-exported, as timetoalign reads the same ones; `believedBy`, the believer's state for every claim, its belief, the most critical of several (false, unlikely, undecided, true: `ANCHOR_BELIEFS`), or `undecided` where it holds none; the row mask `beliefMask` keeps every row whose state is one of the kept states (`null` where all four are kept); and `anchorBelief`, the most critical state among the claims stating one anchor, by which a shown anchor is coloured; the believer and every actor compare as the IRIs they stand for under the manifest's `@context` (`actorIri`: a compact IRI expanded by its prefix, TimeToShare's `expandIri`, else as written), as timetoalign compares them, so a compact and an absolute IRI of one person are one actor wherever the editor compares actors; the **anchor index** built from the columns under that mask (`anchorIndexOf`, `anchors.ts`, `sort.ts`), with the unique coordinates, the AlignmentAnchors (two coordinates on two timelines that a claim states) and the claims stating each, the **potential MatchStamps** (each connected set of anchors, numbered by its first coordinate), the matched spans and the segments between two timelines (both [not yet wired](#not-yet-wired)), and the hit tests (`coordinateNear`, `coordinatesWithin`); and the comparison of a named MatchStamp with the index (`checkMatchStamp`, `compare.ts`): which of its anchors the index still states, in the order of its `alignment_anchors`, with the rows of the claims stating each (`statedBy`), which of its MatchClaims are missing from the table, left out by the belief filter (`claimsFilteredOut`), stating other anchors or new, whether it is out of date (an anchor no longer stated, any such MatchClaim, or an anchor it lacks of the potential MatchStamps its coordinates now fall into), and the potential MatchStamp an Update rewrites it from: the one holding its stated anchor that shows the name, else the largest of those its coordinates fall into, none when no anchor of it is stated. The commands that name, update and remove a MatchStamp are core's ordinary ones ([Commands](#commands)).

### FlowMaps and virtual timelines

A manifest's `flow_maps` order the stretches of a root (TimeToShare's README; drawing specification, section 8.10), each a list of **chunks**, each a Region of the root or of a descendant, a child timeline, a virtual timeline or a gap of a stated length on the root's axis; its `virtual_timelines` apply a FlowMap, or a conversion map, to a timeline, giving it an axis of its own. Core resolves both without a server, as timetoalign does, in the root's number type (`manifest/flowmaps.ts`, `manifest/virtual.ts`, both exported from the main entry):

- `resolveFlowMap(manifest, id)` gives a `ResolvedFlowMap` `{flowMap, timeline, unit, numberType, chunks, stretches, runs, length}`: each chunk `{kind, index, source, written, played}` of kind `region`, `timeline` (a child), `virtual` or `gap`, its written stretch carried to the root's axis level by level (a Region's or child's bounds expressed in the root's number type, `expressAs`, plus the origins on the way up); `stretches`, the flat list of placed stretches, a virtual chunk giving one per stretch of the FlowMap it applies, each chunk placed after the one before (`P(i+1) = Pi + ℓi`, in timetoalign's order of operations, so float axes agree bit for bit); `runs`, section 8.10's runs, consecutive chunks naming one ID (gaps form none), the one run rule that the element listing and layout read; and `length`, the played length `Λ`. The root itself is no chunk (`flow_map_chunk`), nor is anything outside its tree; a gap whose value is no exact value of the root's axis (1/3 on a float axis) is refused. A stretch is half open: `unfold(resolved, written)` gives every played position of a written one, each an `Occurrence {played, occurrence, of, chunk}`, `fold(resolved, played)` the written position of a played one (`null` in a gap), refusing a position outside `[0, Λ)` (`outside_virtual_axis`). Errors are thrown as `FlowMapError {code, path}`, its path into the manifest.
- `resolveVirtualTimeline(manifest, id)` gives a `FlowVirtualTimeline` (kind `flow_map`, its resolved FlowMap) or a `MappedVirtualTimeline` (kind `conversion_map`, the map compiled, its span), both with `{id, map, timeline, through, source, unit, numberType, length}`; `unfoldVirtual` and `foldVirtual` read either. Without `through` the timeline must be the FlowMap's root; `through` a group (`throughGroup`) it takes the FlowMap root's axis and played length, as timetoalign does, without carrying positions through the group; `through` anything else is kept as written, its stated length unevaluated. A conversion map is one of the top-level maps of a timeline (one inside a chain or combination map is refused, `map_not_evaluated`), whose target unit must be the virtual timeline's; its length is the map's value at the holding timeline's length.

The drawing's side lies in `@timetoalign/tilie-core/drawing`. `virtualDrawing(manifest, id)` reads a virtual timeline as the root it is drawn as, holding nothing, and `withVirtualMembers` puts a group's virtual members into its frame in display order, the one builder and the one frame order layout, the element listing and the canvas use (`files.ts`, with `locateVirtual`). `manifest/switched.ts` is the one source of switched cards ([Virtual and switched cards](#virtual-and-switched-cards)): `cardFlow` (the card's `flow`, [Commands](#commands)), `switchOf` (the `Switch {flowMap, named, name}` a card is switched to, or `null`: a FlowMap of its timeline that resolves, or a virtual timeline applying one without `through`), `unfoldingOf` (the stretch pieces `Piece {a, b, P, closed}` of an `Unfolding`, with the carry of another member `m × λT / λM`, exact or in binary64 with the product first), `playedAt` and `playedSpan` (where a written point or extent is drawn, piece by piece) and the counts `piecesHolding` and `piecesReached`. `manifestElements` lists a virtual card's elements (its card, line, name and five bands, its line holding every drawn chunk `{id: V, part: [k]}` of type `chunk` with its label and leader and, from the second drawn chunk on, its boundary `[k, "start"]`, a run once in a block-held card and each chunk in a frame, each chunk that is no gap then holding its window's five bands `{id: V, facet, part: [k]}` (`windowBandKey`), each band what the chunk's sources hold of its facet piece by piece (`windowsOf`), keyed as their own bands key them and carrying `V` as their `window`, so that a `within` naming `V` matches them, its named timestamps, and, on a placed card no group holds, its card elisions, those stored on it, `elisionsUnder`) and a switched card's (its FlowMap's chunks on `T`'s line, and every element of `T`'s tree once per piece reaching it: a conversion map once per piece reaching its timeline's extent, a flow-control element, with its labels, symbol and fork, once per piece holding its position, and no elision; in a switched frame the other members carried, a member's flow control outside it once per piece holding it, a child member's ancestor lines once per piece), so a rule selects them as any other element. `isAtomicSection(manifest, region)`, over `regionClasses`, is the one test of an atomic section (the class `tts:AtomicSection` in any spelling standing for its IRI).

### The workspace view

A workspace is held as a `View`, an immutable plain value: `meta` (`format: "tilie-workspace"`, `version: 8`), `name`, `settings`, `graphical`, `arrangement` where the blocks are arranged ([Arrangements on the canvas](#arrangements-on-the-canvas)), and `blocks`, a map from block id to block. The `name` is never empty, `untitled` (`UNTITLED_WORKSPACE`) for a new workspace, and gives its file's default name. `graphical` holds the workspace's default style rules, the cascade's layer between the built-in defaults and each manifest's own; like the name, it is changed by commands. The settings say how the editor is used on the workspace, and no command changes them ([The store and its journal](#the-store-and-its-journal)): `grid {size, snap}` (8 units, snapping), `blockDefaults` (scale 1, length 400, spacing 8, margin 0, no requested size) and `granularity {denominator, decimals}` (`DEFAULT_GRANULARITY`, 2520 = lcm(1, …, 10), so every tuplet up to tens, and 3), the caps on a position picked on a timeline: `denominator`, a positive safe integer, for fraction axes, and `decimals`, an integer from 0 to 15, for float axes, which also sets the places a float is shown with; an int axis has no setting ([Values on timelines](#values-on-timelines)); `manifests`, how each block's manifest is written to a file (below); and `matchClaimFilters`, by block id, which MatchClaims of the block's MatchClaim table are shown: `{timelines, beliefs}`, the claims between two of the listed timelines (`null` for every timeline, a list in code-point order) whose state for the user is one of `beliefs`, a list of belief states, each once, held in the order of `BELIEF_STATES`; `{timelines: null, beliefs: ["true", "undecided", "unlikely"]}` for a block without an entry (`matchClaimFilterOf`); the table itself is never held. A workspace file whose `settings.granularity` does not read as one is read with `DEFAULT_GRANULARITY`, and a `settings.manifests` or `settings.matchClaimFilters` that is missing or no object as none, each of its entries that does not read being left out (a filter written in an earlier shape gives its block the default), since settings lie outside the journal and nothing replayable is lost; every other member is read strictly. A block has an `id`; a `parent`, the canvas group holding it, `null` in this version; an `index`, its order key among the blocks of the same parent; and the inputs of its drawing besides the manifest's own: `x` and `y` (the canvas position of its drawing's place origin), `scale`, `length`, `spacing`, `margin`, `width` and `height` (each `null` for the content's own size), `places`, `arrangement` where its items are arranged ([Arrangements](#arrangements)), and `manifest`. An arrangement that is not there is none: no view holds an `arrangement` of `null`, a `set` of `null` deletes the member and a file stating `null` is read without it ([Commands](#commands)). `emptyWorkspace()` is the empty one. A view's problems, by block, are a block stored under another key, a malformed id, a missing parent or a cycle of parents, a malformed or shared order key, a canvas number that is not finite, a scale, length or size that is not positive or a spacing or margin that is negative, a malformed place or one that names no container (`unknown_place`), and every schema or invariant problem of a block's manifest.

**Manifest files.** `settings.manifests` says how each block's manifest is written to a file, by block id: `{minimal, styling, serialization, location?}` (`ManifestFile`), the minimal or the complete form, with or without the manifest's `graphical` object, and `yaml` (canonical YAML), `json` (pretty JSON) or `jcs` (RFC 8785 JSON); and, where the editor knows it, `location`, where the manifest's file lies: a non-empty POSIX path relative to the workspace file's folder (a workspace without a file counts the server's root as its folder), as a manifest's `resources` locate its files relative to the manifest, refused with `invalid_value` ("expected a relative POSIX path") when it starts with `/`, holds a backslash or names a scheme or a drive. Import stores the entry its file implies ([The header and the File menu](#the-header-and-the-file-menu)), and Include styling and Minimal, in the Manifest tab and in the Bundle tab, change it ([The inspector](#the-inspector)), each through `configure({manifests})`, outside the journal; a manifest imported or located through the server's files adds its `location`, and a Save into another folder rewrites every location relative to it ([The header and the File menu](#the-header-and-the-file-menu)). A block without an entry, one whose manifest came from no file or one of a workspace file that states none, is written minimal, with its styling, in canonical YAML. An entry outlives its block, which undo may bring back.

Sibling order is a fractional index, a string key that compares with ordinary string comparison, so that a block moves between two others by taking a key between theirs (`indexBetween`, `indicesBetween`, `isIndex`), compatible with Rocicorp's `fractional-indexing` 4.0.0 and written in `core` ([decisions/0016](../decisions/0016-fractional-indexing.md)).

### The store and its journal

`createStore(workspace, {author?, clock?})` holds a workspace, `{view, journal, sessions}`, and returns `view`, `journal`, `sessions`, `editing`, `entries`, `listed`, `changes`, `openings` (the count of every `open`), `canUndo`, `canRedo`, `dispatch(command)`, `configure(change)`, `undo()`, `redo()`, `open(target, kind?)`, `done()`, `cancel()`, `snapshot()`, `setAuthor(name)` and `subscribe(listener)`. `dispatch` stamps the command with its author (by default `Gerhardt`; the editor gives it the user's name, [The user](#the-user)) and its time (ISO 8601, UTC, from the injected clock), applies it, runs the side-effect guard while an editor is open, and appends `{command, author, time}`; it returns `null`, or the `CommandError` of a refused command, which leaves the store unchanged.

**The journal is the authority.** Replaying the journal from the empty workspace, named `untitled`, reproduces the workspace. `createStore` verifies this before it returns: it replays the journal to its end and compares the result's `name`, `graphical`, `arrangement` and blocks with the workspace it was given (the settings are no command's and are never compared), and throws `JournalMismatchError {entry, path}` naming the first differing path when they differ, an empty journal beside a non-empty view included, and a file stating a name that no command of its journal gave (`["name"]`). The editor shows that error as it shows a refused file. A journalled `add` of a block carries every member of its placement, the `blockDefaults` it was added with filled in, so replaying never reads the settings. `readWorkspace` does not replay; reading and verifying are two costs ([Performance](#performance)). The decision record is [decisions/0019](../decisions/0019-workspace-store-and-journal.md).

**Settings outside the journal.** `configure(change)` changes any members of the settings, each given in part, at once: it journals nothing, is never undone or redone, and is refused with the first problem of the settings it would leave, at `["settings", …]`. It gives a new view, so the workspace is marked unsaved and autosaved, and every view the store shows afterwards, at any step of undo, carries the settings last configured.

**Undo by steps.** A change is one command at any depth, owned by the innermost session whose own entries hold it, the session of the editor it was made in, or by none for a command of the main journal. A change that the level in view owns itself is a step of its own; a closed session's changes go by runs, a step being a maximal run of consecutive changes owned by one closed session. `undo` reverts the latest applied step, `redo` re-applies the earliest undone one, so redo mirrors undo step for step, and a new command discards the undone changes. So a closed session holding `a1`, then a closed session of `b1` and `b2`, then `a2` is undone in three steps, `a2`, then `b1` and `b2` together, then `a1`. Views share structure, and a level keeps only some of them, since each holds the whole manifest a change made: the views within `RECENT_VIEWS` (64) changes of its current one, so the latest undos and redos step to the very views, the view after every `VIEW_CHECKPOINT`-th (16th) change further away, and the view before its first change; every change of the store lets go of the others (`forgetViews`), and an undo or a redo to one of them replays at most 15 changes from the known view before it (`viewAt`), or the changes from the empty workspace at the start of a loaded journal. A view so replayed is a new object equal to the one let go, so a view is compared with one held elsewhere, the view last saved, by content (`sameView`, which returns at once on the parts two views share): the header's unsaved mark and the embedding's `onchange` status read it.

**Editor sessions.** Every editor is a **session**: the commands made in it between its opening and Done or Cancel. The store keeps one level per open editor above the main journal; the open editors are `editing`, outermost first, each `{session, target, kind, earlier}`: its session id (ten characters from `0-9a-z`), its target (`{block}`, or `{block, id}` of a group or a timeline) and its kind (`EditorKind`: `bundle`, `group` or `timeline`, the one its target opens, `editorKindOf`; any other address has `no_editor`; or `flow`, the flow-control editor of a root's flow control and FlowMaps, asked for by name, `open(target, "flow")`, on a root timeline only, `no_editor` on any other address). `entries`, `listed`, `dispatch`, `undo` and `redo` act on the innermost level: undo inside a session stops at its start.

- `open(target, kind?)` opens an editor; with one open, the target must lie inside the innermost target and not be it (`not_inside`), except that a timeline's children editor and a root's flow-control editor open inside the timeline's own timeline editor too, and nothing opens inside a flow-control editor (`opensIn`); so a root's flow-control editor opens on the arranger, in its block's bundle editor, in the editor of a group holding the root and in the root's timeline editor, and a child's editors open inside the children editor of its parent. When the last entry holding an applied change on that level is the session of the same target and kind, that session is **unfolded**: taken off the level, keeping its id, its changes undoable and redoable again. Otherwise a fresh session opens, and `earlier` names the last session of the same target and kind, listed read-only in the Journal tab.
- `done()` closes the innermost editor; a session holding entries is stored under its id in `sessions`, and `{session, author, time}`, stamped at Done, is appended to the level below. A session left without entries records nothing.
- `cancel()` closes the innermost editor and restores exactly the state at its opening.
- `changes` counts what Cancel would discard: with `o` the changes applied at opening, flattened at every depth, `c` those now and `p` the length of their common prefix, `(o − p) + (c − p)`.
- `snapshot()` returns the workspace as Save writes it, every open editor folded innermost first as Done would fold it, writing only applied changes.
- `edit(target, commands, kind?)` makes several commands one step, atomic: as if the editor of `target` (of `kind` where given, else the one it opens, `editorKindOf`) were opened, the commands dispatched in it in order and Done pressed, journalled as one closed session `{target, kind?, entries}`, so undo and redo take them as one run, after a save and a reload too. Inside an open editor that editor must open there (`not_inside`, as `open`); where it is the innermost open editor itself, the session is one entry of that editor's own session, one step there. A command may be given as a function of what the earlier ones made (`MadeCommand`, the view each left and the IDs each took), so a later command names an element an earlier one created; one command is journalled as itself, admitted by that editor's guard all the same. A refused command, `no_editor` or `not_inside` leaves the store as it was, its ID minter included (a refused `dispatch` mints nothing either), and notifies nobody; an empty list changes nothing. It adds no command and no kind of journal entry. The Flow control tab's Add, a band list's press and the flow-control editor's Place as card use it ([The inspector](#the-inspector)).

### No side effects

An editor changes only what it edits. While an editor is open, `dispatch` compares the view a command would leave with the view before it and refuses the command with `side_effect` ("`<what>` lies outside `<target>`: an editor changes only what it edits", "lie" where `<what>` names several things) at the path of the first difference outside the innermost editor's target, in this order: the workspace's name, its `graphical` or its arrangement of the blocks; another block; for a group or timeline editor, its block's `scale`, `length`, `spacing`, `margin`, `index`, `parent` or `arrangement`; a key of the block's `places` that the target does not contain, except that a member's editor may change its group's `length`, `scale`, `width` and `height` and the `width` of every sibling's card (they are shared, or kept in proportion by a stretch); and the manifest outside the target, compared with the target reduced to its id and with only the style rules that match an element outside it: first each element, then what each timeline and region states apart from it ("the statements of timeline 'clt_1' lie outside timeline 'clt_3'", at `[<key of its address>, "statements"]`), then the manifest's own members, its `statements` and `provenance` among them ("the manifest's statements in bundle 'Mixed' lie outside timeline 'Score'", at `[<block key>, "manifest", "statements"]`), then the members of its `graphical`, its named MatchStamps compared whole, so a group or timeline editor refuses to name, update or remove one; the `@context` is left out, since it follows from the statements compared. So a group or timeline editor changes nothing stated outside its target: it refuses a `set` of its block's own statements, and a removal inside the target that would prune a reference to a removed element from what an element outside it states. The command's change set, the blocks and settings it touched and the paths it wrote, narrows the comparison to them, with the same result as comparing everything: where every path lies inside the target and no change reached past its path, or the manifest is unchanged, the manifest is not compared at all. An entry of `graphical.order` lies inside when the target contains its group or timeline, and a `style` rule selecting bands lies inside when every band it matches belongs to a contained timeline. A block's `x`, `y`, `width` and `height` are the container adapting, never a side effect; removing or renaming the edited element is refused at the target. The bundle editor contains its whole block.

The flow-control editor of a root is checked by what a command aims at, not by the view it leaves, since every command it admits changes members outside the root (`flow_maps`, `virtual_timelines`, `places`): `editorSideEffect` hands it to `flowRefused`, which admits exactly an `add` of a FlowMap into the root, of a Region or a flow-control element into a timeline of the root's tree and of a virtual timeline applying a FlowMap of the root; a `set` of such a FlowMap's `chunks`, `name` and `computed`, of such a virtual timeline's `name` and `place`, and of what core's setters take of a flow-control element of a timeline of the root's tree (its `name`, `label` and `belief`); a `remove` of such a FlowMap, virtual timeline or flow-control element (core refuses the last, `not_removable`: a flow-control element is rejected by a belief, never removed); and a `style` whose `blocks` name the root's block alone, that names no shared style (`name`), and whose selector lists `addresses`, each resolving (`resolveAddress`) to a flow-control element of a timeline of the root's tree or to a part of one (its symbol, fork or label), so the display controls of a selected flow-control element act there, while a band's controls, whose selectors name no addresses, do not; nothing in another block. Anything else is refused with `side_effect` ("the name of timeline 'Score' lies outside the flow-control editor of timeline 'Score': an editor changes only what it edits"), at `[<address key>, <property>]` or `[<address key>]`, a `style` at the first selector address not admitted, else at the root's. A FlowMap lies outside every timeline editor, its root's included (it is compared whole, as a member of the manifest): only the flow-control and bundle editors change FlowMaps. A virtual timeline's card lies inside the editor of a group having it as a member.

What a target contains is `contains(manifest, container, element)`: a timeline contains itself, everything that belongs to it or to a descendant, its bands among them, and their parts, but no ancestor line; a group contains itself, its title and whatever its members contain.

### The workspace file

A workspace is saved as one pretty JSON file, whatever file form its manifests are exported in, by default `<name>.tle.json` after the workspace's name: `{meta, name, settings, graphical, blocks, journal, sessions}`, every manifest embedded whole with bare IDs, never the canvas engine's state, so a text diff of two files shows only real changes. `writeWorkspace(workspace)` writes `{view, journal, sessions}` with TimeToShare's `dumps` in its `json` serialization; Save and the autosave write `writeWorkspace(store.snapshot())`. A journal entry is exactly `{command, author, time}` or `{session, author, time}`, and a session exactly `{target, entries}`, or `{target, kind, entries}` with `kind` `"children"` for a children editor's or `"flow"` for a flow-control editor's, whose target alone would name the timeline editor (any other `kind` is refused with `invalid_value`, "a session states only the kind children or flow"; the version stays 8, every other session being written as before, so every earlier file reads as it did, while an editor that knows only `"flow"` refuses a file holding a children editor's session), so sessions nest; every session is reached exactly once from the journal (`unknown_session`, `unreferenced_session`, `session_referenced_twice`), and one without entries is refused with `invalid_value`. `readWorkspace(text)` reads the file with TimeToShare's lossless `parseJson` and returns the `Workspace` or a `LoadError` listing every problem at its path in the file; a file of another `format` or of a newer `version` (such as "workspace version 9 is not supported; this editor reads version 8 only") is refused for that alone, and so is a file of an older version, written before the journal carried the IDs its commands minted: "workspace version 7 was written before IDs were journaled and must be imported again: import its manifests into a new workspace". `name` and `arrangement` are the members a file may leave out: a file without a name holds a workspace named `untitled`, and an empty or non-string name is refused with `invalid_value`; the workspace's `arrangement` and every block's, written after its `places`, are written only where there is one, so a file without arrangements keeps its bytes, and a stated `null` is read as none. There is no migration. `writeWorkspace(readWorkspace(text))` returns the text it read, byte for byte, for a file that states its name. A block's manifest is a TimeToShare manifest that timetoalign reads with `AlignmentBundle.from_dict`, or with `Timeline.from_dict` or `TimelineGroup.from_dict` when it holds one root or one group.

### Autosave

The editor keeps the last text of a workspace in the browser's IndexedDB, in the slot its `storage` option names ([decisions/0018](../decisions/0018-indexeddb-autosave.md)). Every change is written one second after the last, and a pending write is made at once when the page is hidden or the editor is destroyed; the text is `writeWorkspace`'s, deferred to the write so the command path never serializes. Beside the text the slot holds the name of the workspace's file and its path on the server or, where the browser can store it, the file's handle, so a restored workspace is saved back into its file. One editor at a time writes a slot, holding the Web Lock `tilie-autosave/<slot>`; a second one shows "Autosave of this workspace is active elsewhere, so changes here are not autosaved." When the editor opens and the slot holds anything but an empty new workspace, it asks "Restore the unsaved workspace from …?", with the time of the workspace's last journal entry; Restore opens it under its file's name, with the file's path or handle where one was kept, marked unsaved, and Discard empties the slot. A saved text whose journal is refused shows "The autosaved workspace cannot be restored." with its problem. A failing autosave is reported once, in a message, as an `AutosaveError` whose `code` is `indexeddb_unavailable` or `indexeddb_failed`, and editing goes on.

## Layout and writers

The drawing of a manifest is specified in the schema repository, not here: TimeToShare's [drawing specification](https://github.com/TimeToAlign/TimeToShare/blob/main/docs/drawing.md) defines the `graphical` object, arithmetic, default styles, font, layout, element descriptors, page, and SVG and TikZ profiles completely enough that another implementation writes the same text byte for byte. The specification, together with the SVG and TikZ fixtures of a schema version, pins the drawing; there is no drawing schema, and a drawing's JSON form is internal.

### The drawing

`@timetoalign/tilie-layout` exports `draw(manifest, placement, layers, metrics)`, a pure deterministic function implementing sections 1 to 8 of the specification for every manifest. The placement supplies the graphical length of every item without one of its own, the block's spacing (the gap between its items at their default `y`) and margin (the empty space above its first item and below its last), the requested block size and the stated places; the layers are the ordered `Graphical` objects of the cascade, from lowest to highest precedence: the specification's defaults, the workspace's `graphical`, and the manifest's own; the metrics are the one advance-width table of the two bundled drawing fonts, DejaVu Sans and Noto Music. The returned `Drawing` holds:

- `width`, `height` and `origin`, the block-local position of the place origin `(Ox, Oy)`;
- `elements`, every drawn element in descriptor order;
- `boxes`, every container as a `DrawnBox`, the block first, then in drawing order: its `kind`, where its place lies in `places`, its `address`, where its holder's place lies (`null` for the block and what the block holds directly), its rectangle in the drawing's coordinates, `displayed` (whether its own rectangle is drawn: always for the block, never for a band), `ancestor {group, member, groups}` for an ancestor card, and `range`, the half-open range of `elements` it emitted (a frame or card its own rectangle when displayed, then everything drawn as part of it; a band none). An item carries its place with the defaults applied and as stated; a card its drawn size before the scale, its spacing (the gap between its rows and between its bands: a member's card's own, a block-held card's its item's, else 4), its margin (likewise, else 0), its `contentCount`, how many contents it holds (its root's row, its root's present bands and its ancestor bands, one on each side holding an ancestor line) and its stated `width`, `height`, `spacing` and `margin`; a band its timeline, facet, signed **level** (1, 2, … above its timeline's line, −1, −2, … below), the number of displayed elements it holds directly, and `line`, the ordinate of its timeline's line, which tells apart the records of a timeline drawn twice. Only a band that draws an element is recorded, on a timeline whose line is displayed;
- `axes`, one `DrawnAxis` per line drawn, or, on a card whose axis has gaps, one per run of the line between them (the stretches drawn at full scale), `run` (the run's number from 0) set from the second run on, the runs of a line sharing its address, and `lineAxes` keying the later ones with `#` and the run's number: the timeline's address, the place key of the `card` whose member drawing draws it, its `line`, the abscissas `xStart` and `xEnd` of its semantic start and end (not of a circle, chevron or end mark; an ancestor line clipped to its member; the reach's where the end is not known), its `unit` and `numberType`, the exact own-axis values `start` and `end` there (`end` null where the length is not known), its `segments` when it is a segment line (`{id, offset, length}` on the segment line's own axis, in canonical order, a length the end section 3.4 gives where the segment's own is not known), its `maps`, the maps drawn at its line, its own and its segments', in the order they are drawn (`{id, owner, xs, xe, yc}`: the map's ID, the ID of the timeline it spans, the abscissas of its ends and the ordinate of its centre; none for an ancestor line), its `mark`, on an ancestor line the wedge an editor shows at a position on it, at the abscissa 0 (`AxisWedge`: its tip ordinate, the corners of its base and its style), drawn as a named timestamp's wedge on that line is, on the side of the line away from the member, in the style of a wedge held by the ancestor line that no named timestamp holds, `null` on every other line, `ancestor {group, member}` on a line of an ancestor card, as its `DrawnBox` carries it (the ancestor cards' lines come after every item's, so a timeline drawn in a card of an item has its first line there), and its `marker` (`null` for an ancestor line, which draws none): where a coordinate marker of the line lies at any abscissa: the wedge's tip ordinate and the two corners of its base (`WedgeCorner`, defined in `rotation.ts`: an offset across from the tip and an ordinate) from where its markers band starts (or would start), the cross-section line's extent and style (`crossSection`: `span`, the top and bottom ordinates of the card drawing the line, which every cross-section line of the line crosses, `gap`, the stretch of it that a line leaves where the wedge at the abscissa 0 and its label stack stand, `null` where they leave none, and the style of one that no named timestamp holds), and `descendants`, the IDs of the displayed descendants that line crosses; and the named timestamps the line holds (`AxisTimestamp`: its ID, the abscissa of its cross-section line, that line's style, `null` where its `display` does not list it, and `segment`, the card's top and bottom ordinates, the span its cross-section segment covers, gap included). The list holds primary coordinates only; secondary coordinates are drawn from core's `cardStamps` (below). The cross-section line of a named timestamp of a timeline `T` crosses the whole card drawing `T`'s line, from its top border to its bottom border (ancestor lines and the other timelines of the card included), leaving a gap only where its own wedge and its label stack stand, so it is at most two pieces. One extent function serves both readers: `crossSection(drawing, lineKey, x)` gives those pieces (`{x, y1, y2, style}[]`) at the abscissa `x`, none where the key names no line or an ancestor line, and `crossedLines(drawing, lineKey)` the lines it crosses, as keys of `lineAxes`, the line's own first, then its timeline's displayed descendants with a line of their own in the same member drawing in traversal order (a segment without a line of its own lies on its segment line's axis), so the line drawn and the timestamp read can never disagree. `wedgeAt(drawing, lineKey, x)` gives the wedge (`{points, style}`) a named timestamp of that line's timeline would draw at `x`, turned by the `rotation` a rule on the band's wedges gives (never one naming addresses), and on an ancestor line its `mark` at `x`, so no rule naming an address changes it, and `groupCrossSections(drawing, lineKey, x)` the pieces a groupstamp lying there would draw across the frame holding the line's card, those of `groupLinePieces` (none for a card no frame holds); all three carry the style of one that no rule addresses, so the canvas marks an unnamed position with the drawing's own geometry (`wedgePoints` in `geometry.ts`); `lineAxes(drawing)` (`axes.ts`) gives the axes to a hit test as `LineAxis` records (the place key of the card drawing it, start, length, segments, maps and, on a line of an ancestor card, `ancestor`), in the order of `axes`, keyed by the card's place key text followed by the timeline's address key, so a timeline drawn in two cards has two entries;
- `elisions`, one `ElisionExposure` per container that decides elisions, an ungrouped root's card, a displayed ancestor card or a frame ([Elisions](#elisions)): its kind, place key and address; its cascaded `elision-mode` and the mode it draws by (`drawn`, `auto` resolved, `once` for a root drawn once behind a row); for an ancestor card the proportion its derived gaps take, whether it is computed (`automatic`), its member's ancestors from the root (`chain`), the depths chosen of them and the `display` of its derived gaps; its gaps from the left, each an `ExposedGap`: the elision's address, its `origin` (`stored`, `default` for a derived gap, `carried` for a group elision's gap in an ancestor card, `arrangement` for a gap that a root drawn once behind a row cuts between two groups, which has a derived gap's address; [Arrangements](#arrangements)), its timeline and elided coordinates, exact on that timeline's own axis, its natural and drawn widths, its edges in block coordinates and, across a frame, its elided coordinates on every other timeline the member cards draw there (`members`, without a value where the group gives none); its runs (`Run {u0, u1, L, D}`, the stretches of proportions drawn at full scale, each with its own scale `L` and shrink `D`); `x0`, where its member drawings start, and `place`, where its item lies, so that a proportion `u` of a run lies at `x = place.x + place.scale × (x0 + L × u − D)`. An application reads every mode, proportion and gap there, never from a drawing tried with another rule;
- `arrangement`, only for an arranged block (`DrawnArrangement`, [Arrangements](#arrangements)): its `direction`, its `row`, the items in the order they are drawn in (for `horizontal` the row), its `roots`, the shared roots drawn once, line by line from the top and on each line from the left (none for `vertical`), and `refused`, where `horizontal` is refused, the conflict (`ArrangementConflict {groups, roots, reason}`), the items then drawn one under another;
- `unmapped`, the code points neither bundled font maps, measured with the table's fallback advance.

Every manifest is drawn the same way: a grey block that opens with the manifest's title (its `name`, else its whole `id`; an anonymous bundle has none); each group a frame, a rounded rectangle holding the group's title and its members' cards one under the other in display order; each timeline drawn as a root in a card, an ungrouped root in one of its own and a member in one in the frame of each group that has it, a raised rounded rectangle with a shadow offset to the right and down. The block has one horizontal layout: every item starts its lines after one name column and ends them with one right margin, and the members of a group share their frame's graphical length and scale. An item's scale scales everything in it about its top left corner. Every container's `spacing` is the gap between its own contents, finite and at least 0, and multiplies no other: the block's between its items at their default `y` (8 by default), a frame's between its cards (8), a card's between its rows and between its bands (4). Beside it every container has a `margin` (0 by default, finite and at least 0, likewise its own): empty space inside its padding above its first content and below its last, the same above and below, which makes the container twice the margin higher and moves its contents down by the margin. The title lies at the block's top left corner, above every item; an item placed higher grows the block upward. Within its card a timeline is drawn with its line and its five bands, stacked in its band order; a band is **present**, and takes room, where it draws an element or is **shown explicitly** (section 6.4, Empty bands): the rule that sets its `display`, the last in rank order matching it and stating one, sets `inline` and names `addresses` or `within`, as the rule a band control writes does (`Cascade.resolveBand`, which reports it while it resolves the band; `Entry.explicit`); an empty band shown explicitly is drawn at least `Wb = BRACE + LABEL_GAP + h(9)` wide (`emptyBandWidth`, 18.4765625 with the bundled metrics), everything beyond it moving out by `Wb` and the spacing, and is the hover and double-click target of its room as every band is, while a band displayed by the initial value alone that draws nothing is absent; each child is drawn whole in a row of its parent's children band, and the children bands of the lines of one row are laid out together. They are **aligned** when every child they place has one band order: each child is then placed by its line extent alone (its line with the lines of its descendants), so it keeps its row whatever its labels, and the lines of that row lay out their bands together facet by facet, the bands of one facet at one level, from one start and as wide as the widest, the markers stacks, the regions with their label tiers and the segment labels each placed by one first fit over the whole row (`children.ts`, `rows.ts`); children bands that are not aligned place each child by its whole extent, its labels included, found from its subtree laid out alone, before its parent's band is fitted. The defaults show the regions band of every timeline, a child's included, but hide a region typed as an atomic section: a selector's `class` also matches the classes a region's `type` statement names (core's `regionClasses`), and the default layer hides `{element: region, class: "tts:AtomicSection"}`. A region or a child keeps its row whatever its label's width: a label that does not fit beside its neighbour moves to a further label tier of its row and is joined to the middle of its element by a thin grey `leader` line (`fit.ts`, `tieredFit`, one rule for region labels, segment labels and the labels of flow control). Flow control holds across a root's whole tree, so only the root of a member drawing draws a flow control band, and it draws the flow-control elements of the root and of every descendant, each at its position on the root's axis; each element stays, for the cascade, in the flow control band of the timeline that states it, so hiding a child's line or flow control band hides that child's elements, and hiding the root's flow control band hides the flow control of its whole tree in that card, the band being then absent. A member that is a child timeline draws in its card's band the elements of its top-level root's tree lying on its stretch, `[0, λ]` of its own axis (core's `memberFlow`), and an arc there only where its target lies on that stretch too. The flow control band (`layout/flow.ts`, sections 6.4 and 6.6) holds, from its start outwards: the flow label tiers, each element's **stack** (the labels its cascaded `label-display` lists of its name, its ID and its own label, initially `name label`, so that naming an element shows its name) placed by first fit; the flow row, where the flow lines end; the symbol row, 2 beyond the flow row so that no glyph meets a line, each element's symbol centred at its position (core's `flowSymbols`: the Noto Music glyph of its manifestation, a jump's destinations' glyphs side by side, 𝄂 for a section break, the text "Fine" for a `fine` break); the fork row, one volta fork per jump with `volta` destinations, of one size whatever the scale: a stem into the jump's position, then one arrow per such destination in the order of their places, the first straight on and the k-th of N bent away from the line by min(35°, 70°/(N − 1)) × (k − 1), each with its passes printed beside its tip (core's `passesText`); and the jump rows of the arcs, one per destination whose distance is not 0 and that is no `volta` destination, labelled with that destination's `label` alone. The defaults hide the arcs (`jump` `display: none`) and show the symbols, the forks and the flow lines: a break red and dotted, a section break dark blue-grey and dashed with its 𝄂, a barline dark grey, each a vertical line from the flow row across the card. Symbols, forks and leaders are element types of their own (`symbol`, `fork`, `leader`), addressed by the part words of the same names. `draw` takes the reading in force of flow control (`FlowReading` or `null`; the editor passes its user's, `readingOf`): an element it does not read is drawn faded, everything it draws with it, and keeps its place in the rows; a section break it does not read starts no section, and a `to` naming a jump target it does not read is no place, so no arc or branch goes there. Without a reading, these are the elements some actor believes `false`, the rejected ones. No root draws a mark of its start or end. A named timestamp is drawn by its timeline, at any depth, as its `display` lists: a wedge in that timeline's markers band; up to three labels stacked outward beside the wedge, its name, its ID and its coordinate with its unit, in that order (core's `TIMESTAMP_LABELS`), each a `label` element; and a dashed light-blue cross-section line held by its timeline's line across the whole card drawing it, from the card's top border to its bottom border, cut only where its own wedge and label stack stand, so hiding the markers band keeps the line; a timeline drawn in several cards draws its timestamps in each. A timestamp named on a child or on an ancestor is drawn the same way: its timeline decides only where its primary coordinate's wedge and labels stand. A **secondary coordinate** of a named timestamp (a coordinate on another timeline of the card it crosses: the timeline's own, its ancestors' and its descendants' among them) is drawn as the primary one is, by the wedge and labels its `display` lists, in that timeline's markers band or beside its ancestor line, its `coordinate` label written in that timeline's number type; the default rules hide every element of one and turn its wedge −45, and a rule selecting `coordinates: secondary` shows them (core's `cardStamps` lists them per card and timeline, `CardStamps.secondaries`; layout's `markers.ts` and `ancestors.ts` read that one list, so a hidden one takes no room). A wedge turns about its tip by its style's `rotation` (`Style.rotation`, section 6.6), a whole number of degrees from −180 to 180 that rules set and that is never inherited, mirrored with the side of the line its band lies on; where no rule sets it, every wedge turns 0, a groupstamp's included; a turned wedge carries its stack of labels along its own axis, and the stack's place and the gap it leaves in the cross-section line follow from the rotation (`stacks.ts`). `MarkerElement` carries the tip and the two corners of the base (`corners`), which `rotation.ts` computes from the sine and cosine of the rotation, each rounded to four decimal places from one table of the sines of 0 to 90 degrees (`sineCosine`, `wedgeCorners`), so every unturned wedge keeps its exact numbers; a turned wedge widens its timeline's extent to its farthest corner (`wedgeSpan`). A **groupstamp**, a named timestamp with `groupstamp: true` on a group's member, draws after every item, before the alignment anchors, one dashed line per frame holding a card that draws its timestamp, at its abscissa, from the upper border of the frame's topmost member card to the lower border of its bottommost, less the card its own cross-section line crosses (`groupstamps.ts`: `frameMemberLines`, the one statement of a frame's member lines; `groupSpans`; `groupstampElements`); each piece is a `cross_section` at the timestamp's line address `{id, part: ["line"]}` in that line's style, and takes no room. The other members' coordinates derive from the group and are never stored; on each member card the line crosses they are the timestamp's secondary coordinates, which a rule showing `coordinates: secondary` draws with their wedges and labels. A group reads a position across its members by the proportion of their drawn lengths, and only where both lengths are known: over the card of a member of unknown length the group gives no coordinate, so that piece of the line is drawn faded (`groupLinePieces`, the one rule of the frame's line: the pieces of the frame spans cut at the borders of such cards, each in the line's style or, over such a card, with the opacity 0.35 in place of the one cascaded; whole and faded where the timestamp's own member is of unknown length), the secondary wedge there is faded and has no coordinate label, and a coordinate listed for it has no value. A match timestamp draws, by default, its cross-section line alone, and no name label without a name. Each alignment anchor of a named MatchStamp is drawn after every item and takes no room (`anchors.ts`, section 8.7): where its `display` lists `line`, the shortest line between the cross-section lines of its two match timestamps, never one drawn in an ancestor card (section 8.8; `timestampSegments`) (horizontal at the middle of their vertical overlap where they overlap, else between their nearer ends), the element `alignment_anchor` `{id: A}` held by the block; where it lists `name`, the MatchStamp's name centred on that line's midpoint, a `label` `{id: A, part: ["name"]}` held by the line. Core's `manifestElements` lists them after every item, after one `cross_section` per groupstamp and frame holding it (held by the frame), so a style rule addresses each as any other element. A segment line draws its segments on its own line, separated by boundaries. A top-level timeline ends with an arrowhead (open for a segment line) or a filled circle when locked; a child ends with an open diamond. A root of unknown length is scaled to its **reach**, the farthest coordinate its content reaches, and a line whose end is open runs on into three chevrons. A member that is a child also draws its ancestors beside its line as **ancestor lines**, each on the side opposite to where the ancestor's own band order puts its children band (below the member by default), in the outermost band of that side, its ancestor band, clipped to the member, running on into a double chevron where the ancestor reaches beyond it; a named timestamp of an ancestor whose position lies within the member's span, its ends included, and a secondary coordinate on that ancestor's line are drawn beside the ancestor line, the timestamp at full opacity and a secondary coordinate faded as the line is; an ancestor line draws no mark of the member's start or end, which the member's `origin` and `length` groupstamps show instead ([Commands](#commands)); unless a rule hides it, its root is drawn whole as an ancestor card behind the group's frame, after the title and before the first frame (`block/ancestors.ts`, section 8.8; [The container hierarchy](#the-container-hierarchy)). `display: none` removes an element from the drawing and its layout; section 2.4 states every way a hidden element hides others. A manifest without timelines or groups, such as one holding only the statements about a work, is imported like any other and draws its block with its title alone; links are never drawn.

### Elisions

Elisions are drawn as the specification's sections 3.2, 6.6 and 8.9 state, by `elisions.ts` with `block/ancestors.ts` and `block/places.ts`:

- **Card and group elisions.** Whether an elision is a group elision is read from its timeline and never stored. One whose timeline lies in a group (the timeline, a virtual timeline included, or one of its ancestors is a member: core's `groupsHolding` over `memberAncestry`, as for a groupstamp) belongs to that group's frame, to each frame where the timeline lies in several groups; every other one is a **card elision** of its timeline's root, drawn in the cards that draw that root whole, the root's own card where it is ungrouped and its ancestor cards, never in a member's card; an elision on a virtual timeline that no group holds is a card elision of its placed card, the root it is drawn as ([Virtual and switched cards](#virtual-and-switched-cards)). Core lists them without drawing anything: `elisionsOn(manifest, T)` the stored elisions on a timeline, `elisionsUnder(manifest, R)` the card elisions of a root's tree, or those on a virtual timeline that no group holds, `groupElisions(manifest, G)` a group's, those on its virtual members included.
- **Modes are style properties** ([decisions/0035](../decisions/0035-elision-modes-are-style-properties.md)). Four properties of the cascade, never inherited, decide what a container draws: `elision-mode` (`auto`, `full`, `proportional` or `custom`, initially `custom`, so by default no card derives gaps and no ancestor card is shortened), read by a card, an ancestor card and a frame, and, read by a card and an ancestor card, `elision-proportion` (`auto`, or a number above 0 and at most 1), `elision-depths` (`auto`, or the depths chosen) and `elision-display` (what every derived gap shows, initially `ellipsis name`). Every card of a root `R` carries the address `{id: R, part: ["card"]}`, so one rule there sets the mode of all of them, beside the `display` that hides its ancestor cards; a frame's rule is addressed `{id: G}`. `full` draws no card elision, `custom` the stored ones, `proportional` the gaps an ancestor card derives, and `auto` the stored ones where the root's tree holds any and the derived ones otherwise. A group elision never counts and is never switched by a card's mode; a frame draws its group elisions under `auto` and `custom`, none under `full` and `proportional`.
- **Derived gaps.** An ancestor card drawing `proportional` derives gaps where it lies outside its member's span, its **protected span**: on the root and on each ancestor of the member down to its parent with its siblings, one depth each, on the depths chosen, deepest first, each stretch one piece whose middle is left out, its elided coordinates rounded outwards to whole units and its two ends kept at full scale. The pieces are drawn at the proportion `r` of their natural widths: the stated one, or, for `auto`, the largest at which they fit beside the member's drawn line and the card fits in the view centred on the frame (`ancestorProportion`), the one value of a drawing that reads the placement's `view`. A derived gap has no ID; its address is `{id: T, part: ["elision"]}` on its timeline `T`.
- **The elided axis.** A container that draws gaps has an axis linear run by run (`CardAxis`, one builder for cards and frames): each piece of a stored elision is one gap, of its natural width or of the width its `width` gives (`widthAs` writes a drawn width in each stored form exactly), a piece clipped where an earlier elision or the protected span took the stretch, and a stored elision not drawn takes nothing. A group elision's stretch lies over proportions of its member's length, so its gap lies at one x in every member card, and the frame is drawn shorter by the sum of its gaps' shrinks; each ancestor card behind the frame carries those gaps over its protected span with the frame's widths (`carriedGaps`), exact on an exact axis of the root. An ancestor card's axis also has two **seams**, where its member's span meets the frame's borders (`frameRoom`): jumps that leave nothing out and draw nothing, a row crossing one drawn whole, so that what lies outside the member's span is drawn outside the frame; a root drawn once behind a row of groups side by side has more, between its groups ([Arrangements](#arrangements)). A member card's ancestor rows lie at the heights of the same rows in its ancestor card (`alignedRows`).
- **What a gap draws.** Every row crossing a gap stops at its edge and resumes after it (`cuts` on lines, maps and region braces). The gap itself is an `elision` element, an invisible extent from the card's top border to its bottom border, or across the frame's member cards, which a rule may fill; it holds exactly one `ellipsis`, centred in the gap and on the card or the member cards together (a stored elision's gap, drawn in its card or carried, whatever its width; a derived or an arrangement's gap only where it is at least 20 units wide), its continuations where its `display` lists `continuation`, and its labels, name, ID and elided coordinates, centred below the ellipsis. The labels' room is a first-fit stack in the markers band of the elision's timeline, after the named timestamps' stacks, so labels shown may move rows; where the labels of neighbouring gaps would touch or overlap, a stack takes the next tier of the band, as a named timestamp's labels do, and a gap never widens and no proportion changes for a label. A carried gap draws its ellipsis and no label.

### Arrangements

An **arrangement** places a block's items, its groups' frames and its ungrouped roots' cards, in an order instead of at their places, as the specification's sections 1, 8.5, 8.6 and 8.8 (Drawn once) state. It is a member of the placement, `Placement.arrangement`, beside `places`: it says where the drawing puts things and is never part of the manifest, so the block stores it (`Block.arrangement`) and any program passing the same placement draws the same ([decisions/0036](../decisions/0036-arrangements-are-placement.md)). An arranged item takes no `x` and no `y` from `places`, which keeps them for when the block is free again; every other member of its place applies.

- **The item order** is core's `itemOrder(manifest, order)`: the items the stored order names, each at its first mention, an ID naming no item passed over, then every item it leaves out, in drawing order, which is name order (core's `compareByName`: a group's title or a timeline's name, else its ID, compared by code point, then by ID; groups first, then the ungrouped roots, then the placed virtual timelines, `ungroupedVirtuals`). `vertical` lays the items out one under another in that order, each at its default `x` and `y` (`placeItems` given the order), their ancestor cards behind them as in a free block, recorded and written in the same order.
- **Side by side**, core decides what stands where (`manifest/arrangement.ts`, exported from `/drawing`). A group **hangs on** a root when one of its members lies inside that root, a child or deeper; its first such member in its display order is its **anchor** there, the member its ancestor card is aligned to, and the anchor's **stretch** runs on the root's axis from its start, the offsets down to it summed, to its end as drawn (section 3.4), exact (`anchorsOf`). A **shared root** is one that two or more groups hang on; a root one group hangs on is none, its ancestor card staying behind that group's frame, bound by no rule. Along every shared root the stretches of the groups hanging on it follow the row from left to right without overlapping, touching allowed, and in every group the anchors on shared roots lie in the top-to-bottom order of their roots' lines. `rowOf(manifest, order)` gives the `Row {row, roots, above, anchors}`, the row keeping the given order wherever the stretches leave it free, `roots` the shared roots in the order they take their lines (starting from the place in the row of the first group hanging on each, ties in name order), `above` the `[upper, lower]` pairs of shared roots some group's anchors order, and `anchors` those on shared roots; or the `ArrangementConflict {groups, roots, reason}`, the first found of: two stretches overlapping on a shared root (the first root in name order), the row in a cycle, or the roots in a cycle (reported as found in name order), worded as the specification words them (`"Theme A" and "Theme B" overlap on "Score" (8–16 ♩)`, `"G" holds "R" above "S", "H" holds "S" above "R"`). An ungrouped root, or a group no member of which lies inside a root, hangs on nothing and keeps its place in the order. `anchorChange(manifest, group, order)` says why a display order would give a group another anchor on a shared root ([Arrangements on the canvas](#arrangements-on-the-canvas)).
- **The row** (`block/arrangement.ts`, `rowLayout`): the items packed from the left in the row by their **row extents** (an item's drawn width and the displayed ancestor cards of roots one group hangs on that reach beyond it), the **row gap** `max(S, 48)` apart (`S` the block's spacing; the standard gap and 8 on either side of it); an item's `x` is its extent's start plus its left overhang. A root one group hangs on keeps its ancestor card behind that group's frame, aligned to the frame's rows and moving with it, as in a block not arranged. Every shared root is drawn once, by its ancestor card behind the first group of the row hanging on it, the **once card**, whose `DrawnBox.ancestor.groups` lists every group hanging on it; that root's ancestor cards behind the other groups draw nothing and are not recorded. The once cards lie on **lines**: each takes the highest line below the lines of the roots `above` names where every extent on that line (from its left edge to its right edge plus twice its shadow) lies `S` apart, so several roots stand side by side on one line where they do not overlap along the row, and the lines lie one under another, their cards never overlapping, each low enough that every group can lie on all of its roots: in a group anchoring on several roots, each later anchor's card is moved down, the gap above it grown, until it lies on its root's line, no card changing its size. Line ordinates and padding are computed line by line. The highest frame, card, once card or ancestor card lies where the first item would (`top`); where no item hangs on a root, every item has its top there. The row is packed again further right where a once card would lie left of the row's start `P`. Once cards are written line by line, left to right, then the ancestor cards of roots one group hangs on, frame by frame in the row, and `Drawing.arrangement.roots` lists the shared roots line by line. A once card that a rule hides draws nothing and keeps its place, so hiding it moves nothing.
- **Runs, gaps and seams.** A once card's line is drawn in runs, over each anchor's stretch at the scale of that anchor's frame, the run continuing to either side of the stretch. Between two anchors next to each other along the root, the scale changes in the room right of the left anchor's frame: in a gap of origin `arrangement` where whole units of the root's axis fit there, at least the standard 32 wide, its elided coordinates rounded outwards to whole units, drawn as every gap is with the card's `elision-display` and addressed as a derived gap `{id: R, part: ["elision"]}`; else at a **seam** (`PlacedSeam`), a jump of the axis that leaves out nothing, has no address, draws nothing and is listed in no exposure, across which a row crossing it is drawn whole. A timeline starting at a seam starts on the run right of it, one ending there ends on the run left of it. Left of the first stretch and right of the last the root runs on outwards from the outer frames' edges, cut by a start gap or an end gap 32 wide where more than 32 of it lies beyond the whole unit within 8 of that edge, so that the card ends at most 40 past its outer frames. A once card draws no card elision and derives none, whatever its mode (its exposure says `drawn: "once"`, proportion 1); stored card elisions of the root are kept and not drawn there. On an exact axis of the root every position is exact.
- **Refusal.** Where `rowOf` refuses the row, the block is drawn `vertical` in the item order, and the drawing states the conflict (`Drawing.arrangement.refused`); nothing else refuses an arrangement.

Packing is one function, layout's `packed(sizes, {direction, gap, start, columns?})` (`arrangement.ts`): `vertical` left-aligned, each content `gap` below the one before; `horizontal` top-aligned, each `gap` right of the one before; `matrix` row by row in `columns` columns, each column as wide as its widest content and each row as high as its highest, each content at its cell's top left. The row of a block is packed with it, and so are the blocks of an arranged canvas ([Arrangements on the canvas](#arrangements-on-the-canvas)).

### Virtual and switched cards

Section 8.10 of the drawing specification draws FlowMaps in two ways, both drawing and both exported ([FlowMaps and virtual timelines](#flowmaps-and-virtual-timelines)).

A **virtual card** draws a virtual timeline (`layout/virtual.ts`). `drawnRoots(manifest, places)` adds to the roots drawn the virtual members of every frame, in its display order (core's `withVirtualMembers`), and the placed virtual timelines, in no group and named by `places`, in name order after the ungrouped roots; each is drawn as the locked root core reads it as (`virtualDrawing`, its line opening with the start circle and, its end fixed by its map, ending in the end circle of a locked root), named with the suffix ` · unfolded from <timeline>` (` · converted from <timeline>` for a conversion map; `cardName`), holding no children, conversion maps, regions or flow control of its own and no ancestors: its five bands draw what its chunks' windows join to them (below) besides its own named timestamps. Its line is a **segment line** of its FlowMap's chunks (`flowOf`, through core's `resolveFlowMap` and its `runs`, carried on the scope as `Scope.flow`, a `DrawnFlow` of `DrawnChunk`s; `line.ts`): a chunk `{id: V, part: [k]}` draws nothing of its own and is found on its drawn span of the line, by its label and by its boundary; its label `[k, "label"]`, the name of its source, else its whole ID, for a gap the pretty text of its stated length, stands above the line in the line's segment-label tiers, before the labels of its segments, with its leader `[k, "leader"]` where it lies in a further tier; a run of `n` consecutive chunks of one source is drawn once, labelled ` × n`, in a block-held card only (a frame draws each chunk). Its line carries a boundary `[k, "start"]` at the start of every drawn chunk but the first, and a **gap line** over each gap (`LineElement.gapLines`), stroked in the grey `#9CA3AF` (layout's `GAP_LINE_STROKE`, the grey of an elision's continuations and of leaders) with the line's own `stroke-width`, `stroke-dasharray` and `opacity`, so solid on a continuous line and dotted on a discrete one, the line's `stroke` never reaching it, which the writers write after the line's pieces and before its marks. A run drawn once leaves a **run gap** `[a, b]` of drawn width 0 (`withRuns`, `CardAxis.runGaps`): a proportion `u` at or after `b` lies at `x(a) + (L × u − L × ub)`, so `x(b) = x(a)` exactly, and the line ends `D` short of `x0 + L`; the run gap is no elision, draws nothing and cuts no row, and the axes leave it out as they leave out an elision's gap (`block/axes.ts`), so a line drawn with a run once has one `DrawnAxis` per run. In a block-held card a gap entry whose natural width is less than the standard gap, 32 (`STANDARD_GAP`), is a **widened gap**, drawn 32 wide, every position after it lying the widening further right (`flowAxis`), so the practice card's 4 ♩ pause is drawn 32 wide; a gap entry or a run gap that a stored elision the card draws reaches into keeps its natural width or is drawn entry by entry, so an elision is never swallowed by a run. **Windows** (`traversal.ts`, from core's `windowsOf`): every chunk of a virtual card that is no gap holds a window, five bands `{id: V, facet: F, part: [k]}`, each holding what the chunk's stretch pieces show of their sources of that facet (their children with everything they hold, regions, maps, named timestamps, flow-control elements; never the sources' elisions, their ancestors' content, or the region a piece plays), unfolded over the chunk's pieces as a switched card unfolds its bands; every drawing keeps its source's address, so selecting it selects it on the source's card and in every window, and an edit of it is an edit of the source. The default layer hides every band inside a chunk (`{element: band, inside: chunk}`, `display: none`), so a virtual card is drawn stripped down, its line, chunks and own named timestamps alone; one rule per facet, `{facet: F, inside: chunk, within: V}` `display: inline`, shows that band of every chunk's window and of everything the windows show (the band list's **From the sources**, [The inspector](#the-inspector)). A placed virtual card that no group holds draws the elisions stored on its virtual timeline as card elisions on its played axis ([Elisions](#elisions)): with run gaps its axis is `runAxisOf`'s, a proportion `u` at or after a run gap `[a, b]` lying at `x(a) + (L × u − L × ub − D)`, `D` the shrinks of the elision gaps between the run gap's end and `u` (0 without elisions), and an elision lying wholly in a run gap draws no gap (`storedPieces` takes the run gaps as already taken); an elision's name label reads the virtual timeline's own name, without the card's suffix. An elision on a virtual member of a group is a group elision of each frame holding it, drawn across the frame on that member's played axis and carried to every other member's, as any group elision. A virtual timeline that does not resolve (a `FlowMapError`, a stated `through`, a timeline other than its FlowMap's root) draws its line alone.

A **switched card** draws a root `T` on the played axis of the FlowMap its `flow` names (core's `switchOf`; [Commands](#commands)), named `T · <name of what flow names>`, locked, from 0 to `Λ`. `traversal.ts` gives the root entry one **piece** entry per stretch piece (`rootPieces`, from core's `unfoldingOf`), each holding `T`'s children clipped to the piece (`Entry.written`, `piece`, `startCut`, `endCut`), and the scope maps positions through `Scope.unfolding`, `at`, `over` and `play`, the identity on a card drawn as written. The line draws the chunks as a virtual card does, addressed with `T`'s ID; regions, children and named timestamps are drawn at each of their played positions (core's `playedAt`, `playedSpan`), a coordinate label writing the written value; a segment cut at a piece's start draws no boundary there and a child line cut at a piece's end no mark; a row names a timeline once (`names.ts` `lineNames`). The card keeps the band configuration of the card drawn as written, every band displayed or hidden and ordered as there: a conversion map is drawn once per piece over its timeline's extent, clipped (`maps.ts`); a flow-control element, with its flow line, symbol, fork and stack, at every played position of its coordinate (`flow.ts` `gathered`, through `playedAt`), an arc only in a piece holding both its from and its to, a jump elsewhere drawing its symbol, fork and stack without an arc; a segment boundary at every played position of its start, a piece's closed end included (`line.ts` `unfoldedBoundaries`); and no elision on any timeline of `T`'s tree. In a frame where exactly one member's card is switched, every other member is **carried** onto that axis through the group (`t = m × λT / λM`, exact, else binary64 with the product first) and drawn once per piece (`line.ts` `drawnEntries`; a virtual member's chunks unfolded, `chunkSpans`), a child member's ancestor lines too, each once per piece it reaches (`ancestors.ts`, `AncestorPiece`), its chevrons and end marks only at its real ends, its timestamps and the flow control of its root's tree at every played position; the frame draws no group elision, and two switched members leave it drawn as written. `block/axes.ts` `unfoldedAxes` gives one `DrawnAxis` per drawn piece (`run` numbering the later ones), read from `MemberDrawn.entries`, `MemberDrawn.ancestorAxes` and `LineMarker.entry`, each named timestamp on the first piece reaching it; an unfolded axis carries no `segments` and no `maps`. Layout's `switched.ts` holds only `unfoldedRoot`, the root the switched card draws; every rule of what is switched and where a position is played is core's.

### The pipeline

`draw` (`block.ts`) orchestrates four stages over one item model, in which a frame with its cards and a block-held card (a frame-less item with one card) take one code path: the places of every item, stated or default, and the block's place origin (`block/places.ts`); the block's one horizontal layout over all its items (`block/horizontal.ts`); each item laid out in its own coordinates (`block/items.ts`); and the shift into the block with the `Drawing`'s records (`block/emit.ts`, with the axes from `block/axes.ts`). A member drawing (`member.ts`) takes one timeline as the root: `traversal.ts` walks its timelines once into an index of children by parent and sources by line, fixes the axis (the length, or the reach, memoized per drawing) and every timeline's end; `timeline.ts` lays out every line with its bands, one module per kind of band filling them (`markers.ts`, `maps.ts`, `children.ts`, `regions.ts`, `flow.ts`); `rotation.ts` turns a wedge's corners; `stacks.ts` places the label stacks carried by turned wedges and the gaps they leave in cross-section lines (markers band and ancestor lines share it); `groupstamps.ts` draws the groupstamps' lines across their frames from the drawn lines and boxes, after every item, its `groupLinePieces` the one rule of a frame line's pieces (shared with the hovered and pinned marks, `groupCrossSections`); `slots.ts` (`rowSlots`) gives, after the first fits and from the rows alone, the conversion maps bands of every row that are laid out together their shared slots, which `mapSlots` (`maps.ts`) finds by key and first fit; `rows.ts` lays out the rows of children bands at every depth, `ancestors.ts` the ancestor lines, and `block/ancestors.ts` the displayed ancestor cards behind their frames; `elisions.ts` decides the elisions of every card and frame and draws their gaps; `block/arrangement.ts` lays out a block arranged side by side, its once cards included, over layout's one packing function (`arrangement.ts`). Line ends, chevrons, the brace and every element's bounds are `geometry.ts`'s, which the writers share. The style cascade (`Cascade`, `INITIAL_STYLE`, `DEFAULT_LAYER`) decides each property per element by the specification's layer order and rule rank, the rules bucketed by the selector members that discriminate them; text widths come from the advances of the fonts' one metrics table, measured once per distinct text. Layout knows nothing about a page.

### The writers

`@timetoalign/tilie-writers` exports `toDescriptors(drawing)`, `writeSvg(placed)` and `writeTikz(placed, options)`. `toDescriptors` returns the ordered SVG-shaped descriptors that the canvas and both writers share; every descriptor carries its `source`: the index of the drawn element it renders, that element's type and address, and its role (`primary`, or `shadow` for a card's shadow). `placed` is a list of `{drawing, x, y, scale}`; the writers find the bounding box of all placed drawings, add a margin of 16 on every side, translate each drawing to the page and apply its scale. `writeSvg` returns one SVG page; `writeTikz` a complete LuaLaTeX document, or with `standalone: false` the `tikzpicture` alone, `fontDirectory` naming the directory of `DejaVuSans.ttf` and `NotoMusic-Regular.ttf`, the second a luaotfload fallback of the sans font for every code point DejaVu Sans does not map and Noto Music does; the SVG's `font-family` is `DejaVu Sans, Noto Music`. Both round the exact value of every double to four decimal places, ties to even.

`pnpm -C app/packages/writers review`, from the repository root, draws every manifest fixture of the installed schema version (not the minimal twins nor the corpus ones), the resize and place cases and the manifests whose application layer shows a band the defaults hide, which are TimeToShare's drawing fixtures, and writes SVG and PNG files plus `index.html` under `app/packages/writers/review/`, rasterized with `rsvg-convert --zoom 2` under a fontconfig that exposes only the two bundled fonts ([decisions/0017](../decisions/0017-rsvg-convert-for-review-renders.md)). `emit-drawings` writes those fixtures into a TimeToShare checkout and never rewrites the hand-derived figures. `draw` draws any manifest file to SVG and TikZ the same way ([`AGENTS.md`](../AGENTS.md#commands)). The application exposes no PNG, JPG, PDF or `.drawio` export.

## The canvas

`@timetoalign/tilie-arranger` builds the canvas on [Svelte Flow](https://svelteflow.dev) (`@xyflow/svelte`), which supplies pan and zoom, node dragging, the selection rectangle, keyboard focus and culling ([decisions/0020](../decisions/0020-svelte-flow.md)). It has two levels: at the workspace level, the **arranger**, blocks are placed freely; inside a block, its groups and timelines are arranged in **editors**, modes of the same canvas. The drawing comes from the manifest, never from an editable drawing surface: a person edits the manifest through commands, never by dragging an arrow endpoint, so a musical relation keeps its meaning whatever the display. Every rule is plain TypeScript tested without a browser; the Svelte components connect those rules to Svelte Flow.

### One node per block

`project` returns one Svelte Flow node per block, in index order. A node's id is the block id; its `position` is the block's `x` and `y`, the canvas position of the drawing's place origin, less the drawing's origin times the scale (`nodePosition`), so a drawing that grows left or up moves nothing else; while the workspace arranges its blocks, the place their packing gives it instead (`blockPlaces`, [Arrangements on the canvas](#arrangements-on-the-canvas)). Its `width` and `height` are the drawing's width and height times the scale, given again as `measured` with `handles: []`, so Svelte Flow never measures a node; one it had to measure would be mounted even outside the view. `selected` follows the selection, `zIndex` is the block's rank by `index`, and `data` carries the block, its drawing and descriptors, its selected elements, the elements a Delete would take with them, the open editor and the addresses inside the edited element. With an editor open, every other node is neither selectable nor draggable and carries `tilie-outside`. A node whose inputs are unchanged is returned as the same object, so a change to one block re-renders one node.

`drawingOf(block, graphical, metrics, overrides?, pane?)` draws a block, its arrangement included, with the layers `DEFAULT_LAYER`, the workspace's `graphical` and the manifest's own, for the **pane** of the canvas showing it, the placement's `view`, which only the automatic proportion of an ancestor card's elisions reads, so the pane is read only for a manifest that draws an ancestor card ([Elisions](#elisions)). Each canvas has its own pane (`Pane`, `pane.ts`, the Canvas's `pane` prop): its size left of the inspector drawer, the first size measured taken at once and every later one `PANE_SETTLE` (150 ms) after the last, so dragging the window or the drawer redraws once, when it stops; until the canvas is measured a drawing takes layout's default view. Every drawing a canvas shows, and every drawing its gestures and keys read, is drawn at its pane. Drawings are memoized per block object and recomputed only when the workspace's `graphical`, the metrics or that pane change identity, descriptors per drawing. A drawing is also kept by its manifest and the values of its placement, the 32 placements of a manifest drawn last, so a resize, which measures the block at many placements on every pointer move, and the preview's block, which draws one of them again, draw each placement once. Neither is stored.

`BlockNode` renders the descriptors as Svelte elements inside one `<svg>` per node, whose `viewBox` is the drawing's block; every attribute is the text the SVG writer writes, so the node's element tree equals the exported SVG's. Text is rendered as text, never parsed as markup. Every drawn element carries its address as the SVG writer writes it, `data-id`, `data-facet` and `data-part` (the part words joined by one space), each only where the address has that member; the writers' `ADDRESS_ATTRIBUTES` is the one list of these attributes. The drawings use the DejaVu Sans and Noto Music files bundled by TimeToShare as two faces, "TiLiE DejaVu Sans" and "TiLiE Noto Music", named in that order in the drawings' font family, with no `unicode-range`, so that a code point DejaVu Sans does not map falls back to Noto Music; the canvas is mounted once both faces have loaded.

**Marks.** The node draws marks after the drawing, never part of it, its descriptors or an export, and never stored. Every mark is one overlay element in one vocabulary: `.tilie-mark`, its kind in `data-mark` (`selection`, `hover` or `holding`), the element address it marks, without its block, in `data-marks` as its `addressKey` (`{}` for the block), and the kind of box in `data-box` (`block`, `frame`, `card` or `band`; none for a boundary's `line`). A box's mark copies the outline of its own rectangle (a card's, not its shadow's). The last drawn card or frame under the pointer inside the edited element is hovered, the block itself never, a segment boundary under the pointer is hovered in the accent colour, and, in every view, the band under the pointer, marked `data-box="band"` (`bandUnder`: in a timeline editor the band hit, which is what a click selects; elsewhere the last band record holding the point among the bands of the card under the pointer, so a child's band before its parent's children band, marked only on that card of its timeline, `Hover.bandHolder`, and only where a press there lies inside the edited element), so that what a double-click opens is seen before it; a drawn element under the pointer is hit before any band and marks none; a selected box shows its selection mark and no hover mark. A hover change adds or removes one mark and touches no drawn element. Every other selected element carries `data-selected`, an element below a selected one `data-affected`, and both glow without a CSS `filter`, which would be rastered again at every zoom step (`canvas.css`): a filled shape or a text takes a halo stroke of the glow's colour behind its fill (`paint-order: stroke`), a stroked rectangle an `outline`, and a stroked line, path or polygon, which CSS cannot give a second stroke, stays as drawn below a selection and, selected, takes the selection's blue on its own stroke, its width and dashes kept, with a `.tilie-halo` under it, a translucent copy of its shape three units wider, carrying no address and letting the pointer through, so a glow follows the stroke and never shows a box. Every box shows a **holding mark** whenever the selection holds an element drawn as part of it other than itself, read from the boxes' ranges, never from where a box lies: 3 px dashed on a card, 2 px dashed on a frame, 1 px dotted on the block, whose node also carries `data-contains-selection`, measured on screen. In a timeline editor the selected bands of the edited timeline are marked the same way (`data-box="band"`). Stroke widths are divided by the zoom. **A cut name's flag.** Layout cuts every row name to its `max-width` and keeps the whole text on the name element it cut (`TextElement.whole`, internal: no writer writes it, so an export shows the cut text alone); while the pointer lies on such a name, the node draws its whole text on an opaque box over the marks, in the block's units and the name's face, size and place (`nameFlagOf`, `NameFlag.svelte`), which lets the pointer through, carries no address and goes when the pointer leaves; its colours are the canvas tokens `--tilie-flag-background` and `--tilie-flag-border`.

**The box reader.** `boxes.ts` is the one reader of `Drawing.boxes` and of the descriptors' sources. A press, a hover and Enter are classified from the drawing: a press reads the hit element's source, a `CanvasSource` (a descriptor's `source`, or an `AffordanceSource` `{kind: "affordance", address}` of a named timestamp the drawing draws nothing of, told apart by `isAffordance`), never the DOM target's form, and Enter takes its target from the drawing by address. It decides what a press hits (a box, a band or another drawn element), what it selects (`pressSelection`), what is hover-marked, and which boxes hold part of the selection; while an editor is open, only what lies inside the edited element. Which editor a double-click opens is decided from the same hit.

### Levels and permissions

One table in code, `LEVELS`, holds every rule of each level: `move`, `nudge` (the arrow keys, on the arranger only), `reorder`, `bands`, `marquee`, `itemsFree`, `frameWidthPinned`, `remove`, `order`, `open`, `lock` and `resize`. `rulesOf(editing, {locked, readonly, arranged})` is the one answer to whether something may run: a locked canvas turns off `move`, `nudge`, `bands` and `resize` and keeps the rest; a read-only canvas turns off everything that writes, opening an editor included, and still selects, pans and zooms. Where the level's contents are arranged (`arrangedOf(view, editing)`: on the arranger the workspace's arrangement, a matrix with its `columns`; in a bundle editor the edited block's, a horizontal one the rules refuse read as vertical, as it is drawn; none in a group or timeline editor), the rules carry it as `arranged` and turn `move` and `nudge` off, the arranged drag and keys taking their place; a locked or read-only canvas drops it, so the lock turns the arranged drag off as it turns moving off. How each kind of box resizes is a second table, `RESIZING`, keyed by kind with a variant for a card held by a frame, naming the levels where it resizes and what each mode writes (`resizingOf(box)`). From the two tables, `fieldsOf(box, level)` lists the place members a Properties field shows for a container at a level, in core's place order, each with whose place it writes and whether the level edits it.

| Level | Moves | Resizes | Reorders | Marquee selects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Arranger | blocks, unless arranged | every block, frame and card | blocks among their siblings (z-order, and the order an arrangement packs them in) | blocks |
| Bundle editor | groups' frames and ungrouped cards, freely, alone or with every other one in the selection, unless arranged | the edited block and every frame and card in it | its items, while arranged | elements |
| Group editor | none | the edited frame and its members' cards; the frame keeps its width while its members fit in it and grows with them past it | the member cards | elements |
| Timeline editor | none | the edited card, a root's or a member's; nothing for a child, which has no card | the bands of the edited timeline | elements |
| Flow-control editor | none (its pane composes FlowMaps, [The flow-control editor](#the-flow-control-editor)) | nothing | nothing | elements; nothing opens inside it |

In the arranger and the bundle editor items lie freely (`itemsFree`) unless they are arranged: an edit of one item's place, by gesture or form, also states the drawn `x` and `y` of every other item that states none, the frame holding a resized member's card excepted, so that nothing else moves. In the group and timeline editors an edit states only the places it changes, and an item below may follow it down, its container adapting.

### The gesture engine

Every drag inside a drawing (moving an item, reordering a frame's cards, moving a band) and every resize handle is one engine, `pointerGesture(strategy, options)`, an attachment driven by a strategy: `item`, `arranged`, `held`, `band`, `resize` or `gap` (`gestures/`). The strategy says whether it takes a press, what value the pointer stands for, how that value is previewed and which command stores it; the engine owns everything else once. A press the strategy takes marks its element `nodrag nopan` before Svelte Flow reads it, so neither a node drag nor a pan starts, on a mouse and a touch screen alike, and captures the pointer; a press it refuses is the canvas's (a click, a pan, a marquee). A press released within 4 px (`CLICK_DISTANCE`, Svelte Flow's `paneClickDistance`) is a click and selects; past it the gesture starts, selecting the dragged box unless it is an item in the selection in the bundle editor, and every move whose value differs is previewed; the release dispatches one command and withdraws the preview; the click a browser fires after a drag is swallowed. Escape and a cancelled pointer end a started gesture without a command. Every gesture's preview is the command applied, the block drawn as `drawingOf(preview(view, command))`, and only that block's node is replaced; the projection never runs again for a preview, and a gesture can never show what the release would not store. Blocks are dragged by Svelte Flow itself, snapped by the canvas ([Numbers on the canvas](#numbers-on-the-canvas)).

The canvas reads the store through `liveStore(store)`, the one reactive adapter, which the editor makes once per store and passes to the canvas, the inspector and its editing bar: each getter (`view`, `editing`, `entries`, `listed`, `journal`, `sessions`, `changes`, `openings`) reads the store again after every change it reports, so no component keeps a copy. Undo and redo are exact, because the canvas shows nothing between two commands that the next command does not store or discard. Each gesture is one command:

- a drag of n blocks is one `set` with n changes `{x, y}`; a keyboard move (an arrow key one grid step, Shift and an arrow key eight) is one `set`;
- a resize of a block, a frame or a card is one `set`;
- a change of block order is one `set` of `index`; Delete is one `remove`;
- in the bundle editor, a move of one item, or of every item in the selection by the same travel, is one `set`;
- in the group editor, a drop of a member card is one `set` of the group's `order`, or nothing when the order is unchanged;
- in the timeline editor, a move of a band, by drag or by key, is one `set` of the timeline's `order`;
- a drag of a gap's edge, or a step of it by key, is one `set` of the elision ([Elisions on the canvas](#elisions-on-the-canvas));
- a choice of the Arrange menu is one `set` of the workspace's or the edited block's `arrangement`; on an arranged canvas a drop or arrow key of a block is one `set` of its `index`, and in the bundle editor of an arranged block a drop or arrow key of an item one `set` of the block's `arrangement` ([Arrangements on the canvas](#arrangements-on-the-canvas)).

Opening an editor, Done and Cancel are store operations, as undo and redo are, never commands. While a block, or a frame or card in it, is dragged or resized, the canvas reports the placement it previews (`onlive`), which the inspector's fields follow.

### Numbers on the canvas

Canvas numbers are doubles, and the positions Svelte Flow reports carry binary noise. Before a number is stored it is quantized: a position to the grid when the workspace snaps to it, every other number (and every position without snapping) to 1/2048 unit, the units per em of the bundled fonts' advance table, so that at an integral font size and scale every text-derived edge and plot origin is stored exactly. While blocks are dragged, Svelte Flow snaps them to the grid and the canvas aligns the dragged block horizontally: its left edge or plot origin moves onto the nearest left edge or plot origin of another block within 6 screen pixels, and a vertical guide is drawn there while the alignment holds; the other dragged blocks move by the same offset.

### Resizing

Every block, frame and card is resized the same way: a block, a frame, an ungrouped root's card and a member's card each offer three modes wherever their level resizes them.

- **Scale** sets `scale`: everything grows or shrinks with its proportions, text included. Only the four corner handles are offered; the new scale is the larger of the two axis ratios, never below 1/8.
- **Box** sets `width` and `height`: the container changes and the content keeps its place at the top left. A request at or below the content's own extent stores `null` (a place's member unstated), so the container follows the content when it grows.
- **Stretch** sets `length` from the left and right handles, `spacing` from the top and bottom ones and both from a corner: along time lengths and distances stretch, vertically the gaps between the container's contents grow or shrink, while markers, line widths and text keep their size. A container's contents are a block's items, a frame's cards, and a card's root row, its root's present bands and its ancestor bands, one on each side holding an ancestor line. A container with exactly one content (a block of one item, a frame of one card, a card of its root's row alone, without a present band or an ancestor band) has no gap, so its vertical Stretch sets its `margin` instead, the equal empty space above and below its contents, and leaves its `spacing` as it is; with two or more contents, or none, it sets its `spacing`. The arranger counts a container's contents from the drawing's records (`verticalStretchOf`), by one rule for the block and for every frame and card. The content grows in piecewise-linear steps, as the drawing specification derives them, so the values that put the dragged edge under the pointer are solved for numerically, each candidate value drawn once; the floors are 8 for `length` and 0 for `spacing` and `margin`, and a value the search leaves where it was drawn is not stated. A container enlarged beyond its content keeps its proportion to the content while it stretches, and a request no larger than the content becomes `null`.

In every mode the dragged edge ends under the pointer and the opposite edge stays put; a left or top handle also stores the new `x` or `y`. A block's horizontal stretch scales every `x`, `length` and `width` its places state by the ratio of the lengths (`blockStretchPlaces`); its vertical stretch changes its `spacing` alone, or its `margin` alone when it holds one item, moving the items at their default `y` and keeping every stated `y` and `height`. A frame or card resizes by changing the block's `places` (section 8.6). A frame or a block-held card: Scale sets its `scale` from a corner; Box its `width` and `height` from any of eight handles; Stretch its `length` and `spacing` (a frame's the gap between its cards, a card's between its rows and between its bands), or `margin` for a frame of one card or a card whose only content is its root's row, every stated width or height of it and of the cards it holds following its content in proportion. A member's card: Scale sets its group's `scale`; a horizontal Stretch its group's `length`, the stated widths of the frame and of every member's card following; a vertical Stretch the card's own `spacing`, or its own `margin` when it holds its root's row alone, the stated heights of the card and its frame following; Box the card's own `width` and `height`; as its frame decides where it lies, it scales from its bottom right corner and sizes from its right and bottom edges. In the group editor the frame keeps its width while its members fit in it: a horizontal Stretch of the members runs from the right only and states the frame's `width` at its drawn width while the cards fit; past it the frame's `width` is unstated and the frame grows with the cards. Sizes are in the item's own coordinates, before its scale, quantized to 1/2048 unit.

When exactly one box that the level resizes is selected, a toolbar centred 10 px above it offers Scale, Box and Stretch, also the keys 1, 2 and 3; it is placed by the rule of Svelte Flow's `NodeToolbar` (`getNodeToolbarTransform`) applied to the box, so it follows the box through every resize, pan and zoom. While boxes are moved, a block dragged by Svelte Flow or an item, a member card or a band dragged inside a drawing, the toolbar and its handles are not shown at all, and they come back at the bounds the move leaves, also when it stores nothing: the gesture strategies that move boxes say so (`moves`), and the canvas's gesture slot reports it while such a gesture is under way. A resize through a handle and a move by keys hide nothing. The pressed button shows its mode's handles. A card's handles act on the card in the frame being worked in, the edited group's or the frame of the card dragged; a selected card address that covers several cards (a member of several groups) with no such frame known offers none. The handles are the arranger's own, since a box's size during a resize is whatever its drawing measures at the previewed placement. The mode is the viewer's own and stays when the selection changes.

### Selection, marquee and deletion

The selection is a set of addresses, `{block}` and `{block, id?, facet?, part?}`, compared as whole objects. It alone decides what the canvas shows as selected; Svelte Flow's own flags are reset to it. A selected address is marked wherever it is drawn, so a timeline drawn both as an ancestor line and as a member is marked on both lines.

- A click on a drawn element selects that element only. A press on a box selects that box: a card (or its shadow), a member's or an ungrouped root's, selects the timeline's cards `{block, id: T, part: ["card"]}`, marked in every frame that draws one (a root's ancestor card selects the root's cards the same way, `isAncestorCard`), a frame outside its cards the group, the block's own area outside its frames and cards the block.
- In a timeline editor, a click on an offered band selects it, `{block, id: T, facet: F}`; a selected band counts as its timeline's line for the contains-selection marks, and a selection rectangle never selects a band.
- Shift and a click add the address to the selection, or remove it. A click on the empty canvas, and in an editor a click anywhere outside the edited element (on its group, on anything else faded, on another block), clears the selection, in the marquee mode too; Shift and a click there leave it.
- Escape cancels a gesture in progress; with none it clears the selection, and with nothing selected it closes the innermost editor.
- A focused block is selected with Enter or Space. A drag on a block, on one of its elements included, moves the block, first selecting it alone when neither it nor an element of it is selected.

A plain drag on the empty canvas pans; Shift and a drag draw a selection rectangle, which replaces the selection with the blocks wholly inside it (in an editor, the elements). The Marquee button ("Add to selection (marquee)") switches to an additive mode, which is also how a touch screen selects several things: a drag on the empty canvas adds what lies wholly inside, and every click, Enter or Space toggles its address.

**Picking.** While a panel awaits an element of the manifest (the Metadata tab's composer, "Pick on the canvas"), the canvas is given `picking`, a callback, and picks instead of selecting: a primary press on a block is taken before anything inside it sees it, and a press on a timeline's line, card, band or a part of it, or on a region or its label, answers the callback with `{block, id}` of that timeline or region (`picking.ts`, `pickable`, reading the press as the inspector reads a selected address); any other press, on the block's own area, a frame, a title, a named timestamp, a map or a flow-control element, picks nothing and is refused with "Pick a timeline or a region; Escape cancels."; a press taken so starts no drag or pan, and its release and click reach nothing more. Escape, wherever the focus is, cancels the pick (the editor's `Picking`, `pick.svelte.ts`, which holds one pick at a time).

Delete or Backspace removes exactly the selected addresses as one `remove`, blocks and elements mixed; a selected part stands for its element. While elements are selected, what a Delete would take with them is marked with `data-affected`. A band is never deleted: its address is left out, and a selection of bands alone is refused with "A band is not deleted; select its elements to delete them." A selected virtual card held by its block (`{block, id: V, part: ["card"]}`) is hidden, not deleted: Delete sends one `set` of its place with every stated member `null`, which takes the card off the canvas and keeps the virtual timeline and its FlowMap; a virtual card in a frame is left out, as is a chunk, which is changed in its FlowMap, and a flow-control element or a part of one, which is rejected by a belief and never removed (each with a hint where the selection names nothing else: "A chunk is changed in its FlowMap; open the flow-control editor of its root to change it.", "A flow-control element is not deleted: reject it with a belief in Properties."), and a selection hiding virtual cards and deleting anything else is refused with a hint. The virtual timeline itself is deleted by "Delete virtual timeline" in the Properties tab ([The inspector](#the-inspector)), one `remove` of `{block, id: V}`. The card address of a root drawn only in ancestor cards is left out too, since it stands for no element of its own. A selection holding a manifest's title is refused whole, since a title goes only with its block. Inside an editor the edited element, or a part standing for it, is never deleted: it is left out, the rest of the selection is removed, and a selection naming nothing else is refused with "Close its editor to delete the edited element."; selecting it marks nothing `data-affected`. A segment that would leave a gap, or a part of one, is dispatched like any target and refused whole by core ("Removing segment 'S' of line 'L' is not supported yet"). The canvas draws no text about a refusal: it reports every refusal, of a command, a key, a drop or a pick, through its `onrefuse` prop, and the editor posts it to the message bar as information ([The component and the page](#the-component-and-the-page)).

**The deletion dialog.** A `remove` that also takes along virtual timelines it does not name (core's `removedBy`: those made from a removed timeline or virtual timeline, or through a removed FlowMap, at any depth; none for a block removed whole) is asked about before it is stored, from Delete on the canvas and from "Delete virtual timeline" alike (`deleteQuestion`; the canvas's `confirmDelete` prop, the editor's `askDeletion` over its confirm dialog): "Deleting `<targets>` also deletes what is made from it: `<virtual timelines>`. Delete all, or hide its card instead?", each thing by its kind and its name, else its ID (core's `addressWords`). **Delete all** is one `remove` naming the targets and each virtual timeline taken along, in each block's order of its virtual timelines, so one undo step. **Hide instead** is offered where every target is a virtual timeline its block places (`hideCommand`, the `set` unstating their places that Delete on a virtual card sends) and where the target is exactly one root (`rootDisplayCommand`: one `style` of `display: none` on the root's card address, its own address and its five bands, so its card keeps its place undrawn, and its line, its ancestor cards behind frames and its ancestor lines in member cards are not drawn, while a member of a group inside the root stays in its frame); several roots, or roots mixed with virtual timelines, are offered no hiding. **Cancel** is focused, and Escape answers it. "Delete all" is previewed with core (`preview`), never checked by a copy of its rules: where core refuses it (a FlowMap left names one of the virtual timelines as a chunk), the question says "They cannot all be deleted." and core's reason, which names each timeline, FlowMap and virtual timeline by its name, else its ID (core's `namedProblem`), and offers only Hide instead, where it is offered, and Cancel. A canvas without a host stores Delete all where core accepts it, and nothing where core refuses it. The bundle's Timelines list in the Properties tab shows a hidden root or virtual card again ([The inspector](#the-inspector)).

### Editors

An **editor** is a mode of the canvas on one element, never a second canvas. There are five kinds: the **bundle editor** of a block, the **group editor** of a group, the **timeline editor** of a timeline, a root's or a child's, the **children editor** of a timeline's children (below), and the **flow-control editor** of a root's flow control and FlowMaps ([The flow-control editor](#the-flow-control-editor)). While a timeline editor is open on a root, or on a descendant of it that no member card below the root draws (no timeline from the root's child down to the edited one is a group member), the root's displayed ancestor cards are raised over every frame where the root is in no group, or is itself a group member that draws an ancestor card (another member descends from it): such a root stands in its own group's frame while its ancestor card lies covered behind the frame holding its descendant, so a double-click on that card opens the root's timeline editor and raises it (`raisedRoot`, the one decision of the raise). The raise changes only the order the block node shows the drawing in, never the drawing: one stacking, the ancestor cards not raised in record order, then every other frame and card, then the raised ancestor cards (`stackingOf`; the block node draws its layers by it, `raisedLayers`). The raised cards unfold out of the member cards they are aligned to, each row of a card moving from its place in the member card to its place in the raised card while the card's rectangle and the rows the member card does not draw fade in, and fold back into them when the editor closes, in 400 ms (`Unfolding` and `animation/`, [decisions/0034](../decisions/0034-unfolding-a-raised-card.md)), at once under reduced motion; a moving card takes no pointer, and the canvas reads the stacking at rest from the start. What the canvas reads follows the raised cards: the pointer reads their lines everywhere and no other line inside them, coordinates are placed on them ([Show coordinates](#show-coordinates)), and the marquee and the editor's focus count what they draw, which they leave out while the cards lie behind the frames. A double-click, a double tap (two taps within 300 ms and 10 px), Enter on one selected address and the inspector's Edit button open an editor by one rule in every view (`editorTargetOf`): what is hit opens its own editor, and what has none the editor of the card holding it. The block opens the bundle editor, a frame its group's, a timeline's line, wherever it is drawn, that timeline's (a child's line the child's), and a flow-control element (its line, symbol, fork, arc or label, hit before any band) the flow-control editor of the root whose tree holds it; a band, the one under the pointer (`bandUnder`) or the band hit, opens its timeline's editor, a timeline's children band the timeline's children editor and a root's flow control band the root's flow-control editor (`editorOf`), so a child's flow control band opens the child's timeline editor; a chunk's label, leader or boundary on a virtual or switched card opens the flow-control editor of its FlowMap's root (`chunkTarget`), and an element a chunk's window shows, held by a timeline other than the virtual timeline (a region, a map, a named timestamp, a child's line, or a part of one), the editor of that timeline, where the element is written (`sourceTarget`); a free spot of a card opens the card's timeline (an ancestor card its root's), and anything else drawn (a name, a label, a region, a stamp, a boundary) the editor of the last drawn card or frame holding it, the innermost where none overlap. Inside an editor, every target inside the edited element opens its own editor on top (`openableIn`), the edited timeline's children band and a root's flow control band inside that timeline's own timeline editor too, and nothing opens inside the flow-control editor, where a double-click anywhere on a block, the edited card, its line and its bands included, closes it as Done does (`doubleClickAt`); elsewhere a double-click on the edited element, or on anything inside it that opens no editor, centres the view on the edited element again as opening does, and one outside it closes the innermost editor, as Done does. The open editors, outermost first, form the **editing path**, the store's `editing`, one source for the canvas, the inspector and the test hook. On touch, a press held 500 ms (`LONG_PRESS_MS`) within the click distance lists the editors under it in a menu beside the canvas (`EditorMenu.svelte` over `editorsUnder`): what a double-click there opens, then the card under the point, then the group of the frame on top, each once and only where it opens, worded by core's `addressWords` ("Flow control of timeline 'Score'", "Timeline 'Score'"); Escape or a press outside closes the menu, and a move beyond the click distance, a cancel or a second finger stops the long press, whose release is no tap.

Opening an editor animates the view in about one second (`FOCUS_DURATION`; at once under reduced motion) so that the edited element is centred left of the inspector drawer with a 10 % margin, the zoom between 0.05 and 4; closing animates back to where that level was entered from; a change of the canvas's size with an editor open centres the element again. The view follows the editing path through the store's subscription (`editorFocus.svelte.ts`). Everything outside the edited element is faded to 0.3 and inert: nothing there is hovered, selected or moved, a click there clears the selection, and a drag there pans. Opening an editor selects its edited element (`editedSelection`): the block in the bundle editor, the group in the group editor, and in the timeline editor the timeline's cards where it is drawn in one, else the timeline itself (a child, which has no card). After every other change of the store, an editor closing, an undo and a redo included, the editor's session prunes the selection by one rule (`existing`): it keeps an address that still resolves and, in an editor, lies inside the edited element (the block itself in the bundle editor), the edited element and its card included, so a change made to the selected edited element keeps it selected; a selected band is kept while its timeline's editor is the innermost open editor of its block. Inside, elements are selected by click and by marquee (`elementsInRect`); what an ancestor card draws, an affordance on its lines included, counts only while that card is raised, so an element drawn there and outside it is taken by its drawings outside, and one drawn only there is not taken (the stacking's `behind`, which the focus reads too).

In the bundle editor an item is in the selection when the selection holds its frame or card, the cards of one of its group's members, or another element its group or root contains by address; a drag on such an item moves every item in the selection by the same travel, the pressed item's place quantized, and keeps the selection; a drag on another item selects and moves it alone. In the group editor a dragged member card (one that anchors its group on a root drawn once excepted, [Arrangements on the canvas](#arrangements-on-the-canvas)) previews the order a drop would give, before the first card whose middle lies below the pointer (`memberOrderAt`), the other cards sliding apart (`slide`) only while a member drag previews, and at once under reduced motion. The dragged card itself never slides: it lies hidden at the slot it would drop into while a copy of it follows the pointer. However the drag ends, stored, cancelled or released in place, the copy then moves from where it was dropped to where its card is drawn (`releaseMotion`, `settle`: √d × 120 ms for a screen distance `d`, easing out), then goes and the card shows; under reduced motion the copy goes at once.

An editor has no side effects ([No side effects](#no-side-effects)); a refused command leaves everything as it was, and its refusal (`onrefuse`) names what lay outside. Done keeps the editor's changes as one entry of the level below; Cancel restores everything as it was when the editor opened. Both are the inspector's.

**A child's flow control in its timeline editor.** Only a root's flow control band draws flow control, so a child drawn as a line in its root's card shows none of it in its own row. While the timeline editor of a child is open, the block node draws below each drawing of the child's line in a card (not the child's own member card, which draws its root's flow control itself, nor an ancestor card) a read-only band (`flowOverlays`, `flowOverlay.ts`): the elements that card's flow control band draws on the child's stretch, drawn as that band draws them and moved down, each flow line across the band, inside a dashed rectangle labelled "Flow control (from <root>)", the root of the child's tree by its name. It is an overlay like the marks: its elements carry no address, it lets the pointer through, nothing in it is selected or moved, and it is no part of the drawing, its exports or the manifest. It appears only where the card draws an element on the child's stretch.

**The children editor** of a timeline `T`, a root or a child, with or without children (editor kind `children`, target `{block, id: T}`, the kind stated in its session as the flow-control editor's is, [The workspace file](#the-workspace-file)), opens from `T`'s children band by the one rule, wherever `T` lies inside the edited element, and on top of `T`'s timeline editor. Its level's rules are the timeline editor's without moving bands (`LEVELS.children`); the editor's focus centres `T`'s children band (`focusedOf`), on opening and when the view is centred again; it opens selecting `T`. It changes what `T`'s timeline editor may change, by the same guard; a child's editors open inside it. The inspector's editing bar names it "Children of `<timeline>`", the Journal "Edited children of `<timeline>`", and its level tab, "Children", lists `T`'s children, each with its offset and length, a row selecting its child ([The inspector](#the-inspector)); a selected child is deleted, renamed and set in the Properties tab, and a double-click on it opens its own editors inside.

### The flow-control editor

The **flow-control editor** edits the flow control and the FlowMaps of one root `R` (editor kind `flow`, target `{block, id: R}`; [FlowMaps and virtual timelines](#flowmaps-and-virtual-timelines)): the flow-control elements of the timelines of `R`'s tree, added in its level tab, the Flow control tab, selected on the card or in that tab's list, whose name, label and belief the Properties tab sets and whose display controls style them ([The inspector](#the-inspector)), and `R`'s FlowMaps, composed in its pane. It opens by the one rule of [Editors](#editors), from `R`'s flow control band and from every flow-control element of `R`'s tree, wherever `R` lies inside the edited element: on the arranger, in the bundle editor, in the editor of a group holding `R`, and on top of `R`'s timeline editor. Outside `R`'s timeline editor no band is hit: a press on `R`'s card hits the card, and `editorTargetOf(view, hit, editing, at)` names the band from the drawing point (`bandUnder`); in `R`'s timeline editor the band hit names itself. `editorOf` turns the band's address into `{target: {block, id: R}, kind: "flow"}`. A child's flow control band opens the child's timeline editor, while the elements it draws open `R`'s flow-control editor. Like every editor it blocks everything else, its level's rules being the timeline editor's without bands and without opening (`LEVELS.flow`), a double-click on a block closing it, and Done and Cancel stand in the inspector's editing bar, which names it "Flow control of `<root>`" (`editorTitle`); what it may store is the guard's `flowRefused` ([No side effects](#no-side-effects)). Whoever opens it, the canvas then asks the server for its convention's findings.

The card stays as it is, marked as edited; the FlowMaps fold out in a **pane** under it (`FlowEditorPane.svelte`, in Svelte Flow's front `ViewportPortal`; the wheel over it pans and zooms the canvas as anywhere else on it, a plain or Shift wheel panning, Ctrl or Cmd with the wheel zooming, and rows wider than the room right of the card scroll by the pane's scrollbar), as wide as the longest FlowMap needs, on the card's scale, and the editor's focus centres the card with its pane. The pane is an overlay like the marks: it carries no address and is no part of the drawing, its exports or the manifest. Its head holds **Add +** and **Recompute**; each **row** (`flowRows` in `flowEditor.ts`, drawn by `flowEditorRows.ts`) is one FlowMap of `R`, the computed ones first, each part ordered by its name (its `name`, else its convention, else its ID), compared by code point, then by its ID (while the editor is open the pane keeps the order it showed, `keptOrder`, so a FlowMap added or renamed meanwhile keeps its place and a new row comes last), with chips for its convention, canonical, provenance methods and findings, its name cut with `…` to the `max-width` of its row card's name, else the root's, at the pane's font size (`PANE_FONT_SIZE`, 9, layout's `cutText`; the whole name shown on hover, `data-whole`), and is drawn as layout's drawing of a virtual card applying it (its line with its gap lines and its chunks' labels, leaders and boundaries only, no window) at the graphical length `L × Λ / λ` (`L` the card's, `λ` `R`'s length, `Λ` the played one), its line aligned with the card's (further right only where the rows' own name column needs the room), so that a played stretch is exactly as long as the written stretch it plays.

**Composing** (`flowEditor.ts`, pure: what each action stores; `flowEditor.svelte.ts`, the session; `gestures/flowDrag.ts`, the drags). Add + opens a new, empty row with a green Done and a red Cancel. Into the edited row the user drags, from the card, its Regions (atomic sections among them), its children and its line, which lands as the Region spanning the whole root (one held already, compared by value, else a new one over `[0, length]`, 0 written in the axis's number type), since the root itself is no chunk; from the other rows a chunk, which lands as its source, or a whole row lifted by its name, which lands as the virtual timeline it gives, written first, named by the row's name (`rowName`), where none applies that FlowMap. A Shift-drag on the card's line sweeps a stretch that is written as a new Region and lands as its chunk; a chunk is moved by dragging it, Alt copies it; "+ Gap" adds a gap at once at the edited row's end, as long as the row's last chunk plays (the root's whole length on a row without chunks; `proposedGap`), selects it and opens its length in the row's head, focused and selected, a field for an exact length in the axis's number type (core's `parseAxisValue`, longer than 0), where Enter writes a new length and Escape closes; where no length can be proposed (the root's length unknown, a FlowMap that does not resolve) the field opens empty and the gap is added once a length is typed. Each gap of the edited row has a handle at its right end, mouse or pen only, whose drag shows the length in a flag and stores it on release (`draggedGapText`: rounded to the coarsest power of ten not coarser than a screen pixel, at least 1 on an integer axis, never 0); a press on a gap entry, or Enter on one of the edited row, opens the same field with its length; a changed length, typed or dragged, is one `set` of the FlowMap's `chunks` (`regap`); without a pointer, Alt+← and Alt+→ move an entry, D copies it and Delete removes it, and Enter on another row's entry and Insert on another row insert it. The first landing writes the FlowMap's `add` into `R`, every further change one `set` of its `chunks`; a new ID is read through the result (`addedId`), never minted by the arranger. The row's Done keeps it; its Cancel undoes every step since the row was opened, the Regions and virtual timelines written for it included. A row of a FlowMap written by hand becomes the edited row on a press or Enter, with its own Done and Cancel and every gesture of the new row; a **computed** row is read-only (its chunks may still be dragged into another row), and **Recompute** (only with a server; disabled otherwise, saying "Recomputing needs a server") asks the server for the flows of `R`'s flow control (the client's `withFlowMaps`, through the canvas's `flows` prop, the editor's `flowServer.ts`), adds each atomic section the answer has and the manifest lacks as a Region, and stores each changed computed FlowMap as one `set` of its `computed` value, the played lengths following, or its `add` where none is held. A convention's findings are asked of the server (`flows`) when the editor opens and after Recompute, and the rows read them from a cache.

**A row dragged out becomes a card.** A row lifted by its name carries a transparent twin of its drawing; the nearer the twin's deciding point (the middle of the twin, `twinMiddle`) comes to the editor's border (the union of the card's and the pane's rectangles), the more opaque the twin, `0.2 + 0.8 × max(0, 1 − d / 96)` at the distance `d` inside it, opaque on and beyond it (`twinOpacity`, `insideDistance`). Released inside the border it flies back to the row and nothing is written (at once under reduced motion); released after the twin's middle crossed the border (never the pointer, which decides only a drop on the new row), the FlowMap's virtual timeline is written, named by the row's name, where none applies it, and its card is placed where the twin's top left lies, rounded to the grid step, by one `set` of its place, the write and the place one step of the flow-control editor (`Store.edit`, the place made from the view the write left); the release on the new row inserts the row as a chunk instead (`dropAt`, `dropIndex`). "Place as card" writes the virtual timeline in the same way, as one step with the place, and places its card at the height of its row, `PLACE_INDENT` (48) units right of the root's card (`rowPlace`, exact, not on the grid), so placed cards stand one under another, sticking out right of the pane while the editor is open and, once it closes, indented under the root.

### Virtual cards on the canvas

A virtual card is a card like any other: a press on it hits the card (`{item: V}`, `{block, id: V, part: ["card"]}`), a drag moves it by one `set` of its place, Scale, Box and Stretch resize it as an ungrouped root's card, a press on a chunk's label selects the label `{block, id: V, part: [k, "label"]}`, a part of the chunk, which the Properties tab and the ribbons read as the chunk (a press on its stretch of the line selects `V`'s line), and Show coordinates reads its line as the root it is drawn as. Three overlays and one control are its own:

- **Ribbons** (`ribbons.ts`, `Ribbons.svelte`): a chunk hovered or selected draws a translucent blue ribbon from its drawn span on the virtual card's line (`drawnChunks`: from its boundary, or the line's start, to the next drawn chunk's boundary, or the line's end) to its written stretch on every line of the source root in the block (its own card, member and ancestor cards; clipped to what each line draws; never into a switched card), read from the drawing alone, and lights every other chunk of the same source on every virtual card (`litSpans`, a `.tilie-lit` line over each drawn span); a run's later entries are not drawn and so draw no ribbon. A chunk is hovered where the pointer hits its label, leader or boundary (`chunkOf`), and else inside one of its **zones** (`chunkZones`, computed once per drawing; `chunksAt`, half-open spans, the line widened by the canvas's `HIT_TOLERANCE` of 8 screen pixels either way): its stretch of the virtual line, up to the top of its label, so its stretch, its label and the space between them; and, once per drawn chunk that is no gap, its written stretch on every source line in a card that is not switched, out to the root's regions band where one is drawn, where every chunk playing the stretch under the pointer shows its ribbon at once. A pointer move scans only the block's zones, never the drawing. The ribbons lie in one `g.tilie-ribbons` that lets the pointer through and carries no address, and fade in, at once under reduced motion.
- **The flow flag** (`coordinates.ts` `flowFlagParts`, `CoordinateFlag.svelte`): on a virtual card's line the coordinate flag adds the written position and its occurrence ("occurrence 2 of 2"; "in a gap"); on a root's line it adds one row per virtual card of the block, in drawing order, listing every played position ("not played" where there is none).
- **The flow switch** (`flowSwitch.ts`, `FlowSwitch.svelte`): in the resize toolbar's row, right of Scale, Box and Stretch (the mode menu holds it as its children, one panel), a list "Flow" on a selected root's card: "As written", then each FlowMap of the root once (none for a frame, a child's card, a virtual card or an ancestor card). Choosing sends the `flow` style ([Commands](#commands)), "As written" `flow: null`, one command on the card, and none where the choice is what the card shows; a locked canvas disables it. A card whose `flow` names a virtual timeline applying a FlowMap of its root without `through` shows that FlowMap chosen (`chosenFlow`, over core's `cardFlow`). The list keeps one width (`SWITCH_WIDTH`, 11rem), cutting a long name with an ellipsis, the whole name its tooltip and each entry's. When a card becomes switched, its chunks move from their written stretch on its line to their played spans over the canvas's motion duration (`flowSwitchMotion.svelte.ts`, `switchMorphs`; never for a preview, at once under reduced motion).

### Bands

In the timeline editor the bands of the edited timeline, a root's or a child's, are arranged (`bands.ts`). A band is offered only in the timeline editor of its own timeline while that editor is the innermost open one, and only while it is recorded; the arranger, the bundle editor and the group editor offer none, though the band under the pointer is hover-marked in every view and a double-click on it opens its timeline's editor ([Editors](#editors)). The **visible** entries are the timeline's recorded bands and `line`, top to bottom, a band's position being the middle of its rectangle and `line`'s the timeline's line. A band dropped at ordinate `y` moves, in the effective band order, to just after the last other visible entry above `y`, or before the first when none is (`bandDrop`), read against the drawing at the press; the line is an entry like a band, so a band dropped across it changes sides, and a release in place writes nothing. Either move is one `set` of the timeline's `order`, its value the new band order, or `null` when that equals the inherited order; moving a timeline's bands also moves those of every descendant without an entry of its own. A press on an offered band is hit where it lies in the band's rectangle, edges included, after every drawn element.

| On an offered band | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Hover | The band under the pointer is marked |
| Click, tap | Selects the band; Shift and a click add it or remove it |
| Drag, mouse or one finger | Previews the band's new place, the other bands sliding apart; the release stores it in one `set`, a release in place nothing; on a locked canvas a drag pans |
| Double-click, double tap | On a root's flow control band, opens the root's flow-control editor on top; on any other band, centres the view on the edited timeline again, no editor opening and none closing |
| Enter, with the band selected | On a root's flow control band, opens the root's flow-control editor on top; on any other band, nothing |
| Ctrl+↑, Ctrl+↓ (Cmd on macOS), with exactly one band selected | Steps the band past the next visible entry, the line included, in one `set`; nothing at the top or bottom or on a locked canvas |
| Escape, during a drag | Withdraws the preview and stores nothing |
| Delete, Backspace | Never deletes a band; a selection of bands alone is refused |

### Order, keys and the lock

The order of blocks is their `index`, projected as `zIndex`; selecting a block does not raise it. Ctrl and `]` (Cmd on macOS) move the selected blocks one place forward past the next unselected block, Ctrl and `[` backward, with Shift to the front or back; only the moved blocks receive new indices. On an arranged canvas that order is also the order the blocks are packed in, so these keys move a block along the arrangement too.

The canvas reads its keys before Svelte Flow does, from one table (`keys.ts`) whose rows each ask `rulesOf` whether they may run; keys typed into a text field are left alone. The canvas is one tab stop (an ARIA `application` named "Arranger"); its keys apply only while the focus is inside it. The file shortcuts and undo and redo are the editor's ([The editor](#the-editor)); they are listed here because they work on the canvas too.

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Tab, Enter or Space | Focus a block, select the focused block |
| Enter, with one box or timeline selected | Open its editor |
| Double-click, double tap | Open the editor of the timeline line or innermost box under the pointer; on the edited element, or inside it where no editor opens, centre the view on it again; outside it, close the innermost editor |
| Wheel, Shift and the wheel | Pan vertically, horizontally |
| Ctrl and the wheel (Cmd on macOS), a pinch | Zoom |
| Arrow keys, Shift and an arrow key | Move the selected blocks one grid step, eight (on the arranger); on an arranged level, move the one selected block (on the arranger) or item (in the bundle editor) one place along the arrangement: Up and Down along a column, Left and Right along a row, in a matrix Up and Down one row |
| Ctrl+↑, Ctrl+↓ (Cmd on macOS) | Move the one selected band (in its timeline editor) |

Svelte Flow pans on the wheel and zooms only with Ctrl or Cmd held; a touchpad pinch, reported as a wheel event with Ctrl, zooms too. Outside macOS, Chromium and Firefox already report a Shift wheel as horizontal travel, which Svelte Flow would discard, so the canvas pans it itself at Svelte Flow's speed (`gestures/wheel.ts`).

The Lock button, or L, locks the canvas: a drag on a block pans instead of moving it, an arranged block or item is not dragged, the arrow keys move nothing, the Arrange menu is disabled (and with it the inspector's level tab's arrangement, [The inspector](#the-inspector)), a drag on a band pans and the band keys move nothing, and the resize handles are hidden with the mode buttons disabled. Selecting, deleting, reordering, panning and zooming stay available. A read-only canvas offers no lock button.

### Overlays, culling and per-viewer state

The resize handles and the alignment guides lie in one overlay layer in front of the nodes and keep their screen size at every zoom. The canvas controls (zoom in, zoom out, fit, Lock, Arrange on the arranger and in a bundle editor, Marquee, Show coordinates, Show matches) are a vertical toolbar of icon buttons at the bottom left, each with an accessible name and a tooltip naming its key. Above 500 blocks only the blocks in view are mounted (Svelte Flow's `onlyRenderVisibleElements`); below that count culling costs more than it saves ([Performance](#performance)).

The selection, the viewport, the lock, the resize mode, Show coordinates and Show matches belong to the viewer: the editor holds them, and they are never stored in the workspace or its file. An arrangement is not the viewer's: it is placement, stored in the workspace, journalled and undone like any change. The open editors are the store's: a file saved with an editor open holds its session folded, and a reopened file opens no editor. A newly opened workspace is fitted into view. Svelte Flow's attribution link is hidden (`proOptions.hideAttribution`, which its MIT licence permits); the About dialog credits it.

### Elisions on the canvas

A gap, its ellipsis and its labels are one thing to the pointer (`gaps.ts`, over layout's `Drawing.elisions` and the gaps' elements): a press on any of them selects the elision, a stored one as `{block, id: E}`, a derived one as `{block, id: T, part: ["elision"]}`, and a gap carried into an ancestor card selects its group elision. Only a stored elision's gaps take handles, a card elision's in its card and a group elision's in its frame. A derived one is selected and never edited on its own: Delete on it is refused ("Automatic elisions are not deleted one by one: choose Custom to edit them."), and choosing Custom for its root stores the card's derived gaps as stored elisions ([The inspector](#the-inspector)).

A selected stored elision shows a handle on each edge its gaps reach, at the ordinate of its timeline's line (`GapHandles.svelte`), and above them the **gap mode menu** (`ModeMenu.svelte`, the resize toolbar's own menu) with two toggles, Coordinates and Width, one or both pressed and never neither, held per canvas, Coordinates alone at first. A drag of an edge by `Δx` (the `gap` strategy of the gesture engine, `gapDrag`): with Coordinates alone, the elided coordinate at that edge moves by `Δx` over the canvas units per unit of its timeline on the run beside that edge (`edgeScale`), snapped to the timeline's start and known end, its children's, and the ends of what the gap may not cross (an ancestor card's protected span, a frame's other gaps) where one lies within the snapping distance, else to the simplest value within the pointer's reach, and the gap keeps its drawn width, so the timeline slides under the handle; with Width alone the drawn width changes by `Δx` and the coordinates stay; with both the drawn width follows the moved edge, so the gap swallows the line and what lies beyond the edge stays put. A width is written in the elision's own form (`widthAs`; a standard gap as `{canvas}`), never wider than its natural width. A flag at the dragged edge shows its value, and the release is one `set` of the elision (`gapCommand`). `[` and `]` (by the character typed) choose the edge the arrow keys move, ← and → move it one grid step under the pressed modes (`gapStep`), and 1 and 2 toggle the modes; the lock hides the handles and disables the menu. With Show coordinates on, nothing is read inside a gap, and the gap under the pointer shows a flag saying what it leaves out ("Score, 0–16 ♩, elided", `gapHoverAt`).

`cardAxis.ts` is the one map between abscissas and the coordinates of the timelines a card or a frame draws (`cardAxisOf`, `cardAxisAt`, `exposureAt`): over the exposure's runs, each position the proportion of the axis it lies on, the card's root (a placed virtual card's virtual timeline, on its played axis) or, across a frame, the member holding its timeline, each run with its own scale, so a handle reads the run beside its edge.

**The arranger and the editor split the gap commands.** The arranger builds every command that reads the drawing's gaps, for the canvas and the inspector alike: a handle's or a key's `set` (`gapCommand`), the one `add` storing the gaps a card derived as it draws them (`defaultsStored`: in drawing order, the elided coordinates exactly, `{canvas: g}` unless the gap is the standard one, the card's `elision-display`, no name), and the one `remove` of a root's card elisions (`defaultsRemoval`). It exports them with the readers the inspector needs, `exposureAt`, `cardAxisAt`, `piecesOf` and `stretchText`, the gap labels' spelling of a stretch. The editor builds the rest from those readings (`inspector/elisions.ts`): the switches of a mode as `style` commands, the proportion, depths and display settings, Add elision, a group's Remove all, and the `set` of each field of a selected elision.

**Motion.** When a block's drawing changes its gaps (a mode switched, a gap added, removed or changed, a proportion moved), every card and frame concerned moves its elements sideways from where the drawing before drew their positions to where the drawing after draws them, rows moving only where labels are shown or hidden, and an ancestor card shown or hidden unfolds out of its member card or folds back into it (`animation/elide.ts`, `cardMotions.svelte.ts`; [decisions/0034](../decisions/0034-unfolding-a-raised-card.md)). Nothing moves during a gesture's or a control's preview, under reduced motion or for a block's first drawing, and the pointer always reads the drawing at rest.

### Region handles

A selected region, selected alone, shows a square handle at each end of each of its braces, at the brace's ordinate (`RegionHandles.svelte` over `gestures/region.ts`: `regionSubjectsOf`, one subject per brace, its card read from the drawing's card box ranges and its axis that card's, or that of the frame holding a member card, `cardAxisOf`). An end takes a handle only where that axis reads its abscissa back as the region's value, so a brace drawn over another axis, as on a switched card, takes none, and an atomic section takes none. A drag of a handle (the gesture engine's strategy `regionGesture`) reads the pointer through the card's axis, so a drag across an elided stretch reads the value on the far run, and beyond the axis or inside a gap by the end's run scale (`regionDrag`); it snaps to the timeline's start and known end, its children's starts and known ends and the other regions' ends, else to core's `snapOnRow` within half a pixel, and is held between 0, the other end (a region of length 0) and the known length. The preview goes through core's `preview`; a flag above the brace shows the region's name and its stretch with the dragged end (`regionText`, `stretchText`), and the release is one `set` of the changed end, exact in the axis's number type (`regionCommand`). Like the gap handles, they follow the level's `resize` rule, so a locked or read-only canvas hides them, and they are hidden while boxes move. The gap and region handles share the handle element (`EdgeHandle.svelte`), the snapping values (`edgeValues.ts`: `exactOn`, `timelineTargets`, `snappedOn`, `axisValue`) and the pointer's travel (`gestures/travel.ts`: `travelOf`, `pixelOf`, `HandlePress`).

### Arrangements on the canvas

Two levels of the canvas arrange their contents ([Arrangements](#arrangements)), through the **Arrange** menu (`ArrangeMenu.svelte`, on the toolbar of the gap modes' `ModeMenu`, `ModeButtons.svelte`, in a Svelte Flow panel beside the controls, opened by the toolbar's Arrange toggle; it reads nothing of the canvas, and `arrangeMenuOf` in `arrangeMenu.ts`, its one model, says what is stated and what a choice stores, so the inspector's level tab draws the same menu): on the arranger the blocks, Free, Vertical, Horizontal or Matrix with its Columns (− and +), the workspace's `arrangement`; in a bundle editor the edited block's items, Free, Vertical or Horizontal, the block's `arrangement`, Horizontal disabled where `rowOf` refuses the items' order, its reason below the choices and in its title; none in a group or timeline editor. A choice is one `set` (`canvasArrangementCommand`, `itemArrangementCommand`): Free states `null`; a block's Vertical or Horizontal the items in the order they are shown arranged (`shownItemOrder`, the drawing order where nothing is stored), core storing Horizontal as its row; Matrix its columns, the smallest number whose square holds the blocks when first chosen (`columnsOf`).

- **Blocks.** While the workspace arranges its blocks, every node lies where `packed` puts it in the blocks' order (`index`), 32 canvas units apart (`ARRANGEMENT_GAP`), from the top-left corner of all the blocks' stated places, the least `x` and the least `y` among them, so that reordering never moves the start (`blockPlaces`, `arrangedPlaces`); each block's stated `x` and `y` are kept and not used, and no block's drawing changes. Svelte Flow drags a node as ever, without snapping or guides; the others are shown at the places its drop would give them (`arrangedDrop`), and the release stores the moved block's `index` alone, between its new neighbours' (`blockIndexCommand`, `indexBetween`), nothing where its place is unchanged. The nodes not dragged go to their new places by a CSS transition of 200 ms (`data-arranged` on the canvas), at once under reduced motion.
- **Items.** In the bundle editor of an arranged block a press on an item, a frame or an ungrouped root's card, reorders instead of moving (`gestures/item.ts` hands it to the `arranged` strategy, `gestures/arranged.ts`): a copy of the item follows the pointer, the others slide to the places the drop gives them (the preview being the command applied), and the release stores one `set` of the block's `arrangement` with the new order, after which the copy settles where its item is drawn, as a member card's does (`endReorder`). Side by side, a drop is refused where the moved group would leave its place among the stretches of a root it hangs on (`rowRefusal`, a clause per root: `"G" stays right of "A", "B" and left of "C" on "R"`): the stored order stays previewed, nothing is stored and the reason is reported through `onrefuse`; an item hanging on nothing goes anywhere.
- **Where a content drops** is one rule, `orderAt(entries, dragged, point, direction)` (`reorder.ts`), read against the places before the drop: before the first other content that does not precede the point, a content preceding it when its middle lies at or above it (vertical), at or left of it (horizontal), or, in a matrix read row by row, when its row lies above the point's or it lies in the point's row with its middle at or left of the point; the group editor's `memberOrderAt` is its vertical call. An arrow key moves the one selected block or item `arrangedStep` places along the arrangement (`steppedOrder`; nothing beyond either end), refused side by side as a drop is ([Order, keys and the lock](#order-keys-and-the-lock)).
- **Cards in a group's editor.** A frame's cards keep their display order and are reordered as ever ([Editors](#editors)). While the block draws its items side by side, a card whose member anchors the group on a root is pinned (`isPinned`): a press on it is not taken, so the roots' order stays the groups'; a drop of another card that would give the group another anchor is refused (`heldRefusal`, core's `anchorChange`: `would anchor "coda" on "Score" instead of "Recapitulation"`).
- **A root drawn once.** The once card is an ancestor card like any other: never dragged, opening its root's timeline editor and raised in it. It counts for every group of its `ancestor.groups`: a group's editor zooms out to the once card behind it whichever group it is keyed by (`elidedReach`), and the stretch a handle of its gaps may not cross spans every group's anchor. A gap an arrangement cuts is a derived gap to the canvas: selected, never edited (`subjectOf` gives none) and never deleted, Custom storing only the gaps a card derives by itself; `cardAxis.ts` gives each edge of it the scale of the run beside that edge.
- **Motion.** When an item's place changes (a choice, a drop, a step), its elements move from its old place to its new one (`itemMotions`, first in `blockMotion`, by the same progress as the elisions' motion), a member card of a moving frame with its frame, and so does an ancestor card behind that one frame alone; on a switch to or from Horizontal the once card moves sideways into its runs while the root's other ancestor cards fade out or in, none unfolding.

## The editor

`@timetoalign/tilie-editor` is the application around the canvas. Every change it makes to a workspace is a command dispatched to the store, so undo, redo, the journal and the autosave cover it as they cover a gesture.

### The component and the page

`Editor.svelte` is the whole editor for one workspace: header, canvas, inspector and dialogs. Its props are the embedding options ([Embedding](#embedding)), `testHook` and `examples`, which shows the Examples menu (the standalone page sets it, an embedded editor never has it); it exports `text`, `load`, `selection`, `select`, `focus` and `flush`. It styles only its own root (`.editor`, `container-type: inline-size`, the drawer `clamp(22rem, 32cqw, 40rem)` wide and at most `60cqw`), names every DOM id per instance with `$props.id()`, takes its shortcuts on its root unless `shortcuts` is `"window"`, and reaches outside itself only through its options. Its parts are modules: the open workspace with its store, file name, handle or path on the server, unsaved state, open, save, import and export (`workspaceSession.svelte.ts`, where a journal that does not reproduce its workspace becomes a refusal coded `journal_mismatch`), with the MatchClaim tables of its blocks (`matchClaims.svelte.ts`, [MatchClaim tables](#matchclaim-tables)), the autosave with its lock and recovery (`autosaving.ts`), the drawer (`InspectorDrawer.svelte`), the one shell of its native modal dialogs (`ModalDialog.svelte`: the `<dialog>` element, its look and one question at a time, a question shown over an open one dismissing it), on which the question dialog (`ConfirmDialog.svelte`), the tables dialog (`TablesDialog.svelte`) and the file dialog (`FileDialog.svelte`) hold only their content and answer, the header's workspace name (`WorkspaceTitle.svelte`), the Fixture menu of test builds (`FixtureMenu.svelte`), the Examples menu (`examples.ts`), one `SHORTCUTS` table driving both the keys and the menu's labels, and the message bar (`MessageBar.svelte`) with its messages (`messages.svelte.ts`). Each example declares its kind and is loaded as a chunk of its own when chosen: a **manifest** example, one of four fixtures of the schema package, is imported as one more block as Import adds a file, and refused as Import refuses it where a block holds that manifest already (core's `blockHolding`, [The header and the File menu](#the-header-and-the-file-menu)); a **workspace** example, `editor/examples/timestamps.tle.json` ("Timestamps": a fraction score with children and maps to ticks, whole notes and seconds, a float segment line of two takes with a map to samples, and named timestamps with different `display` sets), opens as the workspace, under no file, after the question New and Open ask. The example workspace is written by the end-to-end fixture generator (`pnpm e2e:fixtures`) through the store's commands and never edited by hand; `.gitattributes` keeps every `*.tle.json` LF in every checkout, as the generator writes it, so the byte-for-byte check (`pnpm e2e:fixtures:check`) holds on any platform. The menu is never disabled. The message bar is the one place the editor tells the user what happened ([decisions/0037](../decisions/0037-one-message-system.md)): it lies over the canvas, one card per message, newest last, one under another so that no card covers another, each naming its level in words ("Info", "Warning", "Error") before its text, with its problems and a Dismiss button. `Messages` has one verb, `post(level, text, problems?)`, at three levels (`MessageLevel`). **Information** (blue) reports what passes: a refusal of a change to what is open, wherever it is refused (the canvas's through `onrefuse`, a panel's, the store's), a confirmation such as "Copied 13 11/16 ♩.", a failure that can be repeated or worked around at once (a copy, a lookup), and why matches cannot be shown; it dismisses itself after `infoDurationOf(text)`, 3 s (`INFO_DURATION_MS`) or, for a text of more than ten words, 300 ms a word (`INFO_MS_PER_WORD`), and replaces information of the same text still shown. A **warning** (amber) reports something asked for and left undone for a reason other than the change itself (a file not opened for what was picked with it, a table no manifest names, addresses that could not be selected, autosave active elsewhere), an **error** (red) an operation on a file or a server that failed (a refused file, a failed save, a failed autosave); both stay until dismissed. Screen readers hear every message through two visually hidden regions present from the bar's first drawing, `role="status"` for information and `role="alert"` for warnings and errors, each holding one line per message shown, its level's word and its text; the cards carry no live role, and no message takes the focus. A component below the editor posts through the component context, `postOf()`, which gives it the editor's `post`; the workspace session and the autosave take `post` as an option. The same context carries what the editor holds once for every part: the user ([The user](#the-user)), the type profiles ([Type profiles](#type-profiles)), the lookup ([Lookup](#lookup)) and the picking of an element on the canvas ([Picking](#selection-marquee-and-deletion)); `transient.ts` gives a control the way to show a value on the canvas before committing it (`provideTransient`, `transientOf`; the Wedge slider of the Timestamp tab). The drawer renders the inspector only while it is open; the unapplied text, the drafts and the chosen tab live in the drawer and survive closing. The header shows the title the page gives it, a snippet, else a plain name that is no heading, so an embedded editor adds no heading to its host. The health check is the client's `connect(server).health()`, made once when a server is given; an answer that is not a name, a version and a `timetoalign` version, each a string, with a `timetoshare` version, where it has one, a string or `null`, and `files`, where it has one, a boolean (absent, it is read as `true`), fails as an answer outside 2xx does.

`App.svelte` is the standalone page `tilie` serves, and the one host that owns a document: it writes the title (`• ` while unsaved, then the file name and "– TimeLineEditor"), styles `html` and `body`, takes the server's token from the URL fragment and keeps it in the tab's session storage under `tilie.token`, from where a reload of the tab reads it (`page.ts` `takeToken`), gives the header the page's one level-1 heading, and mounts the editor with `server: {url: "api/", token}`, the storage slot `current` and the keys `tilie.inspector.open`, `tilie.user` and `tilie.profiles`, window shortcuts, autofocus and the test hook. A browser keeps what a page stores by its origin, port included, which is why `tilie` serves on one port by default ([The server](#the-server)).

### MatchClaim tables

A block's MatchClaims are read from its package's MatchClaim table, a Parquet file beside the manifest, which the manifest names as a resource of type `MatchClaim` ([MatchClaims and potential MatchStamps](#matchclaims-and-potential-matchstamps)). The editor holds at most one per block (`MatchClaimTables`, `matchClaims.svelte.ts`, owned by the workspace session): the server's answer for the table, the file it was read from, its name and whether a belief has changed it. Tables are held for the session only, never written to the workspace file, and dropped when another workspace is opened. Every other resource a manifest names, a Projections table among them, is neither read nor dropped. **Matches need a running server**: a MatchClaim table is read through `tilie-server`, which checks it against the block's manifest (`POST /api/match-claims`, [The server](#the-server)), and only once the server has answered its health check. The session hands the tables two facts apart: whether the server **computes** (`computes`, it answered its health check), which is all that reading a table, recording a belief and answering provenance and events need, and whether it **offers files** (`files`, the editor's `fileHost`, given where the health answer does not say `"files": false`), which only looking for a table beside a manifest's file and locating a manifest need. A public server computes and offers no files. While none is given, or the one given has not answered, nothing is read and nothing asked: no table, no MatchClaim's provenance or events (`provenance` and `events` give `null`), no belief recorded (`believe` answers `unavailable`'s text), and `MatchClaimTables.unavailable` says why, in plain text: "Matches need a running tilie-server. To start it, run uv run tilie --static-dir ../app/packages/editor/dist in the repository's server folder (or tilie, once installed with pip install tilie-server), then open the editor at the address it prints." (`HOW_TO_START_A_SERVER`); after a failed health check the failure comes first, as the header's offline mark gives it ("The server did not answer the health check (`<message>`)."). That text is the hint and title of the Matchstamp tab's Load MatchClaims… and belief buttons, which it disables, and what an expanded MatchClaim shows in place of its events and, under its Provenance button, of its provenance. Show matches posts `HOW_TO_START_A_SERVER` alone, as information, which stays for its reading time (`infoDurationOf`); a failure of the health check is shown by the header's offline mark and those controls. While the server given has neither answered nor failed its health check (`MatchClaimTables.waiting`), nothing is posted; once it answers or fails with Show matches on, the editor follows the outcome as a press of M then would (`checkHealth`): answered, it loads the named tables; failed, the information is posted once.

**Where a manifest came from.** A block may know a place from which the files its manifest's `resources` name, at locations relative to the manifest, can be read (`ManifestSource`): the folder of the manifest's file on a server that offers files, where the workspace states the block's `location` ([The workspace view](#the-workspace-view)), read through `GET /api/files` (`serverSource`), which serves nothing in a hidden folder (a table there is warned of as "`<location>` could not be read from where the manifest came from: …"), or, in a test build, the Fixture menu's table fixture served beside its table. A manifest picked in the browser, or one whose location the workspace does not state, has none, since a browser gives no access to the files beside a picked file.

**Loading.** A table is loaded in three ways.

- Turning Show matches on (`onmatches`, [Show matches](#show-matches)) loads, block after block in the canvas's one order of blocks (the arranger's `blocksInOrder`), the MatchClaim table every block's manifest names that is not loaded (`loadNamedTables`, `MatchClaimTables.loadNamed`): a table the manifest names by an absolute `http` or `https` URL fetched from that URL by the browser, any other from where the manifest came from, where the table lies there; either without a question, else as the user chooses it in one question per block (after a URL that cannot be fetched, the fetch's message shown, the question of Load MatchClaims…); while no server can read a table nothing is asked: where no block holds a table, or a block's manifest names a table the block does not hold, the message bar gets one information, `HOW_TO_START_A_SERVER`, in place of every question, none while the health check has not answered yet, its outcome followed then ([MatchClaim tables](#matchclaim-tables)); where no block holds a table once the tables are loaded, the information "No MatchClaim table loaded." (`NO_MATCHES`).
- Load MatchClaims… in the Matchstamp tab asks for the MatchClaim table of the block it shows (`choose`) in the same dialog, a table without a location where the manifest names none.
- Import manifest… and Open… in the browser pick several files at once, manifests or a workspace with `.parquet` tables beside them (`openFiles`, `withMatchClaimTable`, `readTables`): each table is a MatchClaim table, taken by the block whose manifest names a MatchClaim table of its file name, else by the only block, else not loaded, with the warning "`<file>` was not loaded: no manifest picked with it names it." Open… opens one workspace file at a time and warns about any other.

The user chooses in the **tables dialog** (`TablesDialog.svelte`, on the dialog shell), one question per block, which a URL field answers in every case (a relative URL is read relative to the page):

- with the server's files and no location of the manifest, "Where is `<manifest>`?": Locate… opens the file dialog on manifest files; the file chosen becomes the block's `location`, so the table is read from beside it now and whenever the workspace is opened again, and a folder not holding it is a warning ("`<url>` is not beside `<file>`.");
- with a location whose folder does not hold the table, "`<url>` is not beside `<manifest>`, at `<path>`": Locate… opens the file dialog on MatchClaim tables, starting beside the manifest, for the session;
- for Load MatchClaims…, "Load the MatchClaim table of `<manifest>`": Locate… opens the file dialog on MatchClaim tables, starting beside the manifest where its location is known;
- without the server's files, wherever a manifest names a table by no `http` or `https` URL and no served example holds it, and for Load MatchClaims…, "Load the MatchClaim table of `<manifest>`": Locate… opens the browser's picker for one `.parquet` file (`openFiles`), whose bytes are the table, posted to the server as any other; nothing is kept of where it lies, so it is picked again after the workspace is reopened, and no manifest is located.

Locate… answers at once; Load takes an entered URL; Cancel and Escape take nothing. A URL or a path that cannot be read is an error. A table loaded through the dialog or from where the manifest came from is confirmed in the message bar ("Loaded the MatchClaims of `<file>`."). A table the server refuses is not held; the error lists its problems by row, or says that the server did not answer or cannot read MatchClaim tables (501).

**Reading on demand.** The Matchstamp tab asks the server for what it shows of the MatchClaims of the MatchStamp it shows, and only of those: their provenance (`POST /api/match-claims/provenance`), once per table and list of claims, and, once one of them is expanded, their events (`POST /api/match-claims/events`), once per table and list of claims; each promise is kept while its tables are held, and a failed one is let go, so asking again asks the server again. A belief the user records goes to the server with the table (`POST /api/match-claims/belief`), which answers the new file; the editor reads that file through `POST /api/match-claims` again and holds it in place of the old one, marked changed. A changed table is kept only by Save MatchClaims…, which saves the file under the table's name, through the same file access as a workspace; Save saves the workspace alone. New workspace, Open… and a workspace example, which drop every table, first ask about a table that a belief changed and that is not saved, in the one question that also names unsaved changes of the workspace ([The header and the File menu](#the-header-and-the-file-menu)); Load MatchClaims… over a changed table asks the same of that table in the tab. The editor holds one `indexesOf` for the canvas, the drawer and the Matchstamp tab (`blockIndexes`): a block's anchor indexes under its MatchClaim filter, for the user as believer, under its manifest's `@context`, with the user's belief per MatchClaim, by which the canvas colours the anchors.

### The user

The editor has one user, the person viewing it (core's `User {name, orcid}`, `workspace/user.ts`): a name, which the store records as the author of every command (`setAuthor`), and an optional ORCID iD, which names the user in the provenance of what they state. `DEFAULT_USER` is "Gerhardt", the store's default author, without an iD. `readOrcid(text)` reads an iD or its address (`https://orcid.org/…`, `http://orcid.org/…`, `orcid.org/…`), white space trimmed and its check character verified (ISO 7064 MOD 11-2), as the bare iD, else `null`. `statedBy(user)` is the IRI `stated_by` names the user with: `https://orcid.org/<iD>`, else, since a name is no IRI, the tag IRI `tag:timetoalign.github.io,2026:tilie:user:` followed by the encoded name (`USER_TAG`); the editor's own proposals are stated by `TILIE_AGENT`, `https://github.com/TimeToAlign/TimeLineEditor`.

The editor holds the user in `UserState` (`user.svelte.ts`), read once at mount from the browser's `localStorage` under the `storage` option's `userKey` (`tilie.user` on the standalone page) as JSON `{name, orcid}`; a stored text that is not that, no key, or storage that is unavailable gives the default user, and a change then holds until the editor goes. File, Your name… and the Metadata tab's Change… open the "Your name" dialog (`UserDialog.svelte`, a native modal dialog): Name and "ORCID iD (optional)"; Save, or Enter in a field, takes the answer once a name is given and the iD, where given, reads as one, makes it the user, remembers it where storage allows and makes it the open store's author; Cancel or Escape changes nothing. The user belongs to the viewer: no setting, command or workspace holds it, and a workspace shows it only as the author of its journal entries, as `stated_by` of the pairs the user stated and as the actor of the user's beliefs.

### Lookup

The Metadata tab looks values up through one `Lookup` (`src/lookup/`, [decisions/0028](../decisions/0028-lookup-services.md)), which the editor makes once, `createLookup({server, services})`, and gives every part through the component context. It asks each system only where it can, and a system it cannot ask sends no request:

| System | Asked | Searches | Resolves |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `wikidata` | from the browser (the Action API: search with statement filters, `wbgetentities`) | every kind | yes |
| `gnd` | from the browser, through lobid-gnd | works and agents | yes |
| `youtube` | from the browser, through oEmbed | nothing | yes |
| `musicbrainz` | only through `tilie-server`'s `api/reconcile/musicbrainz`, with the run's token in a local run ([The server](#the-server)); left out without a server | works, agents, recordings and releases | yes |
| `service:<id>`, a service of the type profiles | from the browser, at its endpoint | every kind | no |

`search(system, kind, text)` gives the things of a kind of value (work, agent, recording, …) a system finds for a text, `resolve(system, iri)` the thing an IRI names, and `reconcile(system, kind, texts)` the best candidate for each text, one request for all of them where the service is a Reconciliation Service API endpoint; a candidate has an IRI, a label, a description, a thumbnail, its cross-references (the things other systems say it is the same as, the IRIs TimeToShare's catalogue recognises among them) and `service`, the upstream endpoint it came from (`https://www.wikidata.org/w/api.php`, `https://lobid.org/gnd/search`, `https://www.youtube.com/oembed`, `https://musicbrainz.org/ws/2/`, a service's own), never the local server. A failure is a `LookupError` naming the system, shown beside the Value field or in the message bar while the other systems go on; an aborted request rejects with the signal's reason. The viewer's services are Reconciliation Service API 0.2 endpoints (`services.ts`, over the generic `ReconciliationClient` that also reaches the server's endpoint): a search asks the service's entity suggestions where its manifest offers them, else one reconciliation query, and an entity's IRI is the service's identifier space followed by its ID.

What leaves the browser, and when: the text typed into the Value field, to the chosen system's service, once typing pauses, the request it replaces aborted; a recognised pasted address, resolved to fill its label; on viewing the tab, every thing each tile names whose system is resolvable, each IRI resolved once, for the tile's thumbnail (`thumbnails.svelte.ts`, kept for the panel's life; an image is never written to a manifest); on "＋ Add vocabulary", the rectangle's first thing, resolved for its cross-references; and on "Propose matches for unreconciled names", the names stated without an identifier. So merely viewing the tab sends the IRIs of the manifest's things to Wikidata, the GND, YouTube or the server, and each service sees the viewer's address and the page's origin, as with any link followed. An editor mounted with `lookup: false` is given `NO_LOOKUP`, which searches, resolves and reconciles nothing and sends nothing; its Metadata tab still takes identifiers, pasted addresses, elements of the manifest and "Other property…".

### Type profiles

A type profile is a type of thing of the viewer's own (`profiles/profiles.ts`): an `id` and a `label`, its "is a" alternatives per catalogue system, each a statements fragment without slots, the verbs usually stated of such a thing, in the order the composer offers them first, and the systems its values are preferably looked up in. Beside the profiles stand the viewer's lookup services, `{id, name, endpoint}`, each the system `service:<id>`, offered wherever a verb's fallback pattern takes a thing. The catalogue the Metadata tab offers is TimeToShare's with the profiles (`withProfiles`): a profile of a catalogue type's ID replaces that type, the others come before the catalogue's types, so a profile's verbs lead where its "is a" is stated. Profiles and services are application settings of the viewer, never part of a workspace or a manifest. They travel as a canonical YAML file, `profiles.yaml`, `{profiles, services}`, read by `readProfiles`, which names the path of the first problem. The editor holds them in `ProfilesState` (`profiles.svelte.ts`), read from `localStorage` under the `storage` option's `profilesKey` (`tilie.profiles` on the standalone page) as that file's text and written there on every change; a stored text that is no profile file gives none, and without the key storage is never touched and the profiles hold until the editor goes, so an embedded editor stores none unless its host names a key.

File, Type profiles… opens the "Type profiles" dialog (`ProfilesDialog.svelte`, a native modal dialog) over a draft: it lists the profiles, adds one, empty or from a catalogue type ("New profile from"), edits the selected one (ID, Label, "Is a" per system from the catalogue's patterns, Verbs with Move up, Move down and Remove, "Look values up in"), deletes one, and lists, adds, edits and deletes services, a service's new ID or its deletion followed in every profile's lookup systems. Import YAML… replaces the draft with a profile file, Export YAML… writes the draft as one; Save checks the draft and makes it the editor's profiles, Cancel and Escape drop it.

### The combobox

The composer's fields are the editor's own combobox (`src/combobox/`, [decisions/0027](../decisions/0027-own-combobox.md)), after the WAI-ARIA Authoring Practices example "Editable Combobox With List Autocomplete": typing never selects an option; the focus stays in the text field, which names the active option through `aria-activedescendant`; the listbox holds the options alone, and a message for an empty result lies beside it; a visually hidden status region announces the count or a running search. Its keys are one pure table (`keys.ts`): the arrows open the list and move the active option, Enter picks it, Escape closes a shown list and, with none shown, clears the text; a key the table handles is consumed, so an Escape that closes the list never reaches the drawer. An option is picked on the primary button's release, the press in the list cancelled so that the field keeps the focus. The options, the loading flag and the text belong to the field's user: a search is driven through a function binding of the text, and an active option that a shrinking list no longer holds counts as none.

```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="tilie/tilie.css">
<div id="timelines" style="height: 600px"></div>
<script type="module">
  import { mount } from "./tilie/tilie.js";
  const editor = mount(document.getElementById("timelines"), { shortcuts: "editor" });
</script>
```

`mount(element, options)` refuses an element that has children or already holds an editor, marks it `data-tilie`, and returns a `TimeLineEditor` handle: `text()` (the workspace as Save writes it), `load(text, name?)` (open without asking, returning the reader's refusal or `null`), `selection()`, `select(addresses)` (address objects), `focus()` and `destroy()`, which writes the pending autosave, then removes the editor. Every option may be left out:

| Option | Default | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `workspace` | the empty workspace | The workspace file text to open |
| `server` | none: no request, About says "no server", no MatchClaim table is read, and files are opened and saved through the browser alone | `{url, token}`: the API's base URL ending in `/` and the bearer token, or `null` for no header |
| `storage` | none: no autosave, and neither the drawer, the user nor the type profiles remembered | `{slot, inspectorKey, userKey?, profilesKey?}`: the IndexedDB slot (its lock `tilie-autosave/<slot>`), the `localStorage` key of the drawer and, where given, the `localStorage` keys of the user ([The user](#the-user)) and of the type profiles ([Type profiles](#type-profiles)); without a key that one holds until the editor goes |
| `lookup` | `true` | Whether the Metadata tab looks values up in Wikidata, the GND and YouTube and, with a server, in MusicBrainz ([Lookup](#lookup)); with `false` no lookup request is sent, and viewing the tab sends none either |
| `shortcuts` | `"editor"` | Where the file and undo shortcuts are taken: inside the editor, or anywhere in the window |
| `autofocus` | `false` | Whether the canvas takes the focus at mount |
| `noticesUrl` | beside `tilie.js` | The licence texts the About dialog links to |
| `onchange` | | Called with `{fileName, dirty}` after every change and every load; the text is `text()` |
| `onselect` | | Called with the selected addresses whenever the selection changes |

An embedded editor never touches its host's document: no title, no global style, no `location` or `history`, no window listener unless asked (but the keydown listener through which Escape cancels a pick on the canvas, held while a pick is awaited), no request to the server without `server`, no request to a lookup service with `lookup: false`, no storage without `storage` and nothing stored under a key it is not given, no test hook. Two editors on one page share no id and no selection. `pnpm build:embed` builds `src/embed.ts` into `app/packages/editor/dist-embed/`: `tilie.js` (its exports kept), `tilie.css` (every style), `assets/` (the lazily loaded codecs and the fonts, by relative URL) and `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.txt`. Library mode is not used, since it would inline the fonts into the stylesheet. Inside a TypeScript workspace the same entry is `@timetoalign/tilie-editor/embed`.

**Planned.** `tilie --allow-origin <origin>`, repeatable and exact, so that a page on another origin may call a server its user runs locally (the public mode, [The server](#the-server), is another thing: a server serving its own editor to visitors): an allowed origin's preflight answered without the token check, and responses carrying `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` and `Vary: Origin`, never credentials, the Host check unchanged. A Jupyter widget mounting the bundle with the kernel's server and token. The bundle served by `tilie-server` to foreign pages. A read-only viewer of one block, built on the canvas's `readonly`, which already writes nothing. How a WebSocket handshake carries the token, which a browser cannot send as a header, is decided with the command channel; the guard already closes a handshake from a foreign host or origin.

## Not yet wired

Two designs are kept as tested code that nothing but its tests uses.

Deleting a segment, a child of a segment line, leaves a gap in its line, and is refused. The design of closing that gap is kept as two modules:

- core's `manifest/segments.ts`, the gap arithmetic: for a segment S of length l in the segment line Λ, **left extends** (the segment before S grows by l), **right extends** (the segment after S starts where S started and grows by l), **left shifts** (Λ's offset grows by l and Λ shortens by l, so the later segments keep their place in the parent) and **right shifts** (every later segment moves back by l and Λ shortens by l); extending needs an unlocked neighbour on that side, shifting needs Λ unlocked, and shifting left needs Λ to be a child; a way is permitted only when the manifest it leaves passes the schema and the invariants; an unknown length stays unknown (`segmentGaps` lists the permitted ways); core's main entry exports only the `GAPS` table and the `Gap` types, which the menu reads;
- the arranger's `GapMenu.svelte`, the menu that will offer those ways: a form named "Delete segment <id>" with one fieldset, "Closes the gap", of four radio buttons, each named after the neighbour that moves, a disabled one titled with why, the first permitted one chosen, then OK and Cancel.

The anchor index ([MatchClaims and potential MatchStamps](#matchclaims-and-potential-matchstamps)) holds two parts that Show matches does not draw yet, both exported from core's main entry: the **matched spans** (`AnchorIndex.spans`, `MatchedSpans`: per interval MatchClaim passing the mask, in row order, its start anchor and its end anchor) and the **segments** of two timelines (`segmentsOf(index, first, second)`, `Segments`: the anchors between them in the first timeline's order and, per gap between neighbouring anchors, whether a matched span covers it, built on demand in one sweep; one timeline twice is refused). They are kept for tinting the areas between the start and the end of two matched intervals and the gaps between neighbouring anchors that no matched interval covers, and for the anchors a MatchStamp inferred between stated ones interpolates from.

## The schema contract

`@timetoalign/timetoshare` supplies the types generated from its one schema document, the validator of the current schema version, `unknownKeys` and `manifestWarnings` (which core reads through its file value), the description specification's statement functions (`withPair`, `withoutPair`, `withCandidate`, `withBelief`, `candidates`, `rectangles`, `samePair`, `expandIri`, `usedPrefixes`, `withContext`) with `FORMAT_CONTEXT` and its catalogue of identifier systems, verbs and types (`defaultCatalogue`, `recognize`), the complete and minimal forms, the canonical order, the lossless reader and the writer of its serializations (canonical YAML, pretty JSON and RFC 8785 JSON), the manifest fixtures with their minimal twins, the rejection and warning cases, and the two drawing fonts with their one metrics table. It migrates nothing, and neither does TiLiE; the schema repository, not TiLiE, is the source of truth, and `timetoalign` proves conformance in its own tests.

Core is typed through the package's typed forms: `completeManifest(value: LosslessManifest): CompleteManifest`, `minimalManifest` and `canonicalManifest`, where `LosslessManifest` is the schema's type with every number a `JsonNumber` and `CompleteManifest` the same with every defaulted key present. `parseJson` throws `JsonSyntaxError` with the `offset`, `line` and `column` of the fault, which the inspector shows. The validators of both languages raise `SchemaValidationError` with a stable `code` naming the rule broken (`not_a_manifest`, `schema`, `representation`, `finite_number`, `duplicate_id`, `dangling_target`, `conversion_map`, `child_unit`, `child_offset`, `child_end`, `segment_line`, `manifest_id`, `group_member`, `order_key`, `display_order`, `band_order`, `timestamp_timeline`, `timestamp_coordinate`, `match_timestamp`, `alignment_anchor`, `matchstamp`, `resource_timeline`, `resource_type`, `calendar_date`, and for statements `context`, `reserved_key`, `iri`, `element_value`, `provenance_duplicate`, `belief_actor`), and refuse a number that is not finite at its path; core reports every such refusal as `schema_violation` with the validator's path and message.

Until schema 0.2.0 is published on npm, every application package that uses the schema takes it through a `link:` to the `js/` directory of the TimeToShare checkout beside this repository (`link:../../../../TimeToShare/js`), built there with `pnpm build`; the `link:` then gives way to `@timetoalign/timetoshare` `^0.2.0`, and a later schema version not yet released is tried through the same `link:` ([`AGENTS.md`](../AGENTS.md#dependency-policy)). The editor's tests let Vite transform the package (`server.deps.inline`), since the Fixture menu reads its fixtures as text.

## The server

`tilie-server` uses FastAPI and uvicorn ([decisions/0008](../decisions/0008-fastapi-and-uvicorn.md)). It runs in one of two modes, which its settings carry as data and one guard, `OriginGuard`, enforces. A **local run** (`tilie`) binds only `127.0.0.1`, generates a random token for each run, accepts only the Host `127.0.0.1:<port>` or `localhost:<port>` and an Origin of `http://` and one of them, and requires `Authorization: Bearer <token>` on every `/api/` route. `tilie` hands the token to the browser in the URL fragment (`http://127.0.0.1:<port>/#token=<token>`), which a browser never sends to a server, so the token never reaches the access log; the page reads it, removes it from the address bar with `history.replaceState`, and keeps it in the tab's `sessionStorage` (key `tilie.token`), so a reload of that tab stays connected; session storage belongs to the one tab and is cleared when it closes, so a new tab opened at the bare address has no token, and a token of an earlier run is answered 401 (the offline mark). A new fragment token replaces the kept one, and a storage that throws keeps nothing, the fragment's token then lasting until the page is left. The token is never put in a path or a query and never in `localStorage`. A **public run** (`tilie --public-host <host>`, repeatable, [decisions/0038](../decisions/0038-public-mode.md)) binds `0.0.0.0`, accepts only a Host equal to one of the given hosts as given and an Origin, where one is sent, of `https://` or `http://` and one of them, checks no token, and does not route the file routes (404); it refuses with 413 a request body over 16 MiB (`body larger than 16 MiB`, at once where `Content-Length` says so, else as soon as the bytes received pass it, before the route runs) and a posted MatchClaim table of more than 1,000,000 rows or over 128 MiB uncompressed (the sum of its row groups' `total_byte_size`), read from its Parquet footer before any row; at most two computations on a posted body run at once, further ones waiting while health and the editor's own files are never held behind them, and uvicorn holds at most 32 connections. It opens no browser and prints `tilie: public mode on port <port>, answering as <hosts>; no token, no file access` on standard error; `--check`, `--root` and `--no-browser` are refused with it. A foreign Host is refused with 403 `forbidden host`, a foreign Origin with 403 `forbidden origin`, in either mode, and every response carries `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`. The server serves the built app from its wheel and implements `GET /api/health`, whose member `files` is `true` in a local run and `false` in a public one, `POST /api/measure-maps`, `POST /api/match-claims`, `POST /api/match-claims/provenance`, `POST /api/match-claims/belief`, `POST /api/match-claims/events`, `POST /api/flows`, `POST /api/flows/flow-maps`, `/api/reconcile/musicbrainz` and, in a local run, `GET /api/folders`, `GET /api/files` and `PUT /api/files`. `POST /api/measure-maps` reads the posted manifest with timetoshare's `loads` (YAML or JSON, any serialization of the format; the editor posts the minimal canonical YAML of the manifest file, its IDs written `#id`) and converts it with `with_bare_ids`, answers each entry of its `measure_maps`, in order, with its rows as timetoalign's `MeasureMap.expand_rows` expands them, the quarter-note values (`qstamp`, `nominal_length`, `actual_length`, `offset_within_measure`) as Fraction pairs, a measure's `volta` as the map writes it (a pass from 1, or a non-empty list of distinct passes; the client keeps `1` and `[1]` apart), and every ID bare, and refuses with 422 and `{detail: {path, message}}` a body that is no UTF-8 text, no YAML or JSON, no object, writes an ID without `#` (at timetoshare's path and message) or holds a measure map timetoalign refuses. `POST /api/match-claims` takes a package's manifest file and its MatchClaim table in one body ([decisions/0030](../decisions/0030-match-claims-answer.md)): the manifest's length in bytes as a little-endian 32-bit integer, the manifest's text in any serialization, then the table's Parquet bytes. It checks the table against the manifest with timetoshare's `match_claim_problems`, reads it in the package form, each coordinate cell's number written once, in the member its number type names, with timetoalign's `MatchClaimField.from_package_table`, and answers in the same framing: a compact JSON header (`format` `tilie-match-claims`, `version` 1, the rows, the timelines with their number types, the actors, each the IRI timetoalign compares it as, the belief words and every array's offset and length) followed by the columns of [decisions/0029](../decisions/0029-match-claim-columns.md) and every row's beliefs as little-endian arrays, each starting at a multiple of eight bytes from the answer's start; it filters nothing. A body it cannot split or read, and a table the check refuses, are answered 422 with `{detail: {path, message}}`, a refused table with the check's `problems` besides (each `{code, row, column, message}`); without timetoshare's table checks or timetoalign's MatchClaim table the route answers 501. Two routes read a MatchClaim's provenance on demand and record a belief ([decisions/0031](../decisions/0031-match-claim-provenance.md)); the server stays stateless, so each takes the whole body of `POST /api/match-claims` again, behind a little-endian 32-bit length and a JSON object of parameters (a parameter unknown or missing is refused with 422 at its name), and checks the table against the manifest as that route does. `POST /api/match-claims/provenance` takes `{claims}`, a list of MatchClaim IDs, and answers JSON `{claims: [{id, provenance}]}` in the order asked, each entry the MatchClaim's provenance entry as TimeToShare defines it (its row's own, else the one the file states for every row, read through timetoalign's `Provenance`), its absent members left out, each belief's actor the IRI timetoalign compares it as, or `null`; an ID the table lacks is refused at `["claims", i]`. `POST /api/match-claims/belief` takes `{claim, actor, belief, date}` and answers the new table, `application/vnd.apache.parquet`: the manifest is read as timetoalign's `AlignmentBundle`, the table's MatchClaims are added to it, and the belief is recorded by its `add_match_claim_belief` (an actor's earlier belief replaced in place, every other actor's standing), the server building no provenance itself; the posted table takes the provenance column of the package table timetoalign writes (`package_table`), a provenance the file stated for every row now stated by each row, every other column and row kept, columns the table type does not declare and the file's other metadata included; the new table passes timetoshare's table checks against the manifest and its `succession_problems` against the posted table (422 with every problem otherwise) before it is answered, and nothing is stored on the server. Both answer 501 where the installed timetoalign cannot read provenance. `POST /api/match-claims/events` ([decisions/0032](../decisions/0032-match-claim-events.md)) takes the provenance routes' parameters, `{claims}`, then the body of `POST /api/match-claims` unchanged; it checks the table against the manifest as that route does and answers JSON `{claims: [...]}` in the order asked, each `{id, timeline_a_id, event_a_id, event_a_name, timeline_b_id, event_b_id, event_b_name, certainty, is_synchronous}` as timetoalign reads the MatchClaim table: an event ID is an event's or, for a side without an event, one of the reserved strings `NOMATCH_SENTINEL` and `CHUNK_SENTINEL`, never `null`, and `null` is an event without a name. The client refuses an answer with a `null` event ID or synchrony as malformed. An ID the table lacks is refused at `["claims", i]`, and the route answers 501 where the installed timetoalign cannot read MatchClaim tables. The table route and the two provenance routes read the manifest as an `AlignmentBundle`, so a manifest timetoalign refuses is answered 422 with its message, and every actor is the IRI it stands for under the manifest's `@context`, every prefix the context declares (timetoalign's `belief_context` and `actor_iri`): a compact and an absolute spelling of one actor are one actor, as the editor compares them; a belief is stored with its actor as the request spells it, and a `stated_by` is answered as written. `/api/reconcile/musicbrainz` answers the Reconciliation Service API 0.2 of the W3C Entity Reconciliation Community Group for MusicBrainz ([decisions/0028](../decisions/0028-lookup-services.md)): `GET` gives the service manifest; `queries=<JSON>` (`GET`, or a `POST` form) reconciliation queries; `extend=<JSON>` data extension with the properties `name` and `sameAs`, the IRIs timetoshare's catalogue recognises among an entity's URL relationships; `GET …/suggest/entity` entity suggestions; `GET …/properties` the properties a type can be extended with. An identifier is `<type>/<mbid>`, so the identifier space `http://musicbrainz.org/` followed by it is the entity's IRI. One MusicBrainz client per application (`tilie_server.musicbrainz`) keeps MusicBrainz's rules of use for every request the editor causes: one request a second, the next starting at least a second after the last, under a User-Agent naming the server, its version and its repository and no person, each answer kept for the run (the 1,024 least recently used), so a viewer's repeated lookups reach MusicBrainz once; requests use the standard library's `urllib` in a thread and form bodies are read with `parse_qs`. A malformed request is refused with 422 and `{detail}`, a failure of MusicBrainz is 502, and a request needing timetoshare's catalogue answers 501 where the installed timetoshare has none. Three routes serve TimeLineEditor's own files below the run's **root folder**, the user's home folder unless `tilie --root <folder>` names another ([decisions/0033](../decisions/0033-server-file-access.md)): `GET /api/folders?path=` lists a folder, its subfolders, then its files of the kinds the editor reads (workspace, manifest and table files: `.tle.json`, `.yaml`, `.yml`, `.json`, `.parquet`, in any case), each sorted by code point; `GET /api/files?path=` answers the bytes of a file of those kinds; `PUT /api/files?path=` writes the request's body into a workspace file (`.tle.json`, in any case) and no other, through a new file beside it that then takes its place (`os.replace`), a replaced file keeping its mode. A path is POSIX and relative to the root, `""` the root itself, and resolved with every symbolic link followed. No route lists, reads or writes a path with a hidden segment below the root (a name starting with `.`), as asked or as resolved, and a file's kind holds for the name asked and for the file a link leads to. A listing leaves out hidden names and every symbolic link leading out of the root, into a hidden place, to nothing, in a loop or to a file of another kind; a link inside the root is listed as what it leads to. Refusals answer `{detail: {path: ["path"], message}}`: 400 "path outside the root" for an absolute path, a NUL character or a path resolving outside the root; 403 "hidden files are not served"; 400 "the links at the path form a loop"; 400 "a file's path does not end in /" where a file route's path ends in `/`; 403 "TimeLineEditor reads only workspace, manifest and table files" for a read, and 403 "TimeLineEditor writes only workspace files" for a write, whose name or resolved target is of another kind, checked before the file's existence, so that no request learns whether a file of another kind exists (a folder asked of `GET /api/files` under a name of no such kind is refused so too); 404 "no such folder" or "no such file"; 409 "a folder lies at the path"; 403 "permission denied" for a file or folder the user may not read, list or write, a file the user may not write never replaced. `tilie` serves on port 8000 and opens the browser (`--no-browser` does not). A browser keeps what a page stores (the drawer's state, the user, the type profiles) by origin, port included, so one port keeps it from run to run; while port 8000 is taken `tilie` serves on a free port and prints a notice saying so on standard error, and `--port` chooses the port, taken as given. `--root` names the root folder of the file routes, an existing folder. `tilie --check` verifies an installation (`--static-dir` checks a development build). The server takes timetoshare through timetoalign's requirement and imports it inside the route, so it starts without it; an installed timetoalign without the measure-map expansion, or a timetoshare without the reader, answers that route with 501; no timetoalign release carries the expansion yet, so the server is installed against the timetoalign checkout beside this repository, and the `timetoshare` package that timetoalign checks statements with against the TimeToShare checkout beside it ([`AGENTS.md`](../AGENTS.md#dependency-policy)).

**The flow routes** (`routers/flows.py`). A manifest states where a timeline's flow jumps and breaks; in which order its stretches are then played is computed by timetoalign alone, and the server stores nothing. Both routes take the body of the MatchClaim provenance routes without a table: a JSON object of parameters behind its length in bytes (a little-endian 32-bit integer), then the manifest file's text, in any serialization timetoshare reads, to the end of the body; timetoalign reads the manifest whole, checking it as a manifest reader does, before the timeline is looked for. `POST /api/flows` takes `{timeline, convention?, believer?, beliefs?}`: `timeline` a bare ID at any depth, `convention` one of `default`, `single` and `printed` (all three without it), `believer` the actor whose beliefs decide which flow-control elements are read and `beliefs` the belief states read for that actor, only with `believer` (an element is read when the believer's state in it, `undecided` where they hold none, is one of them; every state but `false` without `beliefs`; without a believer, the elements no actor rejects); the editor's client sends its user and the states core's `flowReading` derives from the block's belief filter, and timetoalign's `play` and `atomic_sections` apply them. It answers JSON `{timeline, number_type, atomic_sections, conventions}`: the atomic sections in the order of the axis, each `{name, start, end}`, and for each convention asked for its `runs` (pairs of coordinates on the timeline's own axis, as its number type writes them) the `sections` they cover and its `findings`, each `{kind, coordinate, end?, cause?, message}`, `kind` one of `flow_ends_at_break`, `never_played`, `endless_loop`, `ambiguous_targets` and `undetermined_target`, `end` only for a stretch never played, `cause` where timetoalign names one (`single_playback`: a stretch the `single` flow leaves out that the `default` flow plays); `printed` finds nothing. A timeline without flow control is one section and one run. `POST /api/flows/flow-maps` takes `{timeline, believer?, beliefs?, actor?, date?}`, `believer` and `beliefs` as the computing route reads them, and answers, as `application/json`, the manifest file's text with that timeline's flows written (for a child timeline its atomic sections on the child and its FlowMaps on its root) by timetoalign's `create_flow_maps_from_flow_control`, in the minimal form: its atomic sections as regions, typed as such in their statements, and its FlowMaps of the three conventions. A manifest holding flows written before is written again: a region whose stretch is still an atomic section keeps it, a new stretch gets a new region, the FlowMaps keep their IDs, and a region whose stretch is none any more stays, rejected by the belief `false` of `actor` (who asks) on `date` (`YYYY-MM-DD`, the server's day without one); without an `actor` that case is refused. The IDs of the new regions and FlowMaps are minted at random from one seed taken from the request (the manifest as timetoalign writes it before the flows, the timeline's ID and the reading, `believer` and `beliefs`), the regions along the axis, then the FlowMaps `default`, `single` and `printed`, so one request is answered alike in either form of its manifest and on every asking. A refusal answers 422 with `{detail: {path, message}}`, a parameter's at its name and at `["timeline"]` where the manifest lacks the timeline or timetoalign does not write its flows (a length not known, flow control that cannot be played, a section to reject and no actor); a timetoalign that cannot play flow control answers 501, and every other route still answers. The client's `flows(manifest, timeline, {convention?, believer?})` and `withFlowMaps(manifest, timeline, {believer?, actor?, date?})` send these requests and check their answers.

`create_app(settings)` composes the application from one frozen `ServerSettings` (`host`, `port`, `token`, `static_dir`, `root`, resolved once, and the mode: `public_hosts`, `files`, `body_limit`, `table_rows`, `table_bytes` and `computations`, each `None` or empty in a local run; `allowed_hosts()` and `allowed_origins()` give the guard its hosts and origins, and a settings value without a token that serves files is refused), the security middlewares (`OriginGuard`, the Host, Origin and token guard, which checks a token only where the settings hold one; the referrer policy; and, where a limit is set, the body limit inside the guard), the health, measure-maps, match-claims, MatchClaim provenance, MatchClaim events, flows and MusicBrainz routers, the file router where `files` is set, the one MusicBrainz client of the run (`create_app(settings, musicbrainz=…)` takes another, as the tests do to replay recorded answers) and the static files. It serves no generated OpenAPI document: `/api/openapi.json` is 404, and a committed document is planned with the API it describes. SIGINT and SIGTERM stop the server gracefully with exit code 0, also when it was started with SIGINT ignored, as a background job of a non-interactive shell is.

Planned: loading any supported source through `timetoalign` loaders; conversion-map batches; unfolding and traversal diagnostics with fix suggestions; validation; exports; a committed OpenAPI document with a generated TypeScript client; a GitHub token proxy; and the command layer with an MCP server and a command channel for live sessions as the contract for automation.

## Testing and quality

Tests are README-first: every `tests/README.md` states what proves each behaviour, which values its package owns and derives, and where the oracle is; tests follow it, with exact expected values and no thresholds. Fixtures flow from Python to TypeScript, so both sides work from the same examples: `core` loads and saves every 0.2.0 manifest fixture of the schema package byte for byte, loads each minimal twin to its fixture, and refuses every shared rejection case at its pinned path; layout and the writers derive their expected drawings from the specification and equal its SVG and TikZ fixtures byte for byte, the hand-derived figures included; a component test renders the block node for every drawing fixture and compares its element tree with the fixture's SVG. Property tests with fast-check run generated command sequences from every manifest fixture and check that accepted commands keep every manifest valid, refused ones leave the view unchanged, undo and redo restore every intermediate view and mirror each other step for step, Cancel restores its opening, the journal replays to the snapshot, and a workspace file writes back to its own bytes; on the canvas, that projections reuse node objects, that every gesture's command is accepted, and that stretches are monotonic and reversible.

The timing budgets run alone by `pnpm test:budget`, core's and the arranger's in one Vitest run (`app/vitest.budget.config.ts`), one file at a time, so a budget that fails stops none of the others and every figure is reported. The workspace store's bound reading a 300-block workspace with a 1,000-entry journal at 300 ms, writing it at 250 ms and one command at 1 ms; the same test prints "open and verify", reading that workspace and creating its store, journal replay included, as a measured figure without a limit ([decisions/0019](../decisions/0019-workspace-store-and-journal.md)). The anchor index's bound checking the columns of 250,000 generated MatchClaims and building the index from them at 250 ms together, and a hover's hit test at 1 ms ([decisions/0029](../decisions/0029-match-claim-columns.md)). The arranger's bound Show matches over a table of 250,000 MatchClaims at two device pixels per CSS pixel: what the overlay draws of a view at 16 ms, the whole table in view and zoomed in fourfold, and a hover at 1 ms, the whole of it (the hit test, the hovered MatchStamp's overlay with its groupstamp lines and its path, its flag, the view's path looked up); writing a new view's path is measured and printed without a limit ([Show matches](#show-matches)). They bound Show coordinates' hover at 0.15 ms per pointer frame, a reading and its flag's parts, swept along every line of `bundle_shared_parent`, the fixture with the most lines, groups and ancestor cards ([Show coordinates](#show-coordinates)). Layout's redraw cost is pinned by counting, not timing: on a synthetic manifest of 1,000 elements, every element is resolved by the cascade once, every rule tested once per element, and the metrics table's advances read once per distinct text. The tests that need a production build, the attributions and notices file among them, run alone by `pnpm test:build`.

The canvas and the editor are tested end to end with Playwright in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit and on an emulated touch screen ([decisions/0021](../decisions/0021-playwright.md)), in the pinned Playwright image through rootless podman; `app/e2e/README.md` states what each spec proves. Expected values come from the store through the test hook `window.__tilie`, never from pixels, except the zero-tolerance screenshots, made only inside that image. The hook (`load`, `store`, `selection`, `select`, `viewport`, `setViewport`, `locked`, `mode`, `health`, `text`, `editing`, `open`, `done`, `cancel`, `boxes`, a block's container records, and `bands`, a block's recorded bands in client pixels) and the header's Fixture menu exist only where the page asks for them in a development build or a build made with `VITE_TILIE_TEST_HOOK=1`; a production build, and so the wheel, contains neither, nor any fixture file but what the Examples menu offers (four manifest fixtures and the workspaces of `editor/examples/`), and an embedded editor never installs them. The Fixture menu (`fixtures.ts`) offers every fixture of the installed schema package's version: the manifests at the top of the version's directory and below it (`groups/`, `bundles/`, the corpus fixtures and every `.minimal` twin), the manifest each warning case wraps, the MatchClaim table fixture's manifest (`tables/manifest`), its MatchClaim table served beside it so that showing matches reads it without a question, and the end-to-end workspaces, each labelled by its path below the version's directory without `.json` (`bare`, `groups/corpus_tilia`, `warnings/<name>`, `e2e/small`) and sorted by label. It behaves as the Examples menu does: a manifest fixture is imported as one more block into the open workspace, as Import manifest… adds a file, and an end-to-end workspace replaces the open workspace after the question New and Open ask about unsaved changes. The rejection cases and the drawings are left out. The menu is imported only under the literal build condition, so a production build has no trace of it. The workspaces the suite opens are written from the schema package's manifest fixtures through the store's commands by `pnpm e2e:fixtures`, never by hand, and kept LF by `.gitattributes` ([The component and the page](#the-component-and-the-page)). An embed spec mounts two editors into a foreign host page built in embed mode. The lookup is proven on real answers without a network: the editor's lookup tests and the end-to-end suite replay answers of Wikidata, lobid-gnd, YouTube and the server's MusicBrainz endpoint recorded once (`app/packages/editor/scripts/record-lookup.ts`), the server's tests answers of MusicBrainz recorded once (`server/scripts/record_musicbrainz.py`), and a replaying fetch refuses any request it holds no answer for, so the tests pin the exact requests as well as every candidate. axe-core checks the page for accessibility violations ([decisions/0022](../decisions/0022-axe-core.md)). The gates are Biome, Vitest, fast-check, dependency-cruiser, svelte-check, Playwright, axe-core, LuaLaTeX, Ruff and pytest.

Known gaps: the editor's component tests do not check CSS layout or mobile rendering (the end-to-end tests check the drawer's positions and the phone header), nor a fragment carrying an empty token; the File System Access path is tested against stubbed pickers only, the end-to-end tests taking the file-input and download path; the server's static-file tests do not exercise traversal.

## Performance

A drag costs script time on every pointer move in proportion to the number of nodes, culled or not, because Svelte Flow re-adopts its node list on each move: about 1.2 ms per move at 100 blocks, 2.4 ms at 300 and 5 ms at 900 in headless Chromium. Culling reduces what is mounted (42 of 300 nodes in a zoomed view) but adds its own cost to pan and zoom (3.1 ms per wheel event at 300 blocks against 0.4 ms without), so it starts above 500 blocks. One node per block, a complete projection, reused node objects, memoized drawings and a preview that replaces one node keep a gesture from doing any work of TiLiE's own beyond the previewed block.

`pnpm e2e:budget` checks, for a workspace of 300 blocks in Chromium, from the DevTools Protocol's script time: dragging a block at most 3 ms of script per pointer move; panning and wheel zoom at most 1 ms per move or event; one command at most 1 ms; one block's redrawing within one frame (16.7 ms); and, over two blocks of `flow/virtual_cards` with all their virtual cards placed and one root switched onto a flow, hidden and shown again, at most 1 ms of script per pointer move over the blocks and per Ctrl+wheel event; over the same two blocks, a pan or a zoom with a root's card selected at most 1 ms per event, running no command; showing a hidden root again through the Timelines list at most 60 ms of script; and a long session of four rounds (a root hidden and shown, the flow-control editor opened and closed, 50 renames with 25 undone and 25 redone, 60 pan and zoom events) leaving the DOM's nodes and listeners the same from the second round on and the heap, read after three collections, growing by at most 4 MiB from the second round to the fourth (2.5 MiB measured; the store kept the view of every change before, 10.8 MiB). It also bounds the coordinate flag over a PiecewiseMap of 10^5 breaks at one frame of script per pointer move along the line. Opening a workspace costs reading it and replaying its journal; the store's decision record holds the figures. Heavy data is intended to stay out of the DOM, in WebGL panes and columnar tables, with parsing that could block interaction in workers.

## Dependencies and deployment

Each dependency requires an ADR under [`decisions/`](../decisions/), in the MADR structure, weighing writing it ourselves, naming the audited version and a review date ([decisions/0001](../decisions/0001-record-architecture-decisions.md)); the dependency policy, ranges in manifests, exact development pins, lockfiles and the 14-day release window, is stated in [`AGENTS.md`](../AGENTS.md#dependency-policy).

Install the current product with `pip install tilie-server`, then run `tilie`. The wheel contains the built browser app, so starting it needs neither Node.js nor a download. The container image (`Dockerfile` at the repository root) serves the editor and the server in public mode on a hosting platform ([the README](../README.md#a-public-instance), [decisions/0038](../decisions/0038-public-mode.md)). Planned deployment forms are a hosted browser app talking to a user-run local server, a desktop wrapper and collaborative hosting.
